Level 2 · C051·M4

Numerical integration

Your goalEstimate a volume from flow rates sampled at different times, using trapezoids, then state what this calculation allows us to conclude.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

A pump fills a tank. Its flow rate tells us how many litres would pass through in a minute if that rate stayed constant. The volume added also depends on time: a high flow rate for a very short time does not necessarily add much water to the tank.

We have fictional readings: 2 L/min at 0 min, 6 L/min at 2 min, then 4 L/min at 5 min. They do not tell us everything that happens between readings. To calculate, we choose to join the points with straight line segments, as in linear interpolation.

Under a segment, the region between the flow rate line and the time axis forms a trapezoid. Its width represents a duration; its average height represents a flow rate. Their product represents litres. Adding these partial volumes gives numerical integration: a calculation of an accumulated quantity from limited information.

The diagram shows our reconstruction of the flow rate. It does not represent a continuous measurement of the pump. The 3 readings define 2 intervals; their durations differ. We estimate only the volume added, without assuming that the tank was empty.

The rule to remember

For each interval, multiply its duration by the average of its endpoint flow rates; add the volumes and state the interpolation assumption.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

How many litres has the pump added?

Return to the fictional readings (time in min; flow rate in L/min): (0 ; 2), (2 ; 6), (5 ; 4). Calculate the volume under their piecewise linear interpolation. The readings are treated as exact to focus on the choice of reconstruction.

  1. Define the quantity we want to find

    t denotes elapsed time, in minutes; q(t) denotes the instantaneous flow rate at that time, in litres per minute. a and b are the start and end times, with a < b. Here a = 0 min and b = 5 min. The flow rate is assumed to be continuous and nonnegative over this interval; no water is removed from the tank by withdrawal or overflow.

    V=∫abq(t)dt

    V is the volume added, in litres. The integral sign denotes the accumulation of small volumes q(t) × duration over the whole period; dt indicates that accumulation is with respect to time. We do not know q(t) between readings. We will therefore calculate an approximation to V.

  2. Turn the segments into a calculation rule

    n is the number of intervals and has no unit. The times tᵢ are strictly increasing; the one with index 0 equals a, and the one with index n equals b. qᵢ denotes the flow rate read at time tᵢ. The index i identifies an interval, from 0 to n − 1. Its duration hᵢ is positive and is expressed in minutes.

    hi=ti+1−ti

    If the flow rate follows the segment between its endpoints, its average over the interval is the arithmetic mean of the flow rates at both ends. The area of the trapezoid therefore equals the duration multiplied by this average. Tn denotes the sum of the trapezoid volumes, in litres; Σ means “add over all the indicated indices”.

    Tn=∑i=0n−1hiqi+qi+12

    This formula allows different durations. It gives the exact volume of the chosen interpolation and an estimate of the volume delivered by the pump. In our case n = 2.

  3. Calculate durations before volumes

    First interval: 2 − 0 = 2 min. Second interval: 5 − 2 = 3 min. Check: 2 + 3 = 5 min, which is the whole duration 5 − 0. Counting readings instead of intervals would add a duration that does not exist.

    The difference between times is necessary: the same pair of flow rates over a longer interval delivers more water. No common step size can replace the two durations here.

  4. Form the first trapezoid

    First we add the endpoint flow rates: 2 + 6 = 8 L/min. Dividing by 2 gives their average: 4 L/min. We then multiply by the 2 min of this interval.

    2 min×2+62 L/min=8 L

    Another way to check: the rectangle at a flow rate of 2 L/min contributes 4 L. The triangle above it has width 2 min and height 6 − 2 = 4 L/min. Its volume is 2 × 4 / 2 = 4 L. The rectangle and triangle give 8 L, just like the trapezoid.

  5. Form the second trapezoid and combine the volumes

    The flow rate decreases but remains positive: water keeps entering. Its interpolated average is (6 + 4) / 2 = 5 L/min. Over 3 min, this gives 15 L.

    T2=8+15=23 L

    We add litres because each contribution already includes its duration. The average of the readings alone does not account for the time between them. For this reconstruction, the average flow rate over the period is 23 / 5 = 4.60 L/min. Multiplying this exact value by 5 min gives 23 L again.

  6. Check the meaning and state the scope

    Along the drawn segments, the flow rate stays between 2 and 6 L/min. Over 5 min, the volume of this interpolation must therefore lie between 10 and 30 L: 23 L satisfies these bounds. The units also work: min × L/min = L.

    These bounds apply to the interpolation. To extend them to the actual volume, we would need to know that the actual flow rate stays within the same bounds between readings. Peaks can go undetected there. Exact calculation with fractions does not remove uncertainty about this path.

    The result to report is therefore: volume added estimated at 23 L using trapezoids, assuming linear interpolation between readings. A new intermediate reading may reveal a missed variation; on its own, it does not provide a general error guarantee.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I add the flow rates and get litres.”

    A sum of flow rates is still in litres per minute. Multiply an average flow rate by the corresponding duration to obtain a volume. Check the units before adding the contributions.

    Find the average and then the duration in the calculation

  • “Each reading gives a trapezoid of the same width.”

    A trapezoid lies between two consecutive times: 3 readings give 2 intervals. Here their widths are 2 and 3 min. Start again from the positions of the points on the time axis.

    Read the intervals in the diagram again

  • “The operations are exact, so the actual volume is certain.”

    The operations determine exactly the area under the chosen segments. They do not measure the variations between readings. The last exercise asks precisely whether a difference between two reconstructions is an error bound.

    Examine the proposed guarantee in the exercises

Exercise 1 · Basic check

The pump maintains its flow rate

Consider a variation of the first interval: the flow rate really stays constant at 6 L/min for 2 min. Calculate the volume directly, then using the trapezoidal rule. Why must the results agree?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

At a constant flow rate, each minute adds the same amount of water.

Hint 2

The flow rates at both endpoints are equal; their average keeps that value.

Compare with the solution

Direct calculation: 6 L/min × 2 min = 12 L. Using trapezoids: 2 × (6 + 6) / 2 = 12 L.

The trapezoid becomes a rectangle. Since the problem guarantees a constant flow rate throughout the interval here, the interpolation represents the actual flow rate, and the resulting volume is exact under the assumptions of the scene.

Check your reasoning

  • Show both calculations and the result in litres.
  • Justify exactness using the given constant flow rate, rather than just the precision of the operations.

Exercise 2 · Application with new information

A reading reveals a peak

Return to the readings in the example. Add a fictional reading: at 3 min, the flow rate is 8 L/min. Recalculate the volume using trapezoids and all the points. Give the new durations, the partial volumes, the total and the difference from 23 L.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The first trapezoid stays unchanged. Split the second at the time of the new reading.

Hint 2

The new durations are 3 − 2 and 5 − 3; do not give them the same width.

Compare with the solution

From 2 to 3 min, the duration is 1 min and the average is (6 + 8) / 2 = 7 L/min. The volume is 7 L.

From 3 to 5 min, the duration is 2 min and the average is (8 + 4) / 2 = 6 L/min. The volume is 12 L. Check the durations: 1 + 2 = 3 min.

The recalculated part contributes 7 + 12 = 19 L. With the first trapezoid: 8 + 19 = 27 L. The difference between reconstructions is 27 − 23 = 4 L.

The new point contradicts the segment previously chosen between 2 and 5 min. It reveals a variation missed by that reconstruction. The new total uses more information, but the paths between points are still assumed to be linear.

Check your reasoning

  • Keep the first volume and replace only the second with its new contributions.
  • Use the durations specific to the new intervals.
  • Report 27 L as the new estimate and 4 L as the difference between estimates.

Exercise 3 · Analysis of a conclusion

Does this difference guarantee the error bound?

After the new reading, someone claims: “The actual volume is between 23 and 27 L, since the difference is 4 L.” Are the readings, continuity and positivity of the flow rate enough to guarantee this interval? Explain, then propose information that would make an error check possible.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Imagine a continuous bump between two readings that keeps exactly the same values at the measured times.

Hint 2

The difference between two approximations measures their disagreement. To bound the error, we must also constrain what can happen between readings.

Compare with the solution

No. The difference of 4 L compares two reconstructions. It does not bound their distance from the actual volume. Even after the new reading, a continuous, positive flow rate can form a higher peak between two observed times, then return to the recorded values. The same points remain compatible with a volume greater than 27 L.

Guaranteed lower and upper envelopes for the flow rate between readings would allow us to bound the volume by integrating those envelopes. A justified physical assumption of exactly linear variation would also make the trapezoidal calculation exact. Adding measurements helps reveal variations; a guarantee still requires assumptions that constrain them.

Check your reasoning

  • Reject the guarantee without turning the observed difference into an error bound.
  • Describe a different flow rate that preserves the readings while changing the volume.
  • Propose information about the whole intervals, rather than just additional decimal precision.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does the integral of the pump’s flow rate with respect to time represent?

The volume added during the interval: the products flow rate × short duration accumulate. With flow rate in L/min and time in min, the result is in litres.

Recall 2How can you reconstruct the contribution of an interval without rereading the formula?

Calculate its duration, average the flow rates at its endpoints, then multiply. This area is exact for the interpolation segment; using it as the actual volume requires an assumption about the flow rate between readings.

Recall 3Why can we not always put a common step size in front of the whole sum?

The times may be unevenly spaced. Each average flow rate must then be multiplied by its own duration, found by subtracting two consecutive times.

Recall 4What remains uncertain when all the trapezoidal calculations are exact?

The flow rate between readings, and therefore the actual volume. Exact arithmetic certifies the operations on the reconstruction; it does not certify that reconstruction.

Recall 5Why are an extra reading and the difference between totals not enough to guarantee an error bound?

They may reveal a missed variation, but other variations remain possible between readings. An error bound requires justified information about these intervals, such as guaranteed envelopes for the flow rate.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Reconstruct without help: the accumulated quantity and its units; the known times and flow rates; the choice of segments between readings; the duration and then the average on each interval; the sum of the volumes; a geometric check and a units check; the distinction between calculated area and actual volume.
