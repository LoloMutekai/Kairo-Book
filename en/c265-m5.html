Level 4 · C265·M5

Validation outside the simulator and its limits

Your goalCompare a frozen policy with a baseline in trials separate from training, calculate their returns, and limit conclusions to what these trials support.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

A cart must carry a bin from the entrance of a workshop to the loading dock. In simulation, it learned to go through a central door, which is always open there. A detour goes around this door. The direct route seems better, but the program learned to act in the world described by the simulator. What happens if the physical door stays closed?

The policy is the rule that chooses an action from the observed state. We freeze the candidate policy after training: it takes the direct route and waits at the door if necessary. The baseline takes the detour. During evaluation, their rules stay fixed and no random exploration is added. The rule for stopping the cart is part of the setup being evaluated.

We need to distinguish three pieces of evidence: a result in the training simulator; a variant of the model that introduces a problem with the door; and trials on a physical cart, separate from training. Changing the assumptions of the simulator can reveal a weakness. It does not produce an observation from outside the simulator.

The entire scene is fictional, including the traces presented as records from a physical test setup. The rewards, durations, probabilities, and results are invented teaching data, with no actual readings or observed gains. The case file lets us reason about validation; this page carries out no trials.

The rule to remember

Freeze the policy, state the criteria, separate trials from training, and compare with a baseline. Success under a model remains conditional on that model; an external observation remains limited to the protocol used to obtain it.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Does the direct route hold up at a physical door?

In our fictional case file, delivery earns b = 12 points per delivery and each step costs c = 2 points per step, including waiting. The trial stops at delivery or at H = 4 steps. The dimensionless discount factor γ equals 1: all rewards within the window count in the same way. Let us compare the candidate with the detour, then interpret trials separate from training.

  1. Define the quantity being evaluated before choosing a result

    A step is a control interval of fixed duration, identical for both policies. T counts the steps taken up to the stop; L equals 1 delivery if the bin reaches the dock within the window, 0 otherwise. b is expressed in points per delivery; c in points per step. The return G is the total reward, in points. Delivery is counted only once.

    G=bL−cT

    The letter π denotes the candidate policy; “ref” denotes the detour. In this simplified test setup, the observed state comprises the position of the cart and the indication of whether the door is open, both assumed to be measured correctly. For n trials, Gᵢ is the return from trial i, where i is just an index. Ḡ is their mean, calculated by adding the returns and dividing by the number of trials n. Δ will be the candidate mean minus the baseline mean, in points. A positive value favors the candidate in this case file.

    The decision criteria, fixed before the trials, require Δ ≥ 0 points, no contact, and no departure from the test area. A protective stop interrupts the trial, remains in the results, and requires an investigation before resuming. Safety is checked separately from the score. Here, the window is an evaluation choice: stopping at H does not mean that the world has reached a terminal state of the learning problem. No assumed future return is added beyond this limit.

  2. Calculate the advantage in the initial simulator

    The direct route delivers in T = 2 steps, so L = 1. Its cost is 2 × 2 = 4 points. Its return is 12 × 1 − 4 = 8 points. The detour delivers in 3 steps: cost 2 × 3 = 6 points; return 12 − 6 = 6 points.

    The difference is 8 − 6 = 2 points in favor of the candidate. This is an exact comparison within this deterministic simulator, where the door is always open. Repeating the same conditions does not test the blockage missing from the model.

  3. Change an assumption without calling it an observation

    Let us build a variant: at the start of each trial, the door is blocked for the entire window with the stated dimensionless probability p = 1/4 = 0.25; otherwise it stays open, with probability 1 − 1/4 = 3/4. The detour does not go through the door. These probabilities are invented to test the model, rather than estimated from the trials that follow.

    If the door stays blocked, the candidate does not deliver. It incurs 4 steps of cost: 2 × 4 = 8 points; G = 12 × 0 − 8 = -8 points. Here, the symbol J denotes the expected return under this variant: the theoretical weighted average using its probabilities.

    J=(1−p)Gouvert+pGbloqué

    The open branch contributes 3/4 × 8 = 6 points. The blocked branch contributes 1/4 × (-8) = -2 points. Their sum is 4 points; the difference from the detour is 4 − 6 = -2 points. The ranking reverses. This is a counterexample to robustness across these models, rather than a reading from the physical cart.

  4. Separate training, tuning, and external trials

    The fictional case file now describes an enclosed physical test setup, with the cart empty, an independent stop, and monitoring. Before the trials, we record and retain the policy version, the observations it receives, its stopping rule, the reward, the horizon, the criteria, and the door situations. The Q table no longer receives updates. The scenarios used to tune the simulator stay separate from the scenarios reserved for this evaluation.

    The case file reserves 3 situations: door open, door blocked, door open. Each policy is tested separately, resetting to the same starting state with the same door situation; the order in which the policies run is alternated to limit an order effect. States, control commands, deliveries, durations, and interventions are logged. We do not claim to infer the direct route from a trace in which only the detour was taken.

    We also retain the trial with no delivery and its waiting cost. In this case file, checks show no contact: 0 contacts, no departure from the area, and no protective intervention. This finding concerns the trials described. It does not turn the chosen situations into a representative sample of all future uses.

  5. Calculate all returns in the external trial records

    Candidate, door open: L = 1, T = 2, so G = 8 points. Door blocked: L = 0, T = 4, so G = -8 points. Final open trial: G = 8 points. The sum is first 8 + (-8) = 0, then 0 + 8 = 8 points.

    G¯π=8+(-8)+83=83≈2.67 points

    Each detour delivers in 3 steps and earns 6 points. Its sum is 3 × 6 = 18 points; its mean is 18 ÷ 3 = 6 points. Exact fractions are retained until display. A mean from this small, selected case file is descriptive; we assign it neither a confidence interval nor a guarantee of generalization.

  6. Compare and check using another decomposition

    The external difference is Δ = 8/3 − 6 = -10/3 points, or approximately -3.33 points. It is below the threshold 0: the candidate fails the score criterion in this case file, even with 0 contacts observed. The planned decision is to retain the baseline and analyze the blockage before producing a new version.

    Let Ω be the difference between the return of the candidate in the initial simulator and its external mean. This quantity is measured in points: Ω = 8 − 8/3 = 16/3 points, or approximately 5.33 points. It is the difference between these specific numbers, rather than a guaranteed estimate of simulator bias in every situation.

    Check the arithmetic: 3 × (8/3) = 8 points, matching the sum of the traces; the mean stays between -8 and 8 points. Check by recombining: Δ + Ω = -10/3 + 16/3 = 2 points, the simulated advantage 2. This identity recovers the same difference by going through the external comparison. It uses the fact that the baseline earns 6 points here in both settings.

  7. Give a conclusion that respects the scope of the evidence

    The proposed cause is specific: the door being always open favored the direct route. The variant shows that the ranking depends on this assumption; the fictional physical trial records illustrate how we would look for this weakness outside the simulator. The means of the variant and the case file differ because they give different weights to situations. There is no justification for merging them as though they were the same sample.

    Even a candidate that passed these trials would not be validated for a different load, a different door, a delayed sensor, or a different floor. These changes can alter the observed transitions and the states visited by its own policy. A perfectly solved simulator is insufficient if its assumptions are false; a high reward does not cover an omitted constraint.

    If Q, the sensors, or the reward are adjusted after these results are seen, these trials become tuning data. New cases must be reserved for the next evaluation. Moving from the test setup to actual use would still require separate authorization, verification of the setup actually installed, monitoring criteria, and a way to return to the baseline. Our calculation provides evidence for none of these steps.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the results. Reconstruct the return from the blocked trial, then explain how a simulated advantage can coexist with an unfavorable external difference. Specify which piece of evidence is a model, which would be an observation, and why tuning after reading the results requires other trials.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I tested another probability in the simulator, so I have validated the policy outside the simulator.”

    The probability 1/4 is an additional assumption. It gives an expected return under another model without producing a physical reading. Identify the source of the transitions and keep the initial model, the variant, and the external trials separate.

    Reread the three pieces of evidence and their scopes

  • “The blocked trial did not finish, so I will remove it from the mean.”

    The window H and the handling of an unfinished trial were fixed. This route consumes 4 steps and earns -8 points; removing it would hide precisely the weakness being investigated. Retain its trace and explain why it stopped.

    Recalculate all returns, including waiting

  • “I corrected Q after the trials; the same case file now proves that the fix generalizes.”

    The results that were consulted guided the tuning. They can check the fix on known cases, but they no longer provide an independent evaluation. Freeze the new version and reserve other situations before drawing a conclusion.

    Reconstruct the protocol in the final exercise

Exercise 1 · Explained application

Account for a route with no delivery

In the cart scene, the door stays blocked. The trial reaches H = 4 steps; the bin is not delivered. Calculate G and explain why neither 0 points nor the value promised by the Q table replaces this result.

Compare with the solution

L = 0 and T = 4. The bonus is 12 × 0 = 0 points; the cost is 2 × 4 = 8 points. The return is therefore 0 − 8 = -8 points.

The absence of delivery does not erase the time consumed. A Q value is an estimate learned during training; it is not a reward observed within this window. The route remains unfinished at the chosen limit, without this demonstrating a physical terminal state.

  • Separate an estimate of the future from the rewards counted under the protocol.
  • Distinguish the end of the window from a terminal state of the problem.

An additional blocked case

Compare with the solution

The number of trials becomes 3 + 1 = 4. Candidate sum: 8 + (-8) = 0 points; mean: 0 ÷ 4 = 0 points. Baseline sum: 4 × 6 = 24 points; mean: 24 ÷ 4 = 6 points.

Δ = 0 − 6 = -6 points. The results become even less favorable to the candidate in this case file. But these selected cases do not establish the future frequency of blockages; 0 points is therefore not a universal value for the policy. The verdict remains limited to the protocol.

Check your reasoning

  • Change the sum and the number of trials together.
  • Find Δ = -6 points, using candidate minus baseline for the sign.
  • Do not turn a descriptive mean into a guaranteed prediction.

Exercise 3 · Critical transfer

Repair a validation case file

After reading the case file, the team changes the Q table to go around the door. It replays the same cases in a variant of the simulator and announces external validation. It also has old physical traces of the detour alone. Propose a protocol that would actually allow the new candidate to be examined, and specify what this evidence is insufficient to establish.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Separate the policy change, the source of the new transitions, and the selection of cases.

Hint 2

A different action can lead to another state: a detour trace does not necessarily contain the consequences of the direct route.

Compare with the solution

Record and retain the version and settings of the new candidate, fix the reward, horizon, score criteria, and constraints, then reserve external situations that were not used to correct it. A modified model remains a model; the old cases that have been reread now serve for tuning or regression checks.

Plan an authorized external evaluation with defined limits, an independent stop, a baseline, comparable starting states, and logs of states and actions, deliveries, time, and interventions. Retain unfinished trials according to the stated rule. A protective stop must remain in the results. The result must name the version tested and the conditions covered.

The detour traces describe the actions actually taken. They do not by themselves provide the transitions that another policy would have caused. Evaluating a new policy from such traces would require additional assumptions and action coverage, which have not been established here. Finally, neither a favorable score nor the absence of contact in these trials would demonstrate universal safety or an already deployed system in operation.

Check your reasoning

  • Reserve new cases after tuning and identify the frozen version.
  • Distinguish a simulation variant from an external observation.
  • Compare with a baseline under stated conditions and retain failures.
  • Explain why a trace from one policy does not perform the actions of the other.
  • Limit the conclusion and plan monitoring and rollback before any authorized use.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1Why does a simulator variant alone not validate a policy outside the simulator?

It changes stated assumptions and allows us to examine how the result depends on the model. It provides no observation of transitions and rewards from an external environment.

Recall 2What must be frozen and kept separate before examining transfer for the cart?

The policy and its observations, the reward, the window, the criteria, and the stopping setup must be frozen; training, tuning, and situations reserved for evaluation must be kept separate. A change after reading the results requires other cases for a new evaluation.

Recall 3How do we account for the cart trial in which it waits at the door without delivering?

Apply the stated rule G = bL − cT: no bonus, the cost of all steps up to the limit, giving -8 points. Retain the trial; do not invent rewards beyond the window.

Recall 4Why are the old detour traces insufficient to evaluate any candidate policy?

They contain the consequences of the actions taken. Another policy may choose other actions and visit other states; their consequences are not observed in these traces. Suitable assumptions and coverage are still necessary.

Recall 5What do neither a favorable mean nor the absence of contact in a few trials prove?

They prove neither behavior in all future situations, nor compliance with an omitted constraint, nor actual installation of the policy. The conclusion must cite the version, the conditions tested, and the limits of the evidence.

Reconstruct the reasoning
