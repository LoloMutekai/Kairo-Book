Level 1 · C035·M2

Cumulative distribution function

Your goalBuild the cumulative distribution function of a waiting time, then find the probability of an interval by subtracting two cumulative probabilities.

Before you start
c030-m5 : Poisson · c034-m5 : mean value · c035-m1 : univariate density

✿01 See the idea

You are waiting for a shuttle. The question “what is the probability that it arrives after a wait of at most 6 min?” includes all waiting times up to that limit. Imagine moving a vertical ruler to the right: it leaves an increasing share of the possible waiting times behind it.

The scene and its values are fictional. We choose a model of continuous waiting times between 2 and 10 min: intervals of equal duration within these bounds have equal probability. The density is therefore constant on this interval and 0 elsewhere. This choice simplifies the calculation; it does not describe any observed timetable.

The density gives a height. Probability comes from an area under this density. The cumulative distribution function directly gives the area accumulated to the left of a threshold, including the threshold. In the diagrams, the same question appears as an area under the density and then as a height on the cumulative probability curve.

Before 2 min, there is nothing to accumulate in this model. From 10 min onward, all the probability has already accumulated. The cumulative probability can therefore neither decrease nor keep increasing beyond the last possible waiting time.

The rule to remember

F(x) gives the probability up to x. To isolate a slice between u and v, subtract the entire cumulative probability up to u from the cumulative probability up to v.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Arriving by a threshold, then isolating a slice of waiting times

For the same fictional shuttle, let us calculate the probability of waiting at most 6 min, then the probability of waiting more than 6 min and at most 8 min. No actual waiting time is observed: we calculate using the stated model.

  1. Set the density and its units

    X is the random variable representing the waiting time, expressed in minutes. Here, a random variable assigns a duration to each possible shuttle arrival. The bounds are a = 2 min and b = 10 min. The letters t and x denote durations in the same unit; t ranges over waiting times, while x sets the threshold.

    The density f(t) is 1/8 per minute for a ≤ t ≤ b, and 0 elsewhere. Its height is determined by the total area: the width b − a = 8 min, multiplied by the height 1/8 per minute, gives 1. Minutes multiplied by a quantity per minute give a probability with no unit.

    (10−2)×18=1

  2. Define what F accumulates

    P denotes probability under our model. The event X ≤ x means “the waiting time does not exceed the threshold x”. By definition, its probability is F(x), where F is the cumulative distribution function of X. F(x) has no unit and belongs to the interval [0, 1].

    F(x)=P(X≤x)=∫−∞xf(t) dt

    The integral adds up the area under the density to the left of x. The lower limit −∞ means that we include all waiting times below the threshold; it is not a physical waiting time. The symbol dt indicates that we accumulate over the duration t. This integral formula applies to a variable with a density, as in this example.

    As x increases, the previous event remains included in the new one: F is therefore nondecreasing, meaning that it never decreases. It can stay constant where no additional probability is added. With a density, the probability of an exact duration is 0: changing whether the endpoints of an interval are included does not change its probability.

  3. Build the function for all thresholds

    If x ≤ a, the accumulated area is 0. If a < x < b, the accumulated part is a rectangle with width x − a and height 1/(b − a). If x ≥ b, all the area has already been included: F(x) = 1. All three cases are needed.

    F(x)={0si x≤ax−ab−asi a<x<b1si x≥b

    Check where the pieces meet: the fraction equals 0 at a and 1 at b. It therefore joins the plateaus without a jump. Here, the limiting values 0 on the left and 1 on the right are already reached at the bounds; for a density extending without a bound, the cumulative probability may only approach them.

  4. Calculate the requested cumulative probabilities

    The threshold of 6 min is within the bounds. We must subtract a because the area begins at 2 min: the accumulated width is 6 − 2 = 4 min. Dividing it by the total width gives the fraction of the area already accumulated.

    F(6)=6−210−2=48=0.50

    The required probability is exactly 1/2, or 0.50, with no unit. The threshold is in minutes, but the result is a probability. At the threshold of 8 min, the same reasoning gives a width of 6 min: F(8) = 6/8 = 3/4 = 0.75.

  5. Subtract the waiting times already counted

    For thresholds u < v, both expressed in minutes, the event X ≤ v splits into X ≤ u and u < X ≤ v. These parts do not overlap. The cumulative probability F(v) therefore already includes F(u), which must be subtracted to leave only the slice.

    P(u<X≤v)=F(v)−F(u)

    Here u = 6 min and v = 8 min. The probability of waiting more than 6 min and at most 8 min is 3/4 − 1/2 = 1/4, or 0.25. The cumulative probabilities are in the right order: the larger threshold gives the larger cumulative probability.

  6. Check using the area and the complement

    Another way to calculate the slice: its width is 8 − 6 = 2 min. Without using cumulative probabilities, the area of the rectangle gives 2 × 1/8 = 1/4. This gives exactly the same result as the earlier subtraction.

    The complement of the event X ≤ 8 is X > 8: it contains everything missing from the total probability 1. Its probability is therefore 1 − 3/4 = 1/4. The three parts, up to 6, then up to 8, then beyond, sum to 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/4 = 1.

    These checks prove that the calculation is consistent within the fictional model. They do not prove that the waiting time for a real shuttle follows this density.

Explain it in your own words

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The probability of waiting at most 6 min is the height 1/8.”

    1/8 is a density per minute. Multiply it by the accumulated width 4 min: the area is 1/2, with no unit. On the cumulative distribution curve, the height F(6) directly gives this probability.

    Compare the area and the height in the two diagrams

  • “Between two thresholds, I add their cumulative probabilities.”

    The cumulative probability at the larger threshold already includes that at the smaller one. Subtract: F(8) − F(6) = 1/4. Adding would count the waiting times before the smaller threshold again.

    Review the splitting of events and the check using the area

  • “I can extend the fraction (x − a)/(b − a) to all thresholds.”

    The fraction describes the cumulative probability only between the bounds. Before a, the area is 0; after b, it is 1. A supposed probability that is negative or greater than 1 indicates that the wrong branch has been used.

    Fix the rule in the last exercise

Compare with the solution

We subtract all waiting times up to 4 min from the cumulative probability up to 8 min: F(8) − F(4) = 3/4 − 1/4 = 1/2, or 0.50.

The rectangle over the window has width 8 − 4 = 4 min. Its area is 4 × 1/8 = 1/2, the same result as with cumulative probabilities.

Exercise 3 · Diagnosis and repair

A rule that forgets the plateaus

A poster states G(x) = (x − 2)/8 for all thresholds x; G is the name of this proposed rule. Calculate G(1) and G(12). Can it be the cumulative distribution function of our waiting time? Fix it over the entire real line, then explain why the corrected function never decreases.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

A probability must stay between 0 and 1. The proposed thresholds lie outside the possible waiting times.

Hint 2

Before the bound a, nothing has accumulated. After b, everything has already accumulated. Keep the fraction only between the bounds.

Compare with the solution

G(1) = (1 − 2)/8 = -1/8 = -1/8. This number is negative. G(12) = (12 − 2)/8 = 10/8 = 5/4, greater than 1. Neither can be a probability.

The corrected rule is F(x) = 0 for x ≤ 2 min; F(x) = (x − 2)/8 for 2 < x < 10 min; F(x) = 1 for x ≥ 10 min. In particular, this gives F(1) = 0 and F(12) = 1.

The first plateau is constant; the fraction increases as x increases because its denominator is positive; the last plateau is constant. Where the pieces meet, we reach 0 and then 1 without a decrease. The total area and the requirement that the function never decreases are therefore respected.

Check your reasoning

  • Produce -1/8 and 5/4, and explain why they cannot be probabilities.
  • Write the branches before, between, and after the bounds.
  • Justify why the function never decreases and how the pieces join, rather than merely cutting off the invalid values.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does F(x) mean, and what are the units of x and F(x) in this scene?

F(x) = P(X ≤ x), the probability of waiting at most x. The threshold x is in minutes; F(x) is a probability with no unit.

Recall 2In which diagram do you read an area, and in which do you read a height?

On the density f, the area up to the threshold gives the probability. On the cumulative distribution F, the same probability is read directly as the height at the threshold. The density is per minute; the cumulative probability has no unit.

Recall 3Why is P(u < X ≤ v) equal to F(v) − F(u) for u < v?

The event X ≤ v contains X ≤ u and the slice u < X ≤ v, with no overlap. Subtracting F(u) from the total F(v) isolates the slice. The complement beyond v has probability 1 − F(v).

Recall 4Reconstruct the branches of F for the waiting time between 2 and 10 min. Why are plateaus needed?

F(x) is 0 before or at 2 min, (x − 2)/8 between the bounds, and 1 after or at 10 min. No event has yet accumulated before the first possible waiting time; all the probability has accumulated after the last.

Recall 5Can we remove the equals sign at an endpoint without changing the probability in this example? Which assumption allows this?

Yes: X has a density, so an exact duration has probability 0. Including or excluding the endpoints does not change the area here. This reasoning does not automatically apply to a discrete variable.
