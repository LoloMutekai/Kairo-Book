Level 2 · C043·M4

Summary and loss of detail

Your goalCheck that a summary fits within the context and retains the conditions needed to answer the question.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

You are preparing to visit a studio. The full note says: “Studio opens Saturday. Registration required. Badge required. Enter via the street.” To save space, a reminder keeps only “Studio opens Saturday.” Then you ask: “May I enter Saturday with my badge without registering?” The summary has kept the day but dropped the condition needed to answer.

A summary is a shortened representation of a source. It can copy passages unchanged, which is extractive summarization, or rephrase them. Extraction does not automatically retain all the useful conditions; rephrasing must also be checked to ensure it does not change the meaning. Truncation stops at a length limit, possibly in the middle of a condition.

Retrieving a note, choosing the passages to use, then summarizing those passages are separate operations. A detail can disappear because its passage was not selected, because the summary omits it, or because a final cut removes it. You need to find the stage at which the condition left the context being passed on.

This scene, its texts and its budget are fictional. We keep the full note separately: “missing from the summary” means missing from this context, without claiming that the source has been deleted. The task concerns loss of detail rather than building a complete document retrieval system.

The rule to remember

Check space, then meaning: a summary that is short enough must still retain the conditions on which the answer depends. Keep a reference to the full source for other questions.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Which reminder should you keep for the studio visit?

The full note contains 83 characters. The context in this paper exercise has a budget of 100 characters: it contains the recent message “May I enter Saturday with my badge without registering?”, a header shared by the note or summary block, then that block. The proposed general summary is “Studio opens Saturday.” Let us calculate the space and check what this choice allows us to know.

  1. Specify the texts, unit and assumptions

    B denotes the budget, C the length of the recent message and H the length of the header. B = 100, C = 55 and H = 9, in characters. The header is “Context:” followed by a space. The quotation marks on the page are not part of the counted strings.

    We count the characters in the strings in calculs.py: each space, letter and punctuation mark counts, with accented letters in precomposed form. This character count is neither a byte count nor a count of language model tokens. It is determined before the page is typeset.

    This simplified model adds no other messages or separators between the counted blocks. The recent message is kept in full; the summary uses only the remaining space. For a real context, you would also need to count all other content and the headers actually passed on, using the unit of its budget.

  2. Reserve space before summarizing

    A is the space available for the summary body, in characters. Subtract C to keep the question, then H to allow for the header. Here C + H does not exceed B; if these blocks already exceeded the budget, shortening only the summary would not be enough.

    A=B−C−H=100−55−9=36 caractères

    After the message, 45 characters remain; after the header, 36 characters. Ls denotes the length of the full source: 83 characters. Since Ls exceeds A, copying the whole note does not fit this budget.

    Check by addition: 83 + 55 = 138 characters, then 138 + 9 = 147 characters. This is 47 characters over budget. You need to select from or shorten the note block for this context.

  3. Check whether the general summary fits

    Lᵣ denotes the length of the chosen summary, and U the total used, both in characters. For the general summary, Lᵣ = 22 characters. The capacity rule is Lᵣ ≤ A, and U = C + H + Lᵣ.

    U=55+9+22=86 caractères

    The subtotal for the message and summary is 77 characters. The header brings the total to 86 characters; 14 characters remain. Replacing the source body with this summary frees 83 − 22 = 61 characters. This result describes the space occupied by these texts, rather than a measured improvement in the answer.

  4. Count retained facts, then find the decisive condition

    Before counting, define a list of 4 distinct facts: opening on Saturday; registration required; badge required; entry from the street. F is the number of facts on this list, K the number still asserted by the summary, and D the number missing. These are fact counts rather than lengths. p = K/F is the retained proportion, with no unit, and F is strictly positive.

    D=F−K=4−1=3 faits absents
    p=KF=14=0.25

    Check: 1 fact kept + 3 facts missing = 4 facts examined. p is between 0 and 1. This count depends on our list and gives each fact equal weight; it is not a universal quality score. Incorrectly added information would need to be identified separately.

    The decisive point is the registration requirement. The summary says nothing about it. Its silence supports neither the conclusion that registration is optional nor that a badge replaces it. The source says “Registration required.”: in this scene, a badge alone is therefore not enough. We had to reread the source to establish this.

  5. Repair for this question and repeat the checks

    Returning to the note, we prepare the targeted summary “Registration and a badge required.” This rephrasing keeps the requirements relevant to the request. Its length Lᵣ is 34 characters, less than the available space of 36 characters.

    The message and this summary contain 89 characters. With the header: 55 + 9 + 34 = 98 characters; the margin is 2 characters. The body is 49 characters shorter than the full source. Reverse check: 98 + 2 = 100 characters.

    On the list, K is now 2 and D is 4 − 2 = 2 facts. p = 2/4 = 1/2 = 0.50, with no unit. The registration condition is present; the day and entry from the street remain missing. For this request about Saturday, the reminder is enough to recall that a badge alone does not replace registration. Another question might require another detail from the note.

  6. Understand why the summary cannot reconstruct everything

    Imagine the same note with “Enter via the courtyard.” instead of “Enter via the street.” A summary that keeps only “Studio opens Saturday.” is compatible with either note. From that alone, you cannot choose between street and courtyard: that detail is no longer available in this representation.

    Rewriting or lengthening this summary alone provides no new evidence about the correct entrance. To recover this detail, you need to consult the retained full note, identified by its provenance, or another source that establishes it. The summary serves as a reminder for the current need; its reference lets you return to the passages left out.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The summary fits, so it contains what is needed.”

    The length inequality does not read the content. Write down the condition on which the answer depends, then check that it is still asserted. In the studio example, keeping the day does not retain the registration requirement.

    Repeat the space check and the fact check

  • “The restriction is missing from the summary, so it does not exist.”

    A summary is a selection. The restriction may appear in the full note, as registration does here. Return to the source before turning an omission into permission.

    Review the condition left out of the summary diagram

  • “I can ask for a longer version of the summary to recover the exact entrance.”

    The same summary sentence fits both the street and courtyard variants. Without an additional source, a longer version could invent this detail. Retrieve the original note and its provenance to distinguish the variants.

    Try diagnosing the variants of the note

Exercise 2 · Justified repair

Choose useful content for the remaining space

Hint 1

Reuse the available space A, then check the new total U.

Hint 2

Separate opening, registration, badge and entry. Which of these facts prevents you from concluding that a badge alone is enough?

Compare with the solution

34 ≤ 36 characters. The total is 55 + 9 + 34 = 98 characters, with 2 characters of margin. The addition 98 + 2 = 100 checks the budget.

Registration and badge are retained: K = 2 facts; opening and entry are missing: D = 2 facts. The proportion is 2/4 = 1/2, or 0.50 with no unit. The count balances: 2 + 2 = 4 facts.

The reminder is useful here because it retains the registration requirement, precisely what the question concerns. A summary of the same length or retaining as many other facts could lose it. You still need to keep the reference to the note for the opening day or the exact entrance.

Check your reasoning

  • Check the length and the requirements in the full note.
  • Distinguish retained facts from missing facts using the stated list.
  • Justify relevance through the registration condition, without treating the fraction of facts as a measure of reliability.

Exercise 3 · Diagnosis and transfer

Can you recover the entrance from the summary?

In the same scene, the question becomes: “Should I enter from the street or the courtyard?” The original note says “Enter via the street.”; its variant replaces this sentence with “Enter via the courtyard.” All other sentences stay identical. The only available summary is “Studio opens Saturday.” Explain whether it distinguishes the notes, then describe a verifiable repair.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Compare what the summary says in each variant: does it change?

Hint 2

A more detailed answer is evidence only if its detail comes from available information. Which source lets you choose the entrance?

Compare with the solution

The summary is identical in the variants, so it does not distinguish street from courtyard. The absence of the word courtyard does not prove that entry is from the street. The summary targeted at registration and the badge would not identify the entrance either.

You need to retrieve the original note with its provenance, then check the passage about entry. For the original note in this exercise, the answer is the street, supported by “Enter via the street.” A reminder for this new question must retain that passage or a faithful rephrasing of it, and a reference to the note.

The repair brings the missing source into the context. Simply rewriting the general summary is not enough to establish the choice. The new message and reminder will also need to be recounted before assembly; the budget calculated for the previous question does not transfer automatically.

Check your reasoning

  • Show that distinct sources give the same summary here.
  • Support the choice of the street with the original note rather than the summary alone.
  • Plan a reminder suited to the new question and repeat the space check on its actual texts.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1Why can a summary that fits the budget prevent a correct answer?

The budget checks a length. The summary may have removed a decisive condition: in the studio example, the day is retained but the registration requirement is missing. You must also compare the content with the need.

Recall 2How do you calculate the space for the summary while keeping the recent message and its header?

A = B − C − H, where B is the budget, C the length of the recent message and H the length of the header, in the same unit. First check that these blocks can fit, then that the summary’s length does not exceed A.

Recall 3Where can a detail disappear before generation?

The passage may not be selected; the summary may omit the detail; final truncation may cut it off. Retrieving the source therefore does not guarantee that all its details will be passed on.

Recall 4Why does the retained proportion K/F of facts not measure the summary’s entire quality?

F is the number of facts on a chosen list and K the number still asserted. The ratio gives these facts equal weight. It isolates neither the decisive fact for a question nor false additions; it supports reading without replacing it.

Recall 5Why does lengthening the general summary fail to prove the exact entrance to the studio?

The same summary sentence is compatible with entry from either the street or the courtyard. It contains nothing that can distinguish them. You need a source that establishes the detail, then must keep its provenance.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without looking, reconstruct the path from the full note to the context: reserve space for recent messages and headers; choose useful facts; summarize without losing their conditions; recount; compare with the source passage. Then explain why a summary compatible with different notes cannot reconstruct the detail that distinguishes them, and how provenance lets you return to the source.
