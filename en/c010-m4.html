Level 0 · C010·M4

Logarithm

Your goalFind a photo’s zoom setting from its lengths, then check the setting using a power.

Before you start
c006-m5 : composition · c010-m1 : exponents · c010-m2 : roots · c010-m3 : exponential function

✿01 See the idea

You find a print of a square photo. Its side measures 32 cm; the original had a side of 4 cm. The zoom setting has been erased. You only know that each whole step multiplies the side by 2. How can you find the setting?

You can work forward from the original: 4 cm, then 8 cm, then 16 cm, then 32 cm. The setting counts successive multiplications, rather than centimetres added. Before continuing, count the steps in the diagram.

A power answers the question “what factor does this setting produce?” The logarithm asks the question in reverse: “what exponent produces this factor?” So you must first compare the final side with the initial side. Their ratio is 8, with no unit; this factor is the input to the logarithm.

The lengths and settings are fictional, chosen so that the calculations can be repeated exactly. We model a zoom that changes the sides without cropping. We calculate neither the photo’s area nor its print quality.

The rule to remember

Divide the final side by the initial side; find the exponent for the stated base; put this exponent back into the power and calculate the length again.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Reconstruct the zoom for the 32 cm print

The initial side is 4 cm and the final side is 32 cm. The zoom multiplies the side by 2 at each whole step. Let us find its setting, keeping length, factor and exponent distinct.

  1. Name what is known and what is missing

    Lᵢ denotes the initial side and L the final side, both in centimetres: Lᵢ = 4 cm, L = 32 cm. The subscript i means “initial”. n denotes the unknown setting, with no unit. The zoom factor x also has no unit.

    The model’s rule is L = Lᵢ × 2ⁿ. It assumes strictly positive lengths expressed in the same unit and uniform enlargement. A positive integer setting counts doublings; a negative setting corresponds to a reduction.

    L=Li×2n

  2. Remove the starting length by taking a ratio

    Dividing by Lᵢ isolates the multiplicative factor. A difference in lengths would count additional centimetres; it would not tell us how many times the initial side fits into the final side.

    The units cancel: cm/cm gives a number with no unit. We obtain x = 8. This calculation must come before the logarithm.

    x=LLi=32 cm4 cm=8

  3. Read the logarithm as a question about a power

    The base a is the positive number whose powers we take; it must differ from 1. The argument x is the strictly positive number to which we apply the logarithm. The result y is the real exponent we seek, with no unit: “a raised to what power gives x?” The symbol ⇔ means that the expressions state the same relation.

    a>0,a≠1,x>0
    y=loga(x)⇔ay=x

    Here, a = 2 and y is our setting n: n = log to base 2 of 8. The argument is not 32 cm. The base also matters: for this same factor 8, the base 8 would give the exponent 1, since 8 raised to the power 1 equals 8. Writing only “log” would leave the base ambiguous.

    For a base greater than 1, the powers increase as the exponent increases; for a base strictly between 0 and 1, they decrease. In both cases, a positive argument determines a unique real exponent. With the base 1, all powers would equal 1 and this inversion would no longer be possible.

  4. Recognize the factor and find the exponent

    Let us break 8 down into factors of 2. We need 3 factors; the exponent is therefore 3, rather than the factor 8. We do not need a decimal approximation in this case.

    8=2×2×2=23
    n=log2(8)=3

    Not all logarithms are integers. Here we have chosen a factor that is exactly an integer power of the base.

  5. Recalculate the length, then test an incorrect setting

    Putting the exponent back into the zoom rule checks the answer: 4 × 2ⁿ = 4 × 8 = 32 cm for n = 3. We recover the required length.

    Another check works in reverse: 32 ÷ 2 = 16 cm; 16 ÷ 2 = 8 cm; 8 ÷ 2 = 4 cm. It took 3 divisions to return to the original.

    If we used the number 32 directly, we would find log to base 2 of 32 = 5, since 2 raised to the power 5 equals 32. Applied to our original, this setting would give 4 × 32 = 128 cm, which is 96 cm too much. Recalculating the length reveals the forgotten reference length.

  6. Distinguish a positive argument from a negative result

    With no change in size, x = 1, so n = 0: 2 raised to the power 0 equals 1 and the side remains 4 cm. To reduce the side to 2 cm, the factor is 1/2 = 0.5; the setting is -1, because 2 raised to the power -1 equals 1/2.

    The factor 0.5 has no unit; the reduced side is in cm. A negative logarithm is therefore allowed: its argument must remain strictly positive. The factor 0 has no real logarithm, because no real power of a positive base equals 0. A negative argument is excluded for the same reason. The setting 0 corresponds to the factor 1, never to the factor 0.

  7. Combine zoom factors

    Applying the factors t and then u multiplies the side by t × u. Here the letters t and u denote strictly positive factors with no unit; p and q denote their logarithms in the same base a: t equals a raised to the power p and u equals a raised to the power q. The power rule gives t × u = a raised to the power (p + q). The logarithm of the product therefore gives p + q.

    loga(tu)=loga(t)+loga(u)

    Similarly, dividing the factors gives t/u = a raised to the power (p − q). We then subtract the exponents. These rules require the same positive base different from 1 and positive arguments.

    loga(tu)=loga(t)−loga(u)

    With t = 8 and u = 4, the quotient is 2; its logarithm to base 2 is 3 − 2 = 1, checked by 2 raised to the power 1 = 2. Adding factors is a different operation: the last exercise checks why the product rule does not apply to it.

Explain it in your own words

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The print measures 32 cm, so I take the logarithm of 32.”

    Start with the ratio 32 cm / 4 cm = 8, with no unit. The setting 3 gives 32 cm when you recalculate the length. The setting 5 gives 128 cm: it uses a different starting reference.

    Review the ratio and the length calculation

  • “A negative logarithm is not allowed.”

    The argument must be positive. The factor 0.5 is positive and its logarithm to base 2 is -1: this setting reduces the side. The factor 0, however, has no real logarithm.

    Review the cases with no zoom and with reduction

  • “The logarithm of a sum is the sum of the logarithms.”

    A sum of logarithms corresponds to a product of factors, because exponents add when powers multiply. First calculate 8 + 8, then find its power: the last exercise gives an exact counterexample.

    Compare the factors in the composition exercise

A smaller print

Compare with the solution

The factor is 16/4 = 4, with no unit. Since 2 × 2 = 4, the exponent is 2. This is therefore log to base 2 of 4.

log2(4)=2;4×22=16 cm

We recover the final side. Only the lengths are in cm; the factor and setting have no unit. Giving the setting without recalculating the length would leave the check incomplete.

Exercise 2 · Application with sign and domain

A reduction of the same original

Starting from an initial side of 4 cm, we want a side of 1 cm. Predict the sign of the setting, then calculate it and recalculate the length. Someone proposes the setting 0, saying that it reduces everything to 0: explain their confusion and say whether the factor 0 has a real logarithm.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The ratio 1/4 is positive and less than 1. With a base greater than 1, it corresponds to a negative exponent.

Hint 2

2 raised to the power 2 equals 4; negating the exponent gives its reciprocal.

Compare with the solution

We are reducing the photo, so we expect a negative setting. The factor is 1/4 = 1/4, or 0.25 with no unit. Since 2 raised to the power -2 equals 1/4, its logarithm is -2.

log2(14)=-2;4×14=1 cm

The setting 0 gives the factor 1, so it preserves 4 cm. The factor 0 has no real logarithm: powers of a strictly positive base remain strictly positive. We do not replace an undefined result with 0.

Check your reasoning

  • The negative sign is predicted before the calculation and linked to the reduction.
  • The setting -2 is checked against the side of 1 cm.
  • The setting 0, the factor 1 and the excluded argument 0 remain distinct.

Exercise 3 · Composition and counterexample

Multiply factors or add them?

The factor for the first print is 8. An additional zoom multiplies its side by 4. Find the total setting in base 2 by grouping the factors of 2, then recalculate the final length. Next, calculate log to base 2 of (8 + 8) and compare it with the sum of the logarithms of 8. Why does the same rule not apply?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The total factor is 8 × 4. Write each as a power of 2.

Hint 2

For the counterexample, first calculate 8 + 8; then find the corresponding exponent. Do not turn a sum of factors into a product.

Compare with the solution

Successive factors multiply: 8 × 4 = 32. The first contains 3 factors of 2 and the next contains 2; in total, there are 3 + 2 = 5. The logarithm of the product is therefore 5.

log2(8×4)=3+2=5

Length check: 4 × 32 = 128 cm. This side is correct for these successive zooms; in the initial example, it was too large because the additional factor was not required.

The sum of the factors is 8 + 8 = 16. Now 2 raised to the power 4 equals 16, so its logarithm is 4. Adding the logarithms separately would give 3 + 3 = 6, which differs from 4.

The product rule comes from multiplying powers with the same base. Here the expression inside the logarithm is a sum: we must calculate it before finding its exponent. For the photo, adding the factors does not express successive applications of the zooms.

Check your reasoning

  • The product of the factors is 32 and its setting 5 is justified.
  • Recalculating the length gives 128 cm for the successive zooms.
  • The counterexample compares 4 and 6, then explains the operation involved.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does log to base a of x find, and what are the conditions on a and x?

It finds the exponent y such that a raised to the power y equals x. Over the real numbers, a is strictly positive and different from 1, and x is strictly positive.

Recall 2For the photo, why take a ratio before the logarithm, and how can you check the setting?

The ratio final side/initial side gives the factor with no unit. The logarithm in the stated base finds its exponent. To check, multiply the initial side by this base raised to this exponent: you should recover the final side.

Recall 3In base 2, is a negative logarithm allowed? What must be distinguished from the factor 0?

Yes: an argument between 0 and 1 corresponds to a reduction and gives a negative exponent. The factor 0 has no real logarithm. The setting 0 gives the factor 1.

Recall 4What happens to a product and a quotient under a logarithm? Why not apply this rule to a sum?

With the same positive base different from 1 and positive arguments, the logarithm of the product is the sum of the logarithms; the logarithm of the quotient is their difference. This follows from the laws of powers. A sum of factors does not follow this law: log to base 2 of (8 + 8) is 4, whereas the sum of their logarithms is 6.

Recall 5Why must the base accompany the argument when stating a logarithm?

The base tells us which factor is repeated. For the argument 8, the base 2 gives the exponent 3, whereas the base 8 gives 1. The resulting factor is the same; the number of repetitions depends on the base.
