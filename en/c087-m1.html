Level 2 · C087·M1

Circadian rhythms

Your goalDescribe a circadian rhythm by its period, mesor, amplitude and phase, then calculate how its peak drifts without light and how much resetting is needed each cycle.

Before you start
c086-m5 : emotion and cognition · c056-m5 : effect size

✿01 See the idea

Imagine a fictional person in a room with no window or clock. An internal thermometer records their core temperature every 4 hours. Even without daylight, the curve rises and falls repeatedly, roughly once per 24-hour day. This rhythm does not simply follow the sun: the body produces it itself.

In this course, a circadian rhythm is a repeated variation in a biological quantity, with an intrinsic period close to 24 hours, that persists without an external signal. The course presents the suprachiasmatic nucleus of the hypothalamus as the central clock that coordinates these rhythms; it is not modeled here. We only summarize the curve it helps produce.

Four numbers are enough to summarize a regular curve: the period, the duration of a cycle; the mesor, the mean level around which it oscillates; the amplitude, the difference between this level and the peak; and the phase, the time at which the peak occurs.

A second idea: the intrinsic period is not exactly 24 hours. If it is slightly longer, the peak arrives a little later each cycle, and the difference accumulates. Light perceived by the eye acts as a synchronizing cue: it resets the internal clock and keeps it aligned with the calendar day.

All values on this page are fictional. They are used for calculations, not to describe a real person or set a norm.

The rule to remember

A circadian rhythm is summarized by its period, mesor, amplitude and phase; its intrinsic period differs slightly from 24 hours, and light corrects this difference each cycle.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

One day and 10 cycles in a room without light

A fictional person wears an internal thermometer. Six readings, taken 4 h apart, give a peak and a trough. The person then lives without light, with an intrinsic period of 24.5 h. Let us calculate the mesor, amplitude, phase, drift after 10 cycles and the resetting needed.

  1. State the quantities and assumptions

    The quantity tracked is core temperature T, in °C, read at local time h, in hours from 0 to 24. The six readings are fictional: at 2 h, 36.6 °C; at 6 h, 36.5 °C; at 10 h, 36.9 °C; at 14 h, 37.3 °C; at 18 h, 37.5 °C; at 22 h, 37.1 °C.

    The mesor M and amplitude A are in °C. The intrinsic period τ is in hours; here it equals 49 half-hours divided by 2. The number of cycles n is an integer with no unit. The drift Δ, in hours per cycle, measures how far the peak shifts from one cycle to the next.

    Assumptions: a single peak and a single trough per cycle, a regular curve around M; a constant intrinsic period; no effective light during the drift. We calculate in tenths of °C to keep the calculations exact, then convert to °C. Outside these assumptions, the formulas below do not apply.

  2. Read the mesor and amplitude

    Reason: the peak and trough bound the curve; their midpoint gives the mean level, and half their difference gives the amplitude. In tenths of °C, the peak is 375 and the trough 365.

    M=Tpic+Tcreux2,A=Tpic−Tcreux2

    Sum of the extremes: 375 + 365 = 740; half: 370 tenths, or 37.0 °C. Difference: 375 − 365 = 10; half: 5 tenths, or 0.5 °C.

    Check: 37.0 + 0.5 = 37.5 °C at the peak and 37.0 − 0.5 = 36.5 °C at the trough; the other four readings fall within these bounds. Here the mesor is the midpoint of the extremes, not the mean of the six readings: with so few points, this is a rough estimate.

  3. Read the phase

    The phase is the time of the peak: 18 h for the highest reading. The trough occurs at 6 h.

    Check: for a regular curve, the peak and trough are half a period apart. 18 − 6 = 12 h and 24 / 2 = 12 h: the two values agree.

    Limitation: with a sampling interval of 4 h, the true peak may lie between two readings. The phase is therefore only known to within a few hours.

  4. Let the clock run without light: drift

    Reason: if the intrinsic period differs from 24 h, the peak does not fall at the same clock time from one cycle to the next. The difference per cycle is τ − 24, and it adds up cycle after cycle.

    Δ=τ−24,décalage=n×Δ

    Here τ = 49 / 2 = 24.5 h. Thus Δ = 24.5 − 24 = 0.5 h per cycle. After n = 10 cycles: 10 × 0.5 = 5 h. The peak, initially at 18 h, arrives at 18 + 5 = 23 h.

    Check using the total duration: 10 cycles of 24.5 h last 245 h; 10 full days total 240 h; the excess 245 − 240 = 5 h gives the same shift.

  5. Reset with light

    Reason: for the peak to stay at 18 h, the drift must be canceled each cycle. The light signal must therefore advance the internal clock by 0.5 h per cycle, or 5 h after 10 cycles: 23 − 5 = 18 h.

    In this simple model, the advance equals the excess length of the intrinsic period. The actual effect of an exposure depends on its timing, intensity and duration; none of these is modeled here.

  6. Compare amplitude with variability

    Amplitude alone does not tell us whether the rhythm exceeds the noise from one day to the next. Divide it by a fictional daily dispersion of 1 tenth of °C: 5 / 1 = 5.

    This ratio is a difference divided by a dispersion, following the idea of effect size in c056-m5; it is neither a test nor a threshold. A real dispersion would require several days of readings. Finally, this temperature curve alone tells us nothing about the emotion or cognition of c086-m5: linking the two would require other readings.

Explain it in your own words

Why does summarizing the curve by its period, mesor, amplitude and phase not explain what produces the rhythm? Also explain why light must correct the clock each cycle rather than just once.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The circadian rhythm exists only because the sun rises and sets.”

    In the example, the curve continues without light, with an intrinsic period of 24.5 h. Light does not create the cycle: it resets it. Revisit the difference between intrinsic period and synchronizing cue.

    Review the intuition and the drift diagram

  • “An intrinsic period of 24.5 h shifts the peak by 0.5 h just once.”

    The excess adds up each cycle: after 10 cycles, 10 × 0.5 = 5 h, and the peak arrives at 23 h. The total duration of 245 h confirms this.

    Revisit the drift step

  • “The amplitude is the temperature at the peak, so 37.5 °C.”

    The amplitude is a difference from the mesor, or 0.5 °C. The temperature at the peak is the mesor plus the amplitude: 37.0 + 0.5 = 37.5 °C. Redo the first exercise with different extremes.

Exercise 3 · Transfer and justification

A period shorter than the day

Another fictional person has a shorter intrinsic period: 23.5 h. The peak starts at 18 h. Without light, toward what times does it shift, and where is it after 8 cycles? What correction must light provide each cycle, and in which direction? Explain in one sentence why the shift does not happen just once.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Compare 23.5 h with 24 h before calculating anything: is the cycle longer or shorter?

Hint 2

The difference adds up each cycle, but this time it moves the peak earlier.

Hint 3

Resetting must compensate for the drift: the same magnitude in the opposite direction.

Compare with the solution

The cycle is shorter than 24 h: the peak arrives earlier and earlier. Difference per cycle: 24 − 23.5 = 0.5 h. After 8 cycles: 8 × 0.5 = 4 h earlier, so the peak is at 18 − 4 = 14 h.

In this model, light must delay the clock by 0.5 h each cycle, reversing the shift. Check: 8 cycles of 23.5 h last 188 h, or 4 h less than 8 days of 24 h.

The shift repeats because each cycle starts from the already shifted position of the previous one: the excess adds to the existing shift rather than replacing it.

Check your reasoning

  • Direction of the shift identified before calculating.
  • Difference per cycle multiplied by the number of cycles.
  • Correction in the opposite direction, with the same magnitude.
  • Justification based on accumulation from one cycle to the next.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does the intrinsic period of a circadian rhythm measure?

The duration of a full cycle when no external signal corrects it; it is close to 24 hours without being exactly equal to it.

Recall 2How do you obtain the mesor and amplitude from the peak and trough?

Mesor: the sum of the peak and trough, divided by 2. Amplitude: the difference between the peak and trough, divided by 2. Both retain the unit of the measured quantity.

Recall 3How far does the peak shift after n cycles without light?

By n × (τ − 24) hours: toward later times if τ exceeds 24, and earlier times otherwise, because the difference adds up each cycle.

Recall 4What is the role of light in this model?

It acts as a resetting signal: each cycle, it corrects the difference between the intrinsic period and 24 hours. Its actual effect depends on timing, intensity and duration, which are not modeled here.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Name the quantity, its unit and the local time; identify the peak and trough; calculate the mesor and amplitude; read the phase; compare the intrinsic period with 24 hours; multiply the excess by the number of cycles to find the drift; recall that light resets the clock each cycle; retain the fictional values and the limits of the model.
