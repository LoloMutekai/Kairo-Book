Level 2 · C093·M4

Silent sensor and gaps

Your goalDistinguish a silent sensor from a 0 reading, decide when an empty end of a series needs a label, then measure what an invented 0 does to the median, MAD and slope.

Before you start
c090-m5 : stationarity · c056-m5 : effect size · c063-m5 : synthetic dataset and external validity · c025-m5 : O, Omega and Theta · c093-m1 : median, MAD and normal scale · c093-m2 : pairwise slopes and Theil–Sen · c093-m3 : L1 residuals, step, drift and spike

✿01 See the idea

A temperature probe monitors a heated greenhouse, one time slot after another. At each slot, the collector receives a reading or receives nothing. The scene is fictional: its readings are used for calculations, describe no real greenhouse and set no real threshold.

It is tempting to turn this absence into 0 so that the series looks complete. But 0 is a number: it enters the median, the median absolute deviation (MAD) and the slopes. An empty slot is simply missing information. In this scene, the greenhouse is heated and the probe does not drop to 0 degrees: 0 would therefore be an invented code, rather than a temperature.

There are two kinds of gaps. An isolated gap, surrounded by readings, is a brief interruption after which the series resumes. Trailing silence leaves the last known value without any further readings: it no longer tells us about the present. We want to label this silence without claiming to know its cause.

Before looking for a step, drift or spike, we therefore need to decide whether the series can be used: enough valid readings, no excessive gaps, or trailing silence. The thresholds in the scene are fictional and smaller than those in the code read at the end of the page, so that the calculations are easy to follow.

The rule to remember

A gap is not 0: exclude empty slots while keeping the position of each reading, first label any trailing silence, and only then calculate the median, MAD and slopes from the valid readings.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Twelve slots, seven readings and trailing silence

A fictional greenhouse probe sends a temperature in °C at each of 12 slots. It gave 20, 21, 20, 22, 21, 21 and 22 °C at slots 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8 and 9. Nothing arrived at slots 4, 7, 10, 11 and 12. Fictional thresholds: at least 5 valid readings; trailing silence from 3 consecutive empty slots onward; a series is considered to have too many gaps if the fraction of empty slots is strictly greater than 0.3. What does this series tell us, and what would an invented 0 do?

  1. Define the quantities and assumptions

    A slot has a position p, a unitless integer starting at 1, and a reading y in °C, or no reading. Let n be the number of slots, v the number of valid readings and m = n − v the number of empty slots. Let d be the position of the last valid reading. The trailing silence r is the number of consecutive empty slots at the very end: r = n − d. The gap fraction m/n is unitless.

    r=n−d

    Assumptions: an empty slot means that the collector received nothing; the heated greenhouse does not drop to 0, so 0 would be an invented code; valid readings do not drift during the window, otherwise the median and MAD would summarize a mixture of levels (stationarity); the thresholds have not been calibrated for any real greenhouse.

  2. Count readings, gaps and trailing silence

    The last valid reading is at slot 9, so d = 9.

    r=12−9=3

    Slots 4 and 7 are isolated gaps: 2 gaps. In total, m = 2 + 3 = 5, so v = 12 − 5 = 7. The gap fraction is 5/12, or approximately 0.42. Check: 2 + 3 + 7 = 12, and the list in the problem does contain 7 readings.

  3. Apply the tests in order

    First, a combined test: are there at least 5 valid readings and trailing silence of at least 3 slots? Margin for readings: 7 − 5 = 2. Margin for silence: 3 − 3 = 0. The threshold is met exactly, and “at least” triggers the test. Verdict: trailing silence.

    If the gap test came first, the fraction 0.42 would exceed 0.30 by 0.12; the series would be excluded for excessive gaps and the silence would never be labeled. The order of the tests is therefore a design decision. In the code read at the end of the page, trailing silence is tested before insufficient readings and before the gap fraction.

  4. Measure what a 0 would do

    Recall: the median M is the central value of the sorted values, or the average of the two central values if their count is even. The MAD is the median of the absolute deviations from M. Multiplied by the constant k, it estimates a standard deviation under a normal distribution.

    MAD=médiane(|yi−M|)
    σ^=k×MAD,k=1482610000=1.4826

    Case A, with gaps excluded: the sorted readings are 20, 20, 21, 21, 21, 22 and 22 °C. There are 7 values; the value at rank 4 is the median: 21 °C. The absolute deviations are 0 (3 times), 1 for 20 and 1 for 22 (4 times in total); when sorted, the deviation at rank 4 is 1 °C. The normal scale is 1.4826 × 1 = 1.4826 °C.

    Case C, with an invented 0 at each of the 5 empty slots: there are 12 values, namely 5 zeros followed by the 7 readings. The count is even: the median is the average of ranks 6 and 7, both equal to 20, giving 20 °C. The deviations from this median are 20 for each 0, 0 for 20 (2 times), 1 for 21 (3 times) and 2 for 22 (2 times). When sorted, ranks 6 and 7 equal 2: the MAD is 2 °C and the scale is 1.4826 × 2 = 2.9652 °C.

    The zeros are not the majority, so the median resists: it moves by only 1 °C, from 21 to 20. But the MAD changes from 1 to 2 °C: the scale doubles, and the series appears noisier than it is.

  5. Keep the positions when calculating the slope

    The slope of a pair of readings is calculated as shown below, in °C per slot; indices i and j refer to the two valid readings.

    b=yj−yipj−pi

    Take slots 6 (21 °C) and 9 (22 °C). The reading difference is 1 °C, and the position difference is 9 − 6 = 3 slots: the slope is 1/3 °C per slot, or approximately 0.33. If the valid readings had been packed together with the gaps omitted, these two readings would be at ranks 5 and 7, separated by 2: the slope would become 1/2, or 0.50, with the same readings. The gap affects elapsed time; it must remain part of the slope calculation.

  6. Check and limit the conclusion

    Checks: the counts agree (2 + 3 + 7 = 12); the MAD in case A is smaller than in case C (1 versus 2), as expected when adding 5 deviations of 20 °C; the slope returns to 0.33 as soon as the actual positions are used.

    Limits: the series is fictional. It shows the sequence of calculations, rather than the cause of silence (probe, connection or collector) or the quality of thresholds for a real greenhouse, which is a question of external validity. Trailing silence also does not establish that the actual temperature has remained unchanged.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “A slot without a reading is 0; the median will be unaffected.”

    0 is an invented reading. Here, 5 zeros move the median from 21 to 20 °C and raise the MAD from 1 to 2 °C; with as many zeros as readings, the median collapses. Remove the empty slots and keep the position of each reading.

    Review the effect of a 0

  • “For the slope, we can pack valid readings together one after another.”

    A pairwise slope divides by the difference in actual positions. For slots 6 and 9, packing the readings together gives 0.50 instead of 0.33 °C per slot, with the same readings.

    Recalculate a slope with a gap

  • “Trailing silence proves that the probe has failed.”

    It only proves that nothing has reached the collector for 3 slots: the probe, connection or collector may be responsible. The code passage being read labels the situation and the number of missing readings without identifying the cause.

Exercise 1 · Explained application

Another series, another verdict

The same greenhouse, with the same fictional thresholds. A series of 10 slots has valid readings at slots 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 8. Nothing arrived at slots 4, 9 and 10. Apply the tests in order, give the verdict and say how many more empty slots would be needed to declare trailing silence.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Count trailing silence from the last valid slot, then count all empty slots.

Hint 2

In this scene, “too many gaps” means strictly greater than the threshold: compare the fraction with the threshold without rounding.

Compare with the solution

Last reading at slot 8: r = 10 − 8 = 2, less than 3. No trailing silence; 1 more empty slot would be needed. Valid readings: 7, at least 5, so there are enough.

Empty slots: m = 10 − 7 = 3, fraction 3/10 = 0.3. The difference from the threshold 0.3 is 0: the fraction equals the threshold without exceeding it, so the series is not excluded. Verdict: analysis can proceed using the valid readings and their positions. One more missing reading would change the conclusion.

Check your reasoning

  • Trailing silence counted from the last valid reading.
  • Tests applied in the stated order.
  • Equality at the threshold handled according to the rule of the scene.
  • Verdict limited to the fictional series.

Exercise 2 · Explained application

One more gap, half the values are zeros

In the example series, the reading at slot 8 (21 °C) also disappears, and every empty slot is coded as 0. Calculate the median and MAD of the 12 values, then compare them with case A. What can you conclude about the resistance of the median?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Count the zeros, then sort the 12 values: the count is even, so the median is the average of ranks 6 and 7.

Hint 2

For the MAD, recalculate the deviations from the new median before sorting them.

Compare with the solution

There are 6 zeros, followed by 20, 20, 21, 21, 22 and 22. Ranks 6 and 7 are 0 and 20: median (0 + 20) / 2 = 10 °C.

The deviations from 10 are 10 for each 0 and for 20, 11 for 21, 12 for 22. The 8 smallest deviations all equal 10: ranks 6 and 7 fall among them, so the MAD is 10 °C.

With 5 zeros, the median was 20 °C; with 6 zeros out of 12, it drops to 10 °C, a value the probe never gave. The median resists as long as the zeros remain a minority, but it is not immune. This conclusion applies to this fictional series.

Check your reasoning

  • Correct number of zeros and total number of values.
  • Average of the two central ranks for an even count.
  • Deviations recalculated from the new median.
  • Conclusion without generalizing beyond the series.

Exercise 3 · Transfer

A slope across a gap

From the valid readings in the example, keep slot 1 (20 °C), slot 2 (21 °C) and slot 5 (22 °C). Calculate the three pairwise slopes and their median. Repeat after packing these three readings together at ranks 1, 2 and 3. What is the difference, and why?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Three readings give three pairs; for each pair, divide the reading difference by the position difference.

Hint 2

With three slopes, the median is the middle one after sorting.

Compare with the solution

Reading differences: 1 (slots 1 and 2), 2 (slots 1 and 5), 1 (slots 2 and 5). Position differences: 1, 4 and 3. Slopes: 1, 1/2 (or 0.50) and 1/3 (or approximately 0.33) °C per slot. After sorting, the middle slope is 1/2 °C per slot.

Packed together at ranks 1, 2 and 3, the readings give a slope of 1 between the first two, 1 between the last two and 2 / 2 = 1 between the endpoints: the median becomes 1 °C per slot, compared with 1/2 using the actual positions. The readings have not changed: packing them together has erased the time difference created by the gap.

Check your reasoning

  • Three pairs, reading difference divided by position difference.
  • Median taken as the middle of the three sorted slopes.
  • Comparison with the ranks after packing the readings together.
  • Explanation based on elapsed time rather than readings.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1Why do we avoid replacing an empty slot with 0?

0 is an invented reading that enters the median and MAD; a gap is missing information. In this scene, 5 zeros change the median from 21 to 20 °C and the MAD from 1 to 2 °C.

Recall 2How is trailing silence r calculated, and when is it declared?

r is the number of consecutive empty slots at the end: n minus the position of the last valid reading. In this scene, it is declared if r is at least 3 and there are at least 5 valid readings.

Recall 3Why does the order of the tests change the verdict in the example?

The gap fraction (0.42) exceeds 0.30 and would make the series unusable if that test came first. Trailing silence (3 slots) is tested first and labels the situation.

Recall 4What do we divide in a pairwise slope when the series has gaps?

The reading difference by the difference in actual positions: for slots 6 and 9, 1 divided by 3 gives 0.33, rather than 0.50 after packing the readings into consecutive ranks.

Recall 5How far does the median resist invented zeros?

It already moves with 5 zeros out of 12 (20 °C) and drops to 10 °C when 6 values out of 12 are zeros: its resistance has a limit.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Define n, v, m, d and r; remove empty slots without coding them as 0 and keep the positions; first test for enough valid readings together with trailing silence, then for excessive gaps; only then calculate the median, MAD and pairwise slopes using the actual positions; draw no conclusion about the cause of a failure or the validity of the thresholds for a real series.
