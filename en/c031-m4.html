Level 1 · C031·M4

One-sided limits

Your goalCalculate the limits from the left and from the right of a threshold separately, then decide whether a two-sided limit exists.

Before you start
c006-m5 : composition · c027-m5 : complex numbers · c011-m5 : binomial · c014-m5 : invariants · c031-m1 : limit of a sequence · c031-m2 : limit of a function · c031-m3 : continuity

✿01 See the idea

A test valve has a graduated stem. As you pull it out, the flow rate increases. But at the mark a = 5 cm, the control suddenly adds 3 L/min. If you move towards this mark without reaching it, what flow rate do you approach? What if you start beyond it and move back towards it?

From positions below the threshold, the flow rate approaches 5 L/min. From positions above it, the flow rate approaches 8 L/min. The same input mark can therefore have two different values that the flow rate approaches. These separate views give the one-sided limits: first from the left, then from the right.

Left and right refer to the position on the horizontal axis, rather than the sign of the flow rate or the direction of time. You can choose the positions in any order: what matters is how close they are to the threshold while staying on the stated side. The value exactly at the mark answers a different question.

This valve and its values are fictional. We study an ideal, stable rule with no noise that assigns a flow rate to every nonnegative real position. The jump represents a theoretical control action, without claiming to describe the mechanics or response time of a real valve.

The rule to remember

Choose one side of the input, keep the formula valid on that side, and find the value it approaches. A two-sided limit requires both one-sided limits to exist and be equal.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Read the flow rate on either side of the mark

The position x is in cm, the flow rate F(x) in L/min, the threshold a = 5 cm, the coefficient c = 1 L/(min·cm), and the added flow rate b = 3 L/min. The control follows F(x) = cx before the threshold and F(x) = cx + b at and beyond the threshold. Let us calculate the approaches while keeping the sides distinct.

  1. Write the rule with its domain

    The domain is the set of allowed positions: x ≥ 0 cm. F is the name of the function; F(x) denotes the flow rate associated with x. The product cx has the unit L/min: the centimetres in x cancel those in the denominator of c. The added flow rate b has the same unit as the flow rate.

    F(x)={cxsi 0≤x<acx+bsi x≥a

    The equality x = a belongs to the second branch. To approach from the right, however, we use x > a: the threshold itself is excluded from the approach. Here, both sides of the threshold contain positions in the domain as close to a as desired.

  2. Name the values sought by the two limits

    The distance h is a strictly positive length, in cm, that we let tend to 0 cm. On the left, x = a − h with h < a; on the right, x = a + h. Call the corresponding limiting flow rates Lg and Ld, in L/min.

    Lg=limx→a−F(x);Ld=limx→a+F(x)

    The superscripts − and + on a indicate the side of approach. They do not replace a by its opposite or add any quantity to a. A one-sided limit describes all sufficiently close inputs on the side considered, rather than just a chosen list.

  3. Make a first numerical calculation

    Choose h = 1/10 = 0.10 cm. Subtracting this distance puts the input on the left: x = 5 − 0.10 = 4.90 cm < a. The first branch gives F(x) = 1 × 4.90 = 4.90 L/min.

    Adding the same distance puts the input on the right: x = 5 + 0.10 = 5.10 cm > a. We therefore use the second branch: cx = 1 × 5.10 = 5.10 L/min, then F(x) = 5.10 + 3 = 8.10 L/min. The input determines the choice of branch before any flow rate is calculated.

  4. Bring the inputs closer and keep the units

    Take h = 1/100 = 0.01 cm. On the left: x = 5 − 0.01 = 4.99 cm, then F(x) = 1 × 4.99 = 4.99 L/min. On the right: x = 5 + 0.01 = 5.01 cm, cx = 5.01 L/min, then F(x) = 5.01 + 3 = 8.01 L/min.

    The flow rate on the left approaches 5 L/min and the flow rate on the right approaches 8 L/min. In both cases, the error relative to its own target is c × h = 0.01 L/min. These values suggest the limits; they are not enough to prove them.

  5. Justify the limits for every small distance

    Now keep h variable. For every h strictly between 0 cm and a, the formula on the left expands to F(a − h) = c(a − h) = ca − ch. For every h > 0 cm, the formula on the right gives F(a + h) = c(a + h) + b = ca + b + ch. The term ch tends to 0 L/min as h tends to 0 cm, because c is fixed.

    Lg=ca=5 L/min;Ld=ca+b=8 L/min

    Here is why this covers all nearby positions. Set a required tolerance ε, strictly positive, in L/min; ε is read as “epsilon”. Since c is positive, choosing a positive h smaller than ε/c and a guarantees an error ch < ε on both sides. The quotient ε/c is a length in cm. However small the tolerance, such a distance exists: we have justified each limit without extrapolating from a finite table.

  6. Decide whether the sides can share a limit

    When the domain allows approaches from both sides, as it does here, a finite two-sided limit exists if and only if both one-sided limits exist, are finite, and are equal. Necessity follows because an approach from just one side is also an allowed approach towards a. Conversely, if both sides approach the same value, take the smaller of the neighbourhoods that ensure the required tolerance on each side: this covers all nearby inputs other than a.

    Here, Ld − Lg = 8 − 5 = 3 L/min: the targets differ. F therefore has no two-sided limit at a. This leaves its two one-sided limits intact, both finite.

    Check with another calculation: at a distance of 0.01 cm, the flow rates differ by 8.01 − 4.99 = 3.02 L/min. Algebra also gives b + 2ch = 3 + 0.02 = 3.02 L/min. This separation tends to b = 3 L/min, rather than 0 L/min. The two flow rates therefore cannot converge to the same target.

  7. Calculate the value exactly at the threshold separately

    For x = a = 5 cm, the equality selects the second branch: F(a) = ca + b = 1 × 5 + 3 = 8 L/min. Here, the value at the threshold equals the limit from the right; it remains different from the limit from the left. The function is continuous from the right at this threshold, but it is not continuous at a in the usual two-sided sense.

    Moving only the filled point would change F(a) without changing what the positions distinct from a approach. No isolated choice of F(a) could repair the inequality Lg ≠ Ld. To understand the discontinuity, we therefore need to look at the neighbourhoods, rather than just the point at the threshold.

Explain it in your own words

Without reading the results, explain why the added flow rate applies on the right, why the threshold is excluded from the approaches, and why F(a) does not determine whether a two-sided limit exists.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “On the left means that the result is negative.”

    The side describes the inputs: x < a on the left, x > a on the right. In our valve, the flow rates near the threshold are positive on both sides. Write x = a − h with positive h before calculating F(x).

    Find the sides on the position axis

  • “The value at the threshold must give both limits.”

    The equality selects a branch for calculating F(a). An approach uses positions distinct from a. Here F(a) equals 8 L/min, but the positions on the left approach 5 L/min. Work through the three questions separately: left, right, and the point at the threshold.

    Separate the approaches from the calculation at the threshold

  • “If the limits differ, their average is the true limit.”

    An average creates a new target; it does not bring the values of F closer to that target. Compare the distances to the midpoint in the exercise on this objection, then examine what happens to them for an arbitrarily small distance h.

Exercise 1 · Direct application

Move closer to the same mark

Keep the original valve. Take h = 1/1000 cm. Calculate the positions a − h and a + h, then the two flow rates. State the side of each input, the limit its flow rate approaches, and the unit of each result.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Subtracting h places the input below a; the flow rate is then calculated using the corresponding branch.

Hint 2

The added flow rate b appears only in the branch at and beyond the threshold. Express the distance as 0.001 cm for the decimal values.

Compare with the solution

On the left: a − h = 5 − 0.001 = 4.999 cm < a. The flow rate is c(a − h) = 1 × 4.999 = 4.999 L/min. Its target, as h decreases towards 0 cm, is Lg = 5 L/min.

On the right: a + h = 5 + 0.001 = 5.001 cm > a. The product c(a + h) equals 5.001 L/min; after adding the extra flow rate, F(a + h) = 5.001 + 3 = 8.001 L/min. Its target is Ld = 8 L/min.

The inputs approach the same mark; the outputs keep different targets. The flow rate on the left is positive: the word left therefore does not describe its sign.

Check your reasoning

  • Obtain 4.999 cm and 5.001 cm with the correct inequalities.
  • Calculate 4.999 L/min and 8.001 L/min and justify the choice of branches.
  • Name both limits without confusing a nearby value with its target.

Exercise 2 · Diagnose a line of reasoning

The target at the midpoint of the jump

Someone claims that F tends to the average of 5 and 8 L/min. Calculate this average, then the distances from the flow rates obtained for h = 0.01 cm to this target. Are these distances alone enough to rule out a limit? Complete the argument for h tending to 0 cm.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The average is (Lg + Ld)/2. The flow rates in the example for this distance are 4.99 and 8.01 L/min.

Hint 2

For variable h, the flow rates are Lg − ch and Ld + ch. The midpoint stays at a distance of b/2 from each one-sided target.

Compare with the solution

The average is (5 + 8)/2 = 13/2 = 6.50 L/min. The flow rate on the left is lower: the distance is 6.50 − 4.99 = 1.51 L/min. The flow rate on the right is higher: its distance is 8.01 − 6.50 = 1.51 L/min.

Calculations at a single input distance do not settle a question about a limit: positive output distances at this stage could still decrease towards 0 L/min. We need to examine the rule for variable h.

On each side, the distance to the midpoint is b/2 + ch. As h tends to 0 cm, it tends to b/2 = 1.50 L/min, which is strictly positive. The outputs therefore cannot become as close as desired to 6.50 L/min. This average is not a limit; more generally, unequal one-sided limits rule out any two-sided limit.

Check your reasoning

  • Find the average 6.50 L/min and both distances 1.51 L/min.
  • Explain why a calculation at a fixed distance proves neither a limit nor its absence.
  • Justify the limiting distance 1.50 L/min using the expression b/2 + ch.

Exercise 3 · Transfer within the same scene

Reprogram the valve, then isolate a point

The valve receives a new rule: the added flow rate is removed, so its flow rate G(x) equals cx for every position x ≥ 0 cm distinct from a. But exactly at a, we impose G(a) = r = 7 L/min. Determine the limits of G from the left and from the right at a, its two-sided limit if one exists, and its continuity. What value at the threshold would make this new rule continuous?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

G is the name of the new function, with the same unit as F. The positions a − h and a + h are distinct from a: both use cx.

Hint 2

A two-sided limit concerns nearby positions other than a. Continuity then compares this limit with G(a).

Compare with the solution

Both neighbouring branches now use the same rule: G(a − h) = ca − ch and G(a + h) = ca + ch. The term ch tends to 0 L/min. The limits from the left and from the right therefore both equal ca = 5 L/min. The two-sided limit exists and equals 5 L/min.

The isolated value G(a) = 7 L/min differs from this limit: the difference is 7 − 5 = 2 L/min. G is not continuous at a. Replacing only G(a) by 5 L/min would make it continuous, because both sides already approach this value.

This differs from the original rule F: its jump in the neighbourhoods prevented a two-sided limit, and no isolated movement of the point could repair it. Here, the necessary change concerns only the value at the threshold.

Check your reasoning

  • Use cx on each side, without keeping the added flow rate from the old rule.
  • Find a two-sided limit of 5 L/min while keeping G(a) = 7 L/min.
  • Conclude that the function is not continuous, then propose G(a) = 5 L/min and justify it.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What do the signs − and + on the threshold mean in limit notation?

They refer to the inputs: strictly below the threshold for the left, strictly above it for the right. They do not give the sign of the outputs. The point at the threshold is excluded from these approaches.

Recall 2How can you reconstruct both limits of the valve without memorizing the numbers?

Write x = a − h, then x = a + h, with positive h tending to 0 cm. The left branch gives ca − ch; the right gives ca + b + ch. The term ch vanishes in the limit: the targets are ca and ca + b, in L/min.

Recall 3When do the two one-sided limits establish a finite two-sided limit?

If the domain allows approaches to the threshold from both sides, both one-sided limits must exist, be finite, and be equal. Their shared value is then the limit. If they differ, do not replace them by an average.

Recall 4Does changing only the value at the threshold change the limits? What about continuity?

The limits remain unchanged: they use inputs distinct from the threshold. If the limits from both sides are equal, continuity is obtained by assigning this same value at the threshold. If they differ, changing a single point does not repair the jump.

Recall 5Why are a few calculations close to the threshold not enough to prove a limit?

They describe only the chosen positions. A limit must control all allowed positions sufficiently close on the correct side. For the valve, the error ch becomes smaller than any positive tolerance ε by taking a positive h smaller than ε/c and a.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Reconstruct the method on paper: name the threshold and the units; write the inputs a − h and a + h with positive h; select each formula; justify the values approached for every small h; compare them; calculate the value at the threshold separately and conclude about continuity.

What you understand
