Level 4 · C234·M3

Nonlinear filtering

Your goalUpdate an estimate using an observation linked to the state by a nonlinear function, by linearizing that function around the prediction, then identify where this approximation stops being reliable.

Before you start
c233-m5 : verification theorem · c066-m5 : standard errors and diagnostics · c227-m5 : Ito-Tanaka · c234-m1 : state space · c234-m2 : Kalman filter

✿01 See the idea

A square tarpaulin is stretched out on the floor of a workshop. Its side length x, in metres, is the latent state we want to estimate. We do not measure it directly: a fictional instrument reports only the area z, in square metres, with noise. The prediction from the previous step gives a prior estimate of 3 m and a variance of 1 m². The instrument reports 10 m².

If the side length were exactly 3 m, the expected area would be 9 m². The difference of 1 m² provides information, but how much should we update the side length? With a direct reading of the side length, the Kalman filter would give the answer. Here the area is the square of the side length: a curve rather than a straight line. The change in side length corresponding to a given change in area depends on where we are on the curve.

The idea of the extended Kalman filter is to replace the curve with its tangent at the predicted point. The slope H of this tangent tells us how much area is added per metre added to the side length, very close to x̂⁻. It propagates uncertainty in the side length to the area and apportions the difference between prediction and reading. The rest of the procedure is the linear update: innovation, gain, update.

The diagram retains an asymmetry to remember: the difference to be corrected is calculated with the exact function, but the weighting is calculated with the tangent. This tangent is exact only at the point of tangency; the further we move from that point, the larger the error in the first-order approximation becomes.

All values in this scene are fictional: the tarpaulin, the instrument and its noise do not describe any real measurement. This page uses only state space and the scalar Kalman filter; the other chapter prerequisites are not used here.

The rule to remember

Calculate the predicted observation with the exact function h; weight the innovation using the slope of h at the predicted point; then check that the tangent stays close to the curve over the resulting change in side length.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Update the side length of a tarpaulin from its area

Start with x̂⁻ = 3 m and P⁻ = 1 m², a measured area z = 10 m² and measurement noise with variance R = 4 m⁴. The area of a square is h(x) = x squared. Calculate the pair x̂⁺, P⁺ after this observation. All these values are fictional.

  1. Set out the model and its assumptions

    x denotes the unknown side length, in metres. The observation is the measured area z, in square metres. The observation model is z = h(x) + v. The function h gives the area of a square; v is measurement noise with mean 0 and variance R = 4 m⁴ (the variance of an area, so its unit is square metres squared). This value is fictional.

    h(x)=x2

    Assumptions: the prior state is summarized by its mean x̂⁻ = 3 m and its variance P⁻ = 1 m²; the noise v is independent of the prior error; the state distribution is approximated by a normal distribution with this mean and variance; and h is smooth enough to be replaced by its tangent near x̂⁻. The superscript − means “before this reading”, rather than a negative value.

  2. Predict the observation with the exact function

    Evaluate h at the prior estimate, without approximation: 3 × 3 = 9 m². This area is what the model predicts if the side length is exactly x̂⁻.

    z^=h(x^−)=3×3=9 m²

  3. Linearize around the prediction

    Write x = x̂⁻ + δ, where δ is the side length deviation to be corrected, in metres. Expanding the square requires no differentiation rule: the area becomes the area at the predicted side length, plus a term proportional to δ, plus a term in δ squared.

    h(x^−+δ)=(x^−)2+2x^−δ+δ2≈h(x^−)+Hδ

    Keep the first-order term and discard δ squared. The slope is therefore H = 2 × 3 = 6 m²/m, which simplifies to 6 m. The discarded term is the source of the approximation: it is assumed to be small, rather than 0.

  4. Form the innovation using exact h

    The innovation is the difference between the observation received and the predicted observation: y = z − h(x̂⁻) = 10 − 9 = 1 m². Both terms are areas, so the subtraction makes sense. We do not use the tangent here: it is used only to propagate uncertainty.

  5. Calculate the innovation variance and the gain

    An error in side length propagates to the area through the slope H; uncertainty, expressed as a squared error, propagates through H squared. The innovation variance adds this contribution and the measurement noise variance.

    S=H2P−+R=36×1+4=40 m⁴

    The gain K converts an area error into a side length update. Its numerator is the covariance between the side length and the linearized area, P⁻H = 6 m³.

    K=P−HS=640=0.15 m⁻¹

  6. Update the estimate and the variance

    The side length changes by K y, and the variance decreases by the amount contributed by the reading.

    x^+=x^−+Ky=3+0.15×1=3.15 m
    P+=(1−KH)P−=(1−0.9)×1=0.1 m²

    The result is the pair x̂⁺ = 3.15 m and P⁺ = 0.1 m². The reading has moved the side length slightly upwards and greatly reduced uncertainty, because the side length uncertainty, propagated through the slope, contributes more than the measurement noise.

  7. Check the result

    First check, by another route: in information form, 1/P⁺ = 1/P⁻ + H²/R = 1 + 36/4 = 1 + 9 = 10 m⁻², so P⁺ = 1/10 m². This is the value obtained above.

    Second check, on the tangent. At the updated side length, the exact area is 3.15 × 3.15 = 9.9225 m². The tangent gave 9 + 6 × 0.15 = 9.9 m². The difference of 0.0225 m² is exactly δ squared, with δ = 0.15 m, the discarded term. It is tiny compared with the noise standard deviation, 2 m². The approximation is acceptable for this change in side length; it would not automatically be acceptable for a much larger change or much greater uncertainty.

    This check validates the arithmetic and the size of the neglected term in this fictional model. It does not prove that the noise model would describe a real instrument.

Explain it in your own words

Explain why the innovation uses h(x̂⁻) rather than x̂⁻, then why H appears squared in the innovation variance but only once in the numerator of the gain.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The gain remains P⁻ / (P⁻ + R), as if the area were the side length.”

    Without H, we obtain K = 1 / 5 = 0.20, which treats an area error as a side length error. We need K = P⁻H / (H²P⁻ + R) = 0.15 m⁻¹: H converts square metres to metres.

    Review the gain calculation

  • “The innovation is z − x̂⁻.”

    z is an area in m², while x̂⁻ is a side length in m: their difference has no consistent unit. The innovation compares z with h(x̂⁻) = 9 m², giving 1 m² in the example.

    Review the innovation

  • “Linearizing gives the exact distribution after the reading.”

    The tangent is an approximation: it discards a term (δ squared, or 0.0225 m² in the example). This term can become large, or the slope can become 0, as at x̂⁻ = 0 m. First compare this term with the noise standard deviation.

Exercise 1 · Explained application

Linearize at another point

Use the same area model, but a different fictional prediction: x̂⁻ = 5 m, and the instrument reports z = 26 m². Calculate the predicted area, the slope H and the innovation.

Hint 1

The predicted area uses the exact function: side length multiplied by itself.

Hint 2

The slope is 2 times the predicted side length.

Compare with the solution

Predicted area: 5 × 5 = 25 m². Slope: H = 2 × 5 = 10 m²/m. Innovation: y = 26 − 25 = 1 m². The innovation is the same as in the example, but the slope is steeper: the same area difference corresponds to a smaller change in side length.

Check your reasoning

  • The predicted area comes from h, rather than the tangent.
  • The slope is calculated at the predicted point.
  • The units m² and m²/m are distinguished.

Exercise 2 · Complete variation

A noisier reading

Repeat the example with a measurement variance R = 12 m⁴ instead of 4 m⁴, making it 3 times as large. Calculate S, K, x̂⁺ and P⁺, check P⁺ using information form, then compare with the example.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The quantities H = 6 m²/m, P⁻ and y = 1 m² do not change.

Hint 2

Only the denominator S contains R.

Hint 3

Check: 1/P⁺ = 1/P⁻ + H²/R.

Compare with the solution

S = 36 × 1 + 12 = 48 m⁴. K = 6 / 48 = 0.125 m⁻¹. Update: 0.125 × 1 = 0.125 m, so x̂⁺ = 3 + 0.125 = 3.125 m. Variance: K H = 0.75, then P⁺ = (1 − 0.75) × 1 = 0.25 m².

Check: 1/P⁺ = 1 + 36 / 12 = 1 + 3 = 4, so P⁺ = 0.25 m². With more noise, the reading has less weight: the side length changes by 0.125 m instead of 0.15 m and the final variance is larger, 0.25 m² instead of 0.1 m².

Check your reasoning

  • S contains R and the square of the slope.
  • K, x̂⁺ and P⁺ are obtained in order.
  • The information form check confirms P⁺.
  • The comparison states that the noisy reading produces a smaller update.

Exercise 3 · Model limitation

When the slope becomes 0

Take x̂⁻ = 0 m, P⁻ = 1 m², R = 4 m⁴ and z = 10 m². Calculate H, S, K, x̂⁺ and P⁺. What can be said about the update, and why is linearization misleading here?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Calculate H = 2 times the predicted side length, then S, remembering R.

Hint 2

A 0 gain leaves the estimate unchanged, despite the innovation.

Hint 3

Compare x and its opposite: is the area the same?

Compare with the solution

H = 2 × 0 = 0 m²/m. Therefore S = 0 × 0 × 1 + 4 = 4 m⁴, K = 0 and x̂⁺ = 0 m: the innovation is nevertheless 10 − 0 = 10 m², but it produces no change. P⁺ = (1 − 0) × 1 = 1 m²: uncertainty has not decreased.

The tangent is horizontal at this point: to first order, the area does not depend on the side length. Yet an area of 10 m² indicates a side length far from 0, and x and its opposite give the same area, so the reading provides no information about the sign. The extended filter is unreliable at this point; this result is a limitation of the approximation, rather than a property of the tarpaulin.

Check your reasoning

  • H is calculated and identified as 0.
  • K, x̂⁺ and P⁺ are deduced without dividing by 0.
  • The explanation links the failure to the flat tangent and the neglected term.
  • No conclusion is drawn about the true posterior distribution.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What is replaced in the extended Kalman filter?

Around the prior estimate, the observation function h is replaced by its tangent: h(x̂⁻) plus H times the deviation δ.

Recall 2How is the innovation formed when h is nonlinear?

Using the exact function: y = z − h(x̂⁻). The tangent is used only to propagate uncertainty.

Recall 3Where does the slope H enter the gain calculation?

In the numerator, P⁻H, and squared in S = H²P⁻ + R. K converts an observation error into a state update.

Recall 4When does linearization become misleading?

When the discarded term is significant compared with the noise, or when the slope is 0 or changes sign within the region of uncertainty.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Write the observation model z = h(x) + v and its assumptions; evaluate h at the prior point for the innovation; determine the slope H at that point; calculate S = H²P⁻ + R then K = P⁻H / S; update x̂⁺ = x̂⁻ + Ky and P⁺ = (1 − KH)P⁻; check using information form and compare the discarded term with the noise.
