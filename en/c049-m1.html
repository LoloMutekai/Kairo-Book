Level 2 · C049·M1

Higher order

Your goalRecognize the order of a differential equation, choose the necessary initial data, and solve and then verify the motion of a cart.

Before you start
c047-m5 : stability of an equilibrium · c048-m5 : positivity · c027-m5 : complex numbers · c046-m5 : approximation error

✿01 See the idea

A photograph shows a cart to the right of its equilibrium position. It does not tell us whether the cart is moving to the right or already returning to the left. To predict its motion, its position alone is not enough: we also need its velocity.

The cart slides along a horizontal rail. A spring pulls it back toward equilibrium, and a damper opposes its velocity. The position x is measured from equilibrium, with the positive direction to the right; its derivative x′ is the velocity v. Differentiating again gives the acceleration x″, which describes how the velocity changes.

In our fictional scene, the cart starts at 3 m with a velocity of -4 m/s. It is therefore moving to the left. The spring and damper do not necessarily act in the same direction: the spring pulls to the left, while the damper slows this return.

A higher order differential equation relates a function to derivatives beyond order 1. Its order is the order of the highest derivative that actually appears. Here, the law will determine x″ from x and x′: it has order 2. We will determine the entire motion and verify the law, rather than just calculate an initial acceleration.

The rule to remember

Identify the highest derivative, then the data needed at the start. Check the differential equation and the initial conditions separately.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Find the motion of the cart

Fictional data: mass m = 2 kg, friction coefficient c = 6 N·s/m, spring stiffness k = 4 N/m. At t = 0 s, x = 3 m and v = -4 m/s. The spring and friction are linear, the coefficients remain constant, and no other horizontal force acts.

  1. Express the forces and identify the order

    t is time in seconds, measured from the start. x(t) is the displacement in meters; a prime denotes differentiation with respect to t. The spring force is −kx and the damper force is −cx′. Their signs make them oppose the displacement and the velocity, respectively. A newton, denoted N, is a kg·m/s².

    The mechanical relation used is: mass × acceleration = sum of the forces. Under our assumptions, it gives the following equation; each term has the unit of force.

    mx″+cx′+kx=0

    The highest derivative is x″: order 2. The equation is linear because x and its derivatives are multiplied by known coefficients and added, without being multiplied by each other. It is homogeneous: its right side is 0. These words describe the form of the law, rather than how well it fits a real cart.

  2. Isolate the acceleration and specify the initial data

    The mass is strictly positive, so we can divide by m. Let α = c/m = 3 s⁻¹ and β = k/m = 2 s⁻². These coefficients let us write the law in a form that isolates the highest derivative.

    x″=−αx′−βx

    Defining v = x′ replaces this equation with the relations x′ = v and v′ = −αv − βx, each of order 1. The first has the unit m/s; the second, m/s². We must keep both x and v at the start. x″ is not an additional freely chosen datum: the law calculates it.

    More generally, n denotes an integer giving the order of an equation. When we can isolate its derivative of order n using a sufficiently regular law, we specify the initial values of the function and its derivatives up to order n − 1. Here, the law is linear with constant coefficients: every initial position–velocity pair determines a unique solution. A singular equation or a law without uniqueness would require us to reconsider this statement.

  3. Calculate the initial acceleration

    Begin with a local result that will serve as an independent check of the complete solution. At the initial instant, cx′ = 6 × (-4) = -24 N and kx = 4 × 3 = 12 N. Their sum is -12 N.

    Reversing the sign gives a total force of 12 N: friction to the right outweighs the restoring force to the left. Dividing by the mass gives x″ at the start: 12 / 2 = 6 m/s². A positive acceleration is compatible with a negative velocity: at this instant, the return to the left is slowing down.

  4. Find exponentials that satisfy the law

    An exponential is convenient because the derivative of e^(rt) is r e^(rt), and its next derivative is r squared multiplied by e^(rt). e denotes the base of the exponential function; r is a constant rate in s⁻¹, so rt is dimensionless. In a term A e^(rt), A has the unit meter.

    Substituting this term into the normalized law produces the factor below, whose terms all have the unit s⁻². To obtain a nonzero solution, we seek rates that make this factor 0: this is the characteristic equation.

    r2+αr+β=0

    With α = 3 s⁻¹ and β = 2 s⁻², the factor can be written as (r + 1 s⁻¹)(r + 2 s⁻¹). Its roots are r1 = -1 s⁻¹ and r2 = -2 s⁻¹; the subscripts only distinguish them. Their sum is −α and their product is β, which checks the factorization.

    These roots are real and distinct. The corresponding exponentials are independent: neither is a constant multiple of the other on an interval. Because the law is linear, their weighted sum is also a solution. It can match any initial pair and therefore gives the entire family of solutions here. A and B are constants in meters to be determined. Repeated roots and complex roots require other forms, beyond this calculation.

    x(t)=Aer1t+Ber2t

  5. Fit the constants to the position and velocity

    Let x0 and v0 denote the initial values, 3 m and -4 m/s, respectively. At t = 0 s, each exponential equals 1. The position requires A + B = x0. Differentiating requires r1A + r2B = v0. We do have 2 equations for the 2 constants.

    To eliminate B, subtract r2 times the position equation from the velocity equation: (r1 − r2)A = v0 − r2x0. This subtraction has the unit m/s on each side.

    A=v0−r2x0r1−r2;B=x0−A

    The product r2x0 is -6 m/s. The numerator is -4 − (-6) = 2 m/s and the denominator is -1 − (-2) = 1 s⁻¹. Thus A = 2 m and B = 3 − 2 = 1 m. Dividing a velocity by a rate does give the unit of length. The nonzero denominator explains why the constants are uniquely determined.

  6. Write the trajectory and its derivatives

    The required trajectory is x(t) = A e^(r1t) + B e^(r2t), with A = 2 m, B = 1 m, r1 = -1 s⁻¹ and r2 = -2 s⁻¹. This notation preserves the units: the amplitudes are lengths and the arguments of the exponentials are dimensionless.

    x′(t)=r1Aer1t+r2Ber2t
    x″(t)=r12Aer1t+r22Ber2t

    The velocity coefficients are -2 m/s and -2 m/s. The squared rates are 1 s⁻² and 4 s⁻²; the acceleration coefficients are therefore 2 m/s² and 4 m/s². Each differentiation multiplies by the corresponding rate, including its sign.

  7. Check the law for the entire motion, then the start

    Substituting x, x′ and x″ into mx″ + cx′ + kx gives each exponential a coefficient equal to its amplitude multiplied by the sum of m times the squared rate, c times the rate, and k. This coefficient is 0 for each chosen rate. The law is therefore satisfied at every instant, rather than only at the start.

    Check the initial conditions: x(0) = 2 + 1 = 3 m; x′(0) = -2 + (-2) = -4 m/s. The reconstructed initial acceleration is 2 + 4 = 6 m/s²: this is indeed the value obtained directly from the force balance.

    Final numerical check of the law at the start: 2 × 6 + 6 × (-4) + 4 × 3 = 12 + (-24) + 12 = -12 + 12 = 0 N. This local check complements the substitution that holds for all t; on its own, it would not be enough to establish the trajectory.

    For positive times, the exponentials decrease, the amplitudes are positive, and the velocity remains negative: in this particular case, the cart approaches equilibrium without crossing it. This finding applies to this model and these data; we have not measured any real motion.

Explain it in your own words

Why is the initial velocity used to choose A and B while the initial acceleration is imposed by the law? Then reconstruct the checks that apply to the entire motion and those that apply only to the start.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The order can be read from the square of a coefficient or of the position.”

    The order comes from the highest derivative that actually appears. A square of x would change linearity without adding a differentiation. In our law, x″ determines the order 2.

    Review how to express the forces and identify the order

  • “The starting position is enough; acceleration will do the rest.”

    At the same position, different velocities change the friction force and the subsequent motion. The law calculates acceleration from the position–velocity pair. The exercise on release from rest lets you compare these starts.

    Compare different starts for the same cart

  • “The formula matches the initial data, so it is a solution.”

    Matching position and velocity at the start does not guarantee the law at every instant. Differentiate the proposed solution, substitute it into mx″ + cx′ + kx, and check that this residual is identically 0. A single nonzero value is enough to reject the proposed solution.

    Review the check of the law for the entire motion

Exercise 1 · Read the law and calculate

Same position, cart released from rest

Use the same cart and coefficients, but set its initial velocity to 0 m/s at position 3 m. Give the order of the equation, the necessary initial data, and the initial acceleration. Compare its sign with that in the example.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Changing the velocity does not change the highest derivative in the law.

Hint 2

Friction is 0 at the start; the force −kx remains.

Compare with the solution

The law still has order 2. Its initial data are the position 3 m and the velocity 0 m/s. Acceleration is not an independent datum.

The initial force is −4 × 3 = -12 N. The acceleration is -12 / 2 = -6 m/s², to the left. In the example, it was 6 m/s², to the right: friction was slowing a return already in progress. At the same position, knowing the velocity does change the prediction.

Check your reasoning

  • The order is justified by the derivative, rather than the number of terms.
  • Position and velocity are kept as distinct data.
  • The force balance, sign, and unit of acceleration are explained.

Exercise 2 · Solve without changing the law

A faster initial return

Start again from position 3 m, this time with a velocity of -5 m/s. The cart coefficients remain those of the example. Determine the amplitudes A and B, write the trajectory, and check the initial conditions. Must the roots be recalculated?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The roots depend on m, c and k. The amplitudes also depend on the initial data.

Hint 2

Reuse (r₁ − r₂)A = v₀ − r₂x₀, then B = x₀ − A.

Compare with the solution

The law has not changed: r1 = -1 s⁻¹ and r2 = -2 s⁻¹ remain valid. The new numerator is -5 − (-6) = 1 m/s. Dividing by 1 s⁻¹ gives A = 1 m; then B = 3 − 1 = 2 m.

The trajectory is x(t) = A e^(r1t) + B e^(r2t) with these new amplitudes. At the start, A + B = 1 + 2 = 3 m. The velocity is r1A + r2B = (-1) × 1 + (-2) × 2 = -5 m/s. Each term remains an exponential solution: the combination also satisfies the law for the entire motion.

Check your reasoning

  • The roots are distinguished from the amplitudes, and their units are given.
  • The constants are obtained by elimination, keeping the signs of the rates.
  • The initial position and velocity are checked separately.

Exercise 3 · Diagnose a false solution

A correct start, a violated law

Return to the initial data of the example. Consider the proposed solution y(t) = x₀ e^(r₁t) + qt, where y is a position, x₀ = 3 m, r₁ = -1 s⁻¹ and q = -1 m/s. Check its initial position and velocity, then decide whether y describes the same cart. If it fails, explain how to fix it.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Differentiate the term qt: its derivative is q; its next derivative is 0.

Hint 2

A nonzero residual at t = 0 is enough to reject a proposed solution claimed to hold at every instant.

Compare with the solution

At the start, y = x0 = 3 m. The velocity is r1x0 + q = -3 + (-1) = -4 m/s. The initial data are therefore satisfied.

However, y″ at the start is the rate r1 squared multiplied by x0, or 3 m/s². The law requires 6 m/s² for this initial pair. The residual my″ + cy′ + ky is 6 + (-24) + 12 = -18 + 12 = -6 N: it is nonzero. The proposed solution fails despite its correct start.

To fix it, replace the proposed solution with a combination of exponentials that satisfy the law, then impose the initial conditions. This gives A = 2 m and B = 1 m again. Arbitrarily adjusting a slope is not enough: the form of the trajectory must itself satisfy the equation.

Check your reasoning

  • The initial conditions of the proposed solution are actually calculated.
  • Rejection is based on a derivative or a residual, with its unit.
  • The repair reconstructs a solution of the law before fitting the constants.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1How do you determine the order of a differential equation?

Look for the highest derivative that actually appears. In the law governing the cart, x″ gives order 2; a square of x or of a coefficient does not count as a differentiation.

Recall 2Why does the cart require an initial position and velocity?

The law determines acceleration from position and velocity. These quantities can vary independently at the start; their pair determines the motion here. The initial acceleration is then calculated; it is not freely chosen.

Recall 3How can the law governing the cart be written using only derivatives of order 1?

Define v = x′, then keep x′ = v and v′ = −(c/m)v − (k/m)x. These are coupled relations that carry position and velocity; no initial datum has disappeared.

Recall 4What do the characteristic roots and the amplitudes determine?

The roots give the exponential rates compatible with the law with constant coefficients. When the roots are real and distinct as they are here, the amplitudes in their combination are chosen to match the initial conditions.

Recall 5What checks are needed before accepting a trajectory?

Differentiate and substitute to check the equation over the whole interval, then check each initial condition. Units and the initial force balance provide additional checks; equality at a single instant does not prove the law for the entire motion.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without rereading, reconstruct the reasoning: a photograph leaves the velocity unknown; the force balance imposes the acceleration; the highest derivative determines the order; the exponentials are chosen by the law; the amplitudes are chosen by the initial data; substitution and the initial conditions check distinct requirements.
