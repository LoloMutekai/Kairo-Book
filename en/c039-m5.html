Level 1 · C039·M5

Changes of basis

Your goalDescribe the same displacement in two ordered bases, choose the direction of the change of basis matrix, and check the coordinates by reconstruction.

Before you start
c032-m5 : computing rank · c014-m5 : invariants · c039-m1 : subspaces · c039-m2 : linear independence · c039-m3 : bases · c039-m4 : linear maps

✿01 See the idea

A small cart must reach a mark painted on the floor. From its starting point, this mark is 5 m east and 2 m north. However, the controller does not ask for “east, north”: it sets the amount of a command b that moves 2 m east and a command c that moves 1 m east and 1 m north. How can you express the same journey for this controller?

The painted mark does not move when you change the description. You replace the directions used to measure the displacement. As with the bases already studied, the coordinates are the coefficients of a combination of vectors; they only have meaning when the basis and its order are specified.

The scene and its values are fictional. We assume a flat floor, fixed directions, no slipping, and proportional commands: a fraction of a command produces the same fraction of the displacement; a negative coefficient reverses its direction. The displacements add together, and the cart does not turn between commands.

The first diagram shows the commands. The second reconstructs the required journey. Before reading the calculation, work out which command can supply the northward displacement and why some eastward distance will still remain.

The rule to remember

Always write down the starting basis and the destination basis. The matrix whose columns describe the new vectors in the old basis reconstructs the old coordinates; for the reverse conversion, solve the system.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Convert the journey from floor coordinates to controller coordinates

The target journey is still 5 m east and 2 m north. Let us determine the coefficients of commands b and c, then a rule that will convert any journey in the same plane.

  1. Specify the bases, symbols, and units

    E = (e, n) is the ordered basis for the floor: e is the displacement of 1 m east and n is the displacement of 1 m north. B = (b, c) is the ordered basis for the controller, with b = 2e + 0n and c = 1e + 1n. We work with displacements in the plane, using real coefficients.

    v denotes the total displacement. The notation [v]E denotes its coordinate column in E; [v]B denotes its column in B. In [v]E = (x ; y), x and y count displacements of 1 m: they are dimensionless coefficients associated with the signed distances x m and y m. In [v]B = (α ; β), α and β are also dimensionless and set the amounts of b and c respectively. The vectors e, n, b, c, and v represent displacements in metres.

    Order matters: the first coefficient multiplies the first vector. Here x = 5 and y = 2. We need to find α and β such that v = αb + βc; we move neither the origin nor the painted mark.

    v=xe+yn=αb+βc

  2. Build the matrix for the correct direction

    Expanding αb + βc in E gives (2α + 1β)e + (0α + 1β)n. The matrix P therefore collects [b]E and then [c]E as columns, in the order of B. The product P[v]B gives [v]E: it starts from the commands and reconstructs the floor coordinates. P is a dimensionless matrix.

    The direction E ← B written below P in the formula is a reminder of this convention. The term “change of basis matrix” alone may follow different conventions in different texts; the written equality specifies the direction unambiguously.

    [v]E=PE←B[v]B=(2101)(αβ)

  3. Solve for the commands for the required journey

    The north row gives 0α + 1β = 2. Since b contributes no northward displacement, β = 2/1 = 2. This command already contributes 2 × 1 = 2 m east.

    The east row then requires 2α = 5 − 2 = 3. We subtract the contribution of c to find what remains to be supplied; we divide by the displacement supplied by a command b. Thus α = 3/2 = 3/2, or 1.5 times command b.

    [v]B=(322)

  4. Check by reconstructing the displacement

    The contribution αb is (3 m ; 0 m). The contribution βc is (2 m ; 2 m). Their sum gives (5 m ; 2 m), exactly the required journey.

    This check starts from the commands we obtained. It can reveal a matrix used in the reverse direction, an ordering error, or a missing contribution. Merely comparing the two lists of numbers would not be a check: they describe the same object using different reference vectors.

    32(20)+2(11)=(52)

  5. Why the conversion exists and is unique

    For any journey with coordinates (x ; y) in E, the same method gives β = y and then α = (x − y)/2. Every real coordinate column for the floor therefore has a representation in B, and each step determines a single result.

    We also recover linear independence: if sb + tc is the 0 displacement, its north component requires t = 0, then its east component requires 2s = 0, so s = 0. Here the letters s and t denote dimensionless real coefficients. B is indeed a basis for the plane. If the commands were linearly dependent, this guarantee would disappear.

    The matrix Q performs the conversion E → B. Its first row has coefficients 1/2 and -1/2; the second preserves y. P and Q are dimensionless 2 × 2 matrices. Q is the inverse of P: the symbol P with exponent −1 means “inverse conversion”, not the reciprocal of each entry. I denotes the identity matrix of the same size, which leaves every column unchanged.

    Q=P−1=(12−1201)

  6. Check the rule before reusing it

    Applying Q to [b]E gives (1 ; 0): a vector b must correspond to exactly one command b and no command c. Applying Q to [c]E gives (0 ; 1). These two checks establish QP = I; here the product PQ also gives I. They test the basis vectors, not just our particular journey.

    A coordinate conversion thus represents the identity map on displacements: v remains v. The numbers change because the vectors that interpret them change. A physical transformation of the cart would change v; a shift of origin would also require different handling. Here we keep the same origin and the same physical units.

    For other ordered bases of the same space, the method is still to arrange the starting basis vectors, described in the destination basis, as columns. The conversion equality and reconstruction specify the direction; solving a system provides the inverse conversion.

    [v]B=Q[v]E,QP=PQ=I

Explain it in your own words

Hide the matrices. Reconstruct their columns from the commands in the diagrams, state the direction of each conversion, then recover the commands for the initial journey. Why does the number 1.5 not mean a displacement of 1.5 m east?

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The columns of P come from B, so P must convert from E to B.”

    Test a single command b: the column (1 ; 0) is in B, and P transforms it into (2 ; 0) in E. P reconstructs the journey in floor coordinates. To start from floor coordinates, solve the system or use Q.

    Recover the direction using a single command

  • “I can put b and c in the rows; it amounts to the same thing.”

    With column coordinates, each coefficient multiplies an entire column. So write [b]E and then [c]E as columns. Check the product on command b alone; keeping a consistent convention prevents accidentally transposing the matrix.

    Revisit the construction of P

  • “If the coordinates change, the cart ends up somewhere else.”

    The coefficients (5 ; 2) multiply e and n; (1.5 ; 2) multiply b and c. Reconstruct the displacements: both sums reach the same mark. A coefficient of b counts a command of 2 m, not a metre.

    Compare the two descriptions of the journey

Exercise 1 · Direct application

From the controller to the floor

The same cart now receives 1 command b and 3 commands c. Calculate its coordinates in E and the signed distances in metres. State which matrix to use before calculating.

Hint 1

You start with the coefficients in B. First reconstruct 1b + 3c.

Hint 2

Each command c contributes both eastward and northward.

Compare with the solution

The required direction is B → E, so we multiply by P. East: 1 × 2 + 3 × 1 = 2 + 3 = 5. North: 1 × 0 + 3 × 1 = 0 + 3 = 3.

The coordinates are (5 ; 3) in E, corresponding to 5 m east and 3 m north. Inverse check: (5 − 3)/2 = 1 and β = 3 = 3; we recover the starting commands.

Exercise 2 · Solving and checking

Reach a mark to the west

This time the target mark is -1 m in the east direction and 3 m north, from the same starting point. Give the commands in B. A negative coefficient is allowed: explain it and check the journey.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

A negative east coordinate means a displacement to the west. The north row still determines β.

Hint 2

After the commands c, subtract their eastward contribution before dividing by 2.

Compare with the solution

The direction is E → B: we solve P[v]B = [v]E. The north row gives β = 3/1 = 3. These commands c already contribute 3 m east.

The remaining displacement is -1 − 3 = -4 m in the east direction. Thus α = -4/2 = -2. This negative coefficient applies command b in the opposite direction; β remains positive.

Check: αb supplies -4 m in the east direction and no northward displacement; βc supplies 3 m east and 3 m north. The sum is (-1 m ; 3 m). It reaches the mark without changing the basis commands.

Check your reasoning

  • I find (-2 ; 3) in B and keep the order b, c.
  • I justify the sign of α by the westward displacement needed to offset the eastward contribution.
  • I reconstruct both components of the journey.

Exercise 3 · Transfer and diagnosis

The buttons are swapped

Return to the initial journey of 5 m east and 2 m north. The display now uses the ordered basis D = (c, b). Give [v]D and the matrix R that converts D → E. Then explain the error produced if [v]D is passed to the old matrix P.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The displacement must remain v. In D, the coefficient of c is written before the coefficient of b.

Hint 2

Build R by arranging the columns in the order of D, then reconstruct the journey.

Compare with the solution

In B, v = 1.5b + 2c. In D, we write the same sum 2c + 1.5b: [v]D = (2 ; 1.5). D remains a basis; only the order has changed. The matrix R has column [c]E first, then [b]E.

R=(1210),R(232)=(52)

Indeed, the east component is 2 × 1 + 1.5 × 2 = 2 + 3 = 5; the north component is 2 × 1 + 1.5 × 0 = 2. We recover the same displacement.

The old P would instead read 2 commands b and 1.5 times command c. It would give an east component of 4 + 1.5 = 5.5 m and a north component of 1.5 m. The error comes from the mismatch between the order of the coefficients and the order of the columns. Fixing it requires swapping both the coordinate list and the columns that interpret it.

Check your reasoning

  • I find (2 ; 1.5) in D and state D → E for R.
  • I swap the columns without transposing the whole matrix.
  • I distinguish the journey preserved by R from the incorrect journey obtained with P.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does a change of basis preserve in the cart scene?

It preserves the displacement v, its starting point, and its endpoint. The coordinates change because the vectors used to reconstruct v change.

Recall 2How do you build the matrix P that converts from B = (b, c) to E?

Arrange [b]E and then [c]E as columns. The equality [v]E = P[v]B specifies its direction: it reconstructs the displacement in floor coordinates from the commands.

Recall 3How do you go from floor coordinates E to controller coordinates B without guessing the direction of the matrix?

Solve P[v]B = [v]E. For this basis, β = y and α = (x − y)/2. The matrix Q collects this rule; checking Q[b]E and Q[c]E checks its columns.

Recall 4Why must the commands form a basis?

A basis ensures that a displacement in the plane has unique coefficients. Here the north row determines β, then the east row determines α; linearly dependent commands would not provide this guarantee.

Recall 5What must change if the display switches from (b, c) to (c, b)?

Swap the coefficients and the columns of the matrix that interprets them. Otherwise, the same numbers are assigned to the wrong commands and the resulting displacement changes.
