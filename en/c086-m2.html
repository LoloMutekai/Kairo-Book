Level 2 · C086·M2

Reward

Your goalDistinguish a valued outcome from its surprise, adjust an expectation, and specify what would demonstrate reinforcement of behavior.

Before you start
c085-m5 : development and environment · c083-m5 : transfer · c084-m5 : misleading hindsight · c086-m1 : homeostasis

✿01 See the idea

After a walk, you arrive thirsty at a drink stand. Its sign advertises a cool drink. The sign makes you expect something; the drink you receive is the outcome. It can be welcome without being surprising if you already knew exactly what to expect.

Here we call an outcome a reward when the organism assigns it a positive value in the situation being considered. This value depends on state and experience: the same drink is not necessarily equally appealing before and after quenching your thirst. The link with homeostasis is this dependence on state; it is not enough to describe all reward learning.

The sign is a predictive cue: a signal that announces the outcome. Here, learning its value means revising what this signal leads you to expect. The surprise used in our calculation is a signed difference between what is received and what is expected. “Signed” means that we keep the direction of the difference, favorable or unfavorable.

The scene and all its values are fictional. For a pencil-and-paper exercise, we assign the outcome a score of 8 points when the expectation was 4 points. This scoring scale is a convention of the model, not a measurement of pleasure, thirst, or dopamine. It lets us distinguish the quantities without claiming to measure a brain.

Reinforcement refers to an effect on behavior: a consequence increases the subsequent probability of the behavior that preceded it, under comparable conditions. Returning to the drink stand could be the behavior we track. Receiving the drink or increasing an expectation in a calculation is not enough to demonstrate this effect; we would need to observe choices and compare relevant situations. The broader question of what prompts action belongs to the next unit.

The rule to remember

Keep the valued outcome, the expectation before that outcome, their difference, and the change in behavior that remains to be established separate.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

What the sign will lead you to expect on the next visit

On a visit to the drink stand, the expectation is V = 4 points and the outcome received is scored at r = 8 points. We choose a learning rate α = 1/4 = 0.25. Let us calculate the expectation after this trial, then check it another way.

  1. Define the quantities and scope

    V is the expected value when the sign is seen, before the drink is received. r is the value assigned to the outcome received on this trial. V and r are expressed in the same arbitrary points. δ, the Greek letter delta, will denote their difference, also in points. V′ will be the revised expectation; the prime symbol means “after the trial”.

    α, the Greek letter alpha, is the fraction of the difference incorporated into the expectation. It has no unit and lies between 0 and 1 inclusive. Here α = 0.25: we take 1 part out of 4 equal parts of the difference. This choice makes the calculation easier; it is neither estimated from a person nor prescribed as a biological learning rate.

    We track only this signal and its immediate outcome. The scoring scale, context, and state before drinking are assumed to be comparable across trials; no other consequence enters the score. The outcome must be known: a drink stand whose opening status is still unknown is not automatically assigned the value 0.

  2. Calculate surprise in the correct direction

    δ=r−V

    We subtract the expected value from the received value to find out whether the outcome is worth more or less than expected. Here δ = 8 − 4 = 4 points. The sign is positive: the outcome is better than expected on the scoring scale. This does not mean that the drink has a value of 4 points; its assigned value remains 8 points.

    If r = V, the difference is 0 even if the outcome is valued. If r is less than V, the difference is negative; this can happen even when the outcome retains a positive value. This is a comparison with the expectation, not an absolute ranking as good or bad.

  3. Take only a fraction of the difference

    The adjustment is αδ: multiplying a quantity in points by a coefficient with no unit leaves points. Numerically, 1/4 × 4 = 1 point. We change the expectation by this adjustment, not by the whole outcome.

    V′=V+αδ=4+1=5 points

    The model will therefore expect 5 points on a comparable visit. It has not changed the drink already received, and this update is not an observation of the person’s next choice.

  4. Check using another form

    Let us replace δ with r − V and group the terms that multiply V. We obtain a weighted average: a sum in which each value is multiplied by a weight, with nonnegative weights that sum to 1.

    V′=(1−α)V+αr

    The retained weight is 1 − 1/4 = 3/4. The old expectation contributes 3/4 × 4 = 3 points; the received value contributes 1/4 × 8 = 2 points. The sum gives 3 + 2 = 5 points, as in the first calculation.

    Position check: 5 lies between 4 and 8. With a coefficient in the stated interval, this rule does not overshoot the outcome toward which it moves the expectation. If α = 0, it keeps V; if α = 1, it takes r in full. These checks apply to the chosen rule, not to every possible form of learning.

  5. Separate an expectation calculation from evidence of reward

    We know what our rule produces for the chosen inputs. We have not measured how the person actually values the drink or demonstrated that the sign has changed their choices. In particular, no neurotransmitter concentration can be deduced from the score in points.

    To say that a behavior has been reinforced, we would need to define that behavior, observe its repetition on comparable occasions, and examine the role of the consequence. A person might return because the drink stand is on their usual route; the sequence “drink, then return” alone does not resolve this competing explanation.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the equations. Explain why the difference is 4 points but the adjustment is only 1 point. Reconstruct the operations and their units, then say what would need to be observed to conclude that the behavior has been reinforced.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “If surprise is 0, there is no longer a reward.”

    Compare the received value with the expected value. A drink expected at its actual value can remain appreciated even when their difference is 0. The rule then leaves the expectation unchanged; it does not make the outcome disappear.

    Redo the case of an exactly expected outcome

  • “A negative difference necessarily means punishment.”

    An outcome can be worse than expected without being aversive. The absence of the expected drink also gives a negative difference. Punishment is defined by a subsequent decrease in behavior, which would require separate observations.

    Reread the meaning of the difference received − expected

  • “The model increases a dopamine variable, so it proves pleasure and reinforcement.”

    A named software variable and a biological measurement are different objects. Check the written rule, then distinguish whether it is actually called, whether it is validated, and its effect on behavior; none of these steps follows automatically.

Exercise 1 · Direct application

A welcome and exactly expected drink

In the same scene, now suppose V = 8 points and r = 8 points. Keep α = 1/4. Calculate δ and V′. Does the absence of surprise mean that the drink has no value?

Compare with the solution

δ = 8 − 8 = 0 point. The adjustment is 1/4 × 0 = 0 point. Thus V′ = 8 + 0 = 8 points. The rule keeps the expectation because this trial does not contradict it.

The outcome retains its assigned value of 8 points. It is the difference that is 0. This calculation alone cannot establish either a disappearance of pleasure or any particular effect on behavior.

Exercise 2 · Sequence and check

The expected drink does not arrive

Start again from V = 5 points, obtained in the example. On another comparable visit, the advertised drink is not served; the outcome is known. We score this omission at r = 0 point on our scale. Calculate the next expectation with α = 1/4, then check it using the weights. Is the sign of δ enough to establish punishment?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Do not start from either the initial expectation or the previous exercise: this problem sets its own starting point.

Hint 2

The weighted average contains a term αr that is 0; what remains is the retained weight multiplied by V.

Compare with the solution

The difference is 0 − 5 = -5 points. The adjustment is 1/4 × (-5) = -5/4 point, or -1.25 point. Adding it gives V′ = 5 + (-5/4) = 15/4 points, or 3.75 points.

Another route: the received value is 0, so V′ = 3/4 × 5 = 15/4 points. The expectation has decreased, remains positive, and lies between the starting value and the outcome scored at 0. The rule does not forget everything after this omission.

The negative difference describes an omission relative to the expectation. It does not establish punishment: in particular, a decrease in behavior would need to be observed. Furthermore, an outcome that is still unknown could not be replaced by an omission scored at 0.

Check your reasoning

  • Use 5 points as the starting expectation.
  • Keep the negative sign of the difference and the adjustment.
  • Obtain 3.75 points using the weighted average.
  • Distinguish a known omission, missing information, and a behavioral effect.

Exercise 3 · Critical transfer

Same drink, same value?

In another possible continuation of the example, start again from V = 5 points and receive another drink scored at r = 8 points, with α = 1/4. Calculate V′. A classmate then claims: “These points prove that you will return, and we can keep r if you have already quenched your thirst.” Discuss these claims separately.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The new difference is smaller than in the example; still keep the same coefficient.

Hint 2

Identify a claim about behavior and an assumption about the scoring scale. What observations or information do they lack?

Compare with the solution

δ = 8 − 5 = 3 points. The adjustment is 1/4 × 3 = 3/4 point. The new expectation is 5 + 3/4 = 23/4 points, or 5.75 points. The smaller difference gives a smaller adjustment without changing α.

This result is an expectation within the model. To conclude that receiving the drink has reinforced returning to the drink stand, we lack observations of behavior and a comparison that examines other explanations, such as the usual route.

Having already had a drink changes the starting state. The same physical object therefore does not guarantee the same value score. The scoring scale and the comparability of the trials must be reexamined; no data here allow a new r to be calculated. Automatically assigning it 0 would also invent information.

Check your reasoning

  • Calculate the difference from 5 points and obtain 5.75 points.
  • Name a missing piece of behavioral evidence and a competing explanation.
  • Identify the assumption of comparable value and refuse to invent a new score.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1In the drink stand scene, what do r, V, and V′ distinguish?

r is the value assigned to the outcome received; V is the expectation before receiving it; V′ is the revised expectation after this trial. These values use the same fictional points.

Recall 2How can you reconstruct the difference and interpret its sign?

δ = r − V. Positive: better than expected; 0: exactly as expected; negative: worse than expected. This relative sign is not enough to describe the outcome as pleasant or aversive.

Recall 3How can you go from the difference to the new expectation and check the result?

Add the fraction α of the difference to V: V′ = V + αδ. Check using the weights: V′ = (1 − α)V + αr. If α is between 0 and 1, the new expectation stays between V and r. α has no unit; the adjustment remains in points.

Recall 4What does the calculation lack to establish reinforcement of returning to the drink stand?

Observations showing that the consequence increases the subsequent probability of this behavior in comparable situations, with a comparison that examines other explanations. A calculated expectation or a variable name is not enough.

Recall 5Why should you not automatically keep the same r after quenching your thirst?

The organism’s state has changed. The value of the same outcome and the comparability of the trials must therefore be reexamined. The fictional scale is neither a measurement of pleasure nor a dopamine concentration.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without rereading, reconstruct the sequence sign → expectation → outcome → signed difference → adjustment → next expectation, defining the symbols and units. Then explain how an expected reward can give a 0 difference and what evidence would establish reinforcement of behavior.
