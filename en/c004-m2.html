Linear equations

Your goalFind a rental duration from its price by transforming an equality, then checking the solution in context.

Before you start
c003-m5 : scientific notation · c004-m1 : expressions

✿01 See the idea

You find a bicycle rental receipt: 11 €. The rate in this activity includes a fixed fee of 3 € at the start, then 2 € per hour. The receipt shows what was paid; the duration is missing. How can you work backwards through the price calculation?

The price was calculated by multiplying the duration by the hourly rate, then adding the fixed fee. To work backwards, first subtract the fixed fee from the total. The remainder pays only for the hours; then divide it by the cost of an hour.

Think of an equality as two descriptions of the same amount: the price calculation on the left, the receipt on the right. If you subtract an amount, you must subtract it from each side of the equals sign. Otherwise, the amounts no longer describe the same situation.

Everything is fictional: the rate, the receipt and the rental rules. The duration is a whole number of hours, at least 0 hours; the fixed fee is still due even for 0 hours. There are no discounts or other charges. This deliberately simple model does not describe an actual commercial offer.

The rule to remember

Undo the operations in reverse order, apply each operation to both sides, then check the original equality and the rules for the duration.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

What rental duration produced the receipt?

The fixed fee is 3 €, each hour costs 2 € and the receipt shows 11 €. Let us find the nonnegative whole number of hours without trying prices at random.

  1. Give meaning to the letter and the equality

    h denotes the duration we want to find, expressed in hours. F is the fixed fee, in euros; a is the hourly rate, in euros per hour; B is the receipt total, in euros. The known values are F = 3 €, a = 2 €/hour and B = 11 €. The numerical expressions that follow use these units.

    The product a × h is an amount in euros, to which we can add F. An expression, F + ah, lets us calculate a price. The equation F + ah = B asks which values of h make this equality true: these values are its solutions. Here the unknown letter is multiplied by a known number and is not squared; this is a linear equation in one unknown.

    F+ah=B;3+2h=11

    Writing 2h is shorthand for 2 × h. The number 2 is the coefficient of h: it multiplies the duration. Do not add 2 to h. Our rule allows only whole numbers of hours, with h at least equal to 0.

  2. Subtract the fixed fee from each side

    The fixed fee was added after the cost of the hours. Subtracting 3 from each side undoes this addition while preserving the equality. On the left, the fixed fee and its subtraction cancel out; on the right, we calculate the budget for the hours.

    3+2h−3=11−3

    The remaining fixed fee is 0 €. The reduced receipt total is 8 €. This leaves:

    2h=8

    This line says that the cost of the hours is 8 €; it does not yet give 8 hours. The equals sign still connects monetary amounts.

  3. Divide to isolate the duration

    The coefficient 2 multiplies h. We therefore divide each entire side by 2, which is nonzero. On the left, the coefficient becomes 1; multiplying h by 1 leaves h. On the right, we divide the 8 € into hourly costs of 2 € per hour.

    2h2=82;h=4

    The resulting duration is 4 hours. For the units: euros divided by euros per hour give hours. The same method can be written as follows:

    h=B−Fa, avec a≠0

    The subtraction B − F is carried out before the division. Here a is strictly positive. Each transformation is reversible: we can multiply again, then add the fixed fee back. No other value of h satisfies this equation.

  4. Reconstruct the receipt total to check the calculation

    Let us replace h with 4 in the original equation. First multiply the duration by the rate: 2 €/hour × 4 hours = 8 €. Then add the fixed fee: 3 € + 8 € = 11 €.

    3+2×4=11=11

    The reconstructed amount equals the receipt total. Let e denote the difference between the reconstructed price and the receipt total, in euros: e = F + ah − B. Here it is 11 − 11 = 0 €. This 0 difference confirms the equality; we must still check that the duration is allowed.

  5. See what subtracting from only one side breaks

    If you erase only the fixed fee on the left, you incorrectly write 2h = 11. This incorrect calculation would give 5.5 hours. It is a mistaken algebraic attempt, not a new rule for charging for fractions of an hour.

    Substituting it into the original price gives an hourly part of 11 €, then a price of 14 €. The difference is 14 − 11 = 3 €: exactly the fixed fee forgotten in the transformation. This result fails the receipt check, even before we apply the whole hours rule.

    Substitution does not mean writing 3 + 2 + 4 = 9 either. This addition replaces the product with another operation and confuses the roles of the quantities. The cost of the hours is calculated using 2 × 4.

  6. Decide whether the solution is allowed for the rental

    The value 4 is a whole number at least equal to 0: it follows the rule of the scenario. The receipt therefore corresponds to 4 hours in this fictional model.

    This final check is separate from substitution. A negative duration can satisfy an equality transformed correctly, but it does not represent an allowed rental. The exercises will ask you to examine the boundary set by the fixed fee.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the calculation lines. Explain why we first subtract the fixed fee, why we subtract it from each side, then why division gives hours. Next reconstruct the receipt total and name the check that remains after checking the equality.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The fixed fee moves to the other side and changes sign on its own.”

    Write the subtraction on each side before simplifying. Erasing the fixed fee only on the left leads to 5.5 hours and reconstructs 14 €, instead of 11 €. Describing the operation applied to both sides avoids treating this as a magic rule.

    Review subtracting the fixed fee and the incorrect calculation

  • “To replace h, I add the fixed fee plus the rate plus the hours.”

    The coefficient multiplies h. First calculate 2 × 4 = 8 €, then add 3 €. The sum 3 + 2 + 4 gives 9, but does not calculate the rental price.

    Review substitution in the receipt calculation

  • “If the equality is true, I can declare the rental valid.”

    Also check what h represents: the duration must be a whole number and nonnegative. For a receipt total below the fixed fee, the algebraic solution is negative. Correct substitution does not turn this duration into a possible rental.

    Try receipt totals at the boundary set by the fixed fee

Exercise 1 · Guided application

Another receipt, the same rate

The receipt now shows 13 €. The fixed fee remains 3 € and the rate 2 €/hour. Find the duration, then reconstruct the price. Write down the units before opening a hint.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Subtract the fixed fee from each side: the remainder of the receipt total pays only for the hours.

Hint 2

Divide this remainder by the hourly rate, then check the amount in the original equation.

Compare with the solution

The equation is 3 + 2h = 13. Subtracting 3 from each side gives 2h = 10. Dividing by 2 gives h = 5 hours.

Check: 2 × 5 = 10 € for the hours; 3 + 10 = 13 €, matching the receipt. The duration is a whole number and nonnegative. It is therefore allowed in our scenario.

Check your reasoning

  • Write the subtraction and division on each side, in that order.
  • Give the duration in hours and reconstruct the receipt total in euros.
  • Check that the duration follows the rules of the scenario.

Exercise 2 · Independent work on paper

Justify each line

A receipt for 7 € has been found. Solve the price equation by writing the operations on the entire sides before simplifying. Explain why you can reverse the steps, then check the duration in the original price calculation.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

A line can still contain the fixed fee being added and the same fixed fee being subtracted. This notation shows what preserves the equality.

Hint 2

To return to the original equation, multiply each side by the rate, then add the fixed fee to each side.

Compare with the solution
3+2h−3=7−3

Subtracting the same amount from both sides gives 2h = 4. Dividing each side by 2 gives h = 2 hours. We can undo this division by multiplying by 2, then undo the subtraction by adding 3: the solutions are preserved in both directions.

The check starts from the original line: 2 × 2 = 4 €; 3 + 4 = 7 €. The value found is a whole number and nonnegative. It therefore matches the receipt and the duration rule.

Check your reasoning

  • Show the same subtraction on each side, then the same division.
  • Justify division by a nonzero rate and explain the inverse operations.
  • Check the solution in the original equation, including the units and the duration rule.

Exercise 3 · Transfer and objection

An exact result can be rejected

Compare a receipt for 3 € and a receipt for 1 €, still at the same rate. For each, calculate the solution of the equation, check it by substitution and decide whether the duration is allowed. Then answer the objection: “If the receipt total can be reconstructed, the rental is possible.”

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Subtract the fixed fee in both equations. A 0 remainder and a negative remainder mean different things.

Hint 2

The equality and the duration rule are two separate checks. The fixed fee is still due even if no hours are used.

Compare with the solution

Receipt for 3 €: the remainder is 0 €; dividing by 2 gives 0 hours. The hourly part is 0 €, then the price is 3 €. The duration is allowed by our rule, which permits 0 hours with the fixed fee still due.

Receipt for 1 €: the remainder is -2 €; dividing by 2 gives -1 hour. In the algebraic calculation, the hourly part is -2 €, then 3 + (-2) = 1 €. The equality is indeed verified.

This second solution is negative, however: no allowed duration produces this receipt total. Every permitted duration costs at least the fixed fee, which is 3 €. The objection confuses satisfying an equation with meeting the constraints of the situation. Do not sell a negative duration or silently replace the result with 0 hours.

Check your reasoning

  • Obtain 0 hours and -1 hour, showing the operations.
  • Observe that substitution gives an exact equality in both cases.
  • Accept the 0 duration under the stated rule and reject the negative duration.
  • Explain why the second receipt is impossible using the minimum price, without changing the rate.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does the receipt equation ask beyond what the price expression does?

The expression calculates the price from a duration. The equation requires this price to equal the receipt total and asks which duration makes the equality true.

Recall 2Why subtract the fixed fee from each side?

Each side describes the same amount. Subtracting the same amount from each preserves the equality; erasing the fixed fee from only one side changes the equation.

Recall 3How do you find the duration, and why in this order?

The price is calculated by multiplication followed by addition of the fixed fee. Undo the addition by subtracting the fixed fee from each side, then undo the multiplication by dividing each side by the nonzero rate.

Recall 4What calculation checks the duration found?

Replace the letter with the duration in the original equation, multiply the rate by this duration, then add the fixed fee. The reconstructed amount must equal the receipt total.

Recall 5Is exact substitution enough to allow the rental?

The duration must also be a nonnegative whole number. A receipt total below the fixed fee gives a negative algebraic solution, which the scenario excludes even though it satisfies the equality.
