Level 2 · C078·M5

Consent and measurement error

Your goalCheck authorization before analyzing an image, then report an angle with an error bound and explicit limitations.

Before you start
c027-m5 : complex numbers · c040-m5 : least squares · c036-m5 : annotated graphs · c022-m5 : untrusted inputs and injection · c078-m1 : pixel, coordinates and normalization · c078-m2 : landmarks, segments and angles · c078-m3 : degenerate geometry and absence · c078-m4 : external model and adaptation

✿01 See the idea

Camille wants to see their elbow angle in a photo taken from the side. Before opening the image, the tool presents its plan: read this photo, place the shoulder, elbow and wrist landmarks, calculate the angle in the image, and process it on this device for at most 60 s. Camille can refuse or withdraw consent. This is the protocol chosen for our fictional scene.

Camille accepts this scope. This consent allows the planned analysis; it does not make the landmarks exact. If the center of the elbow is blurred, the segments may rotate slightly and the reported angle may change. Authorization and measurement quality answer different questions.

We will use synthetic coordinates and fictional error bounds. The drawing represents only the landmarks in the photo and does not depict a real person. This is neither a personal measurement nor an evaluated device.

A useful result can therefore take this form: nominal angle 90°, compatible with 85° to 95° under the stated assumptions. This interval concerns the angle projected into the image. By itself, it describes neither movement in space nor Camille’s health.

The rule to remember

Before reading the image, check the scope and validity of consent; before drawing a conclusion, state the quantity measured and what bounds its error.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Authorize the photo, then bound the elbow angle

Camille has consented only to the specified photo, local calculation of the angle and a duration of 60 s. At the proposed reading time, 45 s have elapsed since consent, with no withdrawal. The synthetic landmarks are A = (200 ; 100), B = (100 ; 100) and C = (100 ; 200), in pixels. The errors in the directions of BA and BC will be assumed bounded by 2° and 3° respectively.

  1. Check what authorizes the reading

    The scope specifies the input used, the intended measurement, where processing will take place and its duration. Here, a receipt is the record of explicit consent to this scope. The availability of a photo, or an identifier supplied by a tool, does not create this consent.

    Let t denote the elapsed time and D the authorized duration, both in seconds. Our convention includes the end boundary: 0 ≤ t ≤ D. Here, 45 s ≤ 60 s; the time remaining is D − t = 60 − 45 = 15 s. The receipt is known, consent has not been withdrawn, and the photo matches the scope: the planned reading can begin.

    Consent to this photo covers neither a microphone, another image, nor sharing. If a condition is missing, the analysis waits for appropriate new consent. This check concerns the exercise protocol.

  2. Recover the nominal angle from the landmarks

    B is the common vertex. Vector u = A − B represents BA and v = C − B represents BC; their x and y components are displacements in pixels. Thus u = (200 − 100 ; 100 − 100) = (100 ; 0) pixels and v = (100 − 100 ; 200 − 100) = (0 ; 100) pixels.

    The dot product u·v adds the products of the components: 100 × 0 + 0 × 100 = 0 + 0 = 0 square pixels. The vectors are nonzero. They are therefore perpendicular; their nominal interior angle θ̂, read “theta hat” or “estimated theta”, is 90°. The calculation keeps the same scale on both axes; no stretching is applied to the image.

    To track small rotations, let α̂ denote the nominal direction of BA and β̂ that of BC, measured from the horizontal rightward direction, turning toward the bottom of the image. Here α̂ = 0° and β̂ = 90°. We recover θ̂ = β̂ − α̂ = 90°. The hats mark the directions and angle obtained from the landmarks, without claiming they are exact for the photo.

  3. State exactly what the error bound covers

    α and β denote the directions of the ideal segments we are trying to locate in this same image. The signed errors are ea = α − α̂ and eᵦ = β − β̂, in degrees. We assume |ea| ≤ a and |eᵦ| ≤ b, with a = 2° and b = 3°. The bars |…| give the absolute value, meaning the size of a difference without its sign.

    These angular bounds are fictional assumptions. They are derived neither from the pixels nor from a model confidence score. In real use, the bounds would need to be justified by a suitable, authorized method. A detection score does not automatically provide an error in degrees.

    We assume the segments are nonzero and their directions are close enough to those in the drawing to keep β − α between 0° and 180°. Thus the interior angle θ remains θ = β − α, without changing branches. The bounds concern location within the image; they do not bound perspective distortion or a body angle outside the image plane.

  4. Add the bounds and explain why

    Replacing the directions with their nominal values and errors gives θ − θ̂ = eᵦ − ea. The largest difference can occur when the errors rotate the segments in opposite directions. The triangle inequality states that the size of a difference cannot exceed the sum of the sizes:

    |θ−θ^|=|eβ−eα|≤|eβ|+|eα|≤b+a

    The total bound U is therefore a + b = 2 + 3 = 5°. We make no assumption of independence or of a probability distribution. U is a conditional bound, rather than an error actually observed or a standard deviation.

  5. Construct the interval and check its center

    Subtracting U gives the smallest compatible value, and adding it gives the largest:

    θ∈[θ^−U;θ^+U]=[85;95] degrés

    Lower bound: 90 − 5 = 85°. Upper bound: 90 + 5 = 95°. The full width is 95 − 85 = 10°, or 2 × U. It differs from the error bound 5°.

    Check: the sum of the endpoints is 180°, then their average is 180 ÷ 2 = 90°. We recover the center 90°. The interval remains strictly between 0° and 180°: our branch assumption is consistent.

  6. Check using the extreme directions

    Another approach: α belongs to [-2° ; 2°] and β to [87° ; 93°]. To minimize β − α, take β at its lowest and α at its highest: 87 − 2 = 85°. To maximize it, do the reverse: 93 − (-2) = 95°.

    We recover the same bounds without starting directly from θ̂ ± U. A common error that rotates the segments together could cancel out; since no cancellation has been established, we retain the extremes compatible with the given bounds alone.

  7. Report a result within the scope of the measurement

    The report is: “For this photo and these landmarks, nominal projected angle 90°, compatible interval [85° ; 95°] if the direction errors do not exceed 2° and 3°.” It specifies the measurement plane and the assumptions under which the bound holds.

    Reading the same landmarks again gives the same number without testing their accuracy. A systematic landmark offset may persist. If landmarks coincide or are missing, the angle is unavailable; 0° would instead denote a defined angle. No health interpretation follows from this geometric interval.

  8. Check consent again before another reading

    At t = 61 s, the duration would be exceeded by 1 s: another reading is refused, even if the previous angle seems credible. If Camille withdraws consent earlier, we also refuse another reading without waiting for the duration to end.

    Here, withdrawal blocks new readings; by itself, it does not establish that an existing result has been erased. After a restart that loses the receipt, the tool must again have verifiable consent before reading the image. The precision of the angle never restores missing authorization.

Explain it in your own words

Without rereading, explain why valid authorization and a correct interval are two separate requirements. Then recover the extremes by mentally rotating the segments in opposite directions.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I provided the photo, so all processing is authorized.”

    Recover the agreed input, measurement, processing destination and duration. Check explicit consent, any withdrawal, and its validity at the time the input is read. A new input or a different purpose requires its own check.

    Review the check before reading

  • “The errors must cancel out; I subtract the bounds.”

    A positive bound describes the possible size of an error, rather than its sign. Rotate BA in one direction and BC in the other: the differences add up. Recover the extreme directions to check the angle bounds.

    Review the check using the extreme directions

  • “The number is stable and precise, so the posture is good.”

    An identical repetition using the same landmarks does not test their accuracy. State the projected quantity, the assumed bounds and the perspective effects left outside the model. A health conclusion requires other evidence.

    Revise the report in the transfer exercise

Exercise 2 · Application · calculate and check

The wrist becomes less accurately located

In the same photo, the nominal landmarks remain identical, but the bound on the direction of BC changes to 6°. The bound for BA remains 2°. Before calculating, predict whether the width will increase or decrease. Calculate the new total bound, the interval and its width. Why does the center stay the same?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The nominal coordinates have not changed; only the allowed errors change.

Hint 2

Add the bounds, then subtract their sum from 90° and add it to that angle.

Compare with the solution

The range widens: we allow a larger error in one direction. The new total bound is 2 + 6 = 8°. The endpoints are 90 − 8 = 82° and 90 + 8 = 98°.

The interval is [82° ; 98°]. Its width is 98 − 82 = 16°, which is 6° more than 10°. Check: the width is 2 times 8° and the distances from the endpoints to the center are equal.

The center remains 90° because the nominal directions are unchanged and the bounds are symmetric. This expresses greater uncertainty about the same result, without a new observation.

Check your reasoning

  • You predict a wider range before doing the operations.
  • You find an error bound of 8° and a width of 16° without confusing them.
  • You link the unchanged center to the same landmarks and symmetric bounds.

A number does not resolve a refused reading

Camille withdraws consent before the duration ends. After a restart, no receipt is available. The tool proposes reading the photo again and writing: “90.00° exactly, good posture, because the calculation always gives the same result.” Explain which action must be refused and rewrite the result of the initial analysis, which was authorized, using the initial bounds.

Hint 1

Separate the proposed new reading from the historical report of the authorized analysis.

Hint 2

For the initial result, name the measurement plane and the interval assumptions.

Compare with the solution

The new reading must be refused: consent has been withdrawn and no valid receipt is available after the restart. The tool must not open the photo to manufacture its justification. Appropriate new explicit consent would be required before any new reading.

The initial report can be rephrased as follows in the exercise: “Authorized initial analysis: nominal projected angle 90°, compatible with [85° ; 95°] under direction bounds 2° and 3°.” This does not determine the right to retain or share a result: those operations require their own framework.

Decimal places and repeated calculations do not make the landmarks exact. The angle measured in the image is insufficient to conclude “good posture”. Nor does the interval bound the difference between the projected angle and the body angle in space.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What must be checked before opening Camille’s photo?

Verifiable explicit consent for the intended input, measurement, processing destination and duration; it must still be valid and not withdrawn. The availability of the file and an identifier supplied by the tool are insufficient.

Recall 2Why add the bounds on direction errors?

The angle error is a difference of signed errors. Rotations in opposite directions can add up in size: |eᵦ − eₐ| ≤ |eᵦ| + |eₐ| ≤ b + a. No probabilistic independence is required.

Recall 3What does the interval [85° ; 95°] mean here?

It is the range compatible with the nominal value 90° and the assumed direction bounds 2° and 3°. It is conditional on these assumptions and has no stated probability. Its width is 10°, and its half-width is 5°.

Recall 4What changes after withdrawal, expiration or loss of the receipt?

Another reading waits for appropriate new verifiable consent. Withdrawal blocks new readings in our protocol; by itself, it does not demonstrate that a previous result has been erased.

Recall 5Why is a stable angle in the image insufficient to draw a conclusion about posture?

The same calculation using the same landmarks can preserve their systematic error. Our bounds concern directions in the image; they validate neither perspective, the angle in space, nor a health interpretation.
