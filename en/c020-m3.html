Level 1 · C020·M3

Majority, abstention and threshold

Your goalDecide whether a proposal meets a stated rule by distinguishing votes in favour, abstentions, the reference population and equality at the threshold.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

At a community garden, the 12 members present vote on installing a rainwater collection tank. The count gives 5 in favour, 3 against and 4 abstentions. Supporters outnumber opponents. Do they have a majority? Before answering, we need to know among whom.

If you consider only those who chose for or against, you compare 5 people with 8. If you consider all the members, you compare the same 5 people with 12. The diagram changes its frame without changing a single ballot.

Here, an abstention is a recorded response that chooses neither side. It is neither a vote against nor a missing response. It belongs in the total number of members, but our convention excludes it from the expressed votes, meaning the choices for and against taken together.

The scene, the numbers of people and the garden rules are fictional. All the members are present; each submits exactly 1 valid ballot marked for, against or abstention, and every vote has the same weight. For this vote, the stated rule requires a majority strictly greater than half the expressed votes. We will then compare this rule with other explicitly named conventions.

A threshold is a boundary chosen for making a decision. The rule must specify the reference population and how equality is treated: “more than half” and “at least half” impose different conditions. The calculation applies a convention; it does not prove that the decision is good for the garden.

The rule to remember

State who counts in the denominator and whether equality is accepted; check that there are reference votes left; then calculate the share in favour and compare it with the exact threshold.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Counting the tank vote and reaching a verdict

The 12 ballots give P = 5 in favour, C = 3 against and A = 4 abstentions. The stated rule accepts the tank if the share in favour is strictly greater than 1/2 of the expressed votes. Let us find the denominator, calculate this share and check the verdict without rounding.

  1. Name the counts and the rule

    N is the expected number of ballots, equal to the number of members present. P counts votes in favour, C votes against and A abstentions. These counts use the ballot as their unit, with 1 ballot per person and non-overlapping categories. E counts expressed votes: E = P + C. Since all the responses are known, N = P + C + A.

    D denotes the number of ballots used as the reference by a given rule. The share in favour r = P/D has no unit: it compares ballots with ballots. It is defined only for D > 0. The threshold s also has no unit. For a strict rule, acceptance requires r > s; for “at least”, acceptance requires r ≥ s. The sign > excludes equality; ≥ includes it.

    r=PD pour D>0

    In the garden rule, D = E and s = 1/2. The majority is therefore strictly greater than half the choices for or against. Equal voting weights allow us to work with these counts; a weighted rule would require the weights and their reference sum to be specified.

  2. Find the reference population

    Add the choices included in the expressed votes: E = 5 + 3 = 8 ballots. The 4 abstentions remain in the count, but are excluded from this denominator.

    Check with another operation: N − A = 12 − 4 = 8 ballots. We recover E. Then E + A = 8 + 4 = 12 ballots: the expected total is recovered.

  3. Calculate the share in favour and decide

    r=58=0.625>12

    The exact fraction is 5/8; its decimal form 0.625 is also exact. Multiplying this share by 100 gives 62.5 %. The share in favour exceeds half: the proposal is accepted under the stated rule.

    Check without division: since E is positive, P/E > 1/2 is equivalent to 2 × P > E. Here 10 > 8. We can also divide E into halves: 8 ÷ 2 = 4 votes; strictly more are needed, so at least 5 votes in favour are required for this count.

  4. Change only the reference population and examine the effect

    If the convention required a strict majority of all the members, D would equal N = 12. The same numerator would give P/N = 5/12, or approximately 41.67 %. This share is below half: the proposal would not satisfy this other rule.

    Check in votes: N/2 = 6 and 5 is not strictly greater than 6. Abstentions have not been turned into votes against; they have simply remained in the reference population. The calculation does not allow us to choose a rule after seeing which verdict suits us.

  5. Change the threshold while keeping expressed votes as the reference

    Finally, compare a fictional rule stated as “at least 3/4 of expressed votes”, or 75 %. We keep D = E = 8, but set s = 3/4 and accept equality. We need P ≥ s × E.

    sE=34×8=6 voix

    With 5 votes in favour, the threshold of 6 votes is not reached; 1 additional vote in favour is needed if a vote against changes to a vote in favour, keeping E fixed. The threshold is a whole number in this example. When a boundary in votes is not a whole number, find the first whole number that satisfies the inequality, without rounding the share before deciding.

    Another exact check: for a positive denominator, P/E ≥ 3/4 is equivalent to 4 × P ≥ 3 × E. The products are 20 and 24; 20 is less than 24. This method confirms rejection at this threshold.

  6. Write a result that can be checked

    The complete report is: “5 in favour, 3 against, 4 abstentions; 8 expressed votes among 12 members. Strict majority of expressed votes: 5/8, or 62.5 %, so the proposal is accepted.” The count and the rule make the verdict verifiable.

    A rule can leave a proposal unaccepted without there being agreement on the opposite proposal. If E = 0, the share P/E does not exist: write “no expressed votes; no decision obtained under this rule”. Do not replace this impossible division with a percentage of agreement. The final exercise will ask you to examine this case in the same garden.

Explain it in your own words

Cover the calculations. Explain how the same ballots give approval by a majority of expressed votes and no approval by a majority of members. Then reconstruct the condition “at least 3/4 of expressed votes” and explain the role of equality.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “Abstentions must be votes against.”

    Keep three separate counts. An abstention strengthens neither side. It can nevertheless be included in the denominator if the rule covers all the members: its effect depends on the chosen reference population.

    Review the counting frames

  • “There are more votes for than against, so every majority requirement is met.”

    P > C is sufficient for a strict majority of expressed votes in our ballot with two choices. It is not sufficient for a majority of all the members or for the higher threshold. Write out the denominator and the boundary before deciding.

    Compare the rules using the same vote count

  • “Reaching the threshold and exceeding it are the same thing.”

    Replace the phrase with its sign: ≥ accepts equality, > rejects it. Test the case exactly equal to 3/4 in the boundary exercise, using an exact fraction rather than a rounded percentage.

    Test equality at the boundary

Exercise 1 · Application · change a ballot

An abstention becomes a vote against

Start from the initial vote. Someone who abstained now votes against: P remains 5, C becomes 4 and A becomes 3. Calculate the new number of expressed votes and their share in favour. Is the strict majority of expressed votes maintained? Check that the number of members stays the same.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Reconstruct E from the votes for and against; the new vote against enters this denominator.

Hint 2

To compare with half without rounding, compare 2 × P with E.

Compare with the solution

The expressed votes become E = 5 + 4 = 9 votes. The total is 9 + 3 = 12 ballots: nobody has been added to the group.

The share in favour is 5/9, below the initial share 5/8 because the number in favour stays fixed while the denominator increases. For the majority, 2 × 5 = 10 > 9: it is maintained.

The boundary in votes is 9/2 = 4.5 votes. Since the number of ballots is a whole number, 5 votes are enough to exceed it. A fractional boundary does not require us to invent half a vote in the ballot box.

Check your reasoning

  • You find 9 expressed votes while keeping 12 ballots in total.
  • You give the exact fraction 5/9 and justify the majority with an exact comparison.
  • You explain why the share falls even though the number in favour does not change.

Exercise 2 · Boundary · compare two wordings

Exactly at the threshold

Start from the initial vote again. This time, a vote against becomes a vote in favour: P = 6, C = 2 and A = 4. Compare the rules “at least 75 % of expressed votes” and “more than 75 % of expressed votes”. Do they give the same verdict?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

This change of side preserves the number of expressed votes.

Hint 2

Write P/E as a fraction, then check whether equality satisfies each wording.

Compare with the solution

E = 6 + 2 = 8 votes, as at the start. The share P/E = 6/8 = 3/4, or exactly 75 %.

The “at least” rule accepts: 3/4 ≥ 3/4 is true because the shares are equal. The “more than” rule does not accept: 3/4 > 3/4 is false. We therefore need to know how equality is treated before counting the ballots.

This result does not mean that either wording is a better rule. Replacing “at least” with “more than” changes the verdict at the boundary here.

Check your reasoning

  • You keep 8 expressed votes and obtain exactly 3/4.
  • You accept for ≥ and reject for >, explaining equality.
  • You do not replace the stated rule with a preference after the vote.

Exercise 3 · Critique · repair a report

Nobody chooses a side

In a new vote on the same tank, the 12 members abstain: P = 0, C = 0, A = 12. Someone writes “No votes against, so unanimous agreement; the tank is accepted by a strict majority of expressed votes”. Repair the report and explain what a separate calculation using all the members would allow.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Start with E = P + C. Then check whether P/E exists.

Hint 2

No votes against describes a count. Unanimous agreement would require votes in favour; do not infer one from the other.

Compare with the solution

E = 0 + 0 = 0. The division P/E is 0/0, which is undefined. No percentage of agreement among expressed votes can be reported.

Repaired report: “0 in favour, 0 against, 12 abstentions; no expressed votes. The rule based on expressed votes does not produce a decision to accept.” The absence of votes against is not unanimous support.

If a separate rule used all the members, the denominator would be 12, which is strictly positive. The share in favour would then be 0/12 = 0, so there would be no majority in favour. This calculation is defined, but it uses a different reference population; it does not repair the impossible division based on expressed votes.

Check your reasoning

  • You state that the denominator is 0 before any division and leave the share of expressed votes undefined.
  • You write a report without inventing agreement in favour.
  • You distinguish this case from the defined quotient based on the 12 members.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What information is missing from “the majority is in favour”?

The reference population: the expressed choices or all the members, for example. You must also specify whether the rule requires strictly more than its boundary.

Recall 2What do we do with abstentions in our garden vote?

We keep them in their own count A and in the total N. They are excluded from E = P + C under our convention for expressed votes. They become neither votes against nor missing responses.

Recall 3How can we check a strict majority of expressed votes without rounding?

With E > 0, compare P/E with 1/2, or check 2 × P > E. In our vote for or against, this is equivalent to P > C.

Recall 4When the share in favour is exactly equal to the threshold s, which rules accept?

r ≥ s accepts equality; r > s rejects it. Both shares have no unit and must use the stated reference population.

Recall 5What can we conclude if everyone abstains?

E = 0; P/E is undefined. Write that there are no expressed votes and no decision obtained under this rule. No votes against does not prove unanimous agreement.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without rereading, reconstruct the process: classify the ballots; state who counts in D; check D > 0; calculate P/D; compare with the threshold, respecting > or ≥; publish the rule with the counts. Then reconstruct why the tank vote satisfies the majority of expressed votes and fails under the other rules compared.
