Level 4 · C269·M1

Question and search

Your goalTurn a question about learning into an explicit literature search that can be repeated and is open to contradictory findings.

Before you start
c225-m5 : report and limitations · c224-m5 : limitations of documentary evidence · c145-m5 : policy evaluation · c261-m5 : model uncertainty · c009-m5 : reading notes

✿01 See the idea

You want to know whether spacing out review sessions helps people remember words. Typing “best way to learn” may mainly retrieve texts that promise improvement. You do not yet even know whether their readers are learning the same things, for the same amount of time, or whether their memory is tested immediately or later.

Imagine a folder on the table: its cover states a precise question, its dividers show where to search, and each record keeps the route by which it arrived. A record is a descriptive entry for a document: title, authors and reference. Finding its record does not mean you have read the document or checked its conclusion.

Our folder concerns adult students learning word lists. We are looking for a comparison between spaced sessions and a single massed session, with equal total study time and recall without aids after a fixed delay. This unit aims to prepare that search and then check the record count. We are not yet evaluating the studies or concluding that a method is effective.

The entire scenario is fictional: review arrangements, bibliographic indexes, exports and identifiers. The letters stand for invented records, never real articles. The parameters make the question precise; no memory score or experimental gain is provided.

The rule to remember

Decide what you want to compare before searching; also look for what might contradict you; keep the query and the origin of each record.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Open the folder on delayed recall

We are preparing a fictional search, without connecting to a real database. The deliverable will be a written question, a search strategy and a traceable export. The final count concerns distinct records, rather than selected studies.

  1. Write the question before seeing the results

    Target question: among adult students learning word lists, do 3 sessions of 10 min separated by 2 days, compared with a single session containing the same total study time, change the number of words correctly recalled without aids, out of 20 words tested, 7 days after learning ends?

    The population is the group the question concerns. The intervention is the spaced review schedule. The comparator is the massed schedule. The outcome is a number of words recalled; the measurement time is defined from the end of the last session in each group. “Change” leaves open a favorable difference, an unfavorable difference or no difference.

    To make this comparison interpretable, we require the same material, the same total duration and the same test instructions. These are conditions we are looking for in the documents, rather than properties already checked. A comparison that addresses the question does not automatically become causal evidence: its design and biases will need to be examined later.

  2. Check that the total duration is comparable

    Let n be the number of sessions, with no physical unit; t their identical duration, in minutes per session; and T the total duration, in minutes. We add the study time, excluding the waiting days. The assumption of sessions of equal duration allows the following product.

    T=n×t=3×10=30 min

    The comparator is therefore a session of 30 min. Check by addition: 10 + 10 + 10 = 30 min. If the durations differed, we would need to add the actual durations. This equality of time tells us nothing yet about how much is retained.

  3. Turn concepts into search terms

    A precise question contains more requirements than an initial query. We first group similar expressions: “spaced practice” OR “distributed review”. A second block describes the subject area: “memory” OR “recall”. OR accepts either expression in a block; AND requires the concepts in the connected blocks to be present.

    The conceptual query is therefore: (“spaced practice” OR “distributed review”) AND (“memory” OR “recall”). The parentheses keep the synonyms together. This English notation explains the logic; it does not claim to be an executable command in any particular catalog.

    Why not require every detail immediately? An abstract may omit the test duration or the comparison group. We keep these details in the question and will check them in the texts. We do not add “effective” or “better” as a requirement: these words would steer the search toward an expected conclusion.

    For real work, the terms would need to be adapted to the languages of the publications, the indexing vocabulary and the syntax of each source. An indexing vocabulary is a list of terms the database assigns to documents. The English words in this exercise do not constitute a complete international search.

  4. Prepare a record that allows the search to be repeated

    Indexes A and B are fictional sources that describe documents. Before the simulated search, the search log provides for: source name, interface used, date and time, exact query executed, fields searched, filters, number found and export file. A field is a part of the record, such as the title or abstract. A query on these fields is not a search of the full text.

    In our simulation, A and B search the title and abstract using the same conceptual blocks, with no filter on the direction of the outcome. We keep the complete lists below. No real consultation date or existing database name is invented: the log is a template to fill in during a real search.

    Also specify the languages to include, the period covered and how to obtain the texts. Give a written reason for every restriction; report any gap caused by an unavailable document. Repeating a search later may produce a different count because an index can change: the log helps explain the difference but cannot keep the database fixed.

  5. Calculate the number of distinct records

    Export A contains a, b, c, d, e, f: 6 records. Export B contains d, e, g, h, i: 5 records. Here we assume that each export already has no internal duplicates and that the same letter represents exactly the same document. The shared documents are d and e: 2 records present in both exports.

    Let N with subscript A or B be the number of records in the corresponding export, D the number of shared records, R the raw number of entries and U the number of distinct records. Subscripts A and B label the origins. All these counts are integers with no physical unit; always specify whether they count entries or distinct documents.

    R=NA+NB=6+5=11

    Each shared record has been counted in A and then in B. Subtract just one extra copy per shared record; removing both copies would lose the document.

    U=R−D=11−2=9

    The result is 9 distinct records. For d and e, the merged list keeps both A and B as origins. In practice, document identity is checked using references, identifiers and the text if necessary: similar titles are not enough. Different publications describing the same study are not necessarily duplicate records.

  6. Check without simply repeating the same subtraction

    Another route: start with the 6 records from A and add only g, h, i, which are absent from A. B contributes 5 − 2 = 3 new records. The total becomes 6 + 3 = 9 distinct records.

    Final list: a, b, c, d, e, f, g, h, i. It confirms 9 records. The raw count can be reconstructed by adding the extra copies: 9 + 2 = 11. These checks test the consistency of the simulated export; they measure neither scientific quality nor the completeness of the search.

  7. State what remains open before selection

    Here the folder ends with a list to examine. We do not yet know how many documents actually address the question, how many distinct studies they describe or what they show. The next unit will cover study selection.

    To test a real strategy, start with a relevant reference article whose existence and citation have been verified, then investigate why the query retrieves or misses it. A reference article is not evidence of an exhaustive search. Its cited references and works that cite it can open up a supplementary search, to be logged separately. No real reference article is provided in this exercise.

    Plan the stopping rule: search the agreed sources and carry out the agreed supplementary searches, log changes and declare gaps. Stopping as soon as a favorable text appears would follow a different rule, guided by the outcome. Without a real collection of documents, this page concludes with a working method, never a verdict on memory.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the example. Reconstruct the question, the search blocks and the record count. Why do the test conditions appear in the question without all becoming required words in the query? Also explain what the final number does not allow you to conclude.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I search for “effective spacing”: that is my question.”

    Write a question that also allows no difference or an unfavorable difference. Build the query using the concepts and their variants, then check the outcome in the documents. A favorable word is not a criterion of relevance.

    Return to the question and the search blocks

  • “I put every detail into the query to make sure I miss nothing.”

    Each extra requirement can exclude a text whose abstract omits that detail. Distinguish the conditions in the question, to be checked when reading, from the terms needed to retrieve the documents. Test the strategy on known references and document any broadening.

    Review the choice of terms and the limitations

  • “I have 9 records: therefore I have 9 independent pieces of evidence.”

    A record describes a document. Several documents may describe the same study; a document may be irrelevant or methodologically weak. Deduplication checks document identity, rather than the independence of observations or the validity of conclusions.

    Return to the folder and the records diagram

Exercise 1 · Framing · application

Repair an overly broad question

About the same folder, someone writes: “Does spaced review make people more intelligent?” Rewrite a question that the documents sought could actually inform. Calculate the duration of the massed comparator and state when to start counting the delay before the test.

Hint 1

Name the people, the material learned, the arrangements compared and what will be measured.

Hint 2

Use 3 sessions of 10 min and the delay of 7 days after the last session.

Compare with the solution

One possible answer: among adult students learning word lists, do 3 sessions of 10 min separated by 2 days, compared with a single session of 30 min, change the number of words recalled without aids, out of 20 words, 7 days after learning ends?

We obtain 30 min from 3 × 10. The delay starts after the last session in each group. The number of words recalled is a defined outcome; renaming it “intelligence” would extend the conclusion beyond the measurement. The question can be well formulated even if no available document answers it exactly.

Check your reasoning

  • The population, comparator, material and measurement are explicit.
  • The total duration is 30 min and the delay uses a common starting rule.
  • The wording allows no difference and does not turn recall into general intelligence.

Exercise 2 · Diagnosis · reasoning

A query that misses a record

In the same fictional index, record g contains “distributed review” and “memory”, but neither “spaced practice” nor “improvement”. The proposed query requires all these expressions connected by AND. Explain why g is missed, write the corrected blocks and specify what still needs to be checked before including the document.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

A vocabulary variant may be enough to name the same concept: must all synonyms be required?

Hint 2

Separate the vocabulary of practice, the vocabulary of memory and the favorable direction of the outcome.

Compare with the solution

With AND everywhere, the record must contain every expression. g fails as soon as “spaced practice” is missing. Alternative expressions are grouped using OR: (“spaced practice” OR “distributed review”) AND (“memory” OR “recall”). The term “improvement” is removed because the direction of the outcome does not determine relevance.

g satisfies these blocks through “distributed review” and “memory”. The notation still needs to be adapted to the interface, and the population, comparator, duration and delayed test must be checked in the document. The presence of the words guarantees neither the eligibility of the study nor its outcome. Identification and selection remain separate actions.

Check your reasoning

  • OR connects variants of a concept; AND connects blocks of concepts.
  • The favorable word is removed and the parentheses preserve the groupings.
  • The explanation distinguishes the retrieved record from the document still to be examined.

Exercise 3 · Transfer · folder audit

Add a source without inflating the folder

A supplementary search for the same folder produces export C: h, i, j, giving 3 entries. h and i are already in the list combining A and B. Calculate the new raw total and the distinct total. Keep the origins. Then respond to the announcement: “We now have enough records to prove effectiveness.”

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Count only the letters from C still absent from the final list.

Hint 2

Check the new distinct total by removing all extra copies from the raw total.

Compare with the solution

The raw total becomes 11 + 3 = 14 entries. C contains 2 already known records; it contributes 3 − 2 = 1 new record, j. The distinct list therefore contains 9 + 1 = 10 records: a, b, c, d, e, f, g, h, i, j.

Check: the extra copies are those of d, e, h, i, giving 2 + 2 = 4. This gives 14 − 4 = 10 distinct records. The difference between the two routes is 0. h and i keep B and C as origins; d and e keep A and B.

This count checks a collection of documents, with no associated effectiveness threshold. The relevant studies still need to be selected, their methods and results read, and their limitations examined. The new search must retain its source, query, reason and export; it does not allow the previous log to be silently replaced.

Check your reasoning

  • The raw total is 14 entries and the distinct total is 10 records.
  • j is the only record added; multiple origins are preserved.
  • The check using extra copies gives the same total.
  • The record count is presented neither as a number of independent studies nor as evidence of effectiveness.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What must the question specify before the search on review sessions?

The people concerned, the material learned, the arrangements compared, the study time, the outcome measured and the test timing. The wording must allow contradictory findings.

Recall 2Why use OR within a block and AND between blocks?

OR accepts alternative expressions for a concept; AND requires different concepts to occur together. Parentheses preserve the blocks. The actual syntax depends on the interface being searched.

Recall 3What should you keep to understand or repeat a literature search?

The source and its interface, the date, the query executed, the fields, the filters, the count found and the export. Also keep changes and their reasons; the database may change between searches.

Recall 4Why do 6 records and 5 records give only 9 distinct records here?

d and e appear in both exports. The 11 entries contain 2 extra copies: 11 − 2 = 9. Keep each document and all its origins.

Recall 5Why does a traceable list with duplicates properly removed still not answer the scientific question?

It shows what was retrieved and how. It guarantees neither that the studies are suitable, nor that they are independent, nor that their conclusions are sound, nor that the search is exhaustive.
