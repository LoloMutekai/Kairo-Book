Level 2 · C142·M5

Convergence criteria

Your goalChoose a convergence criterion for a real sequence, verify its assumptions and give an index that controls all subsequent terms.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

A model tank contains 2 L and has a capacity of 10 L. At each step, a program adds half the volume still available. It adds a lot at first, then less and less. Will it merely slow down, or approach a specific volume?

Look at the space that remains to be filled. Each addition removes half of it. The volume increases without exceeding the capacity; the shortfall can become as small as we require. These two observations concern the rule for every step, rather than just the appearance of the first few values.

Convergence means that beyond some index, all volumes remain within the required distance of the same limit. The index may change when we require a smaller distance. A display that changes very little on one occasion does not yet provide this guarantee.

The scene and all its values are fictional. We assume an exact rule, with no leaks, no rounding and no minimum dose. The index counts the steps; no physical duration is assigned to it. The diagrams represent this mathematical model.

The rule to remember

Choose the assumptions you can prove: monotonicity and a bound to establish existence, the squeeze theorem to identify the limit, or control of all pairs of sufficiently late terms using the Cauchy criterion.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Guarantee the volume, then the accuracy

The tank starts with 2 L and has a capacity C = 10 L. We follow the rule "add half the shortfall" exactly. Let us prove convergence, identify the limit and find the first index guaranteeing a distance strictly less than 0.25 L for all subsequent steps.

  1. Name the quantities and the required guarantee

    n is an integer greater than or equal to 0; un is the volume after n steps, in litres. C is the capacity, in litres. The shortfall en = C − un is also in litres. The factor 0.5 has no unit. The initial state and the rule are:

    u0=2 L;un+1=un+C−un2=un+C2

    A limit ℓ is a real volume in litres. The symbol ε denotes any strictly positive tolerance, in litres; N is an integer index, with no unit, chosen according to this tolerance. The definition requires the following order:

    ∀ε>0,∃N∈ℕ,∀n≥N:|un−ℓ|<ε

    ∀ means "for every"; ∃ means "there exists"; ℕ denotes the nonnegative integer indices; the bars give the absolute distance. We choose ε, then a single N that works for all subsequent n. Finding just one step with a volume close to the limit does not satisfy this definition.

  2. Work through the first steps and check the additions

    Taking the average of the current volume and the capacity avoids counting the addition twice. At the first step: (2 + 10)/2 = 12/2 = 6 L. At the next step: (6 + 10)/2 = 16/2 = 8 L. Then: (8 + 10)/2 = 18/2 = 9 L.

    Check by another method: the initial shortfall is 10 − 2 = 8 L. Dividing it successively by 2 gives 4 L, 2 L and 1 L. Subtracting these from the capacity gives 6 L, 8 L and 9 L respectively. These calculations check the first terms; a proof must still cover every index.

  3. Prove monotonicity and the bound together

    Initially, the volume is between 0 L and C. If a volume lies in this interval, its average with C remains between that volume and C. By induction, at every index:

    0≤un≤un+1≤C

    This verifies both that the sequence is nondecreasing and that it is bounded above by C. We can check the sign of its increase: the next volume minus the current volume equals en/2, so it is positive or 0. An increase observed over a few steps would have been insufficient.

  4. Apply the bounded monotone sequence criterion

    Every nondecreasing real sequence that is bounded above converges to a finite real limit. A nonincreasing sequence needs a lower bound. Here the assumptions have been proved: the existence of a limit is therefore established mathematically, even before we know its value.

    Why does this criterion work? Let s be the supremum of all the volumes, meaning their least upper bound, in litres. It exists by the completeness of the real numbers. For every ε > 0, s − ε can no longer be an upper bound: some volume exceeds it. All subsequent volumes also exceed it, by monotonicity, and remain less than or equal to s. Their distance from s is therefore strictly less than ε.

    The bound C alone does not yet allow us to assert s = C. An upper bound can be greater than the limit; we must use the precise rule to identify the limit.

  5. Identify the limit by controlling the shortfall

    Subtracting the recurrence from C shows that the shortfall is divided by 2. Repeating this equality from the initial shortfall gives:

    en+1=en2;0≤C−un=en=e02n

    For every ε > 0, we can choose N with 2ᴺ > initial e / ε. Indeed, 2ᴺ ≥ N + 1 by induction, and the integers are unbounded. The shortfall is then less than ε for all indices n ≥ N.

    This uses the squeeze theorem: a quantity between 0 and a bound that tends to 0 also tends to 0. The two bounds must have the same limit. Here en tends to 0 L, so the limit of the volumes is C = 10 L. The sequence can approach this value without ever reaching it at a finite index.

  6. Find and check the first index that meets the requirement

    The chosen tolerance is ε = 1/4 L, or 0.25 L. The shortfalls at indices 4, 5 and 6 are 0.5 L, 0.25 L and 0.125 L respectively. At index 5, the shortfall still equals the tolerance: this does not satisfy the strict inequality.

    n≥6⇒|un−C|=en≤e6=0.125 L<0.25 L

    N = 6 works for the entire subsequent sequence. It is minimal: all earlier shortfalls are at least as large as the shortfall at index 5, which fails the requirement. The volume at index 6 is 10 − 0.125 = 9.875 L. Check: 9.875 + 0.125 = 10 L. The addition checks the stated volume; the decreasing shortfall ensures the guarantee for future steps.

  7. Recognize the Cauchy criterion as well

    The Cauchy criterion compares sufficiently late terms with each other, without requiring the value of their limit. For every positive tolerance ε, it requires an index N such that, for all integer indices p and q greater than or equal to N, the distance between the volumes is less than ε. The symbols p and q have no unit.

    p,q≥N⇒|up−uq|≤C−uN=eN

    Why this bound? By monotonicity and the upper bound, both volumes belong to the interval from u at index N to C. Their distance is no greater than its width, e at index N. Choosing this width less than ε controls all pairs, rather than just two adjacent terms. In the real numbers, a sequence converges if and only if it is Cauchy; the implication from Cauchy to convergence uses completeness. Here this is another proof of existence, rather than an additional requirement to combine with the others.

  8. Distinguish a proof from stopping a calculation

    We have three ways to analyze the same rule: monotonicity and a bound give existence; bounding the shortfall gives the limit and the accuracy; the width of the interval containing all sufficiently late terms gives the Cauchy property. Choose the criterion suited to the available information, without reciting every theorem.

    The guarantee depends on following the rule exactly. A finite log or a test concerning only the last change does not, on its own, establish what will happen at every future index. In our exact model, the shortfall remains strictly positive at every finite index. A device may nevertheless display the capacity after rounding: the display does not change the mathematical limit or prove exact equality.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the calculation. Explain what proves existence, what identifies the limit, then why the chosen index works for all subsequent steps. Where would an error arise if you replaced "at all subsequent indices" with "at the next step"?

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The volume never exceeds the capacity, so it converges.”

    A bound prevents a sequence from leaving an interval; it does not prevent oscillation within it. For our first criterion, also verify that the sequence is nondecreasing at every index, using the averaging rule. The exercise on the finite log shows a bounded continuation that oscillates.

    Review the proof of monotonicity and the upper bound

  • “At index 5, the shortfall equals the tolerance: the strict criterion is satisfied.”

    The definition uses a distance strictly less than ε. The equality 0.25 L = 0.25 L fails the requirement. Index 6 gives 0.125 L, and the decreasing shortfall then guarantees all subsequent steps.

    Review the choice and minimality of the index

  • “Two adjacent volumes are close, so I have verified the Cauchy criterion.”

    The Cauchy criterion requires all pairs of indices beyond the same N, for every positive tolerance. In the model, the interval containing the entire subsequent sequence provides this control. A single comparison does not replace this uniform bound over the indices.

Exercise 1 · Direct application

Reconstruct a step by two methods

After 2 steps, the tank contains 8 L. Find the volume and the shortfall after the next step, first by averaging with C, then by dividing the shortfall. Explain what guarantees that the volume increases without exceeding the capacity.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The shortfall before the addition equals C minus the current volume.

Hint 2

After the addition, the shortfall has been divided by 2. Subtract it from C to recover the volume.

Compare with the solution

By averaging: (8 + 10)/2 = 18/2 = 9 L. By using the shortfall: 10 − 8 = 2 L, then 2/2 = 1 L; the new volume is 10 − 1 = 9 L.

The addition fills only half the positive shortfall. It therefore increases the volume while leaving a positive shortfall. This reasoning comes from the rule; it can be reused in an induction proof, whereas the values at this step alone would not prove the claim for all subsequent steps.

Check your reasoning

  • Obtain 9 L and 1 L by both methods.
  • Keep the units and distinguish the volume, the shortfall and the addition.
  • Justify the bounds using the rule, without generalizing solely from the numerical result.

Exercise 2 · Quantitative proof

Require greater accuracy

In the same model, now require a distance strictly less than 0.125 L from the limit. Find the first index that works and the corresponding volume. Then write a sentence using "for every n ≥ N"; explain why no earlier index works.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

At index 6, the shortfall is 0.125 L. Compare it with the new tolerance, respecting the strict inequality.

Hint 2

Divide the shortfall by 2 again, then use its decrease to control the entire subsequent sequence.

Compare with the solution

Index 6 fails because its shortfall equals the tolerance. The next shortfall is 0.125/2 = 0.0625 L, strictly less than 0.125 L. The first index is therefore N = 7. The corresponding volume is 10 − 0.0625 = 9.9375 L.

For every n ≥ 7, the distance between un and C is en ≤ 0.0625 L < 0.125 L. The shortfalls decrease: every index before 7 has a shortfall at least equal to 0.125 L and fails the requirement. Checking only index 7 would give a correct value, but would leave the required guarantee incomplete.

Check your reasoning

  • Reject equality at the threshold and obtain the first index 7.
  • Give 9.9375 L with a shortfall of 0.0625 L.
  • Quantify over all subsequent indices and justify minimality.

Exercise 3 · Critical transfer

What the log cannot guarantee

The log shows only the volumes at indices 0 to 6 in our example. There is no longer any guarantee about the program after this last index. Someone concludes: "These data prove that the future sequence converges." Construct a bounded continuation of the same log that refutes this conclusion. Explain why it does not refute our proof when the exact rule is maintained.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Keep the entire log unchanged, then imagine that the display alternates between two distinct volumes already encountered.

Hint 2

Choose 9.75 L and 9.875 L. Their distance remains 0.125 L, even at very late indices.

Compare with the solution

Keep the logged terms. Starting at index 7, alternate 9.75 L and 9.875 L indefinitely. The volumes remain between 0 L and 10 L: the continuation is bounded. Yet beyond any index N, we can find two volumes whose distance is always 9.875 − 9.75 = 0.125 L.

For the tolerance ε = 0.0625 L, this distance exceeds ε. No N therefore satisfies the Cauchy criterion; in the real numbers, this continuation does not converge. It suffices to show that a finite log alone does not guarantee convergence.

This continuation abandons the rule of filling half the shortfall and abandons monotonicity. It does not contradict the theorem applied to our exact model. The proof concerns a rule valid at every index; the log shows only a finite prefix. Similarly, a numerical stopping decision must be interpreted according to its test and the assumptions of its algorithm.

Check your reasoning

  • Keep every value in the log and extend it only after its last index.
  • Give a positive tolerance for which all pairs of sufficiently late terms cannot be controlled.
  • Explain which assumption of the proof the continuation abandons, without claiming that the bounded monotone sequence criterion fails.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1In what order should ε and N be chosen, and what must this index guarantee?

For every strictly positive tolerance ε, we must find an index N that guarantees a distance from the same limit strictly less than ε for all n ≥ N. N may depend on ε, but does not change with each term tested.

Recall 2Which assumptions establish the existence of a limit using the monotone sequence criterion, and what remains to be determined?

A nondecreasing real sequence that is bounded above converges; so does a nonincreasing sequence that is bounded below. These properties must be verified at every index. An arbitrary bound is not necessarily the limit: its value requires further justification.

Recall 3Which shortfall calculation identifies the limit of the tank volume and gives an accuracy guarantee?

The shortfall is divided by 2 at each step: eₙ = 8/2ⁿ in litres. It tends to 0 L, so uₙ tends to C = 10 L. Choosing N with e at index N < ε and using the decreasing shortfall guarantees all subsequent steps.

Recall 4Why is comparing two adjacent terms insufficient to verify the Cauchy criterion?

For every positive tolerance, the Cauchy criterion requires a single index N controlling all pairs of indices p and q beyond N. In the model, their distance is at most C − u at index N, the width of the interval containing all sufficiently late terms. The completeness of the real numbers lets us deduce the existence of a real limit.

Recall 5What does a finite log or a very small last change fail to demonstrate on its own?

It does not demonstrate the behavior of all future terms. A stopping criterion describes a decision made by the program; a convergence proof requires a rule and assumptions valid for the entire sequence. Reading the lesson and answering after a hint remain distinct from an independent reconstruction.
