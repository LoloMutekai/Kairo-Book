Unit circle

Your goalScale a disk to a unit radius, locate a sticker by its coordinates and check that it is on the circle.

Before you start
c005-m5 : areas and volumes · c006-m5 : composition

✿01 See the idea

A cardboard disk is fixed to a wall at its center. A sticker is attached to its edge. To describe its position to someone making a larger version of the same disk, giving distances in centimeters alone is not enough: the distances must increase with the radius.

Imagine a disk with a radius of 50 cm. From the center, the sticker is 30 cm to the right and 40 cm above. These dimensions are made up and chosen to allow exact calculations. We view the disk straight on, without perspective distortion.

Taking the radius as the new unit means asking: what fraction of the radius does each displacement represent? To the right, the answer will be 0.6 of a radius; vertically, 0.8 of a radius. In this new coordinate system, the edge is the unit circle, and its radius is 1 with no physical unit.

A circle means only the edge. The disk also includes the interior. The sticker can move to another position on the edge: it stays the same distance from the center, even though its coordinates change.

The rule to remember

Place the origin at the center, keep the same scale on both axes, then divide each coordinate by the radius. The unit circle contains exactly the points at a distance of 1 from the center.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Setting up coordinates for the sticker

The center O is the origin. The sticker P is given by X = 30 cm and Y = 40 cm, for a radius R = 50 cm. Let us calculate its position on the unit circle, then check that it is on the edge.

  1. Set the coordinate system and symbols

    X is the horizontal coordinate: the signed horizontal displacement from O. Y is the vertical coordinate: the signed vertical displacement. To the right and upward, the values are positive; to the left and downward, they are negative. The pair (X ; Y) is always read as horizontal, then vertical. The coordinates of O are (0 ; 0).

    The axes are perpendicular and use the same unit of length, the centimeter. R is a strictly positive length. We work in the plane with the usual distance. OP denotes the length of the segment joining the center O to the sticker P.

  2. First check the physical disk

    The triangle in the diagram is right-angled. The Pythagorean theorem says that the square of its sloping side is the sum of the squares of its perpendicular sides. Here, this gives the distance from the center:

    OP2=X2+Y2

    Let us calculate: 30 × 30 = 900 cm²; 40 × 40 = 1600 cm². The sum is 2500 cm². R squared is 50 × 50 = 2500 cm². The squares are equal; since the lengths are positive, OP = R = 50 cm. The sticker is therefore on the edge, rather than simply inside the disk.

  3. Take the radius as the unit

    Let u and v denote the new coordinates. We divide the displacements by R to find how many radii they represent. The centimeters cancel: u and v have no physical unit.

    u=XR;v=YR

    Horizontally: 30 cm ÷ 50 cm = 3/5 = 0.6. Vertically: 40 cm ÷ 50 cm = 4/5 = 0.8. The normalized sticker position therefore has coordinates (0.6 ; 0.8). The normalized radius is R/R = 1. Here, “normalized” means that we have taken the radius as the unit.

  4. Derive the unit circle equation

    For a point on the edge, X squared plus Y squared equals R squared. Dividing this equality by R squared gives the following rule; the exponent 2 means multiplying the quantity by itself:

    u2+v2=1

    Let us check with the exact fractions: (3/5) × (3/5) = 9/25; (4/5) × (4/5) = 16/25. Their sum is 1. The calculation in centimeters and the one with no unit confirm the same edge.

    Conversely, in this coordinate system, if the sum of the squares is 1, the distance from the center is 1: the point belongs to the unit circle. A sum less than 1 places the point inside; a sum greater than 1 places it outside. The center gives 0, so it belongs to the disk, but not to the circle.

  5. Relate a position to a rotation

    On the unit circle, choose A = (1 ; 0), on the right edge, as the starting point. The directed angle describes the rotation of the radius from OA. The positive direction is counterclockwise. The negative direction is clockwise. This choice of starting point and direction is part of the coordinate system.

    A positive quarter turn, or 1/4 of a turn, leads to the top (0 ; 1). A half turn, or 1/2 of a turn, leads to the left (-1 ; 0). At 3/4 of a positive turn, the point is at the bottom (0 ; -1). After 1 turn, we return to A: a single position therefore does not tell us how many turns have already been made.

    The radian is another unit for measuring this rotation. Let θ, pronounced “theta”, denote the angle in radians, and s the signed arc length traveled from A along the physical edge: s is positive in the positive direction and negative in the other direction. With s and R in the same unit of length, θ = s/R. An arc whose length equals the radius therefore corresponds to 1 radian, abbreviated rad. Here we follow the curved edge; the straight segment between the start and end does not give s.

    θ=sR

    We have not measured the arc leading to P, so we do not assign it a numerical angle. In this unit, the coordinates are used to position and check the sticker. The next unit will define the functions that relate the angle to these coordinates.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I divide by the diameter: that is also the size of the circle.”

    The diameter is 2 radii, or 100 cm here. Dividing by it gives (0.3 ; 0.4), whose sum of squares is 1/4, instead of 1. Divide by R again to obtain a unit radius.

    Review the choice of unit and the check for the edge

  • “The sum of the squares is correct, so the signs do not matter.”

    Squaring removes the sign. (0.6 ; 0.8) and (-0.6 ; 0.8) are on the same circle, but one is on the right and the other on the left. Check the distance and the side of the center separately.

    Review the reflected sticker

  • “For the angle in radians, I measure the segment between the positions.”

    s denotes the curved path along the edge, with its direction. The direct segment between the positions cuts through the disk and measures something else. Review the definition θ = s/R before making any conversion.

    Review rotation and arc length

Exercise 1 · Locating points

From the right edge to the top

Place the sticker at A on the same disk, then make 1/4 of a turn in the positive direction. Give its physical coordinates in centimeters and its coordinates on the unit circle. Explain why the horizontal coordinate becomes 0.

Hint 1

From the right edge, the positive direction goes upward. At the top, are you to the right or to the left of the center?

Hint 2

The vertical displacement equals the radius; then divide each coordinate by this radius.

Compare with the solution

The sticker ends up directly above O. Its horizontal displacement is 0: X = 0 cm. Its height above the center equals the radius: Y = 50 cm.

After dividing by R: u = 0 and v = 1, with no physical unit. The check gives 0 × 0 + 1 × 1 = 1. The top is therefore on the unit circle.

Check your reasoning

  • Locate the top using the positive direction from A.
  • Give (0 cm ; 50 cm), then (0 ; 1).
  • Justify the 0 horizontal coordinate by the vertical alignment with O.

Exercise 2 · Calculation and justification

The reflected sticker

Start again from P. Move the sticker to the position reflected across the vertical axis: it moves to the left of the center, keeping the same height. Give the normalized coordinates of this new position and check that it is on the edge. Does the check distinguish the positions?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Reflection across the vertical axis changes only the sign of the horizontal displacement.

Hint 2

Multiply each coordinate by itself. What effect does the negative sign have on its square?

Compare with the solution

The new horizontal coordinate is 0 − 3/5 = -3/5 = -0.6. The vertical coordinate remains 4/5 = 0.8. The pair is therefore (-0.6 ; 0.8).

The square of the horizontal coordinate is 9/25 and that of the vertical coordinate is 16/25. The sum is 1: the sticker remains on the edge.

The circle check does not distinguish these positions: the squares are the same. The sign of the horizontal coordinate distinguishes left from right. The signed coordinates must therefore be kept in the answer.

Check your reasoning

  • Change only the sign of the horizontal coordinate.
  • Show the exact calculation of the sum of the squares.
  • Explain why belonging to the same circle does not mean occupying the same position.

Exercise 3 · Transfer and repair

A larger disk, an incomplete copy

A disk is made with a radius of 100 cm, or 2 times the original radius. Someone keeps X = 30 cm and Y = 40 cm and claims that the sticker is still on the edge. Check this claim, then give the physical coordinates that would preserve the relative position of P on the new edge.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The new unit is the new radius. Recalculate the normalized coordinates before checking their sum of squares.

Hint 2

For a copy with consistent scaling, the radius and the displacements must change by the same factor.

Compare with the solution

Keeping the old displacements gives u = 30/100 = 3/10 = 0.3 and v = 40/100 = 2/5 = 0.4. The squares are 9/100 and 4/25; their sum is 1/4, less than 1. The sticker is inside the new disk.

To preserve its relative position, multiply each displacement by 2: X becomes 60 cm and Y becomes 80 cm. With the new radius, 60/100 = 3/5 = 0.6 and 80/100 = 4/5 = 0.8.

We recover exactly the original coordinates on the unit circle. Their sum of squares is still 1. Another check: the old displacements gave a distance from the center of 50 cm; this was too small for the new radius of 100 cm. Doubling all the displacements doubles this distance.

Check your reasoning

  • Use the new radius as the denominator.
  • Conclude “inside” from 1/4 < 1.
  • Give (60 cm ; 80 cm) and check the return to the original coordinates.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What defines the unit circle, and does the center belong to it?

It is the set of points at a distance of 1 from O in a coordinate system with perpendicular axes and the same scale on both axes. The center is at a distance of 0: it belongs to the disk, but not to the circle.

Recall 2How do you convert coordinates in centimeters to coordinates on the unit circle?

With the origin at the center, divide each coordinate by the radius R, expressed in the same unit: u = X/R and v = Y/R. The units of length cancel. The order remains horizontal, then vertical.

Recall 3What check confirms that a point is on the edge, and why is it not enough to identify a position?

Check that u squared plus v squared equals 1. This check concerns the distance from the center. Squaring removes the signs: several positions satisfy the same equality.

Recall 4Where does the positive angle start, in which direction does it turn, and where does a quarter turn lead?

It starts from the radius toward A = (1 ; 0), on the right, and turns counterclockwise. A positive quarter turn leads to (0 ; 1). A full turn returns to the same point without erasing the path traveled.

Recall 5What does θ = s/R measure, and what must you measure for s?

θ is the angle in radians. s is the signed arc length along the edge from the chosen starting point, and R is the positive radius, in the same unit of length. You must measure the curved edge, rather than the direct segment between the positions.

What you understand
A position on a circular edge requires a center, a radius and consistent coordinates. Taking the radius as the unit makes it possible to keep the same relative position when the size changes.
