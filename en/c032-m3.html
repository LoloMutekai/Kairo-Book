Level 1 · C032·M3

Inverse

Your goalBuild the matrix that recovers the lengths of ribbon pieces from their assemblies, then check that it undoes the original calculation.

Before you start
c004-m5 : rational equations · c027-m5 : complex numbers · c032-m1 : elimination · c032-m2 : matrix multiplication

✿01 See the idea

In a workshop, all the pink ribbon pieces have the same length, and all the lavender pieces have the same length too. The Long assembly joins 2 pink pieces and 1 lavender piece end to end. The Short assembly joins 1 piece of each colour end to end. We know their total lengths: 7 m for Long and 4 m for Short. How can you recover the length of a piece of each colour?

Imagine removing from the Long assembly all the pieces that make up the Short assembly. Exactly one pink piece remains. The difference in their lengths therefore gives the length of a pink piece. Once you know this, you can use the Short assembly to recover the length of a lavender piece.

Let x be the length of a pink piece and y the length of a lavender piece; p and q denote the total lengths of Long and Short. All these lengths are in metres. The forward calculation assembles the pieces; the inverse calculation works back from the totals to the piece lengths. The same rule for working back must work for other totals if the compositions stay the same.

The scene and its values are fictional. We assume perfectly identical pieces within each colour, exact lengths, and no loss, stretching or overlap at the joins. Before reading the results in the diagram, predict which subtraction isolates the pink piece.

The rule to remember

An inverse matrix undoes an entire transformation. Build the rule for working back, check its products with the original matrix, then apply it to the data.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Build the rule for working back before measuring the pieces

The Long assembly measures p = 7 m and Short measures q = 4 m. Let us recover x and y without changing the compositions. We will first build a rule that works for any pair of totals, then use it on these exact data.

  1. Write the forward calculation and its dimensions

    u is the column of unknown lengths, ordered pink then lavender. b is the column of totals, ordered Long then Short. A is the matrix of compositions: one row per assembly and one column per colour. Its coefficients count the pieces and have no units; each component of u and b is in metres.

    A=[2111],u=[xy],b=[pq]
    Au=b⇔{2x+y=px+y=q

    A has 2 rows and 2 columns; u and b have 2 rows and 1 column. Multiplying each row by each column gives the workshop compositions.

  2. State exactly what inverse means

    The identity matrix I leaves any column of the same size unchanged: it multiplies each component by 1 and adds 0 times the other component. For our size, it is written as follows:

    I=[1001],Iu=u

    A square matrix A is called invertible if there is a matrix B of the same size such that BA = AB = I. B is called the inverse of A. Letters written next to each other here denote matrix products, in the order shown.

    B=A−1

    The exponent indicates the inverse operation; it does not ask you to take the reciprocal of each entry. In our scene, A and B have no units, since the inputs and outputs are all in metres.

  3. Eliminate a length without fixing the totals

    Subtracting the equation for Short from the equation for Long eliminates y. We keep p and q as letters to obtain a reusable rule rather than an answer specific to the current measurement.

    p−q=(2x+y)−(x+y)=x

    Then isolate y in the equation for Short and substitute the expression we have just found for x:

    y=q−x=q−(p−q)=2q−p

    Each addition or subtraction involves lengths in metres. The compositions fix the coefficients of this rule, independently of the totals chosen.

  4. Read off the coefficients of the matrix for working back

    The first row must form x = p − q: its coefficients of p then q are 1 and -1. The second must form y = −p + 2q: its coefficients are -1 and 2. This gives a candidate matrix B.

    B=[1-1-12],u=Bb

    A negative coefficient expresses a subtraction of total lengths. It does not represent a piece with a negative length. B must now be checked as a matrix, beyond a single pair of data values.

  5. Check the compositions in both directions

    Each entry of a product is the sum of the products of a row with a column. For example, the bottom left entry of BA is (-1) × 2 + 2 × 1 = -2 + 2 = 0. Calculating all the entries gives:

    BA=[2−11−1-2+2-1+2]=[1001]=I
    AB=[2−1-2+21−1-1+2]=I

    BA undoes the assembly of a column of lengths; AB reconstructs a column of totals after working back. Both products are the identity here. We calculated them separately: matrices cannot in general be multiplied in either order with the same result. B is therefore the inverse of A.

  6. Apply the inverse to the measured lengths

    Finally, substitute 7 m for p and 4 m for q. The top row of B subtracts the totals: x = 7 − 4 = 3 m. The bottom row doubles Short, giving 2 × 4 = 8 m, then subtracts Long: y = 8 − 7 = 1 m.

    B[74]=[31] m

    The pink piece measures 3 m and the lavender piece measures 1 m. The rows must stay in the stated order: swapping the totals or the colours would change the problem.

  7. Check the answer and its scope

    Let us reconstruct the assemblies with these pieces. Long: 2 × 3 + 1 = 6 + 1 = 7 m. Short: 3 + 1 = 4 m. We recover the data in the correct order; the piece lengths are positive.

    Why can there be no other solution? Any column u satisfying Au = b also satisfies B(Au) = Bb. Since BA = I and matrix products can be regrouped using parentheses, this equality forces u = Bb. Working back therefore leaves no choice between several solution columns.

    The algebraic rule works for any pair of real totals. Some pairs may, however, produce a negative length and be incompatible with physical pieces. Invertibility and whether an order can be made are separate questions. With rounded measurements, an exact check like this would not be enough to assess measurement errors.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the matrices. Recover the subtractions from the assemblies, reconstruct B, then explain the difference between checking these lengths and checking an inverse. Why do we not start by dividing each entry of A?

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The inverse is obtained by replacing each coefficient with its reciprocal.”

    This recipe would give a matrix C with rows (1/2, 1) and (1, 1), with 1/2 = 0.5 and no units. The bottom left entry of CA would be 1 × 2 + 1 × 1 = 3, instead of 0 in the identity. Return to the rule that undoes the assembly, then check the matrix product.

    Revisit the construction and checking of B

  • “It recovers the correct pieces once, so it must be the inverse.”

    One trial checks only one column of data. To identify an inverse, check the entire transformation using BA = AB = I. The missing column exercise practises working with the identity matrix, independently of the current order.

    Complete a column by aiming for the identity

  • “A square matrix always has an inverse.”

    If Long contains exactly two copies of Short, its total no longer lets you separate the colours. Several lengths can give the same totals. A unique rule for working back is then impossible; the final exercise builds this contradiction without any new tools.

Exercise 1 · Application

Reuse the same rule

The original compositions are kept. Long now measures 11 m and Short 6 m. Recover the pink and lavender lengths using B, then check the assemblies separately.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

B stays the same: its first row calculates p − q.

Hint 2

For lavender, double the Short total before subtracting Long.

Compare with the solution

The pink length is 11 − 6 = 5 m. For lavender, 2 × 6 = 12 m, then 12 − 11 = 1 m.

Check Long: 2 × 5 + 1 = 10 + 1 = 11 m. Check Short: 5 + 1 = 6 m. Changing the data required no change to the inverse.

Check your reasoning

  • Find 5 m of pink and 1 m of lavender, justifying the operations.
  • Recover both totals in the correct order and with the unit metre.

Exercise 2 · Construction

Complete the matrix rather than an order

The first column of B is known: (1, -1). Its second column, denoted (r, s), has been erased. r and s are real coefficients with no units. Recover them by requiring the second column of AB to be the second column of I. Check the first column too. These columns are objects for calculation, rather than lengths to make.

A[rs]=[01]
Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The product gives the equations 2r + s = 0 and r + s = 1.

Hint 2

Subtract the second equation from the first, then substitute r into the second.

Compare with the solution

The difference gives r = 0 − 1 = -1. Then r + s = 1 requires s = 1 − (-1) = 2. The missing column is therefore (-1, 2).

Check this column: at the top, 2 × (-1) + 2 = -2 + 2 = 0; at the bottom, -1 + 2 = 1. This is indeed the second column of I.

For the first column, the product gives 2 × 1 + (-1) = 1 at the top, and 1 + (-1) = 0 at the bottom. Together, the columns verify AB = I. The lesson also checked BA = I; none of these checks uses the order totals.

Check your reasoning

  • Write the system in r and s, aiming for the column (0, 1).
  • Obtain r = -1 and s = 2 by elimination, then check both columns of the product.
  • Explain why a negative coefficient in B is not a negative length to make.

Exercise 3 · Transfer

When working back becomes impossible

In the same workshop, only Long is changed: it now contains 2 pink pieces and 2 lavender pieces. Short still contains 1 piece of each colour. The stated totals are 8 m and 4 m. Check that the lengths (3 m, 1 m) and (2 m, 2 m) give these same totals. Can the new matrix have an inverse? Justify your answer without using a formula taught later.

D=[2211]
Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

D denotes the new matrix of compositions. Its Long total is always 2 times its Short total.

Hint 2

An inverse applied to the same column of totals would have to recover each of the two columns of lengths: can it return two different results?

Compare with the solution

With (3 m, 1 m), Long is 2 × 3 + 2 × 1 = 6 + 2 = 8 m and Short is 4 m. With (2 m, 2 m), Long is 4 + 4 = 8 m and Short is 2 + 2 = 4 m. The totals are identical and the lengths are different.

If an inverse of D existed, it would have to take the same column (8, 4) back to these two distinct columns. That is impossible for a calculation rule that assigns a single result to each input. D is therefore not invertible, even though it is square and this order has solutions.

Here, Long provides only twice the information already given by Short. To recover the colours separately, we would need another composition that distinguishes them. Adding a measurement of the same composition would not be enough to define a unique exact rule for working back.

Check your reasoning

  • Check both pairs in both modified assemblies.
  • Relate non-invertibility to two distinct inputs having the same output.
  • Distinguish the existence of solutions for an order from the existence of an inverse of the matrix.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What role does I play in the definition of an inverse?

I leaves any column unchanged. For our size, its diagonal coefficients are 1 and the others are 0. An inverse B of A satisfies BA = AB = I.

Recall 2What does the exponent minus 1 on a matrix mean?

It denotes a matrix that undoes multiplication by the original matrix. It does not mean replacing each entry with its reciprocal; the matrix products must be checked.

Recall 3Without rereading B, how can you recover its rows from the ribbons?

Long minus Short isolates the pink length: x = p − q. Then y = q − x = 2q − p. The rows of B are therefore (1, -1) and (-1, 2), with the totals ordered p then q.

Recall 4Why does checking a pair of lengths not prove that a matrix is the inverse?

Substitution checks this order. The identities BA = AB = I check the rule for all columns, and show that any solution of Au = b is forced to be u = Bb.

Recall 5How can you show that inversion is impossible without a new formula?

Give two distinct columns that produce the same totals. A unique rule for working back could not recover both. This happens when Long is exactly double Short.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without looking at the matrices, reconstruct the sequence: compositions → totals → elimination → coefficients for working back → products equal to the identity → lengths → forward check. Then explain why two different lengths giving the same totals rule out an inverse. Repeating an answer after a hint is still an assisted answer, rather than an achieved level.

What you understand
The inverse undoes a linear transformation when a unique way back is possible. You can construct one for the ribbons and distinguish checking an order from checking a matrix.
