Level 3 · C200·M3

Engle-Granger

Your goalReconstruct the two steps of Engle-Granger, calculate a unit root statistic on estimated residuals, and explain why its decision requires a specific calibration.

Before you start
c101-m5 : rolling-origin forecasting · c145-m5 : policy evaluation · c172-m5 : Euler-Maruyama · c200-m1 : ADF and KPSS · c200-m2 : cointegration

✿01 See the idea

Two stations record the water level in the same canal each day at different locations. Their series may drift together. The useful question is then: after accounting for their difference in scale and their offset, is there a remaining gap whose dispersion stabilizes? A resemblance between the curves does not answer this question.

Engle-Granger follows this idea: fit a relationship between the levels, then look for a unit root in the residuals of that relationship. The residual is what the fitted relationship leaves unexplained at each date. A unit root means here that this gap can accumulate disturbances persistently, instead of remaining stationary.

Prerequisite review: I(1) denotes a nonstationary series whose difference between successive dates is stationary. I(0) denotes a stationary series; in the weak sense used here, its mean and variance are constant and its covariance depends on the lag between dates. Two I(1) series are cointegrated if a nontrivial linear combination of their levels is I(0).

The entire scenario and all readings are fictional. The 6 selected dates allow us to check the algebra, without establishing the integration order of the station series or providing a hydrological study. In an application, the compatibility of the series with I(1) must be examined using a suitable time series, as in the ADF and KPSS unit.

The rule to remember

Fit the levels; test the estimated residuals; use a cointegration calibration consistent with that fit.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

From readings to residuals, then to the statistic

At successive dates a through f, X = (10, 12, 11, 14, 13, 15) cm and Y = (24, 29, 23, 30, 28, 34) cm. The stations have a fixed elevation datum. We regress Y on X with a constant and no time trend, then perform the residual calculation without adding any lagged differences. This specification is for teaching and does not provide a diagnosis of the station series.

  1. Set the relationship and assumptions

    t denotes a date; n = 6 is the number of aligned pairs. xt and yt are the levels, in cm. The candidate relationship is yt = α + βxt + ut: α is an offset in cm, β is a dimensionless scale ratio, and ut is the theoretical gap in cm. A hat indicates an estimated coefficient; êt is the calculated residual, distinct from the unknown theoretical gap.

    e^t=yt−α^−β^xt

    Interpreting the test requires series compatible with I(1), a stable relationship, and an appropriate specification of deterministic terms and lags. A change in the datum or in the relationship requires this framework to be reconsidered. We fix the orientation of Y on X before calculating: this normalization fixes the coefficient of y in the gap at 1. Reversing this choice can give different results in a finite sample.

  2. Estimate the relationship between the levels

    Least squares chooses the coefficients that minimize the sum of squared residuals. A bar indicates a mean over the n dates. The sums below with no stated limits run over these dates. The auxiliary sums, which can be recalculated from the readings, are Σxt = 75 cm and Σyt = 168 cm. The sum of squared xt values is 955 cm² and the sum of the products xtyt is 2135 cm².

    Thus x̄ = 75/6 = 12.5 cm and ȳ = 168/6 = 28 cm. Centering removes the contribution of the mean level, since the line has a constant.

    Sxx=Σxt2−(Σxt)2n=955−937.5=17.5
    Sxy=Σxtyt−(Σxt)(Σyt)n=2135−2100=35

    Sxx and Sxy denote these centered sums, in cm². Sxx is strictly positive: X varies. The slope is β̂ = Sxy/Sxx = 35/17.5 = 2, with no units. The constant is α̂ = ȳ − β̂x̄ = 28 − 25 = 3 cm. Fitting the levels preserves the very long-run relationship we want to examine; do not replace X and Y with their differences here.

  3. Construct the series to be tested

    For each date, first calculate ŷt = α̂ + β̂xt, the fitted level, then êt = yt − ŷt. For example, at a: 3 + 2 × 10 = 23 cm, then 24 − 23 = 1 cm. The other dates follow the same operation. Each row of the table gives the date, the fitted level, and the residual, both in cm.

    The constant makes the residuals sum to 0, up to rounding. This is a property of the fit, rather than a test result: a residual sequence with 0 sample mean can still have a unit root.

  4. Set up the residual test while keeping the lag aligned

    Δêt = êt − êt-1 is the change in the residual between two dates, in cm. The subscript t − 1 denotes the previous date, rather than the next reading. For the case p = 0, where p counts the added lagged differences, the auxiliary regression is as follows. γ is dimensionless; εt is its error, in cm.

    Δe^t=γe^t−1+εt

    We add neither a constant nor a trend to this residual regression; the initial relationship already contained its constant. This is the unaugmented case of the residual ADF test. In an application, p must allow for temporal correlation in the errors: if necessary, add past differences of the residual and recalculate the test. The case p = 0 is not justified by this small table.

    There are m = n − 1 = 5 transitions left. The table aligns the arrival date, the previous residual, and the change; its values are in cm.

  5. Measure the estimated restoring coefficient

    In this step and the following ones, the sums run only over the m transitions. A adds the squares of the previous-residual column; C adds the products of the two columns, row by row; D adds the squared changes. All three are in cm².

    A=(1)2+(2)2+(-2)2+(-1)2+(-1)2=11 cm²

    The sum is C = 1 × 1 + 2 × (-4) + (-2) × 1 + (-1) × 0 + (-1) × 2 = -11 cm².

    D=(1)2+(-4)2+(1)2+(0)2+(2)2=22 cm²

    With no constant in the auxiliary regression, the slope is γ̂ = C/A = -11/11 = -1. Rewriting the same equation in levels gives the estimated autoregressive coefficient ρ̂ = 1 + γ̂ = 0. The unit root null hypothesis corresponds to γ = 0, hence ρ = 1. A negative slope γ̂ alone is not enough to reject this hypothesis.

  6. Calculate the standard error and the statistic

    Let SCR denote the sum of squared errors of the auxiliary regression, in cm². Expanding the squared differences Δêt − γ̂êt-1, then replacing γ̂ with C/A, gives SCR = D − γ̂C. Thus SCR = 22 − (-1) × (-11) = 11 cm².

    The auxiliary regression has m = 5 rows and 1 estimated coefficient: its degrees of freedom are d = m − 1 = 4. We calculate v = SCR/d = 11/4 cm², or 2.75 cm². Here v is the usual variance estimator for this regression, with no guarantee of unbiasedness for the entire procedure using estimated residuals.

    se(γ^)=vA=1/4=0.50

    se(γ̂) denotes the calculated standard error of γ̂, with no units. Its value is positive and satisfies 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4. The statistic τ compares the coefficient with its null value of 0 under the unit root hypothesis:

    τ=γ^−0se(γ^)=-10.50=-2

    τ is dimensionless. Its form resembles that of a Student's t-test, but its distribution under the null hypothesis is not Student's t-distribution. The degrees of freedom used for v do not turn this statistic into a Student's t-test.

  7. Check by another route

    First check: the residuals sum to 0 cm and Σxtêt = 0 cm². These two equalities are the least squares optimality conditions with a constant. Together, they check the slope and the offset.

    Second check: directly recalculate ε̂t = Δêt − γ̂êt-1. Since γ̂ = -1, the sequence obtained from b through f is (2, -2, -1, -1, 1) cm. Its sum of squares is 11 cm², as is D − γ̂C. The dot product of the previous residuals and the auxiliary errors is also 0 cm²: the auxiliary slope satisfies its own optimality condition.

    Finally, converting all heights to a smaller common unit multiplies the residuals and their auxiliary errors by the same factor. A, C, and SCR scale by the square of that factor; γ̂, se(γ̂), and τ remain unchanged. A conclusion that depends on the unit of length therefore contains a calculation error.

  8. Separate the statistic from the decision

    For Engle-Granger, the null hypothesis is no cointegration: the gap retains a unit root. The alternative is a stationary gap around the chosen relationship. The test is left-tailed: reject if τ is smaller than the appropriate critical value. Smaller here means more negative.

    Let q denote the threshold for Type I error risk chosen in advance: the target probability of rejecting the null hypothesis when it is true. c(q) is the corresponding critical value. An Engle-Granger calibration for estimated residuals is required, consistent with the number of series in levels, the deterministic terms, and the sample size. The ordinary ADF table for a directly observed series is unsuitable. Estimating the relationship first changes the distribution of the statistic tested afterwards.

    Our complete calculation result is τ = -2. No critical value or calibrated p-value is provided here, so we make no rejection decision. Furthermore, the 6 dates do not make a conclusion based solely on an asymptotic approximation credible, meaning an approximation valid as the sample becomes large.

    In a suitable study, rejection supports cointegration under the chosen assumptions; failure to reject indicates insufficient evidence, rather than demonstrated absence. Neither establishes causality between stations. Johansen's multivariate method belongs to the next unit.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The curves move together, so they are cointegrated.”

    An apparent common drift can occur alongside a spurious regression. First check the I(1) framework, then examine the unit root of the estimated residuals. Visually low dispersion over our fictional window does not replace this test.

    Review the procedure and the status of the readings

  • “The restoring slope is negative: the test has succeeded.”

    The sign of γ̂ alone does not measure uncertainty. Reconstruct se(γ̂), then τ = -2, and require a cointegration calibration. A negative estimate can occur under the null hypothesis.

    Recalculate the standard error and read the decision rule

  • “I can compare this statistic with the usual ADF table.”

    The tested residual has already been fitted using the same readings: it is not a raw series. Use an Engle-Granger calibration corresponding to the constant in the relationship and its number of series. Without an appropriate table, the decision remains unevaluated.

    Review the distinction between the statistic and the decision

Repair a conclusion about the stations

Someone writes: “The residuals sum to 0 and τ is -2. I compare it with Student's t-table, then announce that the stations are cointegrated and that X causes Y.” Rewrite this conclusion, separating the calculated facts, the missing decision, and causality.

Compare with the solution

The calculated facts are a 0 sum of residuals and τ = -2 for the chosen specification. The 0 sum checks the fit; it is not evidence of stationarity.

An Engle-Granger calibration on estimated residuals is needed, consistent with the deterministic terms, the number of series, and the sample; neither Student's t-table nor an ordinary ADF table can replace it. Without it, rejection is not evaluated. Our very short sequence remains an algebra exercise.

Even a valid rejection would support a stationary relationship between the levels under the assumptions examined, without establishing that X causes Y. One possible wording is: “We have calculated the residual statistic; this exercise establishes neither the statistical decision nor a causal interpretation.”

  • Name the appropriate calibration and the missing information.
  • Remove the causal claim and do not turn failure to reject into evidence of absence.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What procedure does Engle-Granger follow once the I(1) framework has been examined?

Fit a relationship between the levels, keep its residuals, then test their unit root using a cointegration calibration. The estimated residuals are the input to the second test.

Recall 2What does the null hypothesis mean in this test?

No cointegration: the residual retains a unit root. The alternative is its stationarity around the chosen relationship. Failure to reject means insufficient evidence, rather than demonstrated absence.

Recall 3Why is the ADF table for an observed series insufficient?

The relationship was estimated on the same data before the test. This estimation changes the null distribution of the statistic: cointegration critical values suited to the specification are needed.

Recall 4Why does a 0 sum of residuals prove nothing about their unit root?

With a constant, least squares imposes this 0 sum over the sample. This algebraic constraint does not demonstrate a stationary distribution over time.

Recall 5What units do the gap and the statistic τ have?

The residual and its change are in cm; A, C, D, and SCR are in cm². γ̂ and its standard error are dimensionless, as is their quotient τ. Changing the common unit of length does not change τ.
