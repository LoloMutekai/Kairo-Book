Level 1 · C028·M5

Choice trees

Your goalBuild a choice tree, count its complete packs, and check that no allowed pack is omitted or counted more than once.

Before you start
c014-m5 : invariants · c011-m5 : binomial theorem · c028-m1 : sum and product rules · c028-m2 : permutations · c028-m3 : combinations · c028-m4 : inclusion-exclusion

✿01 See the idea

You are preparing a pack for a workshop. The fictional catalogue offers a paper or digital medium. For paper, choose Staples or Rings for the binding, then an Ivory or Blue cover. For digital, choose the Text or Images format, then the delivery method. Text allows Link and Attachment; Images allows only Link.

Imagine tracing the choices with your finger until you reach a pack ready to hand in. Paper → Staples → Ivory is a complete possibility. Digital → Images is still incomplete: Link still needs to be specified. A tree records the decisions already made and shows only the allowed continuations.

The starting point is called the root. A node is a point reached after some choices; a branch leads to the next choice. A path is a sequence of branches followed from the root. Here, a valid leaf is the end of a path that fully describes an allowed pack.

The diagrams show the Paper and Digital branches from the same starting point separately. Read the bottom boxes as the ends of paths: Ivory after Staples and Ivory after Rings give different packs. Conversely, drawing the same pack elsewhere creates no additional possibility.

We are counting possible configurations for a pack, without stock quantities or randomness. The names of the medium and all its options determine the pack; no other detail distinguishes it. The catalogue rules are assumed to be complete and fixed throughout the calculation.

The rule to remember

Start with the complete packs, then work upwards: add the possibilities from distinct branches. Group them into a product only when each choice in the group offers the same number of valid continuations.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

How many packs does the catalogue allow?

The catalogue is still the one in the diagrams: 2 media; for Paper, 2 bindings and 2 covers per binding; for Digital, 2 delivery methods after Text and 1 after Images. Count distinct packs, rather than intermediate decisions.

  1. Define what a leaf represents

    A paper pack is fully described by its medium, binding, and cover. A digital pack is fully described by its medium, format, and delivery method. We keep this description order even if someone makes their choices in another order. The branches from a node cover all the allowed options and describe different options.

    This convention ensures the required correspondence: each allowed pack follows exactly 1 complete path, and each complete path gives 1 allowed pack. The tree is finite. We count neither the starting point nor an incomplete pack as a successful outcome.

  2. Write a local rule for working upwards

    For a node v, N(v) denotes the number of complete packs that extend the choices already made. It is an integer with no physical unit: it counts possible packs. A valid leaf contributes N(v) = 1; a dead end, meaning an incomplete choice with no allowed continuation, contributes N(v) = 0. Simply stopping a line therefore does not make the pack complete.

    If v has children a and b, these letters denote the nodes reached by its immediate branches. Any pack extending v passes through a or b, never both in this description. We can therefore add their counts. If there are other children, add their counts too; if there is only one, copy its count.

    N(v)=N(a)+N(b)

    As we work upwards, the rule preserves what we want to count: the same complete packs, grouped by their next choice. No probability is attached to the branches.

  3. Group the paper packs into a product

    Under Staples, the Ivory and Blue ends contribute 1 + 1 = 2 packs. Under Rings, we again find 2 packs. The paper total is therefore 2 + 2 = 4 packs.

    The product is a shorter way to write this sum of groups of equal size: 2 binding choices, each with 2 complete continuations. It is allowed here because all covers are allowed after each binding.

    N(Papier)=2×2=4 dossiers

  4. Add the unequal digital continuations

    Text gives Text → Link and Text → Attachment: 2 packs. Images gives only Images → Link: 1 pack. We add these distinct groups even though their sizes differ.

    N(Numérique)=2+1=3 dossiers

    The word Link appears after several formats. On its own, it does not identify the pack: Text → Link and Images → Link have different formats. You need to read the whole path to decide whether there is a duplicate.

  5. Combine the media at the root

    The root means “no medium chosen yet”. A pack is paper or digital; the groups do not overlap and cover the catalogue. Their sum gives the required result.

    N(racine)=4+3=7 dossiers

    Multiplying 2 × 2 × 2 blindly would give 8. This calculation assumes the same number of possibilities at each stage of every path. It would add Digital → Images → Attachment, which the rule forbids. The difference is 8 − 7 = 1 pack.

  6. Check with a list, then change the grouping

    Paper list: Paper / Staples / Ivory; Paper / Staples / Blue; Paper / Rings / Ivory; Paper / Rings / Blue. Digital list: Digital / Text / Link; Digital / Text / Attachment; Digital / Images / Link. Every entry is allowed; no complete description is repeated.

    To check that no pack is omitted, start with any allowed pack: its medium places it in the right list, then its binding or format, and finally its last option, locate its description. The lists do contain 7 packs in total.

    Another grouping of the digital part: delivery by link allows 2 formats, Text and Images; delivery as an attachment allows 1 format, Text. We again find 2 + 1 = 3, then 4 + 3 = 7 packs. Changing the order does not change the packs counted.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the counts and redraw the branches. Why can you multiply under Paper, but must keep different group sizes under Digital? Then show how to find any pack from the list in the tree.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I multiply the number of choices at each level, even if the continuations change.”

    Inspect the branches after Images: the attachment is missing. Count the continuations of each format separately, then add 2 and 1. A product of equal groups requires this equality to hold for every node concerned.

    Review the digital continuations and the unjustified product

  • “I count all the nodes, since each represents a choice.”

    The question asks about packs that are ready. Paper → Staples still describes several possible packs: do not add this node to its leaves. An incomplete end with no continuation contributes 0, while an allowed complete leaf contributes 1.

    Return to the definition of N and valid leaves

  • “Link appears several times: the tree counts the same pack several times.”

    Compare the full descriptions: Text / Link and Images / Link differ in format. A duplicate occurs only if the same complete pack has several paths in the chosen counting scheme. Check the whole path, rather than its last label.

    Reread what a path means and the descriptions of the diagrams

The new description is Digital / Images / Attachment. Images now has 1 + 1 = 2 delivery methods. Digital gives 2 + 2 = 4 packs.

Paper still has 4 packs. The total becomes 4 + 4 = 8. Check: exactly 1 pack has been added to the initial total 7, and no old path has disappeared.

Hint 1

Write the digital descriptions from each tree, then put them all in the order medium / format / delivery method.

Hint 2

Digital / Link / Text in the new tree denotes the same pack as Digital / Text / Link in the old one.

Compare with the solution

Link leads to 2 formats and Attachment to 1 format. We again find 2 + 1 = 3 digital packs, then 4 + 3 = 7 packs in total.

The digital paths correspond: Link / Text matches Text / Link; Link / Images matches Images / Link; Attachment / Text matches Text / Attachment, always after Digital. The paper paths remain identical. Each allowed pack is therefore present in each representation.

Adding the counts would give 7 + 7 = 14 occurrences in the diagrams. Each pack would be counted 2 times, once in each tree. To count distinct packs, choose one complete representation without duplicates and keep 7. Another order provides a check on the same set of packs.

  • You match all the digital paths to the initial descriptions, rather than just matching their counts.
  • You distinguish the 14 occurrences in the diagrams from the 7 distinct packs.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does a complete path represent in the workshop pack tree?

The description of all the relevant choices for an allowed pack. You need to read the medium and the successive options; the final label alone is not enough to identify the pack.

Recall 2How do you find a node’s count from its children?

Add the numbers of complete continuations of its children, provided their branches are distinct and cover all the allowed choices. A valid leaf contributes 1; an incomplete dead end contributes 0.

Recall 3Why does the product work under Paper but not directly under Digital in the initial catalogue?

Each binding has 2 covers: we combine 2 equal groups. Text has 2 delivery methods, Images has 1: their sizes differ, so we keep their sum.

Recall 4What must you check so that counting leaves amounts to counting packs?

Each counted leaf must describe an allowed, complete pack. Each allowed pack must have exactly 1 path in the chosen tree. This rules out impossible leaves, omissions, and repeated complete descriptions.

Recall 5Does changing the order of the choices create additional packs?

No, provided the new branches describe exactly the same rules. Match the complete descriptions before comparing the counts. Adding the leaves from several representations of the same catalogue would count the same packs again.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without rereading, reconstruct the catalogue and its tree: state what a complete pack is, include only the allowed branches, then work upwards through the counts. Explain where the sum can be grouped into a product. Recreate the list of 7 packs and check that it covers the catalogue without duplicates. Finish by explaining why changing a rule can change the count, while changing the description order preserves it.
