Level 2 · C067·M5

Pipeline

Your goalAssemble a transformation and a predictor, learn their parameters on the training set, then reuse the frozen pipeline on parcels held out from training.

Before you start
c061-m5 : identifiability and uncertainty · c066-m5 : standard errors and diagnostics · c017-m5 : reproduction protocol · c067-m1 : empirical risk · c067-m2 : training set · c067-m3 : validation · c067-m4 : data leakage

✿01 See the idea

A workshop wants to estimate the time needed to prepare a parcel from its mass. The scales give kilograms. The model will receive a rescaled mass, then return a duration in minutes. If each new batch redefines its scale, the same number no longer has the same meaning for the model.

A pipeline, or processing chain, assembles steps in a defined order. Here, the first step learns the mass bounds from the training parcels; the next fits a straight line to their transformed masses and durations. Learning means determining parameters from data. Applying means using those parameters without recalculating them.

Validation parcels are used to examine a candidate procedure. The test parcel is reserved for the final evaluation, once the procedure has been chosen. The transformation bounds are part of what is learned: they must respect the same boundary as the model. Even without looking at test durations, learning the bounds from test masses would pass held-out information into training.

All masses and durations on this page are fictional. We follow a single workshop, with a single feature, mass. The model is deliberately small enough to repeat every operation on paper; no claim is made about the quality of real predictions.

The rule to remember

Split the sets before any operation that learns from data; learn the transformation and then the model on the training set; keep their parameters together and reuse them in the same order.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

From the scales to the duration, keeping the same coordinate system

Assignment decided before the calculations: training A = (2 kg; 8 min) and B = (4 kg; 12 min), validation V = (3 kg; 11 min), test T = (6 kg; duration hidden until the final comparison). The procedure is fixed: scaling by the training bounds, then a straight line fitted to A and B. The reference values are preparation durations, not transport times.

  1. Define the contract and symbols

    x is the mass of a parcel, in kilograms; y is its reference preparation duration, in minutes; ŷ, with a hat, is the predicted duration. The letters A, B, V and T identify the parcels. Their subscripts serve only to indicate which parcel a quantity belongs to.

    L is the minimum and U the maximum of the training masses, in kilograms. d = U − L is their range, in kilograms; we assume d is strictly positive. g is the transformation: g(x) is the rescaled mass, written z, with no unit. f is the predictor applied to z. a is its intercept in minutes, b its slope in minutes per unit of z. Since z has no unit, b is expressed in minutes.

    z=g(x)=x−LU−L
    y^=f(g(x))=a+bz

    Assumptions: masses are present, finite and measured in the same unit; duration has the same definition for all parcels; parcels are distinct, with no copies across sets. To discuss generalization, we also assume that the held-out parcels represent the intended use and that no change in procedure makes them incomparable. The straight line is an approximation chosen for this exercise. Our fictional values do not verify these assumptions.

  2. Learn the coordinate system on A and B

    The minimum is L = 2 kg and the maximum U = 4 kg, because only the masses of A and B are permitted for this learning step. The range is d = 4 − 2 = 2 kg. Subtracting L places the first bound at the origin; dividing by d expresses each difference in training ranges.

    For A: 2 − 2 = 0 kg, then 0/2 = 0. For B: 4 − 2 = 2 kg, then 2/2 = 1. The ratios have no unit, since kilograms divided by kilograms cancel out.

    We keep L and U. No bounds calculation consults V or T. If all training masses were identical, d would be 0: the division would be undefined. A separate rule for this constant feature would then have to be stated before using the held-out sets.

  3. Learn the line in this coordinate system

    The line connects the transformed points A = (0; 8 min) and B = (1; 12 min). With these distinct horizontal coordinates, fitting a line by least squares here amounts to passing exactly through these points. This small case is no proof that a straight line suits all parcels.

    b=yB−yAzB−zA;a=yA−bzA

    The increase in duration is 12 − 8 = 4 min. The increase in the horizontal coordinate is 1 − 0 = 1, with no unit. Thus b = 4/1 = 4 min. The contribution at A is 4 × 0 = 0 min; a = 8 − 0 = 8 min.

    Check on B: b × zB = 4 × 1 = 4 min, then a + b × zB = 8 + 4 = 12 min, its reference duration. The learned pipeline now contains L, U, a and b; it also keeps the order of operations and the input units.

  4. Apply to validation without learning again

    For V, use the mass 3 kg and the stored coordinate system: 3 − 2 = 1 kg, then zV = 1/2 = 1/2 = 0.50, with no unit. The model receives zV; it does not receive the raw mass.

    The slope contribution is 4 × 1/2 = 2 min. The prediction is 8 + 2 = 10 min. After this prediction, compare it with yV = 11 min. Define r = ŷ − y, the signed difference in minutes: rV = 10 − 11 = -1 min. The negative sign indicates an underestimate.

    V is used to examine this procedure. In our example, no competing procedure is tried and no setting is changed. Such a small validation set demonstrates no reliability. If validation led to a change in procedure, that choice would have to be completed before consulting the test set.

  5. Freeze the pipeline, then open the test

    We freeze the procedure and its parameters. For parcel T of 6 kg, the coordinate system remains L = 2 kg and U = 4 kg. The difference from L is 6 − 2 = 4 kg; zT = 4/2 = 2, with no unit.

    The value zT exceeds 1, because T is heavier than all the training parcels. We do not clip it or move U: those actions would change the procedure. The model will therefore extrapolate, meaning it will extend its line beyond the masses used to fit it. This calculation is defined; its accuracy on real parcels would require further evidence.

    y^T=8+4×2=16 min

    The product b × zT is 8 min, then the sum is 16 min. Only now do we reveal yT = 15 min: rT = 16 − 15 = 1 min. This result is a fictional comparison on this parcel, with no success rate or conclusion about a population. The test cannot then be used to adjust the procedure while retaining the same status as a final test.

  6. Check the pipeline and what must be kept

    Inverse check of the coordinate system: zT × d = 2 × 2 = 4 kg; adding L gives 4 + 2 = 6 kg. We recover the input mass. This check verifies the algebraic consistency of the forward and inverse operations. It does not prove that the bounds come from the correct set: a contaminated scale, inverted with its own parameters, could also pass it.

    Boundary check: the masses of V and T can change the predictions made for them, but they must not change L, U, a or b learned on A and B. The transfer exercise makes this check concrete. The transformation, predictor and their parameters must stay together; an assembled pipeline guarantees this property only if its learning operations actually respect the sets.

    To reproduce this small calculation, keep the A/B/V/T assignment, the fictional values used, the definition of the units, the transformation formula, the learned parameters and the order of calls. For later use, the input will again be a mass in kilograms and the same frozen pipeline will produce a duration. New training will constitute a new version of this pipeline.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the calculations. Describe the path taken by the test parcel, naming what is learned and what is only applied. Then reconstruct the prediction and the inverse check. Why must the bounds travel with the line? An answer reread or obtained with a hint remains an assisted answer; it does not demonstrate an attained level.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I can learn the scale from all the masses, since I hide the test durations.”

    The held-out masses would influence the learned bounds. Our contract predicts new parcels without knowing them in advance: this information must therefore stay out of learning. Define the sets first and learn the bounds using only A and B.

    Review learning the coordinate system on A and B

  • “I keep the line, but I can recalculate the scale at test time.”

    The line learned the meaning of the horizontal coordinates in a specific coordinate system. Another coordinate system changes what it receives. Keep L and U with a and b; the diagnostic exercise quantifies the error caused by their mismatch.

    Diagnose the change in coordinate system

  • “With a pipeline, leakage is automatically impossible.”

    The name of the pipeline does not tell you which rows were used in each learning step. Check that scope and the actual order of operations. The milestone shows a source path where the indices are split after the transformations; you need to examine what these transformations learn.

    Examine the source path and its scope

Hint 2

Subtract L = 2 kg, divide by d = 2 kg, then apply a + bz.

Compare with the solution

The difference from L is 5 − 2 = 3 kg. The transformation gives z = 3/2 = 3/2, or 1.50 with no unit. The result can exceed 1: the new mass is outside the training range.

The contribution is 4 × 3/2 = 6 min; the prediction is 8 + 6 = 14 min. No parameter was learned again. The reference duration is missing: you can report a prediction, but not a measured error on this parcel.

  • Reuse the bounds and the line together, without adding clipping.
  • Distinguish the available prediction from the error that is still unknown.

Exercise 2 · Diagnosis · calculation and repair

The transformation changes, the line stays

A colleague combines A, B and parcel T to recalculate the bounds: the minimum stays 2 kg and the maximum becomes 6 kg. Yet they keep a = 8 min and b = 4 min. What prediction will they give T? Identify the problems, then propose a repair that still allows an honest final evaluation.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Recalculate the range, then the new horizontal coordinate of T before looking at the line.

Hint 2

Separate the use of held-out information for learning from the mismatch between the transformation and the predictor.

Compare with the solution

The mixed range is 6 − 2 = 4 kg. T becomes z = 4/4 = 1, with no unit. With the old line, its contribution is 4 × 1 = 4 min and its prediction 8 + 4 = 12 min, instead of 16 min. The difference between these predictions is 4 min; this is not a measure of improvement in quality.

There is leakage in learning the bounds, because the test mass is involved. There is also an inconsistency in the coordinate system: the line was not fitted in the new coordinates. Refitting the line in this contaminated coordinate system does not repair the data separation; moreover, changing scale does not necessarily change predictions if an affine model is refitted consistently.

To repair the pipeline, start again from A and B to learn the bounds and the line together, then freeze the result. This repair does not make the test already examined in this diagnosis new again: a new claim about quality following choices influenced by it requires another final set held out from those choices. V can be used to choose the procedure; this consulted test must never be presented as independent final evidence.

Check your reasoning

  • Obtain 12 min with the mixed coordinate system and the old line.
  • Name the leakage and the inconsistency between parameters separately.
  • Rebuild the pipeline on the training set and preserve a truly held-out set for any new final conclusion.

Exercise 3 · Transfer · checking protocol

Change the test to check the boundary

Prepare a check without seeking a better score: keep A and B identical, then replace the test mass with 10 kg in a copy of the exercise. What should happen to L, U, a and b, to the prediction for the modified test parcel and to that for the new parcel of 5 kg? Explain what such a check can establish and what it leaves open.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

What is learned depends on A and B. What is predicted also depends on the mass presented to the pipeline.

Hint 2

Calculate the new test horizontal coordinate, but change neither the bounds nor the parcel of 5 kg.

Compare with the solution

In both copies, learning receives exactly A and B. It must therefore keep L = 2 kg, U = 4 kg, a = 8 min and b = 4 min. The modified test gives 10 − 2 = 8 kg, then z = 8/2 = 4. The contribution is 4 × 4 = 16 min; the prediction is 8 + 16 = 24 min.

The prediction for the separate parcel of 5 kg remains 14 min: neither its mass nor the learned pipeline has changed. To check an implementation, compare the learned parameters in the copies and the predictions on this common input. A change to these parameters caused by the test set alone would contradict the stated learning contract.

This check tests a specific separation for the constructed cases. Success does not demonstrate the absence of all leakage: duplicates or a feature containing the future answer could escape this check. No reference duration is provided for the modified test, so its actual error is not calculated.

Check your reasoning

  • State the unchanged parameters and explain why they are unchanged.
  • Find 24 min for the modified test and keep 14 min on the common input.
  • Describe a comparison of parameters and a limit of the check, without turning it into universal proof.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What distinguishes learning a step from applying it?

Learning determines its parameters from the permitted set. Applying reuses those parameters. In the workshop, the bounds and then the line are learned on A and B; the held-out parcels then pass through the same frozen pipeline.

Recall 2Why split before learning the bounds, even if test durations remain hidden?

The bounds depend on the masses: they are already learned parameters. Learning them on the test set would pass its information into the pipeline. For our contract of predicting new parcels, the test must stay out of this learning.

Recall 3What must be kept with the coefficients of the line?

The learned bounds of the transformation, its rule, the units and the order of the steps. The coefficients are fitted to the transformed horizontal coordinates; applying them after another transformation changes their meaning.

Recall 4Why can a transformed mass exceed 1 without the calculation being wrong?

The bounds are those of the training masses. A new mass greater than their maximum gives a ratio greater than 1. The calculation remains defined; the prediction then extends the line beyond the masses used for fitting, and its reliability remains to be evaluated.

Recall 5What must a change to the test set alone preserve, and what is that insufficient to prove?

It must preserve the parameters learned on A and B and the predictions on an unchanged common input. This check tests the boundary on the chosen cases; it is insufficient to rule out all other leakage or to demonstrate prediction quality.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without rereading, draw the path training → learned transformation → learned model, then the path of a new parcel through these frozen steps. Annotate the units and the parameters to keep; repeat the test prediction and its inverse check. Finish by explaining why neither the word pipeline nor an assisted answer counts as proof.
