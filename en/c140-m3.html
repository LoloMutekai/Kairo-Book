Level 2 · C140·M3

Dual

Your goalMove from the soft margin to the dual coefficients, then certify an optimum by reconstructing the separator and its cost.

Before you start
c059-m6 : Deterministic discrete Bellman · c099-m5 : probability calibration · c040-m5 : least squares · c140-m1 : margin · c140-m2 : hinge loss

✿01 See the idea

A workshop wants to sort parts into bins labelled “small” and “large” based on their diameter. Our tiny training batch is fictional: part A measures 10 mm and has the label “small”; B measures 30 mm and has the label “large”. This batch is not used to validate industrial sorting.

Searching directly for a sorting rule means moving a boundary and adjusting its slope. The dual changes the unknowns: it assigns each part a coefficient that measures its influence in the reconstructed rule. The labels keep their signs; the coefficients remain nonnegative.

Imagine that each part can press for a sufficient margin. The parameter C caps this pressure. The coefficients must also balance across the classes: they cannot be chosen independently. This language illustrates constrained calculation without attributing intentions to the parts.

To calculate without mixing millimetres and scores, replace the diameter d with x = (d − 20 mm)/(10 mm). A has coordinate -1 and B 1, both dimensionless. The diagrams show the same parts, first on this axis, then as coefficients to be determined.

The rule to remember

The primal seeks a rule; the dual seeks feasible coefficients attached to the examples. Reconstruct the rule, check its constraints, then compare the two objectives.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

The same sorting task, viewed through its coefficients

We use C = 1, the same cost per unit of margin shortfall for each part. The numerical labels are yA = -1 and yB = 1. All the parameters below are dimensionless because of the diameter transformation. The intercept b is free and has no penalty.

  1. Set out the primal and its units

    The symbol i denotes A or B; Σᵢ means “sum over these parts”. The score s(x) = wx + b is affine: w sets its slope and b its intercept. Classify a part as “large” if s is positive and “small” if it is negative; at a 0 score, leave the classification undecided. The functional margin mᵢ = yᵢs(xᵢ) takes the correct class into account.

    The slack variable ξᵢ, read as “xi”, is a nonnegative margin shortfall. For fixed w and b, its smallest feasible value is max(0, 1 − mᵢ), the hinge loss. The primal minimizes the cost J: a penalty on the slope and a penalty C on the shortfalls. Here x, y, s, w, b, ξ, C and J are dimensionless; only d, the centre and the scale are in millimetres.

    J=w22+C∑ξi
    yi(wxi+b)≥1−ξi;ξi≥0

    Assumptions: fixed labels, strictly positive C, a linear score on this axis, a soft margin and a cost based on the sum of the losses. Using their average would change the numerical meaning of C.

  2. Attach a multiplier to each constraint

    Write the constraints as 1 − ξᵢ − yᵢ(wxᵢ + b) ≤ 0 and −ξᵢ ≤ 0. Attach αᵢ ≥ 0 to the first and μᵢ ≥ 0, read as “mu”, to the second. These are the Lagrange multipliers, dimensionless here. L is the cost augmented by these weighted constraints.

    L=J+∑αi[1−ξi−yi(wxi+b)]−∑μiξi

    At a feasible primal solution, the added terms are nonpositive: L ≤ J. For fixed multipliers, the smallest L obtained by varying w, b and ξ therefore gives a lower bound on every feasible primal cost. The dual will seek to make this bound as large as possible.

  3. Eliminate the primal unknowns

    In L, we now let w, b and ξ vary freely: their constraints have been incorporated. The derivative with respect to w is 0 when w = Σᵢ αᵢyᵢxᵢ. The coefficient of b is −Σᵢ αᵢyᵢ: if it is nonzero, b can drive L towards minus infinity. We therefore need Σᵢ αᵢyᵢ = 0.

    The coefficient of ξᵢ is C − αᵢ − μᵢ. For the same reason, it must be 0: μᵢ = C − αᵢ. With nonnegative αᵢ and μᵢ, we obtain 0 ≤ αᵢ ≤ C. The upper bound comes from the cost of the hinge loss; it does not come from normalization of the weights.

    w=∑αiyixi;∑αiyi=0;0≤αi≤C

  4. Read the dual function

    Let v = Σᵢ αᵢyᵢxᵢ, a dimensionless auxiliary quantity. In L, the terms involving w are (w × w)/2 − wv. Completing the square gives (w − v)(w − v)/2 − (v × v)/2: the minimum is reached at w = v. The terms involving b and ξ have disappeared. What remains is the function D of the coefficients α, to be maximized subject to the preceding constraints.

    D(α)=∑αi−(∑αiyixi)22

    The inputs to the dual are the examples and their labels; its unknowns are the α. In this dimension, expanding the square produces terms αᵢαⱼyᵢyⱼxᵢxⱼ, where j ranges over the same parts as i. We keep ordinary products here: replacing them with a kernel belongs to the next unit.

  5. Reduce the batch to a single unknown

    Centring: 10 − 20 = -10 mm and 30 − 20 = 10 mm. Dividing by 10 mm: xA = -1, xB = 1. The signed contributions are yAxA = 1 and yBxB = 1.

    Balance becomes −αA + αB = 0, so αA = αB. Call their shared value a; its domain is 0 ≤ a ≤ 1. The reconstructed slope is w = 2a. Substitute it into D, then complete the square:

    D(a)=2a−2a2=12−2(a−12)2

    A square is nonnegative: D cannot exceed 1/2. This bound is reached at a = 1/2, or 0.50, a value between 0 and C = 1. We have found the maximum over the entire feasible interval by more than just testing a few values.

  6. Reconstruct the slope and intercept

    Each contribution is αᵢyᵢxᵢ = 1/2 × 1 = 1/2. Their sum gives w = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1. The α are therefore not the score coefficients applied directly to the diameters.

    To recover b, seek a candidate satisfying complementarity: αᵢ[1 − ξᵢ − mᵢ] = 0 and μᵢξᵢ = 0. These equalities make the weighted constraints added to J in L vanish; we will then check the full certificate. Since 0 < αᵢ = 1/2 < C, μᵢ = C − αᵢ = 1/2 is strictly positive. This requires ξᵢ = 0, then mᵢ = 1. A part whose coefficient is strictly inside its bounds therefore lies on the margin boundary.

    For A, yA(wxA + b) = 1 is equivalent to wxA + b = yA, because yA equals -1. Now wxA = -1; therefore b = yA − wxA = -1 − (-1) = 0. B also gives b = 1 − 1 = 0: the two reconstructions agree.

  7. Check the constraints before drawing a conclusion

    The score is s(x) = 1x + 0. For A, it equals -1 and mA = -1 × (-1) = 1. For B, it equals 1 and mB = 1 × 1 = 1. The shortfalls ξA and ξB are 0; all the primal constraints are satisfied.

    On the dual side, the coefficients equal 1/2, respect the cap 1 and give 1/2 × (-1) + 1/2 × 1 = 0. The boundary x = 0 corresponds to the central diameter 20 mm. This rule separates the fictional batch; nothing here measures its quality on new parts.

  8. Certify the optimum using both costs

    The primal gives J = (w × w)/2 + C(ξA + ξB) = 1/2 + 0 = 1/2. The dual gives D = αA + αB − (w × w)/2 = 1 − 1/2 = 1/2. Their gap J − D equals 0.

    Why is this decisive? Every feasible primal solution costs at least as much as every feasible dual bound. Here, a feasible rule reaches a dual bound exactly: no other primal solution can do better and no other dual solution can reach a higher value. This is a certificate of optimality for this specific problem. The reasoning relies on feasibility on both sides; comparing two isolated numbers would not be enough.

Explain it in your own words

Without rereading, recover the origin of the cap C and the balance equality. Then explain how a coefficient strictly inside its bounds allows b to be recovered, and why J = D certifies this batch without proving the quality of real sorting.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The dual coefficients are probabilities that must sum to 1.”

    Their sum is not prescribed. The constraints are 0 ≤ αᵢ ≤ C and Σᵢ αᵢyᵢ = 0. In the initial batch, the sum 1 is a coincidence: with the small C in the exercise, it equals 1/2. No α expresses a class probability.

    Recover the constraints by eliminating b and ξ

  • “I can choose each coefficient separately, then adjust b afterwards.”

    The balance equality comes precisely from the free intercept b. Choosing αA = 1/2, αB = 0 gives a signed sum -1/2, different from 0. This choice is not dual feasible, even though each coefficient respects its cap.

    Test feasibility in the first exercise

  • “If αᵢ is positive, the part must lie exactly on the margin boundary.”

    Margin equality with no shortfall is guaranteed when 0 < αᵢ < C. At the cap αᵢ = C, the shortfall can be positive: with C = 1/4, our batch keeps the correct classes but the signed margin is only 1/2. A positive coefficient and a coefficient strictly inside its bounds are two different conditions.

    Recalculate the batch when the cap becomes active

Compare with the solution

The interval ends at 1/4, before the vertex 1/2. The completed-square form of D shows that the best feasible point is a = C = 1/4, or 0.25. The slope is w = 2 × 1/4 = 1/2. Neither coefficient is strictly inside its bounds: the formula for reconstructing b from a margin with no shortfall does not apply. We test b = 0 and then check.

The scores have the correct signs, with signed margins 1/2. Each shortfall equals ξ = 1 − 1/2 = 1/2. The sum of the shortfalls is 1; the penalty is C × 1 = 1/4. The slope term is (w × w)/2 = 1/8.

The primal cost is J = 1/8 + 1/4 = 3/8. The dual is D = 1/2 − 1/8 = 3/8. The equality and the verified constraints certify the optimum. The classes remain correct on the batch, but the hinge loss is positive: being on the correct side is not enough to reach the target margin.

  • Do not apply the rule for b that requires a coefficient strictly inside its bounds.
  • Calculate the shortfalls and recover J = D = 3/8 without omitting them.

Exercise 3 · Diagnosis and repair

Feasible does not mean optimal

Return to C = 1. Someone takes αA = αB = 1, reconstructs w = 2 and chooses b = 0, ξA = ξB = 0. They announce: “All the constraints are satisfied, so I have the optimum.” Calculate J, D and their gap, then propose a justified repair.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Signed scores above the target margin remain primal feasible.

Hint 2

For the dual, subtract (w × w)/2 from the sum of the coefficients.

Hint 3

A positive gap does not certify the optimum; compare with the pair already constructed in the course.

Compare with the solution

The coefficients are feasible and balanced. The signed margins equal 2, so the proposed primal solution is also feasible. However, J = 4/2 = 2 and D = 2 − 2 = 0. The gap equals 2.

This gap alone is not enough to prove that each side is suboptimal: it removes the equality certificate. Here we have more evidence: the known primal cost 1/2 is better than 2, and the known dual value 1/2 is better than 0. Replacing each coefficient with 1/2 gives w = 1, b = 0, then J = D = 1/2.

Check your reasoning

  • Check feasibility on both sides before calculating the costs.
  • Find J = 2, D = 0 and a gap 2.
  • Distinguish the absence of a certificate from proof of suboptimality through an explicit comparison.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1Which unknowns does the dual use in place of the primal unknowns?

The primal chooses w, b and the shortfalls ξ. The dual chooses the multipliers α attached to the examples; the slope is reconstructed as w = Σᵢ αᵢyᵢxᵢ.

Recall 2Where do the balance equality and the cap C come from?

The free intercept b requires Σᵢ αᵢyᵢ = 0 for the minimum of L to be finite. The shortfalls require μᵢ = C − αᵢ; with nonnegative αᵢ and μᵢ, this gives 0 ≤ αᵢ ≤ C.

Recall 3When can an example be used to recover b from the margin boundary?

If 0 < αᵢ < C at the optimum, complementarity requires ξᵢ = 0 and yᵢ(wxᵢ + b) = 1. Then b = yᵢ − wxᵢ. At the cap, this conclusion with no shortfall is not guaranteed.

Recall 4Why can an equality J = D certify an optimum?

Every feasible dual solution provides a lower bound on every feasible primal cost. If a feasible primal solution reaches that bound, neither problem can improve its value. The constraints on both sides must be checked.

Recall 5What do neither the coefficients nor the certificate for our batch prove?

The coefficients are not class probabilities. The certificate concerns the stated mathematical problem; it measures neither quality on new parts nor actual use in a program.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Start again with the same parts and reconstruct the chain on paper: soft margin and cost J; nonnegative multipliers; elimination of w, b and ξ; dual constraints; maximum of D; reconstruction and checking of the primal. Finish by explaining why equality of the costs is a proof that depends on feasibility. Reading or an assisted answer does not validate this skill.
