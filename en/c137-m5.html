Level 2 · C137·M5

Rank selection

Your goalChoose the smallest number of PCA axes that reaches a cumulative variance threshold, then calculate what the reconstruction loses.

Before you start
c048-m5 : positivity · c050-m5 : conditioning · c060-m5 : approximations and assumptions · c036-m5 : annotated graphs · c137-m1 : centering · c137-m2 : covariance · c137-m3 : optimal projection · c137-m4 : SVD and PCA

✿01 See the idea

A test rig tracks the small displacements of a plate using 3 sensors. At each recorded instant, we obtain 3 coordinates in millimetres. To make the description more compact, we want to retain fewer coordinates while keeping a stated proportion of the observed variation.

Principal component analysis, or PCA, has already provided perpendicular directions, ordered by decreasing variance. A coordinate along such a direction is called a score. Keeping the first scores retains the largest variations; discarding the others prevents exact reconstruction of all the displacements.

In our fictional scenario, axes A, B and C carry 9, 4 and 1 mm² of variance respectively. The first axis therefore does not capture all the movement. We add the contributions in order, then stop at the first cumulative total that is sufficient.

All the readings, thresholds and variances on this page are fictional. The sensors use the same unit and have the same weight in the error; we center their readings without dividing them by their standard deviations. Rank selection here concerns the reconstruction of these readings, without promising fault detection or an actual saving in storage.

The rule to remember

Set the threshold before choosing; add the variances in decreasing order; take the first rank that reaches the threshold, then quantify the loss and state what this criterion does not guarantee.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

How many coordinates should we keep for the plate?

The 5 centered observations contain p = 3 variables in mm. The eigenvalues of their covariance are λa = 9, λb = 4, λc = 1 mm², already ordered. We require at least 90 % of the variance to be retained. Let us find the smallest admissible rank, then the sum of squared reconstruction errors.

  1. Set the convention before dividing

    X is the centered data table: n rows of observations, p columns of sensors. Each column has a mean of 0. Here n = 5 and p = 3; the covariance is calculated with the divisor n − 1 = 4. Another convention would change the stated variances and the conversion factor for the total error.

    λa, λb and λc denote the variances of the scores along directions A, B and C. These directions are perpendicular and have unit length: the scores remain in mm, and the variances in mm². The eigenvalues are nonnegative. We assume n is greater than 1 and the total variance is strictly positive. The decomposition and directions are given; we do not recalculate them in this unit.

  2. Establish the reference total and the criterion

    T denotes the total variance, the sum of the variances of all the axes. We divide by this total to express a proportion with no unit. In the scenario:

    T=λa+λb+λc=9+4+1=14 mm²

    r is the integer number of leading axes retained, between 1 and p. Vᵣ is the sum of their variances; Cᵣ = Vᵣ/T is the cumulative proportion retained. The threshold τ, the Greek letter “tau”, is a proportion with no unit, greater than 0 and less than or equal to 1. Here τ = 90/100 = 0.90.

    Cr=VrT≥τ

    We seek the smallest r that satisfies this inequality. Reaching the threshold exactly is sufficient. The threshold is a requirement chosen for this reconstruction; PCA does not supply a universal percentage to adopt.

  3. Add until the threshold is first reached

    With r = 1, Vᵣ = 9 mm²: Cᵣ = 9/14, or approximately 64.29 %. This is below 90 %; the first axis is not sufficient.

    With r = 2, we add the contribution of B: Vᵣ = 9 + 4 = 13 mm². The cumulative proportion is 13/14, or approximately 92.86 %. This exceeds the threshold. Since the previous rank fails, the smallest rank we retain is r = 2.

    914<90100≤1314

    With all the axes, the cumulative proportion would be 1, or 100 %. This rank also satisfies the threshold, but it does not satisfy our requirement to keep as few axes as possible.

  4. Relate the rank to what reconstruction removes

    Xᵣ is the centered reconstruction obtained by retaining the first r scores and their directions. In each observation, the remainder is perpendicular to the retained part. The Pythagorean theorem therefore allows us to add the squares of the discarded coordinates, then sum over all observations.

    Rᵣ = T − Vᵣ denotes the total residual variance, in mm², with the divisor n − 1. Eᵣ denotes the sum of squared reconstruction errors over the entire table, also in mm². In the following formula, the subscript F on the norm symbol indicates that its square is the sum of the squares of all entries in the error table:

    Er=‖X−Xr‖F2=(n−1)Rr

    Singular value decomposition, or SVD, provides the same check. σᵢ, the Greek letter “sigma”, is the singular value of axis i, in mm; i identifies an axis in decreasing order, and λᵢ denotes its variance in mm². The square of σᵢ equals (n − 1)λᵢ. The error Eᵣ is the sum of the squares of the discarded singular values. Summing the singular values without squaring them does not measure this error.

  5. Calculate the loss and check it another way

    At rank 2, only direction C is removed. Rᵣ = 14 − 13 = 1 mm². This directly recovers the variance of C, 1 mm²: a first check.

    E2=4×1=4 mm²

    Another route: the squares of the singular values are 36, 16 and 4 mm² respectively. Their total is 56 mm²; axes A and B retain 52 mm² of it. The difference 56 − 52 = 4 mm² recovers Eᵣ.

    To obtain the mean squared reconstruction distance per observation, we divide Eᵣ by n: 4/5 = 0.80 mm² per observation. This is not Rᵣ: the divisors n and n − 1 correspond to different conventions.

    The lost proportion is Rᵣ/T = 1/14, or approximately 7.14 % of the total variance. The retained and lost proportions add up: 13/14 + 1/14 = 1. The calculation covers all the observations; on its own, it does not bound the error of each reading.

  6. State exactly what the choice allows

    We would store 2 scores per observation instead of 3, along with the directions and means needed for reconstruction. The score count alone does not give a final file size: this shared information and the storage format still have to be included. The original matrix here has rank 3, because its eigenvalues are positive; our approximation has rank 2. We have chosen a reduction, rather than discovered an exact dependency between the sensors.

    Low variance is not proof of noise. The displacement carried by C could matter for a particular alarm. Our threshold guarantees only the proportion of variation retained and the overall error calculated on this table. It guarantees neither a correct decision nor the same error in future tests.

    The choice also depends on how the variables are prepared. Changing the scale of a single sensor or dividing each column by its standard deviation changes the problem and may change the selected rank. If T = 0, all the centered readings are 0: the fraction Cᵣ is undefined and this case must be handled separately. We do not replace this impossible quotient with an invented percentage.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I compare the variance of each axis separately with the threshold.”

    The threshold concerns the cumulative variance of the retained axes, divided by the total. Add A, then B, and check that the previous rank fails. Make the exact comparison before rounding the displayed percentage.

    Review the cumulative calculations and when they first reach the threshold

  • “The sum of the discarded singular values is the squared error.”

    You must add their squares. With the covariance divided by n − 1, also multiply the residual variance by n − 1 to obtain the total error Eᵣ. Distinguish this sum from its mean per observation.

    Review the SVD check and the units

  • “A direction with little variation must be noise.”

    Variance describes the magnitude of the observed variations. It does not determine their usefulness for an alarm. State the reconstruction guarantee, then specify the task that would need to be checked separately.

    Examine the constraints and the objection in the last exercise

Compare with the solution

9/14 is greater than 3/5, or 60 %. Rank 1 is sufficient; no smaller positive rank is possible. The exact fraction is approximately 64.29 %.

Axes B and C are discarded: Rᵣ = 4 + 1 = 5 mm². Then Eᵣ = 4 × 5 = 20 mm². Check: the squares of the discarded singular values give 16 + 4 = 20 mm². The loss increases when going from rank 2 to rank 1, as expected.

When a rounded display overstates the result

The threshold rises to 93 %. A display with no decimal places gives 93 % for axes A and B. Can we retain just these axes? Decide using an exact comparison, and give the rank and the final total error.

Hint 1

A display rounded to 93 % may come from a smaller value. Compare 13/14 with 93/100.

Hint 2

The denominators are positive: compare 13 × 100 with 14 × 93.

Compare with the solution

The cross products are 1300 and 1302. The first is smaller than the second by 2: 13/14 is therefore strictly less than 93/100. The exact cumulative proportion, approximately 92.86 %, does not reach 93 %. The rounded display must not determine the decision in its place.

We must add C and take r = 3. The retained proportion reaches 1, or 100 %; Rᵣ = 0 mm² and Eᵣ = 0 mm². This result is exact for the given table with all its axes. It does not predict the errors of new sensors or new observations.

  • Establish the exact inequality using the cross products and their positive denominators.
  • Choose rank 3 and obtain a total error of 0 mm².
  • Explain why a display rounded to the threshold does not prove that it has been reached.

Exercise 3 · Transfer · examine a specification

An incompatible budget and promise

The test rig allows at most 1 score per observation, but still requires at least 90 % of the variance to be retained. A colleague proposes keeping only A: “That is enough; the other directions are noise.” Respond on feasibility, quantify the loss from this proposal and state what the spectrum lets us say about noise.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The first direction already retains the most variance among projections onto a single direction. Does the maximum allowed rank reach the threshold?

Hint 2

Separate a reconstruction constraint from a question about the origin or usefulness of the movement.

Compare with the solution

With at most 1 score, the best proportion retained by an orthogonal projection is 9/14, approximately 64.29 %, below 90 %. Optimal projection is the subject of prerequisite c137-m3. Changing only the direction cannot exceed this limit. The requirements are incompatible for this PCA; rank 2, needed for the variance requirement, exceeds the budget.

The proposal loses 5 mm² of variance and produces 20 mm² as the sum of squared errors. A lower rank cannot improve this error. The budget, threshold or representation requirement must be revised, without claiming that the requirements are already satisfied.

The spectrum does not allow us to identify B and C as noise. They could contain movement useful for an alarm. We would need to know that task and check it using appropriate observations; the variance proportion alone does not provide that proof.

Check your reasoning

  • Show the incompatibility using the cumulative proportion at the maximum allowed rank, and recall the optimality of the first axis.
  • Quantify the loss from the proposal using the definitions of Rᵣ and Eᵣ.
  • Propose revising a requirement without treating an incompatibility as success.
  • Distinguish low variance, noise and information useful for a task.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1How do we choose the smallest rank for a retained variance threshold?

Order the variances in decreasing order, calculate the total T, then the cumulative proportions Vᵣ/T. Retain the first rank that reaches the threshold, checking that the previous one fails. Exact equality is sufficient; rounding to the threshold is not.

Recall 2What is the difference between residual variance and the sum of squared errors?

With the covariance divided by n − 1, Rᵣ = T − Vᵣ is the residual variance and Eᵣ = (n − 1)Rᵣ is the sum of squared errors over the table. For the mean squared distance per observation, divide Eᵣ by n.

Recall 3How do the singular values provide the same check on the loss?

The square of σᵢ equals (n − 1)λᵢ under our convention. Adding the squares of the discarded singular values gives Eᵣ. Their squares, rather than their values, are what we add to measure this error.

Recall 4What should we do if the total variance T is 0?

Do not divide by T. All the centered observations are then 0; the cumulative proportion is undefined. This absence of variation must be handled separately, specifying what representation is needed.

Recall 5Does retaining a large proportion of variance guarantee a good alarm?

No. It describes the overall reconstruction of the table prepared with a given scale. A direction with little variation can contain a decisive signal. The quality of the alarm and its behaviour on new observations require a separate check.

Reading milestone · distinguish two uses of the word rank

What you understand
The compression rank is a number of axes chosen according to an acceptable loss. The numerical rank diagnoses the number of dimensions that a calculation can distinguish with its tolerances. The same word therefore does not imply the same contract.
