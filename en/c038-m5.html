Level 2 · C038·M5

Proof of life and proof of result

Your goalDistinguish recent activity from an export that meets its requirements, by linking each piece of evidence to its source, its attempt and its time.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

You ask a small program to export an equipment record. Its indicator flashes: it has sent another sign of life. Then it displays “done”. Yet when you open the record, you read “ready”, although you had requested “needs calibration”. The program has been active; the requested content has not been produced.

Proof of life establishes a specific activity at an observed time: the presence of a process, a response to a query or a heartbeat produced by a loop. These observations do not all have the same scope. A recent heartbeat indicates that this loop sent a signal recently; it could freeze immediately afterwards. It guarantees neither progress on the export nor the operation of all the other tasks.

Proof of result compares the expected effect with what a reader observes at the correct location. For our export, we need to identify the correct attempt, open the destination file and compare its values with the request. The “done” message remains a statement by the producer. An old, correct record does not prove that the new request succeeded.

The entire scene and all its values are fictional. We are studying a local export and its verification, without measuring a real service. Freshness, which we have already encountered, is used here to limit what the sign of life allows us to conclude.

The rule to remember

Always ask: what activity was observed, what result was read back, for which attempt and at what time? Keep these verdicts separate.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

An active export with an incorrect record

At 120 s, attempt dawn requests a record containing exactly the fields item = probe, status = needs calibration and location = shelf. At 145 s, the worker reports “done”. At 150 s, we check its heartbeat and read back its destination.

  1. Define what the observations represent

    The worker is the program responsible for the export. Its heartbeat is a periodic message sent by its own loop, separate from the writing task. The reader is a separate check that opens the destination, rather than reusing the content displayed by the worker.

    Let d be the time of the request, h the time of the last heartbeat, r the reported time of saving the file and t the time of the check. All these elapsed times are in seconds from a common origin: d = 120 s, h = 142 s, r = 145 s and t = 150 s. The threshold chosen for the exercise is τ = 20 s; the Greek letter τ is read as “tau”.

    We assume a common clock that does not move backwards, timestamps assigned to the correct events, an authentic heartbeat from the expected worker and a file that stays stable during readback. The marker dawn is assigned to this attempt and is not reused. These assumptions make comparison possible; a negative age or an uncertain source would require an unknown verdict. The threshold is a fictional convention, not a recommended setting for a real system.

  2. Calculate the age of the sign of life

    The age a measures the time elapsed since the heartbeat. We therefore subtract its time h from the check time t. The inclusive rule accepts the heartbeat if its age lies between 0 s and τ, including both limits.

    a=t−h=150−142=8 s
    0≤a≤τ

    Here 0 s ≤ 8 s ≤ 20 s: the signal satisfies the rule. The margin before the threshold is 20 − 8 = 12 s. This margin does not predict how long the program will remain alive; it only indicates when this same heartbeat will stop being recent enough.

  3. Link the record to the request

    We open the intended destination and read the attempt marker dawn there. This marker is metadata, separate from the equipment fields. Its correspondence with the request must be checked before interpreting the content. The filename alone is not enough, because it could refer to an old export.

    The reported save time satisfies d ≤ r ≤ t: 120 s ≤ 145 s ≤ 150 s. The reported delay after the request is r − d = 145 − 120 = 25 s; the reported age of the saved file at the check is t − r = 150 − 145 = 5 s. These calculations check that the chronology is consistent. They do not prove the content or measure the program’s actual computation time.

  4. Compare the values, field by field

    Readback gives item = probe, status = ready and location = shelf. The item field matches; the location field also matches; the status field contradicts the expected value “needs calibration”. Having all the lines present does not make their values correct.

    N denotes the number of expected fields, C the number of fields whose key and value match exactly, and M the number of expected fields that do not match. These are counts of fields, with no physical unit. Here N = 3, C = 2. Each expected field is classified exactly once; a missing field or a field with an incorrect value counts as a mismatch. The contract also excludes extra fields.

    M=N−C=3−2=1

    The contract requires all fields to match, no extra lines and the correct attempt. With 1 mismatching field, the result does not meet the requirements. If the destination were unreadable, we could not calculate C: the observation would be unknown, rather than containing 0 matching fields by default.

  5. Give separate verdicts

    Activity: a recent heartbeat from the worker was observed, according to the declared threshold. Result: the file for attempt dawn was read back, but its content does not meet the requirements. The established problem is the content mismatch; these observations do not tell us which instruction caused it. A heartbeat therefore does not prove progress, and a completion statement does not prove the requested effect.

    Proof of result must state its scope: here, whether this record meets this request at the time of readback. Even a correct record would not prove that someone subsequently used it, or that a service is still available now. Conversely, an export can be correct and its program then stop: the result obtained and current activity remain separate questions.

  6. Check the calculation and prepare the repair

    Check the timing by adding the parts back together: 142 + 8 = 150 s. Another route from the request: 120 + 25 + 5 = 150 s. Check the ranking of fields by conformance: 2 matching fields + 1 mismatching fields = 3 expected fields.

    To repair the export, we would need to correct how the status field is produced, start a new attempt with a clear name, then read back its destination and repeat the full comparison. No new attempt is run in this lesson. A new indicator light or another “done” message would not be enough to verify the repair.

Explain it in your own words

Without looking at the page, explain why activity is established while the result does not meet the requirements. Then name the readback needed to verify a repair and what would remain unknown if that readback were impossible.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The heartbeat is recent, so the export succeeded.”

    The heartbeat establishes that its loop sent a signal recently. Identify the attempt, open the destination and compare its values: our status ready is still incorrect despite a recent signal.

    Review the age calculation and the content check

  • “The file exists and contains the correct number of lines: that is proof.”

    Also check the attempt marker and every key–value pair. An old file or an incorrect value can retain the same name and the same number of lines. The result contract determines what needs to be compared.

    Try the comparison, then audit the attempt

  • “If I can no longer read a heartbeat, I know the result is lost.”

    A failure to read leaves activity unknown; a timestamped signal that is too old no longer satisfies the freshness rule. Neither fact erases a record that has actually been read back. Look separately for proof of the result and for the cause of the missing signal.

    Return to the separate questions of life and result

Exercise 1 · Apply a rule with limits

The same heartbeat gets older

The last heartbeat is still timestamped 142 s, with no new signal. With the inclusive threshold of 20 s, until what time does it satisfy the rule? Then give its age and the verdict at 165 s. What can you say about the export from this calculation alone?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The time limit is the heartbeat time plus the allowed duration. At equality, the rule is still satisfied.

Hint 2

After the limit, distinguish a signal that is too old, a process that has actually stopped and the result of the export.

Compare with the solution

The limit is 142 + 20 = 162 s. At this time, the age is 162 − 142 = 20 s: equality is accepted.

At 165 s, the age reaches 165 − 142 = 23 s. It exceeds the threshold by 23 − 20 = 3 s: this heartbeat is too old according to our rule. This does not demonstrate that the process has stopped; the signal might also no longer be reaching the reader. Whether the export meets its requirements still needs its own readback.

Check your reasoning

  • Find the limit and include equality.
  • Give the age and the amount by which it exceeds the threshold, in seconds.
  • Do not infer a definite shutdown or a failed export solely from the ageing of the signal.

Exercise 2 · Compare with a contract

All the lines are present

A variant of the same export dawn: its destination carries the correct attempt marker and the 3 expected fields, with no extra fields. Readback gives item = thermometer, status = ready, location = shelf. The expected content remains item = probe, status = needs calibration, location = shelf. Calculate C then M and justify the verdict despite a recent heartbeat.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

A key that is present with a different value is not a matching field.

Hint 2

Compare each pair, then use M = N − C.

Compare with the solution

Only location = shelf matches. So C = 1 matching field. The item thermometer does not match probe; the status ready does not match needs calibration.

M = 3 − 1 = 2 mismatching fields. The contract is violated, even though the number of lines is correct and the heartbeat is recent. Repairing only the status field would still leave the item incorrect; the new attempt must be read back in full.

Check your reasoning

  • Compare the values as well as the field names.
  • Identify the mismatches and the matching field precisely.
  • Give a verdict on the content independently of heartbeat freshness.

Exercise 3 · Audit incomplete evidence

An old success, then a new observation

At 150 s, the visible file carries the attempt marker mist and a save time of 100 s; all the equipment fields match the expected content. The current request is still dawn, started at 120 s. The heartbeat log is unreadable. Can we reach a conclusion about the worker’s life and the result of dawn? In a second, independent observation, the destination carries dawn, its save time is 145 s and all the fields match; the log remains unreadable. Which verdicts change?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Start with the attempt identity and the order of the dates, before counting the correct fields.

Hint 2

A result can be checked on disk even if the heartbeat channel cannot be read.

Compare with the solution

First observation: activity is unknown, because the log is unreadable. The record mist is 150 − 100 = 50 s old and precedes the dawn request by 120 − 100 = 20 s. Its correct content does not prove the result of dawn: the observed evidence belongs to a different attempt. This alone does not establish that dawn failed everywhere; its result remains unestablished by this observation.

Second observation: the identity matches, the save time lies between the request and the check, and the destination has been read back with all fields matching. Under the assumptions of the example, the result of dawn is established for this record at the time of the check. Current activity remains unknown, because we have not repaired the log reading. This second observation is a fictional variant, not an actual repair of the system.

Check your reasoning

  • Refuse to attribute mist to dawn and use the dates to justify that it came earlier.
  • Keep the verdict unknown for an unreadable channel.
  • Accept the limited scope of the second readback without treating it as proof of current life.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What exactly does a recent heartbeat from the worker prove?

That the identified loop sent a signal at the timestamped time and that this signal satisfies the declared freshness rule. It guarantees neither continuous activity, progress on the export nor a result that meets the requirements.

Recall 2Which checks make the record usable as proof of result?

Identify the correct attempt, check that the chronology is consistent, open the destination and compare all its content with the retained contract. The conclusion applies to this record at the time of the check.

Recall 3Why does a perfect old record fail to validate the new request?

Content that meets the requirements is not enough to link the effect to the requested attempt. The attempt identity must be checked; the filename can stay the same across several exports.

Recall 4How do you distinguish incorrect content from unreadable content?

Incorrect content has been observed and contradicts an expected value. Unreadable content does not allow comparison: its verdict remains unknown, without replacing the missing observation with a count of 0.

Recall 5Can you verify the result without knowing whether the worker is still active?

Yes: a record from the correct attempt can be read back and meet the requirements after the program has stopped or despite an unreadable heartbeat channel. The evidence remains limited to this record, with no guarantee of future availability.

Reconstruct from memory: describe the path from request to worker to destination to reader. State the age rule and its assumptions. Explain why the attempt identity and a full comparison of the content are necessary. Finish with separate verdicts for activity and result, keeping the unknowns.
