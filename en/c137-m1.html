Level 2 · C137·M1

Centering

Your goalCenter a table of readings by subtracting each column’s mean from its values, and check what this operation preserves (differences, dispersion, units) and what it removes (the position of the point cloud relative to the origin).

Before you start
c048-m5 : positivity · c050-m5 : conditioning · c060-m5 : approximations and assumptions · c036-m5 : annotated graphs

✿01 See the idea

A fictional sensor takes 3 readings of the temperature t, in degrees Celsius, and relative humidity h, in percent, in a room. Each reading is a point in a plane: a number on the t axis and a number on the h axis. The values are invented for the exercise; they do not describe any real room.

In the first diagram, the three points A, B and C are close to one another but very far from the origin O, the point where t and h equal 0. This origin is a choice of coordinate system, rather than a property of the room: changing the temperature scale would move O without changing anything in the room. The point cloud is described by its center G (the means of t and h) and how the points spread out around G.

Centering shifts the coordinate system: we subtract each column’s mean from its values, and G becomes the origin, as in the second diagram. Nothing rotates or stretches: the points keep their differences from one another and their units. Only the position of the point cloud relative to O is removed.

This position gets in the way when we want to discuss spread. Measuring dispersion from the origin combines two pieces of information: the distance from the center to O, and the spread around the center. Later units in this chapter will examine how this spread is distributed; here, we prepare for that work through centering and its checks.

The figures follow the rule for annotated graphs (c036-m5): named axes, units, reference points and a common scale. As in c060-m5, each calculation states its assumptions before using them.

The rule to remember

Centering subtracts each column’s mean from its values: the point cloud shifts without changing shape, the columns then have a mean of 0, and dispersion around the center does not change.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Center three sensor readings

The fictional sensor recorded three readings: A (17 °C ; 57 %), B (23 °C ; 45 %) and C (26 °C ; 48 %). We center both columns, check the result, then compare dispersion around the center and around the origin.

  1. Define the quantities, units and assumptions

    The table has n = 3 rows (the readings) and two columns: t, temperature, in °C; h, relative humidity, in %. The mean of t is written mt and the mean of h is written mh: these are the coordinates of the center G. The centered value of a reading is written with a prime: t′ for temperature, h′ for humidity. The variance of t is vt, and that of h is vh.

    Assumptions for this unit: each column is numerical, with no missing values; all readings have equal weight; variance is calculated by dividing by n (the population variance of the 3 readings, with no inference beyond them). Units follow the column: °C and % for centered values, °C² and %² for variances. A variance is a sum of squares divided by n: it is never negative, applying the same positivity check as in c048-m5.

  2. Calculate the means

    The mean of a column is the sum of its values divided by n. For t, with readings indexed by i:

    mt=1n∑i=1nti

    With our data, we find the sum in two additions: 17 + 23 = 40, then 40 + 26 = 66.

    mt=17+23+263=663=22 °C

    For h: 57 + 45 = 102, then 102 + 48 = 150, so:

    mh=57+45+483=1503=50 %

    The center is therefore G = (22 °C ; 50 %).

  3. Subtract each column’s mean

    Subtract the mean of each value’s own column from that value. The operation never mixes t and h, and it keeps the units:

    ti′=ti−mt et hi′=hi−mh

    For t: A gives 17 − 22 = -5 °C, B gives 23 − 22 = 1 °C, C gives 26 − 22 = 4 °C. For h: A gives 57 − 50 = 7 %, B gives 45 − 50 = -5 %, C gives 48 − 50 = -2 %.

    The centered points are A′ (-5 ; 7), B′ (1 ; -5) and C′ (4 ; -2). The center G is now at the origin, as in the second diagram.

  4. Check: the sum of deviations is 0

    Correct centering leaves a mean of 0 in each column. This is no coincidence: by definition, n times the mean is the sum of the values.

    ∑i=1nti′=∑i=1nti−nmt=0

    For t: we add the centered values in two stages.

    -5+1=-4 ; -4+4=0 °C

    Repeat the same check for h; you should get 0 %. A nonzero result indicates an addition error or an incorrectly calculated mean. Keep this result: it will be used two steps later.

  5. Measure dispersion around the center

    Variance is the mean of the squared deviations from the mean. The centered values are exactly these deviations:

    vt=1n∑i=1n(ti−mt)2

    For t: the squares are 25, 1 and 16, their sum is 42, and:

    vt=25+1+163=423=14 °C²

    For h: the squares are 49, 25 and 4, their sum is 78, and:

    vh=49+25+43=783=26 %²

    These variances are the same before and after centering: centering changes only the origin. They still have different units, °C² and %², so comparing 14 with 26 as two numbers of the same kind would be a misinterpretation.

  6. Separate position and spread

    What would happen if we measured the spread of t from the origin by taking the mean of the squares of the raw values? The squares are 289, 529 and 676, their sum is 1494, and their mean is 498 °C². This value is different from the variance 14 °C². To see why, write each value as t′ + mt and expand the square:

    ∑i=1nti2=∑i=1n(ti′+mt)2=∑i=1nti′2+2mt∑i=1nti′+nmt2

    The middle term contains the sum of deviations, which is 0 according to the previous step. This leaves the following decomposition:

    1n∑i=1nti2=mt2+vt
    289+529+6763=498 °C² ; 222+14=484+14=498 °C²

    Of 498 °C², the part 484 °C² comes from the position of the center, and the part 14 °C² comes only from the spread. Centering removes the first part and leaves the second.

  7. Recognize the limits of the calculation

    Centering does not make the columns comparable: the units and scales remain, and dividing each column by its standard deviation is a separate operation that this unit does not cover. Centering is also insufficient to analyze the point cloud: how t and h vary together will be the subject of covariance in C137·M2.

    The means we subtract are those of these 3 readings. For a new reading, we reuse these same means (see the exercise on the additional reading). Finally, this calculation describes the supplied data; it says nothing about a real room and does not choose a variance convention for you.

Explain it in your own words

Explain in your own words why the sum of deviations from the mean is 0, then why this property makes the middle term disappear in the decomposition of the mean of squares. Which part of 498 °C² does centering remove?

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “Centering brings values to the same scale or makes them comparable.”

    Centering only subtracts a constant from each column: the origin moves, while differences between readings and units remain. In the example, the variance of t is still 14 °C² and that of h is still 26 %². Changing the scale is a separate operation, which is not covered here.

    Review the step on dispersion around the center

  • “For a new reading, I recalculate a mean and subtract it.”

    The mean belongs to the reference data and is kept in memory. In the exercise, the additional reading becomes (2 ; -9) with the means 22 and 50. If centered using its own value, it would be 0 everywhere and would lose its position relative to the point cloud.

    Redo the exercise on the additional reading

  • “The mean of squares around the origin already measures spread.”

    It combines the position of the center and the spread: 498 = 484 + 14 in °C². Only the part 14 describes spread; the rest depends on the choice of origin, as the offset exercise showed.

    Return to the image of the point cloud far from the origin

Exercise 1 · Explained application

One more reading

The fictional sensor records a fourth reading D: 24 °C and 41 %. Give its centered position using the means from the example, then explain why it should not be centered using its own value.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Which means were used to center A, B and C? They are kept for D.

Hint 2

Subtract the mean of t from the temperature, and the mean of h from the humidity, keeping the sign.

Compare with the solution

Temperature: 24 − 22 = 2 °C. Humidity: 41 − 50 = -9 %. The centered reading is therefore D′ (2 ; -9).

If D were centered using its own value, both its coordinates would be 0: its position relative to A′, B′ and C′ would be lost. If we recalculated the means using all four readings, the first three centered points would also change, and the coordinate system would no longer be the one in the diagrams.

Check your reasoning

  • The means from the example are reused without recalculation.
  • Each coordinate keeps its unit and sign.
  • The reason for rejecting centering a reading using its own value is given: its relative position would be lost.

Exercise 2 · Guided transfer

A thermometer with an offset

Fictional variation: the thermometer adds 10 °C to every temperature (a fault invented for the exercise), while humidity is unchanged. For column t, give the new mean, the centered values, the variance, then the mean of squares around the origin. What changes and what stays the same?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Adding a constant to every value adds the same constant to the mean.

Hint 2

For the last question, use the decomposition into position plus spread, with the new mean.

Compare with the solution

The new mean is 22 + 10 = 32 °C. For A, the deviation becomes (17 + 10) − 32 = -5: the deviations, and therefore the three centered values -5, 1 and 4, are identical to those in the example, and the variance is still 14 °C².

However, the mean of squares around the origin becomes 32² + 14 = 1024 + 14 = 1038 °C², instead of 498 °C². Centering has absorbed the offset; the origin has not.

Check your reasoning

  • The mean changes by exactly the added constant.
  • The finding is stated: centered values and variance are unchanged.
  • The decomposition into position plus spread explains the new number, with its units.

Exercise 3 · Written reasoning

Predict without recalculating

Without squaring the raw humidity values, predict the mean of their squares from mₕ and vₕ. Then check on paper using the three raw values, and explain why the prediction works using the sum of deviations.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Reuse the decomposition from the step on position and spread, replacing t with h.

Hint 2

For the check, square each raw humidity value, add the results, then divide by n.

Compare with the solution

The square of the mean is 50² = 2500 %², the variance is 26 %², so the prediction is 2500 + 26 = 2526 %².

Check: squaring 57, 45 and 48, adding the results, then dividing by 3 gives 2526 %² again. The formula works because, when h is written as h′ + mh, the cross term contains the sum of deviations, which is 0 by construction. Here position contributes 2500 compared with 26 for spread: measured from the origin, dispersion would mainly describe where the center is.

Check your reasoning

  • The prediction uses the square of the mean plus the variance, without recalculating the raw squares.
  • The units are %² and the manual check is attempted.
  • The explanation cites the fact that deviations sum to 0.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does centering a column of readings do?

It subtracts the column’s mean from each value: the mean becomes 0, differences and units are preserved, and only the origin changes.

Recall 2Which mean should be subtracted from a new reading?

The mean of the reference data, kept in memory; do not recalculate a mean using the new reading alone.

Recall 3What is the mean of squares around the origin in terms of the mean and variance?

The square of the mean plus the variance: the cross term disappears because the sum of deviations from the mean is 0.

Recall 4Does centering make columns with different units comparable?

No: it keeps units and scales. It only removes the position of the point cloud relative to the origin.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Reconstruct: a table of readings, one column at a time; the column’s mean; subtraction of this mean from each value; a check that the deviations sum to 0; unchanged variance, because it does not depend on the origin; the mean of squares around the origin equals the square of the mean plus the variance; a new reading centered using the stored means; limits: units, scales and stated assumptions.
