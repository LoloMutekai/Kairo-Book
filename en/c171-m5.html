Level 3 · C171·M5

Multidimensional case

Your goalApply Ito's formula to a function of two processes driven by correlated Brownian motions, identify the covariation term and use it to obtain an exact mean, with its assumptions and units.

Before you start
c170-m5 : strong Markov property · c057-m5 : Hessian · c165-m5 : duality · c171-m1 : adapted integrands · c171-m2 : isometry · c171-m3 : Ito integral · c171-m4 : Ito's formula

✿01 See the idea

Imagine a fictitious rectangle whose two sides fluctuate over time. Side X starts at 2 u and side Y at 3 u, where u is an arbitrary unit of length. The area X × Y therefore starts at 6 u². Both sides are shaken by Brownian noise, but these noises are not independent: some of the disturbance is shared, like a wave lifting two nearby floats. All values in this scene are fictitious; they are used for calculation, not to describe a real system.

With a single process, Ito's formula adds a second order correction to the usual derivative, because the square of a small Brownian increment equals its duration on average. With two processes, we also need to know the value of the product of two different small increments. If they move together, this product is nonzero: on average it equals ρ times the duration, where ρ is the correlation of the two noises.

The area diagram shows what changes. A small change in X and Y adds three pieces: the strip Y dX, the strip X dY and a small corner dX dY. In ordinary calculus, the corner is negligible compared with the strips. In Ito calculus, it is not: its mean is proportional to the duration, so it accumulates as a drift in the area, even though neither X nor Y has any drift of its own.

This corner is exactly what the multidimensional case adds to the case of a single variable. We keep the same example throughout: the mean of the area X × Y after 2 s.

The rule to remember

For dX = σ₁ dW₁ and dY = σ₂ dW₂, the products of increments are dW₁ dW₁ = dt, dW₂ dW₂ = dt, dW₁ dW₂ = ρ dt, and every product containing dt is 0. The second order term in Ito's formula is half the trace of the product of Σ and the Hessian of f, multiplied by dt.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

The mean area of a rectangle with correlated sides

Two fictitious processes, X(t) = x₀ + σ₁ W₁(t) and Y(t) = y₀ + σ₂ W₂(t), start at x₀ = 2 u and y₀ = 3 u. Their amplitudes are σ₁ = 2 u/√s and σ₂ = 5 u/√s, the noise correlation is ρ = 0.6 and the duration is T = 2 s. Let us calculate the exact mean of the area A(T) = X(T) Y(T), then check it by two other methods.

  1. Set out the model and its assumptions

    W1 and W2 are two standard Brownian motions defined with the same filtration (Ft), meaning the same accumulated information. Their correlation ρ is a constant strictly between −1 and 1, and E[W1(t) W2(t)] = ρ t. Time t is in seconds; W(t) has standard deviation √t, so σ1 and σ2 are expressed in u/√s.

    X(t)=x0+σ1W1(t)
    Y(t)=y0+σ2W2(t)

    To construct such a pair, take a second Brownian motion B, independent of W1, and set:

    W2(t)=ρW1(t)+βB(t), avec β=1−ρ2

    The variances add: ρ² t + β² t = t, so W2 is indeed a standard Brownian motion, and E[W1 W2] = ρ t. Here β equals 0.8, because 0.8² + 0.6² = 1.

  2. Read the products of increments

    Over a short interval of duration dt, the increments dW1 and dW2 have size of order √dt. Their cross product equals ρ dt on average. Rigorously, this is the covariation ⟨W1, W2⟩, the limit of sums of products of increments over increasingly fine partitions, like the quadratic variation ⟨W1⟩(t) = t of a single Brownian motion.

    ⟨W1,W2⟩(t)=ρt

    The resulting calculation rules can be summarized as follows, with products containing dt equal to 0.

    dW1dW1=dtdW2dW2=dtdW1dW2=ρdtdtdW1=dtdW2=dtdt=0

    Since dX = σ1 dW1 and dY = σ2 dW2, the covariation of X and Y is dX dY = σ1 σ2 ρ dt = 6 dt, with dt in seconds and a result in u². The instantaneous covariances are arranged in the symmetric matrix Σ, with unit u²/s:

    Σ=[σ12σ1σ2ρσ1σ2ρσ22]=[46625] u²/s

  3. Write Ito's formula for two variables

    Let f(x, y) be a function of class C², with no explicit time dependence. Write f_x, f_y for its first partial derivatives and f_xx, f_xy, f_yy for its second partial derivatives. With a single variable, the second order term was half of f″ times dX dX. With two variables, we must keep the three possible products, with the cross product counted twice because f_xy = f_yx:

    df(X,Y)=fxdX+fydY+12(fxxdXdX+2fxydXdY+fyydYdY)

    The trace of a square matrix is the sum of its diagonal entries. If H_f denotes the Hessian of f, meaning the matrix of second derivatives, the sum of the three terms can be written using the matrix Σ from the previous step:

    df=fxdX+fydY+12tr(ΣHf)dt

  4. Apply the formula to the area

    For f(x, y) = x y, we have f_x = y, f_y = x, f_xx = f_yy = 0 and f_xy = 1. The Hessian therefore has two nonzero entries, off the diagonal:

    Hf=[0110] pour f(x,y)=xy

    The product ΣH_f has diagonal entries σ1σ2ρ and σ1σ2ρ, giving the second order term. The differential of the product of X and Y is therefore:

    d(XY)=YdX+XdY+σ1σ2ρdt

    Value of the second order term, in u² per second:

    tr(ΣHf)=6+6=12 u²/s,1212=6 u²/s

  5. Integrate and take the mean

    Integrating from 0 to T and replacing dX by σ1 dW1 and dY by σ2 dW2 gives:

    A(T)=x0y0+∫0Tσ1YdW1+∫0Tσ2XdW2+σ1σ2ρT

    The two stochastic integrals have adapted, continuous integrands. Since X(s) and Y(s) are Gaussian, E[Y(s)²] = y0² + σ2² s and E[X(s)²] = x0² + σ1² s: these expectations are bounded on [0, T], so the integrands are square integrable. The isometry and martingale property of the Ito integral then give each integral 0 mean. The deterministic term in dt remains:

    E[A(T)]=x0y0+σ1σ2ρT=6+6×2=18 u²

  6. Check by two other methods

    First method, without Ito's formula: expand A(T) = x0y0 + x0σ2W2(T) + y0σ1W1(T) + σ1σ2 W1(T) W2(T). The two linear terms have 0 mean. This leaves σ1σ2 E[W1(T) W2(T)], with E[W1(T) W2(T)] = ρ T.

    E[W1(T)W2(T)]=ρT=0.6×2=1.2 s,10×1.2=12 u²

    Second method, using a walk with 2 steps of 1 s. At each step, two signs ξ and η equal −1 or 1; they have the same sign with probability (1 + ρ)/2 = 0.8 and opposite signs with probability 0.2. Over 2 steps, the sums S_ξ and S_η play the role of W1(T) and W2(T):

    E[ξη]=0.8−0.2=0.6, puis E[SξSη]=2×0.6=1.2 s

    All three methods give 18 u² for the mean area. This result is a mean under the fictitious model; it does not describe any particular path, and the area is signed, because the Gaussian sides can become negative.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The noises are correlated, but dW₁ dW₂ is still 0, like dt dW.”

    Only products containing dt are 0. The product dW₁ dW₂ equals ρ dt: it is a covariation, not a negligible remainder. It vanishes only if ρ is 0. Review the table of products of increments.

    Review the diagram showing the table of products

  • “For f(x, y) = x y, the second derivatives in x and y are 0: there is no Ito correction.”

    The pure derivatives f_xx and f_yy are 0, but the mixed derivative f_xy equals 1 and multiplies dX dY. The trace of ΣH_f adds these two mixed contributions. Without this term, the mean area would stay at its initial value.

    Reread the step that applies the formula to the area

  • “The variance of a W₁(T) + b W₂(T) equals (a² + b²) T, even when the noises are correlated.”

    The covariances must be included: the variance equals (a² + 2 a b ρ + b²) T. The cross term 2 a b ρ vanishes only if ρ is 0. The second exercise applies this calculation to a difference.

Exercise 1 · Explained application

A correlation with the opposite sign

Keep x₀ = 2 u, y₀ = 3 u, σ₁ = 2 u/√s, σ₂ = 5 u/√s and T = 2 s, but take ρ = -0.6. Calculate the mean of the area X(T) Y(T). What does it become when ρ equals 0?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Only ρ changes: the second order contribution in the example is σ₁ σ₂ ρ T.

Hint 2

First calculate σ₁ σ₂ ρ in u² per second, then multiply by the duration and add x₀ y₀.

Compare with the solution

The product of the amplitudes is 10. With ρ = -0.6, the instantaneous covariation is -6 u²/s. Over 2 s, it contributes -6 × 2 = -12 u². The mean area is therefore 6 + (-12) = -6 u².

For ρ = 0, the contribution is 0 and the mean remains 6 u², the initial value: without covariation, the corner dX dY has 0 mean.

Check using the walk from the example: the steps have the same sign with probability (1 + ρ)/2 = 0.2. They have opposite signs more often, E[ξ η] = -0.6, and exact enumeration gives -6 u² again.

Check your reasoning

  • Use negative ρ in the product σ₁ σ₂ ρ, keeping its sign.
  • Keep the unit u² for the contribution and the mean.
  • Recognize that 0 ρ removes the covariation contribution.

Exercise 2 · Guided transfer

Variance of a difference of sides

With ρ = 0.6 and T = 2 s, calculate the variance of X(T) − Y(T). Give the unit, then check the result by replacing W₂ with ρ W₁ + β B.

Var(X(T)−Y(T))=Tv⊤Σv, avec v=[1−1]
Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The constant x₀ − y₀ adds no variance: this leaves σ₁ W₁(T) − σ₂ W₂(T).

Hint 2

Expand the variance using the covariation: variances σ² T for the two terms, and a cross term with a minus sign.

Hint 3

For the check, group the coefficients of the independent W₁ and B, then add their squares.

Compare with the solution

By bilinearity of covariance, the variance equals (σ1² + σ2² − 2 σ1 σ2 ρ) T. Per second, this gives 4 + 25 − 12 = 17 u²/s. Over 2 s, the variance is 17 × 2 = 34 u².

Check: with W2 = ρ W1 + β B, the fluctuating difference becomes (σ1 − σ2 ρ) W1 − σ2 β B. The first coefficient is -1, and the second has absolute value 4. Their squares are 1 and 16, with sum 17 u²/s, again giving 34 u² over 2 s.

Check your reasoning

  • Include the cross term with its minus sign.
  • Obtain the same variance through two different decompositions.
  • State the unit u², the unit of the variance of a length.

Exercise 3 · Transfer

Another function of the two sides

For f(x, y) = x² y and the data from the example (ρ = 0.6), calculate E[X(T)² Y(T)] using Ito's formula for two variables. Explain what makes the mean of the stochastic integrals 0.

Hf=[2y2x2x0] pour f(x,y)=x2y
Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The second derivatives are f_xx = 2 y, f_xy = 2 x and f_yy = 0.

Hint 2

The second order term is y σ₁² + 2 x σ₁ σ₂ ρ: it depends on X and Y.

Hint 3

Take the mean: E[X(t)] = x₀ and E[Y(t)] = y₀, because dX and dY have no drift, and the second order term is affine in X and Y.

Compare with the solution

At the initial time, the second order term is 3 × 4 + 2 × 2 × 6 = 12 + 24 = 36, in u³/s. Since it is affine in (X, Y) and X and Y keep their means x0 and y0, its mean remains 36 at every time.

The integrands of the stochastic integrals are 2 X Y σ1 and X² σ2. They are polynomials in Gaussian variables, square integrable on [0, T], so these Ito integrals have 0 mean. This leaves E[X(T)² Y(T)] = x0² y0 + 36 × T = 12 + 72 = 84 u³.

Check: expanding (x0 + σ1 W1)² (y0 + σ2 W2) and taking the mean, with the third order moments of a centered Gaussian pair equal to 0, gives x0² y0 + T (y0 σ1² + 2 x0 σ1 σ2 ρ), the same expression.

Check your reasoning

  • Find f_xx and f_xy, then f_yy equal to 0.
  • Apply the factor 2 only to the cross term.
  • Justify calculating the mean of the second order term using the means of X and Y.
  • State the unit u³ of the result.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What is the product dW₁ dW₂ for two Brownian motions with correlation ρ?

It equals ρ dt. Only products containing dt are 0; dW₁ dW₁ equals dt.

Recall 2What second order term does Ito's formula add for a function of two processes?

Half the trace of the product of Σ and the Hessian of f, multiplied by dt, where Σ contains the instantaneous covariances of X and Y.

Recall 3What does the matrix Σ in the example contain, and what is its unit?

On the diagonal, σ₁² and σ₂²; off the diagonal, σ₁ σ₂ ρ. Its unit is u²/s, because Σ dt is a covariance of lengths.

Recall 4Why do the stochastic integrals disappear from the mean area?

Their integrands are adapted and square integrable: the Ito integral then has 0 mean. The deterministic integral in dt remains.

Recall 5What does the mean calculated in the example fail to prove?

It holds under a fictitious model; it describes neither a path nor a real system, and the area is signed because the sides can become negative.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Reconstruct without support: set out the two processes, their noises and the correlation ρ; read the products of increments, with ρ dt for the cross product and 0 for every product containing dt; arrange the covariances in Σ; apply Ito's formula with the Hessian; use the assumptions on the Ito integrals to show that their means are 0; conclude with the units, then check by another method.
