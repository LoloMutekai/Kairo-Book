Level 0 · C010·M1

Exponents

Your goalRead a power as repeated factors, calculate a zoom, and explain 0 and negative exponents.

Before you start
c006-m5 : composition

✿01 See the idea

A square photo has a side length of 4 cm in a fictional mockup. The zoom button doubles its displayed side length with each click. After the first click, the side length is 8 cm; at the next click, you double this new length. You do not start again from the original photo each time.

Before reading on, imagine 3 clicks. Would you write 2 × 3, or a product containing 3 factors equal to 2? A factor is a number multiplied by another number. Here, each click contributes the same factor 2, applied to the previous result.

A power is a short way to write this product. Its base gives the repeated factor; its exponent, the small number at the top, counts the factors when the exponent is a positive integer. The zoom factor has no unit. The side length of the photo is expressed in centimetres, written cm.

With no clicks, the photo stays at its original size. A reverse click divides the side length by 2; it reduces a positive length. We are describing an ideal zoom, with no screen limits or rounding. The stated dimensions belong to the mockup; they are not measurements of your screen.

The rule to remember

Count the factors, calculate their product, then multiply the initial length by it. To go back, divide by the factor you applied.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Count the doublings and learn to undo them

With an initial side length of 4 cm and a factor of 2 per click, let us calculate the side length after 3 clicks. We will then compare no clicks, reverse clicks and groups of clicks, all from this same starting point.

  1. Name what we are counting

    L denotes the initial side length, here 4 cm; ℓ denotes the resulting side length, also in cm. The letter a denotes a strictly positive base with no unit, here 2. The letter n is an integer with no unit: positive for zoom clicks, 0 for no clicks, and negative for reverse clicks. The notation aⁿ is read as “a to the power n” and denotes the total factor.

    In ℓ = L × aⁿ, the power applies only to a. The centimetre belongs to L, not to the base. We will use a positive base and integer exponents; fractional exponents and roots are not used here.

    ℓ=L×an

  2. Expand before calculating

    For a positive integer n, aⁿ is the product of n factors a. The original photo is already there before any clicks: we multiply its side length by 2 with each click. The exponent 3 therefore counts 3 factors 2, without being a factor itself.

    23=2×2×2=4×2=8

    The multiplication 2 × 3 would give 6: that is a different operation. In the repeated product, we multiply from left to right and keep each result for the next multiplication.

  3. Apply the factor and check by reversing the steps

    The factor is 8, with no unit. We still need to multiply it by the initial side length to give an answer in centimetres.

    ℓ=4×8=32 cm

    Another route: 4 → 8 → 16 → 32 cm, doubling at each arrow. Reverse check: 32 ÷ 2 = 16, then 16 ÷ 2 = 8, then 8 ÷ 2 = 4 cm. We return exactly to the starting length.

    The incorrect calculation would give 4 × 6 = 24 cm. The difference is 32 − 24 = 8 cm, for these fictional data only.

  4. Understand the 0 exponent

    With no clicks, no transformation is applied: the side length stays at 4 cm. The factor that preserves a length is 1, because multiplying by 1 does not change it. This is the meaning of a to the power 0 for a positive base.

    a0=1;20=1

    We can check this by applying and then undoing a block of 2 clicks. The net count is 2 − 2 = 0. The net factor is 4 ÷ 4 = 1; the side length is 4 × 1 = 4 cm. A 0 base is excluded from our domain: this reasoning assigns no convention to it.

  5. Read the negative sign as a reciprocal

    For a positive integer n, a to the power −n denotes the reciprocal of aⁿ. The reciprocal of a nonzero number is the number that gives 1 when multiplied by it. Here, the sign of the exponent indicates reverse clicks; it does not put a minus sign in front of the length.

    a−n=1an;2−2=14=0.25

    From the starting point, 2 reverse clicks give 4 ÷ 2 = 2 cm, then 2 ÷ 2 = 1 cm. The direct calculation also gives 4 × 1/4 = 1 cm. Check: 1 × 4 = 4 cm. A negative exponent therefore does not count a negative number of factors: it asks us to undo a product.

  6. Put clicks in sequence: add the exponents

    The letters p and q denote integer exponents with no unit, for the same base a. When sequences of clicks follow one another, their counts add and their factors multiply. For positive exponents, we simply combine the written factors; reciprocals extend the same rule to negative exponents.

    ap×aq=ap+q

    With p = 2 and q = 3, the count is 2 + 3 = 5 clicks; the factor is 4 × 8 = 32. The side length is 4 × 32 = 128 cm. Check using lengths: after the first block, 16 cm; after the next, 16 × 8 = 128 cm. This law applies to the same base and a product, not to a sum of powers.

  7. Undo a sequence: subtract the exponents

    Dividing by a to the power q means applying the reciprocal factor a to the power −q. The count becomes p − q; division is allowed because a is positive and its powers are nonzero.

    apaq=ap−q

    After 5 clicks, undo the last 3. This leaves 5 − 3 = 2 clicks. The remaining factor is 32 ÷ 8 = 4 and the side length is 128 ÷ 8 = 16 cm. We recover the side length obtained after the first block: that is our check. If we undo more clicks than we have applied, the count becomes negative and the reciprocal rule applies.

  8. Repeat a block: multiply the exponents

    Repeating a block of p clicks q times, for positive p and q, gives p × q clicks. The parentheses indicate that the outer power applies to the factor for the whole block. With the definitions of the identity factor and the reciprocal, the law remains valid for integer p and q and positive a.

    (ap)q=ap×q

    A block of 2 clicks has the factor 4. Repeated 3 times, it gives 2 × 3 = 6 clicks; its factor becomes 4 × 4 × 4 = 16 × 4 = 64. The side length is 4 × 64 = 256 cm. Check: 256 ÷ 4 ÷ 4 ÷ 4 gives 4 cm again. Repeating a block and putting blocks of different sizes in sequence are distinct operations.

Explain it in your own words

Cover the calculations. Explain why the exponent counts factors for ordinary clicks, why no clicks preserve the length, and why reverse clicks keep the length positive. Then reconstruct the grouping rules by counting clicks.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I multiply the base by the number of clicks.”

    Write one factor 2 per click, then multiply. For 3 clicks, the product is 8, whereas 2 × 3 is 6. Only then bring in the initial side length: 4 × 8 = 32 cm.

    Return to “Expand before calculating” in the example

  • “The 0 exponent erases the photo.”

    With no clicks, the length must stay at 4 cm. We must therefore multiply by 1, not by 0. To check, apply a block of clicks and then undo it: dividing the nonzero factor by itself gives 1.

    Find the identity factor again in the example

  • “A negative exponent gives a negative length.”

    The negative sign of the exponent calls for a reciprocal. With our positive base, 2 reverse clicks multiply by 1/4, or 0.25 with no unit. They give 1 cm. Repeat the divisions in the diagram, then check by multiplying by 4.

Compare with the solution

With no clicks: exponent 0, factor 1, side length 4 cm. Multiplying by 0 would erase the length; that does not describe the absence of a transformation.

  • The negative exponent is explained through the reciprocal; the exact factor comes before its decimal form.

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Draw the clicks in each block, then count all the clicks in each trial.

Hint 2

Putting blocks in sequence adds their counts. Repeating the same block multiplies its count by the number of repetitions.

Hint 3

To undo the last clicks of the first trial, divide its side length by their total factor.

Compare with the solution

The counts differ. First trial: 2 + 3 = 5 clicks, factor 4 × 8 = 32, side length 128 cm. Second trial: 2 × 3 = 6 clicks, factor 4 × 4 × 4 = 64, side length 256 cm.

The second side length is 2 times the first: 256 ÷ 128 = 2. This corresponds to the extra click between the counts 5 and 6 in this scene.

In the first trial, undoing 3 clicks leaves 5 − 3 = 2 clicks. We divide by 8: 128 ÷ 8 = 16 cm. This is the side length after the initial block; this check justifies the minus sign in the quotient law.

Check your reasoning

  • You distinguish the counts 5 and 6 by explaining the grouping.
  • You justify the side lengths 128 and 256 cm from the same initial length.
  • Reversing the steps uses division and recovers 16 cm, without subtracting a number of centimetres.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1In a power with a positive integer exponent, what do the base and exponent tell us?

The base is the repeated factor; the exponent counts its occurrences in the product. For 3 clicks with a factor of 2, the product 2 × 2 × 2 is 8, with no unit. The initial side length is then multiplied by this factor.

Recall 2Why does a positive base to the power 0 equal 1?

No clicks preserve the length: the identity factor is 1. Applying and then undoing the same block also gives its factor divided by itself, so 1. A 0 base is outside the chosen domain.

Recall 3How can we explain a negative exponent without referring to a negative length?

For a positive base a and a positive integer n, a to the power −n equals the reciprocal of a to the power n. Here, 2 reverse clicks give the factor 1/4, or 0.25 with no unit, and reduce 4 cm to 1 cm.

Recall 4For the same positive base, how do we handle a product and a quotient of powers? Justify using clicks.

For integer exponents p and q, the product of a to the power p and a to the power q equals a to the power (p + q): the clicks follow one another. Their quotient equals a to the power (p − q): we undo the clicks in the denominator. The positive base avoids any division by a 0 factor.

Recall 5Why does a power of a power multiply the exponents?

For positive counts, a block of p clicks repeated q times contains p × q clicks. Raising (a to the power p) to the power q therefore gives a to the power (p × q). With a positive base, identity factors and reciprocals extend this law to integer exponents. The parentheses mark the block being repeated.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Reconstruct without reading the answers: start from the initial side length, write the product that corresponds to the clicks, then find the identity factor and the reciprocal factor by undoing this product. Finally, explain why putting blocks in sequence, undoing a block and repeating a block lead respectively to adding, subtracting and multiplying the exponents; finish each side-length calculation with a unit and a reverse check.
