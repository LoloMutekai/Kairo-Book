Level 4 · C244·M1

Initialization

Your goalConstruct an SVI starting point from wings and a central value, then check what this starting point actually supports.

Before you start
c243-m5 : SSVI · c051-m5 : error diagnostics · c220-m5 : numerical reproducibility · c198-m5 : independent validation

✿01 See the idea

An analyst receives a slice of implied variances for a single expiry. She wants to position an SVI curve before asking an algorithm to fit it. Starting from arbitrary numbers forces the algorithm to recover the size, position and tilt of the slice as well. A constructed starting point already gives it these reference values.

Look at the wings, meaning the left and right ends of the plot. Imagine extending each with a straight line. Their opening gives a slope scale; the difference in their tilt gives an asymmetry; their intersection provides a horizontal and vertical reference point. A height taken at the center then sets the rounding between these wings.

The points are fictional, chosen to allow an exact calculation. The straight lines will be estimates obtained from finite points: we will not claim to have observed the asymptotes at infinity. The course constructs a starting point for a slice; it does not carry out the full calibration or the checks for absence of arbitrage.

In the figures, x is the scaled logarithmic horizontal coordinate and y is the scaled total variance. Their definitions and scales follow in the example. The lines in the first diagram explain the construction; they do not represent additional quotes.

The rule to remember

Estimate the wings, recover their parameters, match a central value, then check the candidate before any optimization.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Prepare an SVI slice before optimizing it

The fictional dataset is given in the x and y coordinates below. Choose the outer points in pairs to estimate the wings. Keep the central value to construct the rounding and another point to check the starting point. No real data or market performance is being measured.

  1. Define the coordinates and data

    For a fixed expiry, T is the remaining time in years, K is the option strike price and F is the forward price for the same expiry, both in the same currency unit. T, K and F are strictly positive. k = ln(K/F) is their logarithmic ratio, with no unit; ln denotes the natural logarithm. Implied volatility ν is expressed in inverse square roots of a year; total variance w is the product of T and the square of ν and is therefore dimensionless. We receive w directly, without recalculating an option price.

    To work with integers, we use x = 10k and y = 1000w. One y unit represents 1/1000 of total variance and one x unit represents 1/10 of k. The coordinates remain dimensionless; “x unit” and “y unit” name their plotting scales.

    xy observé-592-256140444768

    The first two rows are used for the left wing, the last two for the right wing. The central row is an observed reference point, rather than an assumed minimum. An additional point from the same slice, (2.5 ; 43), will be reserved for checking and is not used to set the parameters.

  2. Write the model and state the assumptions

    The raw SVI form uses the 5 parameters (a, b, ρ, m, s). a sets a level, b a slope scale, ρ the asymmetry, m a horizontal shift and s a positive width. The letter s replaces the sigma sometimes used for this width: it does not denote volatility ν. All these parameters are dimensionless in the coordinate k.

    w(k)=a+b[ρ(k−m)+(k−m)2+s2]

    In the scaled plot, write A = 1000a, B = 100b, M = 10m and S = 10s. A is in y units, B in y units per x unit, M and S in x units; ρ does not change. The factor for B comes from 1000/10 = 100: scaling both axes does not multiply the slope by the same factor as the height.

    y(x)=A+B[ρ(x−M)+(x−M)2+S2]

    We consider the case where the estimated slopes are nonzero and have opposite signs, B is strictly positive, |ρ| is less than 1 and S is strictly positive. The lines approximate the wings; their parameters are not already optimized results. The SVI form is reviewed in the work of Gatheral and Jacquier; reconstruction from the wings is discussed by LeFloc’h. Matching the value y(M), as done here, is a teaching choice that completes the construction using the model, distinct from his approach of matching the minimum.

  3. Estimate the slopes in a common direction

    p_g and p_d denote the left and right slopes, in y units per x unit. In each pair, we move toward increasing x. A difference in vertical coordinates divided by a difference in horizontal coordinates measures the change in height per horizontal unit.

    pg=56−92-2−(-5)=-363=-12
    pd=68−447−4=243=8

    The left side goes down: its sign is negative. The right side goes up: its sign is positive. This check of direction prevents replacing the left slope with its absolute value. The finite points provide secants; calling them “wings” does not turn them into exact limits.

  4. Find the intersection of the lines

    Write the lines as y = p_g x + q_g and y = p_d x + q_d; q_g and q_d are their vertical coordinates at x = 0, in y units. Using a point on each line gives q = y − px.

    On the left: q_g = 56 − (-12 × -2) = 56 − 24 = 32 y units. On the right: q_d = 44 − 8 × 4 = 44 − 32 = 12 y units.

    At the intersection, the two expressions for y are equal. Rearranging the terms gives (p_d − p_g)M = q_g − q_d. The slope difference is 20 y units per x unit and the difference in vertical intercepts is 20 y units.

    M=qg−qdpd−pg=2020=1

    The intersection height is A = q_g + p_g M = 32 + (-12) = 20 y units. An independent check using the other line gives 12 + 8 × 1 = 20 y units. This intersection corresponds to parameters M and A of the candidate’s asymptotes, rather than its value y(M).

  5. Separate opening and asymmetry

    Far from the center, the square root approaches |x − M|. On the left, it behaves like −(x − M); on the right, like x − M. The model’s asymptotic slopes are therefore B(ρ − 1) and B(ρ + 1). Their difference eliminates ρ; their sum eliminates the opposite terms.

    B=pd−pg2=202=10
    ρ=pd+pgpd−pg=-420=-0.2

    B is 10 y units per x unit; ρ is -1/5 with no unit. To check in a way other than through the fractions, reconstruct the wings: 10 × (-0.2 − 1) = -12 and 10 × (-0.2 + 1) = 8. The signs and initial values are recovered.

  6. Match the value at the reference coordinate M

    The wings have not fixed S: changing the rounding does not change their asymptotes. Here M coincides with the observed central horizontal coordinate 1, whose height is 40 y units. Replacing x with M makes the term ρ(x − M) vanish, and the square root equals S, since S is positive.

    y(M)=A+BS⇒S=y(M)−AB=40−2010=2

    The height to add is 20 y units. Dividing by B converts this height into a width: S = 2 x units. Check: 20 + 10 × 2 = 40 y units. If the height were 0 or negative, this rule would not provide the strictly positive S assumed here; the starting point would need to be reconsidered.

    M is not the location of the curve’s minimum when ρ is nonzero. Our matching step did not use an observed minimum. If the intersection fell between observed horizontal coordinates, we would need to state how y(M) was estimated and check that estimate; our dataset avoids this additional step.

  7. Return the parameters in the raw coordinates

    The initial vector in the scaled coordinates is (A, B, ρ, M, S) = (20, 10, -0.2, 1, 2). The scale changes must be undone before supplying a raw form in k to another program.

    a = 20/1000 = 0.020 ; b = 10/100 = 0.100 ; m = 1/10 = 0.100 ; s = 2/10 = 0.200. The parameter ρ remains -0.2. All are dimensionless; a and b relate to total variance, m and s to k.

    The values are finite, b and s are positive and |ρ| is less than 1. Also, a is positive here, which keeps this variance curve positive. These elementary checks do not replace the financial constraints: the next unit will study calibration feasibility.

  8. Evaluate the starting point and retain its limitations

    At the reserved point x = 2.5, the distance from the reference coordinate is δ = x − M = 1.5 x units. The letter δ denotes only this difference. Its square is 9/4, S squared is 4; the expression under the square root is therefore 25/4 squared x units. The positive square root is 2.5 x units, since 5/2 × 5/2 = 25/4.

    y(2.5)=20+10[-0.2×1.5+2.5]=42

    The asymmetric term is -0.3 x units; the expression in brackets is 2.2 x units; its contribution to y is 22 y units. The observed point is 43 y units: the residual, defined as model minus observation, is -1 y unit. In raw total variance: 0.042 − 0.043 = -0.001, dimensionless.

    The check recovers the central value by construction, but leaves this difference at the reserved point. It does not measure performance outside the sample: this point belongs to the same fictional slice. We have neither minimized an error over all points nor proved a global optimum, meaning the smallest error among all allowed parameters. Keeping the dataset, scales, wing rule and vector makes it possible to reproduce this starting point.

    A local initialization depends on the wings selected. If their slope difference is very small, the quotients defining M and ρ become sensitive to small data errors. If it is 0, the intersection formula no longer applies. Preparing other documented starting points can explore this dependence; their comparison must keep the same error function, constraints and stopping criteria. This still does not certify a global optimum.

Explain it in your own words

Without looking at the formulas, explain what each part of the dataset fixed. Why is A different from y(M)? Why is recovering y(M) insufficient to declare the calibration successful?

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The left slope is the size of the drop, so it is positive.”

    Calculate both differences in the same direction, toward increasing x. On the left, y decreases while x increases: p_g is -12, including its sign. This sign is needed to recover B and ρ.

    Review the slopes and their reconstruction

  • “The intersection of the wings gives the observed minimum.”

    The intersection provides M and A. At this horizontal reference, the curve has height A + BS, above the intersection; for nonzero asymmetry, M is not even the horizontal coordinate of its minimum. Use the stated value y(M), without renaming it the minimum.

    Review the distinction between intersection and rounding

  • “My starting point fits the center: the solver will certainly find the right solution.”

    The center is matched by construction. The reserved point leaves a residual of -1 y unit. Optimization, constraint checks and a comparison of sensitivity to starting points remain to be done. An assisted answer or reading these checks does not establish any attained level.

    Audit the conclusion of the last exercise

Exercise 2 · Independent reconstruction

Go from the lines to the vector

Start again from p_g = -12, p_d = 8, q_g = 32 and q_d = 12, in our x and y scales. The value at the central horizontal coordinate 1 is 40. Recover A, B, ρ, M and S, then the raw vector (a, b, ρ, m, s). Check the result by reconstructing the slopes and central value.

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Start with the intersection: set p_g M + q_g equal to p_d M + q_d.

Hint 2

The slope difference gives B; their sum gives Bρ. At x = M, the positive square root equals S.

Hint 3

The vertical conversion factor does not apply in the same way to a slope and a height.

Compare with the solution

The slope difference is 20; the difference in vertical intercepts is 20. Thus M = 20/20 = 1 x unit and A = 32 + -12 × 1 = 20 y units.

B = 20/2 = 10 y units per x unit. The sum of slopes is -4: ρ = -4/20 = -1/5. Finally S = (40 − 20)/10 = 2 x units.

Reverse checks: B(ρ − 1) = -12 ; B(ρ + 1) = 8 ; A + BS = 40. These recover the construction data by another route.

The raw vector is (0.020, 0.100, -0.2, 0.100, 0.200), dimensionless. It describes the same curve after x = 10k and y = 1000w. Dividing B by 1000 instead of 100 would no longer describe the same wings.

Check your reasoning

  • Distinguish the intersection height from the curve’s value at the center.
  • Justify dividing by B in the formula for S and check that S is positive.
  • Reconstruct both slopes before converting the scales.
  • Recover the raw vector without changing ρ and explain the factor for b.

Exercise 3 · Calculation and critical discussion

Assess a starting point without overstating its validity

A report states: “The center is exact, so the global minimum has been obtained; the starting point can be kept for every slice.” Use the reserved point (2.5 ; 43) to answer with a calculated residual. Then explain what would happen to the intersection rule if p_d and p_g were equal in another estimate of the wings from the same dataset. Propose a next step that can be checked.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

At this point, δ is 3/2 and the square root is 5/2. Calculate the expression in brackets before the final height.

Hint 2

Distinguish “we imposed a point” from “we minimized a function.” Also look at the denominator of M.

Compare with the solution

The expression under the square root is 9/4 + 4 = 25/4. Its positive square root is 5/2, checked by squaring it. The expression in brackets is -1/5 × 3/2 + 5/2 = 11/5 ; y = 20 + 10 × 11/5 = 42 y units. The model minus observation residual is 42 − 43 = -1 y unit, or -0.001 of total variance.

This difference refutes the idea of an exact fit to the entire slice; by itself, it does not demonstrate that the vector is not a minimum of a given error function. Residuals can remain when an error function is minimized. Here no minimization has even been performed: the conclusion of a global minimum has no proof.

If p_d = p_g, their difference is 0. The rule then divides by 0; it does not provide a unique intersection, even if the lines coincide. This construction must be suspended and another starting rule documented, rather than silently replacing the denominator.

The next step is to keep the dataset and residual convention, state the error function and constraints, then compare several documented starting points under the same conditions. Convergence, feasibility and sensitivity will need to be assessed separately. The reserved point is an internal check of our slice, rather than validation on an independent market.

Check your reasoning

  • Present the residual with its sign, definition and conversion to total variance.
  • Do not confuse a nonzero residual with proof of a failure to reach an optimum.
  • Identify the impossible division and refuse to hide the degenerate case.
  • Define a comparison of starting points without promising a global optimum.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What do the difference and sum of the wing slopes provide?

The difference p_d − p_g gives 2B; the sum p_d + p_g gives 2Bρ. We can infer B and ρ if the difference is nonzero, preserving the signs of the slopes.

Recall 2What do the coordinates of the intersection of the wings indicate, and what do they not indicate?

M is its horizontal coordinate and A its vertical coordinate. They give neither y(M), which equals A + BS, nor directly the curve’s minimum when ρ is nonzero.

Recall 3What information sets the width S in our construction?

The value y(M) at the intersection’s horizontal reference: S = (y(M) − A)/B. This rule assumes that B is positive and y(M) is greater than A. In our dataset, the horizontal coordinate M is observed; otherwise we would need to state how its value is estimated.

Recall 4Why does the conversion of B differ from that of A?

A is a height; B is a height per horizontal unit. With x = 10k and y = 1000w, A = 1000a but B = 100b. M and S are 10 times m and s; ρ does not change.

Recall 5What remains to be proved after constructing a finite starting point and matching the center?

The actual fit to the dataset, feasibility under the selected constraints and sensitivity to starting points. The central match is imposed rather than optimized. Neither the presence of an algorithm nor reading its formula proves a global optimum or an actual execution.
