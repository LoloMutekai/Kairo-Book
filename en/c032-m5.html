Level 1 · C032·M5

Calculating rank

Your goalCalculate rank by counting pivots after elimination, then distinguish redundancy from contradiction in an inventory.

Before you start
c004-m5 : Rational equations · c027-m5 : Complex numbers · c032-m1 : Elimination · c032-m2 : Matrix multiplication · c032-m3 : Inverse · c032-m4 : Determinant

✿01 See the idea

A workshop prepares red, blue, and green batches. Each batch contains 1 white sheet. It also contains 2 colored sheets if it is red, 1 if it is blue, and 3 if it is green. The batch colors are simply names for the types: they do not describe the color of the sheets.

The inventory reports 12 white sheets, 22 colored sheets, and 34 sheets in all. The last count seems to add information. Yet it already follows from the others: adding white and colored sheets gives the total, whatever the mix of batches.

Rank counts the relations that remain independent, meaning those that cannot be reconstructed by adding multiples of the others. To calculate it, elimination transforms the rows until repeated information appears as 0 rows. We then count the pivots rather than the original counts.

The quantities and batches in this workshop are fictional. We assume exact counts, fixed batch contents, and that each sheet belongs to a single category. We will calculate a rank and interpret its consequence for this inventory.

The rule to remember

Use reversible operations to obtain row echelon form, count the pivots in the columns of the unknowns, then check the right-hand side separately.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

An inventory with a redundant total

How many independent relations do the counts of 12 white sheets, 22 colored sheets, and 34 sheets in all provide? Determine the rank of the coefficients, then what these counts allow you to find out about the batches.

  1. Define the unknowns, units, and table

    x, y, and z are the numbers of red, blue, and green batches, in batches. The coefficients give sheets per batch; the right-hand sides are in sheets. In the matrices, we write their numerical values in these units. A denotes the coefficient matrix and b the column of counts. The system is written as A multiplied by the column (x, y, z) = b.

    The white row gives x + y + z = 12. The colored row gives 2x + y + 3z = 22. The total row gives 3x + 2y + 4z = 34. In [A | b], the bar separates the coefficients from the right-hand side: the latter is not part of A.

    [A│b]=[111│12213│22324│34]

    A has 3 rows and 3 columns. To calculate its rank, we work over the real numbers using exact fractions. The physical meaning will then require nonnegative whole numbers of batches; this constraint does not change the rank of A.

  2. Specify what is being counted

    A row echelon form places the 0 rows at the bottom. In each nonzero row, the first nonzero coefficient is a pivot; it is farther to the right than the pivot in the previous row, and the coefficients below it are 0. The rank r is the number of these pivots. For a matrix with m rows and n columns, r exceeds neither m nor n. m, n, and r are counts with no unit.

    Swapping rows, multiplying a row by a nonzero number, or adding a multiple of another row to a row preserves rank. These operations are reversible: they destroy no relation. In the system, apply the same operation to the right-hand side to preserve the solutions as well.

    If the entry intended for a pivot is 0, look for a nonzero coefficient below it and swap rows. If the entire remaining column is 0, move to the next column without counting a pivot. A pivot does not have to equal 1; it must differ from 0.

  3. Use the white row to eliminate x

    Let B denote the white row, C the colored row, and T the total row. These are row labels. The coefficient of x in B is 1: we keep it as the first pivot. To eliminate the coefficient 2 of x in C, replace C with C − 2B.

    The new row C has coefficients 2 − 2 × 1 = 0, 1 − 2 × 1 = -1, then 3 − 2 × 1 = 1. On the right: 2 × 12 = 24 sheets, so 22 − 24 = -2 sheets. This negative number comes from subtracting counts; it does not represent negative stock.

    For T, replace T with T − 3B. Its coefficients become 0, 2 − 3 = -1, then 4 − 3 = 1. On the right, 3 × 12 = 36, then 34 − 36 = -2 sheets.

    [111│120-11│-20-11│-2]

    The transformed rows C and T are identical. The second pivot, in column y of C, is -1. It does not need to be normalized.

  4. Reveal the row that adds nothing

    Now replace the transformed row T with T − C, using the current rows. The coefficient of y gives -1 − (-1) = 0; that of z gives 1 − 1 = 0; the right-hand side gives -2 − (-2) = 0 sheets.

    [111│120-11│-2000│0]

    The last row means 0 = 0. It imposes no new constraint. The remaining rows are in echelon form and have 2 pivots, in columns x then y. Thus rank(A) = 2. The 3 counts do not provide 3 independent relations.

  5. Check the rank another way

    In the original matrix, T = B + C coefficient by coefficient. The rank therefore cannot reach 3. To check that it does reach 2, take the small square formed by rows B and C and columns x and y. Its determinant is 1 × 1 − 1 × 2 = -1, which differs from 0. These rows therefore provide independent relations.

    This determinant is a minor of A: the determinant of a square submatrix. Here it provides an independent check on the pivot count. The determinant of the whole matrix A is 0, since T = B + C. This means only that the rank is not maximal for this square matrix, not that its rank is 0.

  6. Interpret the consequence without assuming a unique stock

    The right-hand side is consistent with the operations: the last row is a true equality. The system has solutions, but its rank 2 is less than the number 3 of unknowns. Column z has no pivot: we can choose z and then determine y and x, subject to the constraints on the batches.

    Check concretely that (x, y, z) = (4, 5, 3) batches works. White: 4 + 5 + 3 = 12 sheets. Colored: 2 × 4 = 8, 3 × 3 = 9, then 8 + 5 + 9 = 22 sheets. The total is indeed 34 sheets.

    Another stock is (6, 4, 2) batches: white 6 + 4 + 2 = 12 sheets; colored 12 + 4 + 6 = 22 sheets, since 12 = 2 × 6 and 6 = 3 × 2. These distinct stocks both satisfy the physical constraints: the inventory cannot distinguish between them.

    Rank concerns A. Changing only a count in b does not change that rank; it can nevertheless make the system contradictory. The last exercise checks this. With rounded data, deciding that a coefficient is numerically 0 would require a justified tolerance; our exact equalities do not establish that tolerance.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the tables. Reconstruct the operations on C and T, then explain why the total adds no pivot. Why did the right-hand side also have to be transformed before discussing the solutions?

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I see 3 nonzero rows at the start, so the rank is 3.”

    Count after elimination. Here T is the sum of B and C: it becomes 0 after subtraction. Locate the 2 pivots in the row echelon form.

    Review how the total row becomes 0

  • “The determinant is 0, so the rank is 0.”

    For square A, a 0 determinant rules out maximal rank. It does not count the remaining pivots. The small determinant -1 proves here that the rank reaches 2; the relation T = B + C prevents it from being larger.

    Review the check using a minor

  • “A pivot in the right-hand side column increases the rank of A.”

    This column belongs to [A | b]. A row of 0 coefficients with a nonzero right-hand side signals a contradiction. Compare the matrix ranks, specifying which matrix you are counting pivots in.

    Try the contradictory count

Exercise 1 · Reconstruct a step

Complete the elimination

In the original inventory, the transformed rows C and T both equal (0, -1, 1 | -2). Which operation completes the row echelon form? Give the rank of A and that of [A | b].

Hint 1

Subtracting identical rows also cancels their right-hand sides.

Hint 2

Count the pivots that remain, without counting each nonzero coefficient separately.

Compare with the solution

Replace T with T − C. It becomes (0, 0, 0 | 0). B keeps its pivot in x and C its pivot -1 in y. There are 2 pivots left.

A and [A | b] both have rank 2, because the right-hand side creates no additional pivot. The final row expresses 0 = 0: it is redundant.

Exercise 2 · Independent calculation

Replace the total with new information

Keep the white and colored counts. Replace the total with a count of the white sheets from red batches alone: 4 sheets, so x = 4 batches. The new row is (1, 0, 0 | 4). Obtain row echelon form, give the rank of the coefficients, and find the batch quantities.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

After C − 2B, subtract B from the new row, then subtract the transformed row C.

Hint 2

The last row must give a nonzero coefficient of z. Solve it before working back up to y and x.

Compare with the solution

C becomes (0, -1, 1 | -2), as before. The new row minus B becomes (0, -1, -1 | -8), since 4 − 12 = -8.

Subtracting the transformed C gives (0, 0, -2 | -6): for z, -1 − 1 = -2; on the right, -8 − (-2) = -6. The last pivot is nonzero. The rank is 3, equal to the number of unknowns.

The last row gives z = -6 / (-2) = 3 batches. Then −y + z = -2, so y = 3 − (-2) = 5 batches. Finally x = 12 − 5 − 3 = 4 batches. Substitution gives 12 white sheets, 22 colored sheets, and 4 white sheets from red batches. Each unknown has a pivot: the solution is unique.

Check your reasoning

  • Perform the operations on the right-hand sides too.
  • Show the last pivot -2 and justify the rank 3.
  • Find the batch quantities with their units and check the modified counts.

Exercise 3 · Explain a diagnosis

The total contains an error

Start again from the coefficients of the original inventory, keeping 12 white sheets and 22 colored sheets. The total count now reports 35 sheets. Without changing A, determine the ranks of A and [A | b], then decide whether any stock can satisfy all the counts.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The relation between the coefficients remains T = B + C. What does the same subtraction do to the counts?

Hint 2

Repeat T − 3B, then T − the transformed C: the residual on the right is decisive.

Compare with the solution

The matrix A has not changed: rank(A) = 2. After T − 3B, its right-hand side is 35 − 36 = -1 sheets. The transformed C keeps the right-hand side -2.

The last subtraction gives -1 − (-2) = 1 sheet. The final row is (0, 0, 0 | 1). It requires 0 = 1, which is impossible. In the augmented matrix, the right-hand side column has an additional pivot: rank([A | b]) = 3.

No stock satisfies these exact counts. Direct check: the white and colored sheets total 34 sheets, whereas the count reports 35. The discrepancy is 1 sheet. The counts need to be checked; counting more pivots in A would mean switching matrices during the reasoning.

Check your reasoning

  • Keep the rank 2 of A.
  • Calculate the residual 1 and distinguish the rank 3 of the augmented matrix.
  • Conclude that the counts are contradictory under the assumption of exact counting.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1How do you calculate rank without counting the original rows?

Use reversible operations to obtain row echelon form, then count its nonzero pivots. 0 rows do not count.

Recall 2What should you do if the entry intended for a pivot is 0?

Look for a nonzero coefficient below it and swap rows. If the remaining column is entirely 0, move to the next column without counting a pivot.

Recall 3Why does the total row not increase the rank of our inventory?

Its coefficients are the sum of those in the white and colored rows. It becomes 0 after elimination; only 2 pivots remain for 3 unknowns.

Recall 4What does a row of 0 coefficients with a nonzero right-hand side mean?

A contradiction: no values of the unknowns can satisfy this row. The additional pivot belongs to the augmented matrix, not the coefficient matrix.

Recall 5Does a 0 determinant imply 0 rank?

No. For a square matrix, it rules out maximal rank. Here the rank is 2: a small nonzero determinant confirms it, despite the 0 determinant of the whole matrix.
