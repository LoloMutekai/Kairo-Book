Level 2 · C059·M4

KKT

Your goalSolve an allocation problem with a cap by checking every KKT condition, then justify the scope of the resulting certificate.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

You have 6 L of water to distribute between 2 tanks. You would like 5 L in the first and 3 L in the second. These targets require 8 L, so they are out of reach. The question becomes: which feasible allocation comes closest to them?

We will call the allocated volumes x and y, in litres, and the available cap B, here 6 L. The tanks accept continuous volumes and do not overflow within the range studied. Water may be left unused. The quantities and the preference criterion are fictional; we are not describing a real installation.

The criterion will use a quadratic penalty for deviations from the targets, with the same weight for each tank. At the chosen point, adding water to either tank would still improve this criterion, but the cap prevents it. The slopes of the criterion therefore need not be 0: the effect of a constraint whose limit is reached can balance them.

An inequality is active when its limit is reached exactly; it is inactive when there is strict slack. The Karush, Kuhn and Tucker conditions, abbreviated KKT, associate a multiplier with each inequality. An inactive constraint must have a 0 multiplier. An active constraint can have a positive or 0 multiplier: reaching a limit is not enough to determine its effect.

The rule to remember

Write the inequalities in the chosen direction, look for the active constraints, solve the balance of slopes, and check feasibility, signs and complementary slackness before drawing a conclusion.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Allocate water as close as possible to the targets

Keep the targets a = 5 L and b = 3 L, and the cap B = 6 L. We minimize half the sum of squared deviations. The constant factor does not change the best point; it simplifies the derivatives. All constraints, including the lower bounds, are part of the problem.

  1. State the problem and its units

    a and b denote the fixed targets; x and y are the volumes to choose. All these quantities, along with B, are in litres, written L. The function f is our penalty in L². It treats deviations symmetrically: exceeding a target and falling short by the same amount give the same square.

    f(x,y)=12[(x−a)2+(y−b)2]

    Write each inequality in the form “function ≤ 0”. g_B = x + y − B describes the cap, while g_x = −x and g_y = −y describe the lower bounds. The subscripts B, x and y identify the relevant constraint; each g is in litres. A point is feasible if all these inequalities hold.

    gB≤0,gx≤0,gy≤0

  2. Write the KKT system before simplifying

    The Lagrangian ℒ adds the criterion and the weighted constraints: ℒ = f + λg_B + μg_x + νg_y. λ, μ and ν are the multipliers for the cap, the bound on x and the bound on y. They are in litres: each multiplier × constraint product has the same unit L² as f. We minimize with g ≤ 0, so the multipliers must be nonnegative.

    Stationarity means that the partial derivatives of ℒ with respect to x and y are 0. The gradient is the vector of these derivatives, in litres. Stationarity gives the following equations, where the minus sign before μ and ν comes from g_x = −x and g_y = −y.

    x−a+λ−μ=0y−b+λ−ν=0

    The full system simultaneously requires feasibility of the point, the signs of the multipliers, stationarity and complementary slackness. The last condition makes the product of each multiplier and its own constraint 0. The products have unit L².

    λ≥0,μ≥0,ν≥0λ(x+y−B)=0μ(−x)=0,ν(−y)=0

    If a constraint is strictly negative, only a 0 multiplier can make its product 0. If it equals 0, the product is already 0: it does not force the multiplier to be strictly positive. If an inequality is reversed, the sign of its term in ℒ must also be adjusted.

  3. Know what these conditions can prove

    f and the constraints are continuously differentiable. To claim in general that a local minimum satisfies KKT, a constraint qualification is also needed. A sufficient condition is linear independence of the gradients of the active constraints: none is a combination of the others. We will check this at the chosen point. Without a constraint qualification, the necessity of KKT cannot be invoked automatically.

    Here f is strictly convex: its Hessian is the identity matrix, with diagonal entries 1 and off-diagonal entries 0, all dimensionless. The constraints are affine and the feasible region is convex. In this setting, a point and multipliers satisfying KKT give a global minimum; strict convexity of f makes the point unique. Outside the convex setting, KKT alone does not certify a global minimum.

    Necessity helps us look for a candidate. Sufficiency allows a conclusion once the system has been checked. These statements have different assumptions; the equations alone cannot replace them.

  4. Propose a set of active constraints

    Without constraints, setting the gradient of f to 0 gives (a, b) = (5, 3) L. Its total 8 L exceeds B by 2 L: this point is infeasible. So try an active cap x + y = B, with x and y strictly positive. This positivity is a working assumption, to be checked afterwards.

    The lower bounds would then be inactive: μ = ν = 0 L. Stationarity gives x = a − λ and y = b − λ. Adding these equalities, then using the cap, gives B = a + b − 2λ. Here we therefore split the shortfall equally between the tanks because the quadratic weights are identical.

    λ=a+b−B2=22=1 L

  5. Calculate the point, then check the whole system

    We obtain x* = 5 − 1 = 4 L and y* = 3 − 1 = 2 L. The star marks the candidate we will certify. Feasibility: x* + y* = 6 L; g_B = 0 L, g_x = -4 L and g_y = -2 L. The volumes are positive, so the chosen assumption works.

    Signs: λ = 1 L, μ = ν = 0 L are nonnegative. Stationarity in x: -1 + 1 − 0 = 0 L; in y: -1 + 1 − 0 = 0 L. The derivatives of the criterion alone are -1 L and -1 L; it is the derivatives of the Lagrangian that are 0.

    Complementary slackness: λg_B = 1 × 0 = 0 L²; μg_x = 0 × (-4) = 0 L²; νg_y = 0 × (-2) = 0 L². Each product pairs the correct constraint with the correct multiplier.

    Only the cap is active. Its gradient is (1, 1), which is dimensionless and nonzero; the set consisting of this vector alone is linearly independent. The stated constraint qualification is satisfied here. The point satisfies every block of the KKT system: convexity now allows us to conclude that it is the unique global minimum.

  6. Calculate the penalty and check it another way

    Deviations: -1 L and -1 L. Squares: 1 L² and 1 L². Sum: 2 L². The penalty is therefore f(x*, y*) = 2/2 = 1 L². This is the value of our fictional criterion, not a measure of real quality.

    Here is an independent check of the solution obtained using multipliers. Let (u, v) be any other feasible allocation, in litres. Expanding the squares around (x*, y*) gives exactly:

    f(u,v)−f(x*,y*)=(u−x*)2+(v−y*)22+λ(B−u−v)

    The first term is a nonnegative sum of squares. The second is nonnegative too: λ = 1 L is positive and B − u − v is the water left available, which is nonnegative. Every penalty difference is therefore nonnegative, and can be 0 only if u = x* and v = y*. This expansion directly proves optimality and uniqueness over the whole region, without merely comparing a few trial points.

  7. Respect the limits of the result

    The solution depends on fixed targets, divisible volumes and a quadratic criterion with equal weights. Tanks with limited capacity would require additional inequalities. Requiring whole-number volumes would change the problem. We do not extend the certificate beyond the stated assumptions.

    We have learned to construct and check a KKT certificate for this allocation. Constructing a general algorithm, studying a dual function or measuring a real gain are other tasks.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the system and reconstruct it from the constraints. Explain why the lower-bound multipliers are 0, why the gradient of the criterion is not 0, and which assumption allows a global minimum to be concluded.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I must set the gradient of f to 0, even on the boundary.”

    The point where the gradient of f is 0 is the target, which is infeasible here. On the boundary, check the balance in the Lagrangian: the cap offsets the slopes -1 L and -1 L through λ = 1 L.

    Review the system and the derivative checks

  • “All inequalities become equalities, and every limit reached has a positive multiplier.”

    An inactive constraint keeps its slack and requires a 0 multiplier. An active constraint allows a 0 multiplier: complementary slackness does not give the converse implication. The last exercise includes a change of active set and a case where a limit is reached without a positive multiplier.

    Compare the active constraints as the cap changes

  • “Stationarity is enough to prove that a point is best.”

    You must also check feasibility, signs and complementary slackness. To draw a global conclusion using KKT, use the stated convexity. To invoke necessity, check a constraint qualification; do not transfer these guarantees to an arbitrary problem.

    Reread the assumptions and the global certificate

Exercise 1 · Guided diagnosis

Feasible, therefore optimal?

With B = 6 L, someone proposes (x, y) = (3, 2) L. Identify the active constraints, then determine whether any multipliers can satisfy KKT. Do not automatically apply the formula for an active cap.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Calculate the total, then compare each volume with its lower bound.

Hint 2

If all the constraint inequalities are strict, complementary slackness forces all multipliers to be 0. What remains of stationarity?

Compare with the solution

The total is 3 + 2 = 5 L, and g_B = -1 L. The volumes are strictly positive: no constraint is active. The point is feasible and complementary slackness forces λ = μ = ν = 0 L.

The gradient of f is (-2, -1) L. With 0 multipliers, stationarity fails. This point does not satisfy KKT and is not optimal: the volumes can still be increased a little towards their targets without reaching the cap. Feasibility answers a different question from optimality.

Check your reasoning

  • You show the strict slack and identify no constraint as active.
  • You deduce that the multipliers are 0 from complementary slackness.
  • You use the nonzero gradient to rule out KKT, under the assumptions of this scenario.

Exercise 2 · Independent calculation

A little more water

The cap becomes B = 7 L; the targets and criterion remain the same. Find the optimal point and all multipliers. Check the blocks of the KKT system and the value of the criterion, keeping exact fractions before giving decimals in litres.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The target still requires 8 L. Try an active cap with positive volumes.

Hint 2

Use λ = (a + b − B)/2, then check that the calculated volumes are positive.

Compare with the solution

The shortfall is 8 − 7 = 1 L. We obtain λ = 1/2 = 1/2 L, or 0.50 L; x = 9/2 L, or 4.50 L; y = 5/2 L, or 2.50 L.

The total 7 L reaches B. The volumes are strictly positive: μ = ν = 0 L. The cap multiplier is positive. The derivatives of ℒ are −1/2 + 1/2 = 0 L; each complementary slackness product is 0, either because the cap is reached or because the multiplier is 0.

The deviations from the targets are all equal to −1/2 L. The penalty is therefore half of 2 identical squares: f = 1/4 L², or 0.25 L². Convexity is preserved, so the verified candidate is the unique global minimum.

Check your reasoning

  • You find the multiplier and the volumes as exact fractions, with their units.
  • You check the signs, the total, the lower bounds and all complementary slackness products.
  • You justify the conclusion from the KKT system to the global minimum and calculate f.

Exercise 3 · Transfer within the same scenario

When a lower bound becomes active

The cap drops to B = 1 L. Show why the assumption “strictly positive volumes” fails, then solve by trying an empty second tank. Give the point, the multipliers and f. Finally, with B = 8 L, the target reaches the cap: must its multiplier be strictly positive?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The formula for the previous regime gives λ = 7/2 L. Look at the sign of the resulting volume y.

Hint 2

Try y = 0 L and x = B. x is positive, so μ is 0. Then use the stationarity equations to find λ and ν.

Hint 3

When the cap equals the total of the targets, the gradient of f is already 0. Check whether all the multipliers can be 0 too.

Compare with the solution

The old regime gives λ = (8 − 1)/2 = 7/2 L, x = 3/2 L and y = -1/2 L. The last volume is negative: the proposed regime is infeasible, even though the simplified equations were solved.

With y = 0 L and an active cap, x = 1 L. The bound on x is inactive, so μ = 0 L. Stationarity in x gives λ = 5 − 1 = 4 L; stationarity in y gives ν = 4 − 3 = 1 L. The signs are correct. The cap and the bound on y are active; their products are 0. The product for the bound on x is 0 because μ = 0 L.

The point is feasible and stationary with these multipliers. By convexity, it is the unique global minimum. The squared deviations are 16 L² and 9 L²: f = 25/2 = 25/2 L², or 12.50 L². We therefore cannot simply replace a negative volume with 0 without recalculating the system.

For B = 8 L, the point (5, 3) L reaches the cap while making the gradient of f 0. The multipliers λ = μ = ν = 0 L satisfy KKT. This is an active constraint with a 0 multiplier: “active” does not mean “strictly positive”.

Check your reasoning

  • You reject the first regime because of the negative volume, without certifying it as optimal.
  • You identify the new active set and recalculate the multipliers, including the lower-bound multiplier.
  • You check KKT and f, then provide the case of an active cap with a 0 multiplier.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1For minimization with g ≤ 0, what sign must the multiplier have in ℒ = f + λg?

λ must be nonnegative. Keep the direction of the inequality, the sign in the Lagrangian and the sign of the multiplier consistent.

Recall 2What must be checked in addition to stationarity of the Lagrangian?

Feasibility of the point, the signs of the multipliers and complementary slackness for each multiplier–constraint pair. None of these checks replaces the others.

Recall 3What can be deduced from an inactive constraint? And from an active constraint?

An inactive constraint requires a 0 multiplier. An active constraint allows a positive or 0 multiplier: reaching the limit alone does not determine which case applies.

Recall 4Which assumptions distinguish local necessity from a global certificate on this page?

For local necessity, continuously differentiable functions and a constraint qualification, here independence of the active constraint gradients. For global sufficiency, a convex problem with the required signs and all KKT conditions. Strict convexity of the criterion makes the optimal point unique.

Recall 5How can you repair a calculation that gives a negative volume?

Reject the assumed regime, try making the relevant lower bound active, recalculate the multipliers and check all of KKT again. Clipping the volume alone can destroy stationarity and the total.
