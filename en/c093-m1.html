Level 2 · C093·M1

Median, MAD and normal scale

Your goalCalculate the median, MAD and robust scale of a small dataset, then explain why these three quantities are unaffected by how far an outlier reading moves away, while the mean follows it.

Before you start
c090-m5 : stationarity · c056-m5 : effect size · c063-m5 : synthetic dataset and external validity · c025-m5 : O, Omega and Theta

✿01 See the idea

A timer records the response time of a small routine five times. The values in this scene are fictional: they are used for calculations and do not describe a real machine. In milliseconds (ms), in the order recorded, the readings are 14, 12, 90, 13 and 15. The third timing was interrupted by a background task.

Which time should we take as typical? The mean is 28.8 ms. It exceeds four of the five readings and describes none of them: a single value pulls it upwards. The median avoids this problem. Sort the readings from smallest to largest and take the middle one, here 14 ms.

What about spread? Find the distance of each reading from the median, ignoring the direction of the deviation, then take the median of these distances. This number is called the MAD, which stands for median absolute deviation. Here it is 1 ms. The interrupted reading, 76 ms from the median, does not change it: only its rank matters, rather than its size.

A MAD is not yet a standard deviation. A normal distribution is the bell curve determined by a mean and a standard deviation σ. For this distribution, half the values lie within 0.6745 σ of the centre. The MAD is therefore about 0.6745 σ, and σ is about 1.4826 times the MAD. This coefficient gives a robust scale σ̂, interpreted as a standard deviation: this is the normal scale in the title.

Two limitations apply from the start. Five readings provide only a rough estimate. Also, the coefficient assumes that the bulk of the readings approximately follows a normal distribution; it does not establish this. A fictional dataset, as used here, illustrates a calculation but says nothing about its validity for real readings.

The rule to remember

Sort to find the median; measure the absolute deviations from the median; take their median to obtain the MAD; multiply by 1.4826 to obtain a scale comparable to a standard deviation, unless the MAD is 0.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Five readings, including one interrupted timing

The five fictional readings, in ms and in the order recorded, are 14, 12, 90, 13 and 15. Let us calculate their median, MAD and robust scale, assess the interrupted reading using a threshold of 3 chosen for the example, then compare with the mean.

  1. Define the quantities and assumptions

    The 5 readings xᵢ are times in ms, assumed to be independent, from the same routine and without a trend (stationarity from unit C090·M5 is an assumption here, rather than a result). The median m, in ms, is the middle value after sorting; for an even number of readings, it is the mean of the two middle values. The absolute deviation eᵢ, in ms, is the distance from xᵢ to m. The MAD, in ms, is the median of the eᵢ.

    ei=|xi−m|
    MAD=médiane(ei)

    The robust scale σ̂, in ms, equals k times the MAD, with k = 1.4826, a dimensionless coefficient rounded to four decimal places. The robust z, which is dimensionless, is the deviation from the median divided by σ̂.

    σ^=k×MAD
    z=x−mσ^

    Assumptions: the bulk of the readings approximately follows a normal distribution; fewer than half the readings are contaminated; the MAD is nonzero when dividing by σ̂. Without the first assumption, σ̂ is only a scale for interpretation. Without the second, the median itself can be pulled away. Without the third, z is undefined: the last exercise shows this.

  2. Sort and find the median

    Sort the readings: 12, 13, 14, 15, 90. There is an odd number of them, so the median is the third, with the same number of values on either side. It is 14 ms. Sorting is essential: the third reading recorded, 90 ms, is not the median.

    Check: two values are smaller (12 and 13) and two are larger (15 and 90). Comparison sorting takes on the order of n log n operations, a concept from unit C025·M5; this cost matters for long datasets, but not for five readings.

  3. Calculate the absolute deviations

    Subtract the median from each reading and keep the distance, because signed deviations on either side would cancel each other out. In the order recorded, in ms: |14 − 14| = 0, |12 − 14| = 2, |90 − 14| = 76, |13 − 14| = 1 and |15 − 14| = 1.

    Check: no deviation is negative. The 0 deviation belongs to the reading that is the median, as expected when there is an odd number of readings.

  4. Find the MAD

    The sorted deviations are 0, 1, 1, 2, 76. The median of this list is the third value: the MAD is 1 ms.

    Check: at least three deviations are less than or equal to the MAD, and at least three are greater than or equal to it. If the deviation of 76 ms became arbitrarily large, it would remain last in the sorted list and the MAD would stay the same.

  5. Convert to the normal scale

    If half the values from a normal distribution lie within 0.6745 σ of the centre, then MAD ≈ 0.6745 σ, so σ ≈ MAD ÷ 0.6745 = 1.4826 × MAD.

    σ^=1.4826×1=1.4826 ms

    Check: 1.4826 × 0.6745 is almost 1, because the two numbers are reciprocals, up to rounding. With only five readings, σ̂ provides a rough indication.

  6. Assess the interrupted reading

    The robust z for the reading of 90 ms divides its deviation from the median by the scale.

    z=761.4826=51.26

    The threshold of 3 is a choice for this example, with no special authority. The result exceeds it by a wide margin: the reading is flagged. The flag does not explain the cause: a background task, a sensor or actual slowness? Check: z is dimensionless, since ms are divided by ms.

  7. Compare with the mean and standard deviation

    The mean can be calculated directly.

    x̄=1445=28.8 ms

    With the usual standard deviation, calculated by dividing by n − 1 = 4, a standard result, accepted here without proof, bounds the squared ratio of the deviation from the mean to the standard deviation. A note in the code reviewed in the milestone also recalls this result.

    (n−1)2n=165=3.20

    The square of the threshold is 9. But 3.20 is less than 9: with five readings, no reading, however large, can exceed the threshold of 3 using this method. The robust z for the same reading is 51.26. Check: the upper bound is attained when a single reading differs from four identical readings, a case checked in calculs.py.

Explain it in your own words

Explain in your own words why the median and MAD stay the same if the interrupted reading becomes even larger, while the mean follows it. Also state when this protection stops working.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The MAD is the standard deviation, so I can compare it directly with a threshold stated for a standard deviation.”

    The MAD is the median of the absolute deviations; for a normal distribution it is about 0.6745 σ. Multiply it by 1.4826 to obtain σ̂ before comparing it with a standard deviation scale.

    Review the conversion to the normal scale

  • “The median is the middle value of the list in the order recorded.”

    Find the median after sorting. In this scene, the middle reading in the order recorded is 90 ms, while the median is 14 ms. With an even number of readings, take the mean of the two middle values.

    Review the sorting diagram

  • “If the MAD is 0, the robust z is 0, or we divide anyway.”

    A 0 MAD gives a 0 scale: the division is undefined, and 0 does not mean “nothing abnormal”. State the case, then choose a declared fallback or a tolerance based on the instrument.

Exercise 1 · Direct application

Two new readings

Fictional values. The dataset in the example has a median of 14 ms and a robust scale of 1.4826 ms. Two new readings arrive: 17 ms and 19 ms. With a threshold of 3 for the robust z, which should be flagged? Also give the upper limit in ms.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

First calculate the deviation from the median, then divide by the robust scale.

Hint 2

The upper limit is the median plus the threshold multiplied by the robust scale.

Compare with the solution

For 17 ms: 17 − 14 = 3 ms, so z = 3 ÷ 1.4826 = 2.02. It is below the threshold of 3: no flag.

For 19 ms: 19 − 14 = 5 ms, so z = 5 ÷ 1.4826 = 3.37. It exceeds the threshold: flag it.

m+seuil×σ^=14+3×1.4826=18.4478 ms

Both methods agree: 17 is below the upper limit and 19 is above it. A flag calls for a check; it does not prove a fault, and five reference readings remain a weak basis.

Check your reasoning

  • The deviation from the median is calculated before division.
  • The dimensionless z is compared with the threshold.
  • The upper limit gives the same verdict by the other method.

Exercise 2 · Transfer to an even number

Without the interrupted reading

Fictional values from the example. Remove the interrupted reading. Four readings remain, an even number. Calculate their median and MAD, then compare with the dataset of five readings.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

With an even number, the median is the mean of the two middle values.

Hint 2

Apply the same rule to the median of the absolute deviations.

Compare with the solution

Sorted: 12, 13, 14, 15. The two middle values, 13 and 14, sum to 27; the median is 27 ÷ 2 = 13.5 ms, a value that is not a reading.

The deviations are 1.5, 0.5, 0.5 and 1.5 ms for 12, 13, 14 and 15. Sorted: 0.5, 0.5, 1.5, 1.5. The two middle values, 0.5 and 1.5, sum to 2; the MAD is 2 ÷ 2 = 1 ms.

Comparison: removing the interrupted reading changes the median from 14 to 13.5 ms and here leaves the MAD at 1 ms, while the mean would fall back into the cluster of four readings. The unchanged MAD is a coincidence of this dataset, rather than a rule.

Check your reasoning

  • Sorting comes before finding the middle.
  • The two middle values are averaged for both the median and the MAD.
  • The comparison stays limited to this dataset.

Exercise 3 · Diagnosing an edge case

A 0 scale

Another fictional dataset: four readings are 20 ms and one is 45 ms. Calculate the median, MAD and robust scale. What happens to the robust z for the value 45 ms? Propose an honest remedy.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The deviations of four identical readings from the median are all equal to each other.

Hint 2

Division by a 0 scale is undefined: do not invent either 0 or a huge value.

Hint 3

Another scale can be used if declared, for example the mean of the absolute deviations.

Compare with the solution

Once sorted, the dataset starts with four values of 20 ms; the median is the third value, 20 ms. The deviations are 0 for the four repeated readings and 25 ms for the other one. After sorting, the third deviation is 0: the MAD is 0 ms, so σ̂ = 1.4826 × 0 = 0 ms.

The z for the value 45 would be 25 ÷ 0: division by 0, which is undefined. Writing 0 would say “nothing abnormal”, even though the deviation is obvious; writing a manually chosen huge value would be just as arbitrary.

Remedy: state that the scale is 0 and the robust z is undefined, then choose a declared fallback. Example: the mean of the absolute deviations, 25 ÷ 5 = 5 ms. It needs its own coefficient to be interpreted as a standard deviation; this exercise does not specify that coefficient. Another approach is a tolerance based on the resolution of the instrument.

Check your reasoning

  • The 0 MAD is calculated, rather than assumed.
  • The z is declared undefined, without inventing 0 or infinity.
  • The fallback is named and its limitation stated.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1How do you find the median of a dataset?

Sort the dataset; take the middle value if there is an odd number of values, or the mean of the two middle values if there is an even number.

Recall 2What does the MAD measure, and how do you calculate it?

It measures spread, in the units of the data: the median of the absolute deviations from the median of the dataset.

Recall 3Why multiply the MAD by 1.4826?

For a normal distribution, the MAD is about 0.6745 σ; multiplying by 1.4826 gives a scale interpreted as a standard deviation, assuming the bulk of the data is approximately normal.

Recall 4Why does the median resist an outlier reading, and how far does this protection extend?

It depends only on ranks: making an extreme value more extreme does not move it. The protection stops when half or more of the readings are contaminated.

Recall 5What should you do when the MAD is 0?

Declare the scale 0 and the robust z undefined, then use a declared fallback, without inventing 0 or infinity.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Sort; find the median, either the middle value or the mean of the two middle values; calculate the absolute deviations from the median; take their median to obtain the MAD; multiply by 1.4826 to obtain σ̂, valid if the bulk of the data is approximately normal; divide the deviation from the median by σ̂ for the robust z; if the MAD is 0, declare the scale 0 instead of dividing.
