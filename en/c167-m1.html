Heat

Your goalWrite the heat equation for a fictional bar, divide it into time and length steps, calculate two steps by hand, then explain why the ends, the initial profile and the step size determine the result.

Before you start
c166-m5 : spectral truncation · c049-m5 : series solutions · c127-m5 : Green and Stokes

✿01 See the idea

A thin metal bar 3 cm long has both ends immersed in baths kept at the same temperature. We use u for the difference between the temperature at a point on the bar and the bath temperature, in °C: at the ends, u is therefore 0 °C. Local heating has just stopped, leaving a hot profile near one end. What happens to this profile, minute by minute? All values in this scene are fictional: they are used for calculations, not to describe a real metal.

The idea fits into one sentence: a point hotter than the average of its neighbours cools down, a point colder than that average warms up, and the larger the difference from its neighbours, the faster the change. To express being hotter than the neighbours, we use the curvature of the profile, written uxx: this is the second derivative of u with respect to position x, in °C per cm². It is negative at the top of a peak and positive at the bottom of a trough.

The heat equation relates this curvature to the rate of change ut, in °C per minute:

ut=αuxx

The coefficient α, the thermal diffusivity, is positive and is expressed in cm² per minute. We take α = 1 cm²/min, a fictional value. Its sign matters: with the opposite sign, peaks would become more pronounced instead of spreading out.

To calculate by hand, we divide the bar into 3 segments of 1 cm. This gives four grid points: the two ends, fixed at 0 °C, and two interior grid points whose temperature differences are the unknowns. We also divide time into steps of 0.25 min. This grid replaces the continuous curve with a few numbers; it approximates the curve rather than reproducing it exactly.

Two kinds of data accompany the equation: the values imposed at the ends and the initial profile. Without the first, nothing specifies where heat can leave; without the second, there is nothing to propagate.

The rule to remember

Each interior grid point adds to its temperature difference r times (left neighbour + right neighbour − twice its own value), where r = α Δt / Δx² has no unit; the ends keep their imposed value.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Two heat steps on a fictional bar

A bar 3 cm long, with grid points at positions 0, 1, 2 and 3 cm. Differences from the bath temperature: 0 °C at both ends, 8 °C at position 1 cm and 0 °C at position 2 cm. With α = 1 cm²/min, Δx = 1 cm and Δt = 0.25 min, let us calculate the temperature differences after two steps (fictional values).

  1. Name the quantities and state the assumptions

    u0, u1, u2 and u3 denote the temperature differences from the baths, in °C, at the four grid points from left to right; u0 and u3 are fixed at 0 °C. A subscript labels the grid point k, and a superscript labels the step n: the initial instant corresponds to n = 0. Initially, u1 = 8 °C and u2 = 0 °C.

    In ut = α uxx, replace the rate of change with (temperature difference after − temperature difference before) / Δt and the curvature with (left neighbour − twice the point value + right neighbour) / Δx². This finite difference approximation gives an explicit step: it uses only values from the previous instant.

    ukn+1=ukn+r(uk−1n−2ukn+uk+1n)

    Assumptions: a thin, homogeneous bar, so α is constant; no heat source; ends held at 0 °C; heat exchange described along the x axis alone. If any assumption fails, the step formula must be reconsidered.

  2. Calculate r and check its unit

    The number r combines α, Δt and Δx. The units cancel: (cm² per minute) × minute / cm² leaves no unit. The number r is therefore dimensionless, which confirms that the discretization is consistent.

    r=α×ΔtΔx2=1×0.2512=0.25

    Here r = 0.25. In practical terms, at each step a grid point exchanges with each neighbour the fraction r of the temperature difference between them.

  3. First step, point by point

    Grid point at position 1 cm. Its neighbours are the end (0 °C) and the next grid point (0 °C), with sum 0; twice its own value is 16. Its doubled value therefore exceeds the sum of its neighbours by 16 °C, and it gives up the fraction r of this excess: 0.25 × 16 = 4 °C.

    u11=8+0.25×(0−2×8+0)=4 °C

    Grid point at position 2 cm. Its neighbours have values 8 + 0 = 8; twice its own value is 0. Their sum exceeds its doubled value by 8 °C, and it receives 0.25 × 8 = 2 °C.

    u21=0+0.25×(8−2×0+0)=2 °C

    Both grid points are calculated from the initial values: the second does not use the temperature difference already updated at the first. After one step, or 0.25 min, we have 4 °C and 2 °C.

  4. Second step

    We start again from 4 °C and 2 °C. For the first grid point, the neighbours sum to 2 and twice its own value is 8: the excess is 6 °C and the loss is 1.50 °C.

    u12=4+0.25×(0−2×4+2)=2.50 °C

    For the second grid point, the neighbours sum to 4 and twice its own value is 4: the difference between their sum and its doubled value is 0 °C, so it does not change.

    u22=2+0.25×(4−2×2+0)=2 °C

    After 2 steps, the temperature differences are 2.50 °C and 2 °C. The profile, initially sharply peaked, flattens while falling towards the bath temperature.

  5. Read the same result through two simple modes

    On these four grid points, two profiles keep their shape at each step. Mode A: the two interior grid points have the same temperature difference. Mode B: they have opposite temperature differences. Any initial profile can be split into these two modes with the amplitudes a and b below.

    a=u1+u22 , b=u1−u22

    For our initial profile, a = 4 °C and b = 4 °C. Calculating a step directly for each mode shows that A is multiplied by 1 − r = 0.75 and B by 1 − 3 r = 0.25 at each step.

    a→(1−r)a , b→(1−3r)b

    After two steps: a = 4 × 0.75 × 0.75 = 2.25 °C and b = 4 × 0.25 × 0.25 = 0.25 °C. Recombining gives u1 = a + b = 2.50 °C and u2 = a − b = 2 °C: we recover exactly the calculation point by point. These are the first two sine modes of the series for the bar, reduced to a mesh of three segments; retaining two modes acts as a truncation.

    In the continuous version, the mode of index n decays with the exponent below, where L is the length of the bar. Our coarse mesh represents the fast mode less well than the slow mode: agreement with the continuous exponential is better for A than for B.

    bsin(nπxL)e−α(nπL)2t

  6. Check the result

    Heat balance. For a homogeneous bar, the sum S of the two interior temperature differences at step n is proportional to the heat content. Exchanges between the two interior grid points cancel out; only heat leaving through the ends counts, and each end receives r times the temperature difference at its neighbouring grid point.

    Sn=u1+u2 ; Sn+1=Sn−rSn

    The sums are 8, 6 then 4.50 °C. The losses are 0.25 × 8 = 2 then 0.25 × 6 = 1.50 °C: 8 − 2 = 6 and 6 − 1.50 = 4.50. The heat balance checks out.

    Bounds. With no heat source, no temperature difference can leave the interval between 0 °C and the initial maximum, 8 °C. This is indeed the case. Stability. Both factors 0.75 and 0.25 have absolute value less than one. The criterion for mode B is below: for this bar with three segments, the step remains acceptable as long as r does not exceed approximately 0.67. This threshold depends on the mesh; we do not prove it for a finer grid.

    |1−3r|≤1⟺r≤23

Explain it in your own words

Why does the sum of the two interior temperature differences decrease even though exchanges between these two grid points lose no heat? Also say what would change in the balance if the ends were no longer held at a temperature difference of 0.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “Only the equation matters: the ends are a detail.”

    The baths hold the ends at 0 °C and determine where heat leaves. This is why the sum of the temperature differences goes from 8 to 6 then 4.50 °C. With different end conditions, the same initial profile would give a different heat balance.

    Redo the heat balance

  • “A larger time step gives the same result in fewer steps.”

    With a step of 1 min, four times as long, r is 1: the first grid point loses 16 °C from its 8 °C and ends up 8 °C below the bath temperature. With no heat source, this is impossible. The factor for mode B then has absolute value greater than one: its amplitude grows and its sign alternates at each step.

    Test the oversized step

  • “All modes of the profile decay at the same rate: the whole profile simply falls together.”

    The slow mode is multiplied by 0.75 at each step, and the fast mode by 0.25. The profile does more than fall: it flattens, because its rapid variations disappear first.

    Review the two modes

Exercise 1 · Application with explanation

The slow mode alone

The same bar, the same α, Δx and Δt, with ends at 0 °C. Initially, both interior grid points have a temperature difference of 4 °C. Calculate one step for the first grid point using the neighbour formula, then recover the result using the factor 1 − r.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The neighbours of the first grid point are the end and the second grid point. Calculate their sum, then twice the value at the grid point.

Hint 2

For the check, the two grid points have equal values: simply multiply the initial temperature difference by 1 − r.

Compare with the solution

The neighbours have values 0 °C and 4 °C: their sum is 4 °C. Twice the grid point value: 8. Excess: 8 − 4 = 4 °C. Loss: 0.25 × 4 = 1 °C. New temperature difference: 4 − 1 = 3 °C, and the second grid point has the same result by symmetry.

Check: 4 × 0.75 = 3 °C, the same result. Since the profile is already mode A, a single factor describes everything.

Check your reasoning

  • Add r times (left neighbour + right neighbour − twice its own value) at each grid point.
  • Use the initial values for both grid points.
  • Recover the same number using the factor 1 − r.

Exercise 2 · Application with explanation

Two modes, two steps

Initially: 6 °C at the first grid point, 2 °C at the second, with ends at 0 °C. Decompose this profile into modes A and B, calculate the temperature differences after two steps using the factors, then check the sum of the two differences.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

For mode A, take half the sum; for mode B, half the difference.

Hint 2

Multiply a twice by 0.75 and b twice by 0.25, then recombine using + and −.

Compare with the solution

Amplitudes: a = (6 + 2) / 2 = 4 °C and b = (6 − 2) / 2 = 2 °C. After two steps: a = 4 × 0.75 × 0.75 = 2.25 °C and b = 2 × 0.25 × 0.25 = 0.125 °C.

Recombination: u1 = 2.25 + 0.125 = 2.375 °C and u2 = 2.25 − 0.125 = 2.125 °C.

Check: the sum 2.375 + 2.125 = 4.50 °C, as in the example, because the initial profile has the same sum 8 °C and the sum is multiplied by 1 − r at each step.

Check your reasoning

  • Calculate a and b using the sum and difference, divided by two.
  • Apply each factor for the correct number of steps.
  • Recombine using a + b and a − b, then check the sum of the two temperature differences.

Exercise 3 · Transfer

An oversized step

We want to advance faster: Δt = 0.75 min, with α and Δx unchanged, and initial temperature differences 8 °C and 0 °C. (a) Calculate r. (b) Calculate one step for both grid points and explain why the result is physically impossible. (c) Use the factor for mode B to determine the largest acceptable value of r for this bar, then the maximum time step.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

For (b), the initial temperature difference at the first grid point is 8 °C and the excess of twice its value over the sum of its neighbours is 16 °C; compare the loss with 8 °C.

Hint 2

For (c), the factor for B is 1 − 3 r; require its absolute value to be no greater than one, then return to Δt = r Δx² / α.

Compare with the solution

(a) The step is tripled: Δt = 0.75 min, so r = 3 × 0.25 = 0.75.

(b) First grid point: loss 0.75 × 16 = 12 °C, so the temperature difference is 8 − 12: it falls 4 °C below the bath temperature. Second grid point: gain 0.75 × 8 = 6 °C, taking it from 0 to 6 °C. A bar with no heat source cannot become colder than its baths: the step is unsuitable.

Interpretation using modes: the factor for B is 1 − 3 r = 1 − 2.25, with absolute value 1.25. This amplitude exceeds one: the error would grow at each step while changing sign.

(c) |1 − 3 r| ≤ 1 gives r ≤ 0.67, hence Δt ≤ 0.67 × 1 / 1 = 0.67 min. Our step of 0.75 min exceeds it. This threshold applies to this mesh of three segments, not to every grid.

Check your reasoning

  • Calculate dimensionless r from α, Δt and Δx.
  • Show a temperature difference below the bath temperature and reject the result.
  • Link the failure to a factor with amplitude greater than one.
  • Give the bound on r, then convert it into a time step.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does uₜ = α uₓₓ say about a point hotter than the average of its neighbours?

Its curvature uₓₓ is negative, so uₜ is negative too: it cools down. The larger the difference from its neighbours, the faster it changes. The coefficient α is positive, in cm² per minute.

Recall 2How is r calculated for an explicit step, and what is its unit?

r = α Δt / Δx². The units cancel: r is dimensionless. In the example, r = 0.25. Each grid point exchanges with each neighbour the fraction r of their temperature difference.

Recall 3What data, besides the equation, determine the calculation in the example?

The temperature differences imposed at the ends (0 °C) and the initial profile (8 °C and 0 °C at the interior grid points). Without the end conditions, we do not know where heat leaves; without the profile, there is nothing to propagate.

Recall 4What are the factors per step for modes A and B, and which decays faster?

A is multiplied by 1 − r = 0.75, B by 1 − 3 r = 0.25. B decays faster: the profile flattens because its rapid variations disappear first.

Recall 5What happens when the factor for mode B has absolute value greater than one?

Its amplitude grows at each step and its sign alternates: the resulting temperature differences are impossible. For this bar with three segments, this happens as soon as r exceeds approximately 0.67.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Use u in °C, x in cm and t in minutes; write uₜ = α uₓₓ with positive α; set the end conditions and initial profile; divide into steps and calculate dimensionless r; update each interior grid point using its neighbours and values from the previous step; check the heat balance, the bounds and the factors for each mode; reject a step whose r makes the amplitude factor of a mode exceed one in absolute value.

Heat and relaxation: reread an exact step formula

What you understand
For each mode of the bar, heat brings it back towards equilibrium by a factor at each step, and a factor of the form 1 − k Δ is only the linear version of an exact step: it can become wrong as the step grows. A variable relaxing towards a baseline is related to this mechanism, but is not a heat equation.
