Level 4 · C244·M5

Bid-ask diagnostic

Your goalCompare the volatility of a calibrated SVI curve with the bid-ask ranges for a slice, measure each deviation in half-widths, then distinguish a true range violation from an unusable quote.

Before you start
c243-m5 : SSVI · c051-m5 : error diagnostics · c220-m5 : numerical reproducibility · c198-m5 : independent validation · c244-m1 : initialization · c244-m2 : constraints · c244-m3 : quasi-explicit calibration · c244-m4 : admissible interpolation

✿01 See the idea

An SVI slice describes an implied volatility curve for a single expiry. After initialization, constraints, quasi-explicit calibration and admissible interpolation, one question remains: is this curve compatible with the market quotes for four options with this expiry?

The market displays a range rather than a single price. The bid is the best price offered by a buyer, and the ask is the best price requested by a seller. Here we express them directly in implied volatility, in ticks: one tick is one tenth of a volatility point, and 10 ticks make one point, meaning one percent of annualized volatility. The midpoint of the range is a useful reference; it is not a traded price.

A deviation of 3 ticks between the curve and the midpoint carries a different weight in a range of 4 ticks than in a range of 24 ticks. We therefore measure the deviation in half-widths of the range, making it comparable across options.

The diagnostic answers a single question: does the model value lie between the bid and the ask? It does not establish that the curve is free of arbitrage, that the calibration is good, or that a trade could be executed at this level.

The quotes, curve values and slice are fictional. They are used to work through an example; they describe neither a real market nor a configuration to reuse.

The rule to remember

For each option: calculate the midpoint M and half-width h of the range, measure the residual r = V − M, then the score z = r / h. If the range is valid, meaning that the ask is strictly above the bid, the curve is within the range exactly when the absolute value of z does not exceed 1.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Four options in a fictional slice, from range to verdict

A fictional slice contains four options, named low, central, high and extreme according to their strikes. The ranges and calibrated curve values, in volatility ticks, are: low 240 to 264 with a model value of 258; central 198 to 202 with 203; high 205 to 217 with 209; extreme 236 to 256 with 228. For each option, let us decide whether the curve falls within the range, and by how much it falls outside otherwise.

  1. Define the quantities, units and assumptions

    For a given option, B is the bid, A the ask and V the implied volatility of the curve, all in ticks. The curve comes from the raw SVI total variance w(k), where k is the logarithm of the ratio of the strike to the forward price, T is the maturity in years, and a, b, ρ, m, s are the slice parameters. These lowercase letters belong to the SVI formula; the uppercase letters B, A, V, M and L refer to the range and the model.

    w(k)=a+b(ρ(k−m)+(k−m)2+s2)

    The model volatility is derived from this variance, then expressed in tick units. The parameters come from the calibration in the preceding units; here we take the V values as given and do not recalculate them.

    V=w(k)T

    Assumptions: the four quotes refer to the same expiry and the same instant, converted to volatility using a common convention whose conversion this unit does not repeat; each range is valid, meaning B is strictly below A; the V values are outputs from a calibration that has already been performed. If the second assumption fails, the score below no longer has any meaning.

  2. Calculate the midpoint, width and half-width

    The midpoint M is the average of the bid and the ask. The width L is their difference. The half-width h is the distance from the midpoint to each boundary, so the bid is M − h and the ask is M + h; this property will link the score to the range.

    M=(B+A)2
    L=A−B
    h=L2

    Low option: M = 504 / 2 = 252 ticks, L = 264 − 240 = 24 ticks, h = 12 ticks. Central option: M = 400 / 2 = 200, L = 4, h = 2. High option: M = 422 / 2 = 211, L = 12, h = 6. Extreme option: M = 492 / 2 = 246, L = 20, h = 10. All these quantities remain in ticks.

  3. Measure the residual, then the score

    The residual r is positive when the curve is above the midpoint and negative when it is below. The score z divides r by h: it is dimensionless, and equals 1 when the curve touches the ask, minus 1 when it touches the bid.

    r=V−M
    z=rh

    Low option: r = 258 − 252 = 6 ticks, z = 6 / 12 = 0.50. Central option: r = 203 − 200 = 3 ticks, z = 3 / 2 = 1.50. High option: r = 209 − 211 = -2 ticks, z = -2 / 6 = -0.33. Extreme option: r = 228 − 246 = -18 ticks, z = -18 / 10 = -1.80.

    The contrast is clear: the residual for the low option (6 ticks) is 2 times that for the central option (3 ticks), but its score (0.50) is lower than the central score (1.50). The width of the range makes the difference.

  4. Decide: inside, outside, and by how much

    The condition on z is equivalent to a direct comparison with the boundaries: V − M must lie between −h and h, which means M − h ≤ V ≤ M + h, or B ≤ V ≤ A according to the preceding step.

    B=M−h,A=M+h
    |z|≤1⟺B≤V≤A

    Low option: the score 0.50 is within the region, so the curve is inside. Central option: the score 1.50 exceeds 1, and the value 203 exceeds the ask 202, with an excess of 203 − 202 = 1 in ticks. High option: the score -0.33 is within the region, so the curve is inside. Extreme option: the score -1.80 falls outside the region, and the value 228 is below the bid 236, with an excess of 236 − 228 = 8 ticks.

    Only the low and high options keep the curve within their ranges; for the central and extreme options, it falls outside. The central option falls only slightly outside, but its range is very narrow.

  5. Check by another method

    First method: compare V directly with the boundaries. Low: 240 ≤ 258 ≤ 264 is true. Central: 203 exceeds 202. High: 205 ≤ 209 ≤ 217 is true. Extreme: 228 is less than 236. All four verdicts agree with those from the scores.

    Boundary cases: if V equals M, then r and z equal 0; if V equals A, then z equals 1. A score outside these bounds therefore does lie outside the range.

    Unit check: M, L, h, r and V are expressed in ticks; z has no unit. Adding a residual in ticks to a dimensionless score would be an error.

  6. Interpret the verdict without overinterpreting it

    This verdict establishes compatibility between the curve and the supplied quotes, and nothing more. It establishes neither the absence of calendar or butterfly arbitrage, nor the quality of the calibration across the whole slice, since four points do not cover the entire strike interval, nor the possibility of executing trades at these levels: a quote may be stale, indicative or for a tiny size.

    A point outside the range is a signal to examine: quote timestamps, volatility convention, curve rigidity. It is not an instruction to move the curve until it fits. Before calculating any score, you must also check that L is strictly greater than 0.

Explain it in your own words

Explain in your own words why the absolute value of z being less than or equal to 1 is equivalent to B ≤ V ≤ A, then why the central option falls outside its range despite having the smaller residual.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The smallest residual in ticks identifies the best point.”

    A residual must be assessed relative to the width of the range. For the low option, 6 ticks remain within a range of 24 ticks; for the central option, 3 ticks fall outside a range of 4 ticks. Compare the scores rather than the raw ticks.

    Review how to measure the score

  • “Being within the range proves that the calibration is good and free of arbitrage.”

    The test compares the curve with a few displayed quotes. It checks neither calendar nor butterfly arbitrage, areas without quotes, nor the possibility of execution. Treat it as a necessary condition in a diagnostic, never as a certificate.

    Reread what the diagnostic answers

  • “A crossed range or a range of 0 width still gives a usable score.”

    If the ask is below or equal to the bid, the half-width is negative or 0: the score changes sign or the division fails. First check that L exceeds 0; otherwise exclude the point and flag it. The last exercise demonstrates this.

Exercise 1 · Worked application

A fifth point

A fifth fictional point is quoted at 150 to 160 ticks and the curve value there is 158 ticks. Calculate M, h, r and z, then state whether the curve is within the range.

z=rh
Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Start with the midpoint, then the width: the half-width follows from it.

Hint 2

The residual keeps the sign of V − M: it is positive if the curve is above the midpoint.

Compare with the solution

Midpoint: (150 + 160) / 2 = 310 / 2 = 155 ticks. Width: 160 − 150 = 10 ticks, so h = 5 ticks. Residual: 158 − 155 = 3 ticks. Score: 3 / 5 = 3/5, or 0.6.

The score does not exceed 1: the curve is within the range. Direct check: 150 ≤ 158 ≤ 160. This verdict establishes only this compatibility.

Check your reasoning

  • Midpoint, width and then half-width calculated in that order.
  • Dimensionless score, compared with one.
  • Verdict confirmed by direct comparison with the bid and the ask.

Exercise 2 · Inverse reasoning

By how much should the central range be widened?

At the central strike in the example, keep the midpoint 200 and widen the range symmetrically until the model value 203 is exactly on a boundary. What width do you obtain, by what factor has the original width been multiplied, and what does this factor tell you about the score? This widening is a measurement tool, not a proposed quote.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

A boundary is one half-width from the midpoint; the model value is at a distance r from the midpoint.

Hint 2

The new half-width must equal the absolute value of the residual.

Compare with the solution

The new half-width must be 3 ticks, so the width becomes 3 × 2 = 6 ticks and the upper boundary falls on 203. The factor is 6 / 4 = 3/2, or 1.5. It equals the central option score 1.50: with the midpoint fixed, the absolute value of the score is the minimum widening factor required for the range to contain the model value.

Measuring this mismatch does not authorize changing the quote. The question asks only how much wider the observed range would need to be to contain the curve.

Check your reasoning

  • Midpoint unchanged, half-width equal to the absolute value of the residual.
  • Factor obtained by dividing the widths and linked to the score.
  • No quote is presented as having been corrected.

Exercise 3 · Boundary case

A crossed quote

A sixth fictional point displays a bid of 210 ticks and an ask of 208 ticks; the curve value there is 212 ticks. Calculate the width, then the score that a mechanical application of the formula would produce. What do you do with this point?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Calculate the width first: its sign tells you whether a range exists.

Hint 2

If the half-width is negative, what happens to the sign of the score for the same residual?

Compare with the solution

Width: 208 − 210 = -2 ticks, which is negative: the ask is below the bid, so there is no valid range. To examine the pitfall, the mechanical formula gives M = 418 / 2 = 209 ticks, r = 212 − 209 = 3 ticks, h = -1 ticks and z = 3 / (-1) = -3.

This score is misleading: the curve is above the midpoint, but the negative sign would suggest that it is below the range, and its absolute value would suggest a range violation. Neither verdict makes sense, because the bid and the ask do not define a band. The point is excluded from the count, flagged as a crossed quote to check (timestamp, source, swapped columns), and counted as neither inside nor outside.

Check your reasoning

  • Width calculated first and recognized as negative.
  • Mechanical score presented as misleading, with no inside or outside verdict.
  • Point excluded and flagged rather than silently corrected.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does the bid-ask range represent in this diagnostic?

The band of quoted volatilities between the best bid and the best ask; the diagnostic tests whether the model value falls within it, without treating that value as a traded price.

Recall 2How is the deviation of the model from the midpoint measured, and what threshold separates inside from outside?

Divide the residual V − M by the half-width h; the curve is within the range when the absolute value of the score does not exceed 1, which is equivalent to B ≤ V ≤ A.

Recall 3Why can a smaller absolute residual be more serious than a larger one?

Because the range is narrower: in the example, 3 ticks fall outside a range of 4 ticks whereas 6 ticks remain within a range of 24 ticks.

Recall 4What should you do with a quote whose ask is below the bid?

Exclude it from the count and flag it: its width is negative, so there is no band and the mechanical score has no meaning.

Recall 5What does a curve contained within the ranges fail to prove?

It proves neither the absence of arbitrage, nor the quality of calibration away from the quoted points, nor the possibility of execution: only compatibility with supplied quotes.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Reconstruct from memory: check that each range is valid, with the ask strictly above the bid; calculate the midpoint, width and half-width; measure the residual of the curve relative to the midpoint; divide it by the half-width to obtain the score; conclude inside if the absolute value of the score does not exceed 1, otherwise give the excess in ticks; finally state what the test does not prove.
