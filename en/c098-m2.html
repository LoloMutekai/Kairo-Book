Level 2 · C098·M2

Cash, units and total value

Your goalKeep a small ledger of cash and units, calculate its total value at each point in time using the price known then, and distinguish a trade from a price change.

Before you start
c097-m5 : risk and horizon · c067-m5 : pipeline · c090-m5 : stationarity · c037-m5 : controlled cleaning · c098-m1 : chronology, delay and available information

✿01 See the idea

Imagine a practice ledger with just two columns: the cash kept in a drawer and the number of units of a fictional asset that you hold. Each row of the ledger represents a point in time. To find what the ledger is worth at that point, you need to express both columns in the same unit of measurement, here the fictional euro.

The cash is already in euros. Units of the asset are counted as units; to convert them, multiply them by the price of a unit, in euros per unit. The product units × euros per unit gives euros, and only then does adding it to the cash make sense. Adding a number of units directly to a number of euros would mix two units of measurement.

When you buy at the displayed price, cash falls by the cost of the units, and the units held are worth that same amount: the ledger has changed in form but its value is unchanged. Selling reverses this process. The total value changes only if the known price changes while units are held. As in C098·M1, the value at a point in time uses only the price known at that point.

The values on this page are fictional: they illustrate a calculation and describe neither a real market nor an achieved result. Ledger assumptions: a single asset, one price at each point in time, trades executed at the displayed price, indivisible units and cash that is never negative. Fees, slippage and the end of the simulation are left to the next unit.

The rule to remember

The total value is the cash plus the number of units multiplied by the price known at that point in time; a trade at the displayed price transfers value between cash and units without changing the total, and only a new price changes it.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

A small ledger tracked at five points in time

A fictional ledger starts with 1000 € in cash and 0 units. It buys 8 units at a price of 60 € per unit. The price then rises to 65 €. The ledger sells 3 units at this price, and then the price falls to 55 €. Let us calculate cash, units and total value at each point in time, then check the result using a second method.

  1. Name the quantities and set the assumptions

    C is the cash in the ledger, in fictional euros (€). N is the number of units held, a nonnegative integer, measured in units. P is the price of a unit at the point in time being considered, in euros per unit (€/unit), as known at that point. V is the total value of the ledger, in euros.

    V=C+N×P

    The product N × P is expressed in units × €/unit, which gives euros: it can be added to C. Assumptions: a single fictional asset, one price at each point in time, trades executed at the displayed price, indivisible units and cash that is never negative. This value is a valuation at the known price rather than an amount received in cash.

  2. Value the starting position

    At the start, the ledger contains 1000 € and 0 units. The units are converted using the price at that point in time: 0 units are worth 0 euros, whatever the price.

    Vdépart=1000+0×60=1000+0=1000 €

    The entire ledger is then cash: the total value is 1000 €.

  3. Buy at the displayed price

    The purchase costs 8 × 60 = 480 €. Cash decreases by this cost and the number of units increases by the number bought: 1000 − 480 = 520 € and 0 + 8 = 8 units.

    Vachat=520+8×60=520+480=1000 €

    Check: the total value has changed by 1000 − 1000 = 0 €. Cash has fallen by 480 €, and the units add exactly 480 € at the same price.

  4. Let the price rise

    No trade takes place: cash remains 520 € and the number of units remains 8. Only the known price changes to 65 €, so the units are worth 8 × 65 = 520 €.

    Vhausse=520+520=1040 €

    Check using the change: the rise is 65 − 60 = 5 € per unit, and 8 × 5 = 40 €. This equals the difference 1040 − 1000 between the two total values.

  5. Sell some units at the known price

    Selling 3 units brings in 3 × 65 = 195 €. Cash increases and the number of units decreases: 520 + 195 = 715 € and 8 − 3 = 5 units.

    Vvente=715+5×65=715+325=1040 €

    Check: the total value stays at 1040 €, as at the previous point in time (1040 €). A trade at the displayed price neither creates nor destroys value.

  6. Let the price fall

    The known price changes to 55 €. The 5 remaining units are worth 5 × 55 = 275 €, and cash remains 715 €.

    Vbaisse=715+275=990 €

    The ledger is now worth 990 €, which is 10 € less than at the start. Until the units are sold, this difference remains a change in valuation.

  7. Check using a second method

    Let us recalculate without using the totals again. Between the rise and the fall, the price dropped by 65 − 55 = 10 € per unit, on 5 units held: 5 × 10 = 50 €. The rise had added 40 €.

    50−40=10 €=1000−990

    Both methods give 10 €. Cash changed only during trades, and the total value changed only through price changes affecting the units held at the time. The agreement validates the arithmetic rather than the choice of model.

Explain it in your own words

Why does the purchase leave the total value unchanged even though cash falls? What actually changed the total value in this sequence, and why does the number of units held matter at that point?

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The cash shown is the value of the ledger.”

    After the purchase, cash is 520 € while the total value is 1000 €: the 8 units are still worth 480 €. Always add units × price to cash before drawing a conclusion.

    Review the purchase step by step

  • “Buying loses value because cash falls.”

    Cash falls by 480 € but the units are worth 480 € at the same price: the change in total value is 0 €. Only a new price, applied to units held, changes the total.

    Reread the exchange of cash for units

  • “Units remain valued at the purchase price until they are sold.”

    Each point in time uses the price known then. Using the purchase price, the value after the fall would be 715 + 5 × 60 = 1015 € instead of 990 €: the ledger would seem worth more than it is at the latest known price.

    Revisit the price fall

Exercise 1 · Direct application

Revalue after a rise

Return to the ledger just after the purchase: cash 520 €, 8 units. The known price changes to 70 € per unit (a fictional value). Calculate the total value, then check it using the price change.

Hint 1

Convert the units into euros using the current price before adding them to cash.

Hint 2

To check, calculate the price rise per unit, multiply it by the number of units held and compare with the value just after the purchase.

Compare with the solution

Value of the units: 8 × 70 = 560 €. Total value: 520 + 560 = 1080 €.

Check: the rise is 70 − 60 = 10 € per unit, so 8 × 10 = 80 €. And 1080 − 1000 = 80 €. The two methods agree; cash has stayed the same because no trade took place.

Check your reasoning

  • The units are converted into euros before the addition.
  • Cash stays unchanged when there is no trade.
  • The value is checked using a price change.

Exercise 2 · Application with a constraint

Buy as many units as possible

In the final state of the example (cash 715 €, 5 units), the known price is 50 € per unit (a fictional value). You buy the largest whole number of units that the cash allows, without making cash negative. Give this number, the remaining cash and the total value before and after the purchase.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Divide cash by price, then keep only the integer part: a unit cannot be split in this ledger.

Hint 2

After the purchase, add the units bought to the units already held before valuing them.

Hint 3

Compare the two total values: a trade at the displayed price should leave them unchanged.

Compare with the solution

Quotient: 715 ÷ 50 = 14.3. Only 14 whole units can be bought: 14 × 50 = 700 € does not exceed the cash available, whereas one more unit would exceed it.

Remaining cash: 715 − 700 = 15 €. Units held: 5 + 14 = 19. Value after: 15 + 19 × 50 = 15 + 950 = 965 €.

Value before: 715 + 5 × 50 = 715 + 250 = 965 €. The two values are identical: the purchase has only changed the form of the ledger.

Check your reasoning

  • The number of units is an integer and cash remains nonnegative.
  • Cash and units are updated in opposite directions.
  • The values before and after are compared in the same unit of measurement.

Exercise 3 · Ledger diagnosis

Find the double counting

A worksheet states, after the price fall in the example, V = 715 + 8 × 55 = 1155 €. The cash amount of 715 € is correct. Find the error, give the correct value, measure the overestimate and explain its cause.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Count the units actually held after the sale, rather than just those that were bought.

Hint 2

Compare the overestimate with the value, at the current price, of the units sold.

Compare with the solution

The worksheet uses 8 units even though 3 have been sold: 5 remain. The correct value is 715 + 5 × 55 = 990 €.

The overestimate is 1155 − 990 = 165 €, which is 3 × 55 = 165 €. The units sold are counted twice: their sale proceeds are already in cash, and the worksheet still values them as units held.

Check your reasoning

  • The number of units held is recalculated after the sale.
  • The overestimate is linked to the units sold and valued at the current price.
  • The double counting is explained in terms of cash and units.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1How do you calculate the total value of a ledger of cash and units?

V = C + N × P: cash plus the number of units multiplied by the price known at that point in time, all in euros.

Recall 2What units of measurement do C, N, P and N × P have, and what can be added?

C is in euros, N in units, P in euros per unit and N × P in euros. Only N × P can be added to C.

Recall 3Why does a purchase at the displayed price leave the total value unchanged?

Cash falls by the purchase cost and the units bought are worth exactly that cost at the same price: value passes from cash to units.

Recall 4What can change the total value without any trade?

A new known price: the change equals the number of units held multiplied by the price change.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Name cash, units and price with their units of measurement; value each point in time using only the price known then; write V = C + N × P; update cash and units in opposite directions for a trade at the displayed price; check that the total value changes only through a price change affecting the units held; reject negative cash and fractional units in this ledger.
