Level 1 · C035·M3

Normal and exponential distributions

Your goalChoose between variation around a centre and a memoryless wait, then calculate the corresponding probabilities within a stated model.

Before you start
c030-m5 : Poisson · c034-m5 : mean value · c035-m1 : univariate density · c035-m2 : cumulative distribution function

✿01 See the idea

You arrive at a shuttle stop to travel to a workshop. Once you are on board, the journey usually takes around 20 min: some trips are a little faster, others a little slower. Before boarding, you wait for a departure. This wait starts at 0 min and can last a long time. Both durations belong to the same situation, but their distributions have different shapes.

For the journey time, a symmetric bell curve places small deviations on either side of a centre: this is the shape of the normal distribution. For the wait, a decreasing curve gives more weight to short waits while retaining a tail towards longer durations: this is the shape of the exponential distribution.

These curves are densities. A height is not a probability: the area over an interval gives a probability. Since the horizontal axis is in minutes, the density is in inverse minutes; the area and the probability are dimensionless. The total area is 1.

The shuttle and all its parameters are fictional. We assume symmetric, stable variation in the journey time. For the wait, we assume departures at the same average rate, independent over disjoint intervals, with no regular timetable. We do not assume that every real duration follows one of these distributions.

The rule to remember

First examine the possible values and the mechanism: the normal distribution describes a centre and symmetric variation; the exponential distribution describes a positive wait whose elapsed time does not change the distribution of the remaining wait.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

What can we predict at the shuttle stop?

The journey time X is modelled by a normal distribution with mean μ = 20 min and standard deviation σ = 2 min. The wait W is modelled by an exponential distribution with mean 4 min. Let us calculate the probability of a journey taking at most its mean time and the probability of waiting beyond 4 min, then check the results.

  1. Name the variables and parameters

    X is the random journey duration; x is a possible value of that duration. W is the random waiting duration; t is a duration threshold. All four quantities are expressed in minutes. P denotes a probability; E denotes a theoretical mean under the chosen distribution, and Var denotes the variance, the mean squared deviation from the centre.

    μ is the mean of X, in minutes. σ is its strictly positive standard deviation, in minutes; its variance is σ squared, or 2 × 2 = 4 square minutes. The notation N(μ, σ squared) gives the mean followed by the variance, rather than two means.

    We are not calculating the total duration W + X and need no assumption of independence between W and X. The initial assumptions concern each distribution separately.

  2. Read the normal density and change scale

    fX(x)=1σ2πexp(−(x−μ)22σ2), pour x réel

    f with subscript X is the density of X. π denotes the circle constant; exp is the exponential function, with exp(0) = 1. The positive square root is denoted by √. Squaring makes the density equal at equal distances from μ. The factor before the exponential ensures a total area of 1; it also gives f its unit of inverse minutes.

    In the exponent, square minutes are divided by square minutes: the result is dimensionless, as required by the exp function. To count deviations in standard deviations, subtract the mean and then divide by σ:

    Z=X−μσ,Z∼N(0,1)

    The symbol ∼ means “has the distribution”. Z is the dimensionless standardized variable; its mean is 0 and its variance is 1. Φ(z), read “phi of z”, denotes P(Z ≤ z), its cumulative distribution function. Thus P(X ≤ x) = Φ((x − μ)/σ). For x = 22 min, the deviation is 22 − 20 = 2 min, then z = 2 / 2 = 1.

  3. Obtain an exact normal probability by symmetry

    For x = μ, the numerator x − μ of the centred threshold is 0: we split the bell curve at its centre. The areas to the left and right are equal and their sum is 1. Each is therefore 1 / 2 = 0.50.

    P(X≤20 min)=Φ(0)=12=0.50

    Check: P(X > 20 min) is also 0.50 and the two probabilities sum to 1. However, P(X = 20 min) is 0: a single duration from a continuous distribution has no area. This last equality does not mean that the neighbourhood of the centre is empty.

  4. Parameterize the exponential distribution by its rate

    λ, read “lambda”, is the strictly positive departure rate, in departures per minute. Its value is the reciprocal of the mean wait: λ = 1 / 4 = 1/4 departures per minute, or 0.25 departures per minute. This rate is not a duration. For the exponential distribution, E(W) = 1/λ = 4 min; its variance is the square of this mean, or 16 square minutes.

    Let us specify the departure assumption: over an interval of duration t, the number of departures follows a Poisson distribution with mean λt; counts over disjoint intervals are independent. Your arrival provides no additional information about these departures. This model allows us to derive the waiting time distribution; knowing only a mean wait does not establish it.

    fW(t)=λexp(−λt) pour t ≥ 0;fW(t)=0 pour t < 0

    f with subscript W is the waiting time density. Its support, the set where the distribution can place probability, is the half-line starting at 0. λt is dimensionless. At the origin, the density height is 0.25 per minute, whereas P(W = 0) is 0.

  5. Go from the density to the waiting time tail

    S(t) denotes the probability of exceeding t, namely P(W > t). It is called the survival function, even when it simply describes a wait. For nonnegative t, let us integrate the density from t to infinity. Here u is the integration variable, also in minutes:

    S(t)=∫t∞λexp(−λu)du=exp(−λt)

    Why this result? An antiderivative of λ exp(−λu) is −exp(−λu). At infinity, it tends to 0; at the lower limit, it is −exp(−λt). Subtracting the value at the lower limit gives exp(−λt). The cumulative distribution function F with subscript W is therefore P(W ≤ t) = 1 − S(t). For negative t, F is 0 and S is 1.

    An independent check using the prerequisite on Poisson: under our assumption of independent departures at a constant rate, the number of departures in a duration t has mean λt. Waiting longer than t means counting 0 departures in that duration. The probability of this 0 count is indeed exp(−λt). This reasoning concerns the stated model, without claiming that real departures follow it.

  6. Insert the data and keep the result exact

    For t = 4 min, the product λt is 1/4 × 4 = 1, which is dimensionless. The negative sign gives the argument -1 of the exponential. We obtain:

    P(W>4 min)=exp(-1);P(W≤4 min)=1−exp(-1)

    These are exact results. exp(-1) is a definite number, left in symbolic form; no numerical table is needed for this answer. The mean 4 min does not promise a departure at that instant: the tail probability remains strictly positive there.

    Check: exp(-1) is between 0 and 1, and adding it to its complement gives 1. At t = 0, S is 1; as t increases without bound, S tends to 0. A longer wait must make the event “no departure yet” less probable.

  7. Understand what elapsed time changes

    “Memoryless” refers to the distribution of the remaining wait. The symbol | means “given that”. If all you know is that no departure has occurred for s minutes, then, for nonnegative s and t:

    P(W>s+t|W>s)=S(s+t)S(s)=exp(−λt)

    The event W > s + t is contained in W > s: the conditional probability is the ratio of their probabilities. In the numerator, exp(−λs − λt) contains the same factor exp(−λs) as the denominator. It cancels. The result therefore no longer depends on s. The total elapsed time remains real; only the model for the additional wait is unchanged.

    In our situation, after 4 min without a departure, the probability of waiting more than another 4 min is the same exp(-1) as at the start. The transfer exercise asks you to reconstruct this ratio.

  8. Check the scope of the models

    The normal distribution guarantees no maximum interval: here μ ± σ gives the markers 18 and 22 min, not barriers. Its symmetry gives the probability at the centre; another threshold requires Φ or numerical integration and does not justify an invented percentage.

    The normal distribution assigns positive probability to values below 0 even when the mean is positive. For a duration, this limits its interpretation: we use it as an approximation around the centre, without treating a negative duration as a possible journey. Truncating it would change its distribution and probabilities. The exponential distribution avoids this support difficulty but assumes memorylessness. Departures at fixed times or a varying rate invalidate this assumption.

    The calculations show the consequences of fictional parameters, not their suitability for an observed shuttle service. Choosing a distribution family requires these assumptions; seeing a vaguely similar curve is not enough.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the formulas. Explain why half appears at the centre of the normal distribution, then derive the exponential tail by integrating its density. Finish by explaining why having already waited a long time does not make a departure imminent in this model.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The curve height gives the probability of that exact minute.”

    Check the units: the height is per minute; a probability is dimensionless. You need an area. A single duration has 0 probability; half the bell curve corresponds to an entire side of the centre.

    Review symmetry and the area calculation

  • “The parameter of the exponential distribution is the mean in minutes.”

    Identify the convention before substituting for a letter. Here λ is a rate in departures per minute and the mean is its reciprocal: 4 min corresponds to 0.25 departures per minute. The product λt must be dimensionless.

    Review the parameterization and its units

  • “Since I have already waited, there must be less time left to wait.”

    Under the exponential model, the ratio of the tail probabilities gives the original distribution for the additional wait. Recalculate it. If the shuttle follows a known timetable, change the assumption: this property should no longer be applied automatically.

    Repeat the transfer exercise after a wait without a departure

Exercise 1 · Direct application

A threshold above the centre

Keep the journey model. Standardize the threshold 22 min, then write P(X ≤ 22 min) using Φ. Compare this probability with 0.50, without inventing a table value. What is P(X = 22 min)?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Subtract the centre before dividing by the standard deviation. The resulting threshold is dimensionless.

Hint 2

The threshold is above the centre; between them, the normal density is strictly positive.

Compare with the solution

The deviation is 22 − 20 = 2 min. The standardized threshold is 2 / 2 = 1. The required probability is therefore Φ(1).

It is strictly greater than Φ(0) = 0.50, because a positive area is added between 20 and 22 min. It remains less than 1, because the tail beyond 22 min is positive. The probability of the single duration 22 min is 0, regardless of this cumulative area.

Check your reasoning

  • Subtract the centre and then divide by σ, with the minutes cancelling.
  • Write Φ(1) and use an area to justify its comparison with one half.
  • Distinguish the cumulative probability up to the threshold from the probability of an exact duration.

Exercise 2 · Explained calculation

The wait exceeds a longer duration

Still at the stop, before observing any departure, calculate exactly P(W > 8 min) and P(W ≤ 8 min). Compare the first with P(W > 4 min).

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Calculate the rate times the threshold before applying the exponential function.

Hint 2

The cumulative distribution function is the complement of the tail probability, not the density at the threshold.

Compare with the solution

The product is 1/4 × 8 = 2. The tail probability is therefore exp(-2) and the complement is 1 − exp(-2). These probabilities are dimensionless.

exp(-2) is less than exp(-1), because the exponential function is increasing and -2 is less than -1. Another check: exp(-2) = exp(-1) × exp(-1), a probability multiplied by itself. This probability is strictly between 0 and 1, so its square is smaller.

Check your reasoning

  • Use the rate reciprocal to the mean and obtain λt = 2.
  • Give the tail probability and its complement, without adding the unit minute to the result.
  • Explain why the tail probability decreases as the threshold increases.

Exercise 3 · Transfer and critique

No departure so far

You now know that W > 4 min. What is the probability that W exceeds 8 min in total, meaning more than another 4 min of waiting? Write the conditional ratio. If you then learn that departures follow known fixed times, is this exponential ratio still justified?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The event of exceeding the longer threshold is contained in the event of exceeding the shorter one. Divide their probabilities.

Hint 2

A ratio of exponential expressions with the same base is obtained by subtracting the arguments.

Compare with the solution

The additional duration is 8 − 4 = 4 min. The ratio is P(W > 8 min) / P(W > 4 min) = exp(-2) / exp(-1) = exp(-1). This recovers the original tail probability for an additional 4 min, not the tail probability for the total time.

The argument can also be calculated directly: −λ × 4 = -1. These two approaches agree. The elapsed time remains part of what happened; it drops out of the conditional distribution of the remaining wait.

With known fixed times, the assumption of departures at a constant rate with no memory no longer describes the available information. You need to use the timetable and the arrival time; the data in this exercise do not allow a new probability to be calculated.   • Form the ratio of two tail probabilities and explain the inclusion of the events.
  • Recover exp(-1) from the additional duration.
  • Reject the exponential assumption after fixed departure times are announced.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1In the shuttle situation, what do μ and σ describe, and what limitation applies to the normal distribution?

μ is the mean journey duration and σ is its standard deviation, both in minutes. The variance is σ squared. The normal distribution is symmetric over all real numbers; applied to a duration, it remains an approximation because it allows negative values.

Recall 2How do you transform a journey threshold x into a dimensionless threshold and then into a probability?

Subtract μ and then divide by σ: z = (x − μ)/σ. The probability P(X ≤ x) is Φ(z). At the centre, symmetry and the total area give Φ(0) = 1/2.

Recall 3What relationship links λ to the mean wait? What are their units?

The mean wait is 1/λ, in minutes; λ is a rate in departures per minute. Here 4 min corresponds to 0.25 departures per minute. λt is dimensionless.

Recall 4Reconstruct the exponential tail probability and cumulative distribution function from the density.

For nonnegative t, integrating λ exp(−λu) from t to infinity gives S(t) = exp(−λt). The cumulative distribution function is 1 − S(t). For negative t, the tail probability is 1 and the cumulative distribution function is 0. A density height does not replace this integral.

Recall 5What does “memoryless” mean, and when does this assumption become doubtful for the shuttle?

Given that the wait already exceeds s, the probability of waiting more than another t is S(s + t)/S(s) = S(t). This describes a distribution of the remaining wait without erasing the elapsed time. A known timetable or a variable rate calls this assumption into question.
