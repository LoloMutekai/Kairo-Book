Level 1 · C018·M4

Producer and consumer

Your goalConnect a produced piece of data to the call that reads it and the effect that depends on it, then identify a broken connection.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

At the counter of a notebook storeroom, a screen shows 12 notebooks available. Someone is given 5 of them. The stocktaking program calculates the new stock and places a record in a register. Yet the screen still shows 12 notebooks. Where did the data stop?

Here, the producer is the function that prepares and stores the stock record. The consumer is the function that reads this record to update the screen. These words describe roles in relation to a piece of data: the same component can consume an input and produce another output.

The register is the shared point through which the data passes. Storing a record makes it available to a reader; this alone does not trigger reading or display. We call the concrete connection between these components wiring: which component calls which, with which register and which record.

The scene and quantities are fictional. Consuming the stock record means using information; it does not remove any notebooks from the storeroom or delete the record in our model. The physical issue of notebooks and the computer read are different events.

The rule to remember

Follow the same data from the producer to the register, then from the reader that is called to the expected effect. A defined function or a visible value is not enough to establish this path.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

From the calculated stock record to the updated screen

In the fictional storeroom, the starting stock is 12 notebooks and 5 notebooks are issued. The screen still holds the starting stock. Follow publication and then a call to the reader, with no other stock movement along this path.

  1. Specify the data and the calculation boundary

    The record contains the item “notebook”, a whole number of available notebooks and a record label, here “after issue”. The supply register stores this record under the key “current”: the name used to find it. A new stock record replaces the current record; we are not describing a message queue.

    Let D denote the starting stock, S the quantity issued and P the published quantity. A denotes the quantity displayed at the time being considered; E denotes the signed difference A − P. All these quantities are counted in notebooks. The sign − means subtraction. The labels “before” and “after” locate the call to the reader in time; they do not indicate a change of unit.

    Assumptions: the quantity issued does not exceed the stock, the quantities describe the same item, storage succeeds, reading returns the current record and no other movement occurs. The screen directly shows the quantity read, with no conversion. The actual handover of notebooks has been counted separately in this scene.

  2. Call the producer to calculate what remains

    The function relever describes how to calculate and publish the record. Its definition remains a recipe until a call reaches it. In our sequence, we call relever with the supply register, the stock D = 12 notebooks and the quantity issued S = 5 notebooks.

    We subtract the quantity issued because these notebooks leave the storeroom. This gives P = 12 − 5 = 7 notebooks. This number is the output of the producer’s calculation; at this stage, it tells us nothing about what the reader has done.

    P=D−S=12−5=7 cahiers

  3. Storing a record does not change the screen

    The producer saves the “after issue” record, with quantity 7, under the key “current” in the supply register. It stores a complete new record: the reader does not need to modify this record to use it.

    The screen keeps its old value A before = 12 notebooks. In this model, storage does not automatically update the display. The register and the screen are distinct objects; only a call intended to display data can change the screen.

  4. Measure exactly what is missing

    Compare the display with the current stock record. We subtract P from A to express how much the screen exceeds the record: E before = 12 − 7 = 5 notebooks. Here, this positive difference means that the screen reports too many notebooks.

    The producer’s local result is correct, but the expected effect has not yet been obtained. This does not tell us whether the reader was never called or read another record: we must follow its call.

    E=A−P=12−7=5 cahiers

  5. Pass the record to the consumer

    We now call afficher with the same supply register object. The function reads the key “current”, obtains the “after issue” record, then uses its quantity field to replace the screen value. The stock record read gives A after = 7 notebooks.

    The data is consumed because it is used for this display. It remains available in the register. Creating a copy register of the same type and giving it to the reader would not connect the reader to the record stored in supply: we must find the same point through which the data passes, rather than just a compatible interface.

    The chain to find is therefore: call to relever → storage in supply under “current” → call to afficher with supply → reading of “after issue” → quantity assigned to the screen.

  6. Check the number and its origin

    Check the stock calculation: 7 notebooks remaining + 5 notebooks issued = 12 notebooks at the start. This count reconstructs the initial batch without using the screen.

    Check the display: E after = 7 − 7 = 0 notebooks. The displayed quantity and the stock record agree. To attribute this agreement to the connection, our sequence must also identify the register read and the “after issue” label passed to the screen field.

    A difference of 0 notebooks is necessary for this direct display, but it is not enough to prove the wiring: an old record could contain the same quantity. And a screen that matches the stock record does not prove that the initial physical count was correct. We have checked the path described in the scene without observing a real application.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The function afficher exists, so the stock record is consumed.”

    A definition describes possible work. Find the call that reaches afficher after publication, then the line that uses the record received. Without this path, the mere existence of the function demonstrates no reading.

    Follow the call to the reader in the example

  • “The registers have the same interface, so they share their records.”

    A shared interface guarantees a compatible way to request a read, rather than shared content. In the diagram, supply receives the new stock record and copy keeps the old one. Pass the object that actually receives the stored record to the consumer.

    Compare supply and copy in the diagrams

  • “The screen shows the right number: the connection is proven.”

    The equality can be a coincidence. Find the exact record read, then the use of its quantity. Simply noting equal values does not distinguish a read from the right register from an old value or a value fixed in advance.

    Examine the misleading equality in the last exercise

The path stops after storage

A new stock record awaits reading

Hint 1

The new stock starts from 7 notebooks. Storage changes the record, rather than the old screen value.

Hint 2

After the call to the reader, the quantity in the “next” record replaces the screen quantity.

Compare with the solution

The storeroom had 7 notebooks. The new issue removes 2 notebooks: 7 − 2 = 5 notebooks published in “next”. Before reading, the screen stays at 7 notebooks. Its difference from the record is 7 − 5 = 2 notebooks.

The call to afficher with supply reads “current”, which now points to “next”. The screen changes to 5 notebooks. The final difference is 5 − 5 = 0 notebooks.

Independent stock check: 5 notebooks remaining + 2 notebooks from the new issue + 5 notebooks from the previous issue = 12 notebooks at the start. The “next” record also lets us distinguish this read from reuse of “after issue”.

  • You justify the difference 2 before reading and 0 after reading.

Exercise 3 · Diagnosis and objection

A misleading equality

In a variation of the same storeroom, the “after issue” record in supply and an old record in copy happen to contain 7 notebooks. The producer writes to supply and the consumer reads copy. Someone announces: “The difference is 0, so the wiring is correct.” Respond and propose a repair. What observation during the additional issue would distinguish the paths?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Does equality of quantities identify the record read?

Hint 2

Repeat the additional issue, leave copy unchanged and follow the label of the new stock record.

Compare with the solution

The screen can be numerically correct while ignoring the stock record produced. A difference of 0 notebooks does not prove a connection between supply and the reader: the objects and records differ. The repair is to pass supply to the reader and use the quantity returned by its read of “current”.

After the additional issue, supply publishes “next” with 5 notebooks. A read from the unchanged copy still returns 7 notebooks and leaves a difference of 2 notebooks. A read from supply can give 5 notebooks, but the number alone remains insufficient: we must observe that it obtains “next” from supply and uses this quantity for the screen.

In a real program, a trace of these steps must be connected to the same execution. Reading the code gives the possible path; it does not replace this observation. We do not run any real stock management program here.

Check your reasoning

  • You do not treat an equality as proof of origin.
  • You repair the object passed and the use of the data, rather than simply changing the screen text.
  • You distinguish the results 7 and 5 and ask for the label of the stock record actually read.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What roles do the functions relever and afficher play in relation to the stock record?

In relation to the stock record, relever prepares and stores it; afficher reads it and uses its quantity. The roles are relative to this data, rather than permanently attached to a class.

Recall 2Why does a defined reading function not prove consumption?

The definition describes the work. A call must reach this read, retrieve the expected record and use the result for the desired effect.

Recall 3What should you follow between publication and the screen?

The register actually written to and then read, the key “current”, the label of the record received and the use of its quantity for the screen. An identical interface does not guarantee shared content.

Recall 4Does reading the stock record remove notebooks or delete the record?

In our model, no. Consuming means using the information. The physical issue of notebooks is separate, and the register keeps its record after reading.

Recall 5Why does a screen value equal to the stock record not prove the wiring?

The same quantity can come from an old record or another register. The call, the record received and its use must be connected to the same execution; the value alone does not give its origin.
