Level 1 · C040·M1

Dot product

Your goalCalculate a dot product by pairing the correct components, then interpret the resulting number using its sign and unit.

Before you start
c039-m5 : changes of basis

✿01 See the idea

A cart moves across a workshop, travelling east and north. The force you apply pushes east, but also pulls slightly south. The eastward component therefore acts with the motion; the southward component opposes it. How can you find the overall result without losing this opposition?

Consider the east and north directions separately. In each direction, multiply the force component by the displacement component. Then add the signed contributions. This calculation is called the dot product: it turns the vectors into a number, here the work done by this force during the displacement.

The scene and all its values are fictional. The displacement d is (3 ; 2) m: 3 m east and 2 m north. The constant force F is (4 ; -1) N: its negative northward component means that it pulls south. N stands for the newton, a unit of force; m stands for the metre.

Work is expressed in joules, written J: 1 N multiplied by 1 m gives 1 J. The sign indicates whether this force acts with or against this displacement overall. It does not measure how tired the person pushing becomes. Before reading the result in the diagram, predict whether the southward contribution will cancel out the entire contribution along the east axis.

The rule to remember

In the same orthonormal basis, multiply the components along the same axis, keep their signs, then add. The result is a scalar: a number, accompanied by its unit in a physical application.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

The work done by the force along the cart’s path

For d = (3 ; 2) m and F = (4 ; -1) N, let us calculate W = F · d. The cart is represented by a point; the force applied to this point is constant along the straight path. We calculate the work done by this force alone, without assuming that the other forces do no work.

  1. Set the coordinate frame and quantities

    The x axis pointing east and the y axis pointing north are perpendicular. For each direction, the basis vector has unit length; such a basis is called orthonormal. Force and displacement must be described in this same basis. The formula that follows is for the Euclidean dot product; copying the sum of component products after changing to an oblique basis would not necessarily preserve this product.

    d is the displacement vector, in metres; its components d_x and d_y are the signed displacements along x and y. F is the force vector, in newtons; F_x and F_y are its components along the same directions. The letters F and d denote vectors here, even without an arrow. W denotes their dot product, interpreted as work in joules in our model.

  2. Define the operation before substituting numbers

    Let u = (u_x ; u_y) and v = (v_x ; v_y) be vectors with real coordinates in the chosen orthonormal basis. For this abstract definition, their coordinates have no units. The subscripts x and y name the axes; they are not factors. The central dot · denotes the dot product; the × sign denotes ordinary multiplication of numbers.

    u·v=ux×vx+uy×vy

    This sum is a real number: that is what “scalar” means. Keeping the pair of products without adding them would define a different operation. In the cart application, each product has the unit N × m, hence J; the contributions can therefore be added.

  3. Pair the components along the east axis

    The eastward force and the eastward displacement are both positive. Multiply F_x by d_x because they refer to the same axis. The work associated with this direction is 12 J.

    Fxdx=4×3=12 J

  4. Keep the sign of the northward contribution

    F_y is negative, while d_y is positive. Their product is negative: as the cart moves north, this force component pulls south. Removing the sign would erase the opposition that we specifically wanted to account for.

    Fydy=(-1)×2=-2 J

  5. Add and interpret the scalar

    W=F·d=Fxdx+Fydy
    W=12+(-2)=10 J

    The total is positive: along this path, this force does positive work despite its component opposing the motion. The result 10 J has no eastward or northward components. It is neither the remaining force, nor the displacement, nor the person’s total energy expenditure.

  6. Check the calculation by splitting the displacement

    Under a constant force, we can check the total using a checking path: 3 segments of 1 m east, then 2 segments of 1 m north. The eastward segments contribute 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 J; the northward segments contribute (-1) + (-1) = -2 J. The sum gives 10 J again. This detour is used for checking; it does not claim to describe the original straight path.

    This check relies on F being constant: in this model, work adds across successive displacements. The signs and unit must therefore be preserved when splitting the displacement. Reversing the roles in the numerical calculation, d · F, also gives 3 × 4 + 2 × (-1) = 10 J: this checks the symmetry of the operation, without physically exchanging force and displacement.

  7. Recover the properties from the formula

    u and v keep the meanings given above; w denotes another vector with real coordinates, and λ, read “lambda”, is a real number with no unit. The commutative and distributive properties of ordinary multiplication give the following rules. The sum v + w is the component by component sum; λu multiplies each component of u by λ.

    u·v=v·u
    u·(v+w)=u·v+u·w
    (λu)·v=λ(u·v)

    Symmetry means that the order of the vectors does not change the result. Distributivity lets us split a displacement into contributions. Homogeneity means that a factor applied to just one vector multiplies the result by that factor: the exercises will check this using the cart.

    u·u=ux2+uy2≥0

    With the same vector on both sides, the squares are positive or 0; their sum is 0 only if all the components are 0. With different vectors, this positivity is not required: a dot product can be negative or 0. Here we need neither to calculate a length using a square root nor to calculate an angle.

Explain it in your own words

Cover the formulas. Explain why the northward contribution is subtracted, why the result is a number and why the axes must be the same. Then reconstruct the sum and its unit from the diagram, without reading the total.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I keep the pair of products: that is the dot product.”

    The pair (12 ; -2) keeps the contributions separate. The dot product requires their sum: 12 + (-2) = 10 J. Check the type of your output: the total is a number, not a new arrow.

    Revisit the definition and the addition giving the total

  • “I can multiply the components in any order.”

    You can swap the factors within a single product, but the axes must stay paired. Crossing F_x with d_y and F_y with d_x gives 8 + (-3) = 5 J: the unit still looks plausible, yet this calculation no longer follows the contributions in the diagram.

    Review the contributions axis by axis

  • “The word product requires a positive result.”

    Each multiplication keeps the signs of its factors. Positivity is guaranteed when a vector is dotted with itself. With different F and d, a reversed displacement can give -10 J; work through the calculation again before replacing a contribution by its absolute value.

    Try the cart’s return journey

Exercise 1 · Application · components and factor

Extend the displacement

The force stays F = (4 ; -1) N. In the same workshop, multiply the entire initial displacement by 2, keeping its direction. State what you expect to happen to the work, then calculate the displacement components, the contributions and their sum.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The factor applies to d_x and d_y. The force stays as in the example.

Hint 2

Factor out the common factor from F_x × (2 × d_x) + F_y × (2 × d_y).

Compare with the solution

The work must be multiplied by 2, because just one input to the dot product is multiplied by this factor. The new displacement is (2 × 3 ; 2 × 2) = (6 ; 4) m.

Along x: 4 × 6 = 24 J. Along y: (-1) × 4 = -4 J. The sum is 24 + (-4) = 20 J.

Check using the property: 2 × 10 = 20 J, the same total. Distributivity justifies this equality; observing it for these values alone would not prove it for all vectors.

Check your reasoning

  • The displacement components change together; the force components stay fixed.
  • The northward contribution keeps its negative sign.
  • The result in joules agrees with the check using the common factor, which you can justify.

Exercise 2 · Reasoning · sign and direction of travel

Return the cart to the start

Start again from the initial data. The cart undergoes exactly the opposite displacement, while the force stays F = (4 ; -1) N. Predict the sign of the work, then calculate it. Explain in particular what happens to the contribution of the southward force.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The opposite displacement is (0 − 3 ; 0 − 2) m. Do not change the force.

Hint 2

In the y contribution, the force and displacement components are now both negative.

Compare with the solution

The displacement becomes (-3 ; -2) m. The work changes sign because d is replaced by its opposite in a homogeneous operation.

Along x: 4 × (-3) = -12 J. Along y: (-1) × (-2) = 2 J. The southward force now acts with the southward displacement; this contribution becomes positive.

The total is -12 + 2 = -10 J. It is indeed the opposite of 10 J. Negative work done by this force along this path is consistent; the positive contribution along the north axis is not enough to cancel the negative contribution along the east axis.

Check your reasoning

  • You reverse the entire displacement without reversing the force.
  • You justify the product of the signs for each direction.
  • You obtain the opposite total to that of the outward journey and keep its unit.

Exercise 3 · Inverse problem · exact calculation and objection

Obtain 0 work without removing the force

Keep the initial displacement (3 ; 2) m. The eastward force component is now 3 N; its northward component, denoted a and expressed in N, is to be chosen. Find the exact value of a that gives W = 0 J, then write it as a decimal in newtons. Respond to the objection: “0 work means that the force or the displacement is 0.”

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The eastward contribution is known. The northward contribution must be its opposite.

Hint 2

In the equation 3 × 3 + a × 2 = 0, isolate a by dividing by the northward component of the displacement, which is nonzero.

Compare with the solution

The contribution along the east axis is 3 × 3 = 9 J. To make the total 0, we need a × 2 m = -9 J. The component d_y is nonzero: division is allowed and gives a force in J/m, or N.

a=-92 N=-4.50 N

The exact fraction is -9/2 N. The decimal form -4.50 N is also exact here. The negative sign requires a southward component. Check by substitution: (-9/2) × 2 = -9 J, then 9 + (-9) = 0 J.

The displacement is nonzero; so is the force, since its eastward component is 3 N. Yet their dot product is 0: the contributions cancel. A 0 total therefore does not support the objection. Nor does it mean that no force acts or that no energy is expended anywhere in the workshop.

Check your reasoning

  • You write the equation for the total before isolating the unknown component.
  • You obtain the exact fraction, sign and unit of a, without rounding during the calculation.
  • You check by substitution and refute the objection using the nonzero components of the same example.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1How can you reconstruct the dot product in our plane, and what type of result does it give?

In the same orthonormal basis, u · v = u_x × v_x + u_y × v_y. Add products of corresponding components; the result is a real number, with a unit if the quantities have units.

Recall 2What checks on the coordinate frame are needed before multiplying the components?

The vectors must be described in the same basis; for our Euclidean formula, this basis must have perpendicular directions of unit length. Pair x with x and y with y. An oblique basis does not allow this sum to be copied without care.

Recall 3Why is the northward contribution negative on the outward journey and positive on the return?

The force keeps a southward component. On the outward journey, it is multiplied by a positive northward displacement; on the return, by a negative northward displacement. The product changes sign: the work of this component first opposes, then assists the motion.

Recall 4Why does multiplying only the displacement by a factor multiply the work by that factor?

Each displacement component receives the factor. In the sum of products, it is therefore common to all terms and can be factored out using distributivity. The force stays fixed; the factor is counted only once.

Recall 5Does a 0 dot product require one of the vectors to be 0? What distinction is needed?

No: opposite contributions can cancel for different vectors, as in the cart’s force adjustment. However, u · u is a sum of squares, which is 0 only if u is 0.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without rereading, draw the cart’s path and reconstruct the sequence: same orthonormal basis; components along the same axis; signed products; scalar sum; unit; check by splitting the displacement. Then explain what changes when the displacement doubles or reverses, and why a 0 total does not require a 0 force.
