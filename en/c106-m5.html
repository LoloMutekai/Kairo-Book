Level 3 · C106·M5

Representation transfer

Your goalReuse a frozen visual extractor, calculate an update to its new head, and distinguish this adaptation from evidence of validity on new images.

Before you start
c103-m5 : regularization · c036-m5 : annotated graphs · c106-m1 : discrete convolution · c106-m2 : channels · c106-m3 : pooling · c106-m4 : deep architectures

✿01 See the idea

A pottery studio wants to sort its photographs of ceramics into “cup” and “bowl”. It already has a convolutional network trained on another collection of object images. Does it need to relearn from the pixels everything that makes an image useful for classification?

We can remove this network’s final decision and keep the part that transforms the image into numerical features. This part is the extractor; its output is a representation. The convolutions, channels and spatial pooling studied earlier contribute to this transformation. A new head, placed after this output, learns the studio’s classification task.

Pretraining is the training already carried out on the source task. Transfer means reusing some of what was learned for the target task. The old categories do not automatically become the new studio’s categories. A representation can preserve useful edges without having already learned our “cup or bowl” decision. Its components do not necessarily have a simple meaning that we can name.

We begin by freezing the extractor: its parameters are not modified while the new head learns. It still calculates a different representation for each photo. Fine-tuning then means also modifying some of the extractor’s parameters, if justified. This option must be evaluated; it promises no improvement.

The entire scene, features and values on this page are fictional. Our miniature representation has 2 components, supplied as data so that the calculation can be repeated by hand. We do not invent pixels or weights for a supposedly trained network.

The rule to remember

Transfer the representation, learn the new decision, then check on held-out images whether the reused features actually suit the target task.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Learn from a cup photo without modifying the extractor

Our cup photo produces r = 2 and t = 1. The target label is y = 1 for a cup, compared with 0 for a bowl. The head starts with a = 0, c = 0, b = 0. We take a single step of size η = 1/8; this choice is for teaching, rather than a recommended setting for a real model.

  1. Define the boundary between representation and decision

    x is the photo after fixed preprocessing: size, channel order and normalization must match what the extractor expects. f denotes the pretrained extractor, θ the set of its parameters, and z = (r, t) its output. r and t are the 2 features. The expression fθ(x) means “apply f, with parameters θ, to x”. We are given its output; we do not recalculate its convolutions here.

    z=fθ(x)=(r,t);s=ar+ct+b

    a and c are the weights of the new head; b is its bias, an offset independent of the photo; s is its score. We therefore have 3 parameters to learn: 2 weights and 1 bias. The normalized pixels, r, t, weights, bias, score and label have no physical unit here. Preprocessing is not a parameter to relearn in this exercise.

    We decide “cup” when s ≥ 1/2 and “bowl” otherwise. This threshold is a convention of the toy model. The linear head does not constrain s to lie between 0 and 1: the score is not a probability.

    Calculation assumptions: fixed image and label, no randomness in f, unchanged internal state of f, no regularization added to the loss, and all gradients calculated before changing any weight. Freezing the weights is not enough if a block still modifies its internal statistics: these must also remain fixed to preserve the same representation of the same input.

  2. Calculate the score before adaptation

    Each weight multiplies the corresponding feature. The contributions are a × r = 0 × 2 = 0 and c × t = 0 × 1 = 0. Their sum is 0; adding b = 0 gives s = 0.

    The initial classification therefore reports “bowl” for this cup photo. This mismatch does not call for changing the features by hand: we will train the head using the label.

  3. Measure the error and find the directions for the update

    e is the signed error s − y. It is 0 − 1 = -1. We choose the squared loss L, half the square of this error. This is a calculation choice for the toy model, rather than a requirement for classifying images.

    e=s−y;L=e22

    The square is 1, so L = 1/2 × 1 = 1/2 = 0.50, with no unit. The derivatives of L tell us how the loss varies when each parameter changes slightly; together they form the gradient.

    Ga=∂L∂a=er;Gc=et;Gb=e

    The derivative of the half-square with respect to the score is e; the derivative of the score with respect to a is r, with respect to c is t, and with respect to b is 1. The chain rule multiplies these effects. The gradient for a is -1 × 2 = -2, for c it is -1 × 1 = -1, and for b it is -1. All have no unit in this model.

  4. Apply the step only to the new head

    η, the Greek letter eta, is the learning rate, with no unit here. Descending the loss means subtracting η times the gradient. A prime denotes the value after this update; all gradients use the initial head.

    a′=a−ηGa;c′=c−ηGc;b′=b−ηGb

    For a, the learning rate multiplied by the gradient is 1/8 × (-2) = -1/4, so a′ = 0 − (-1/4) = 1/4. For c, this product is 1/8 × (-1) = -1/8, so c′ = 0 − (-1/8) = 1/8. For the bias, it is 1/8 × (-1) = -1/8, so b′ = 0 − (-1/8) = 1/8.

    The gradients are negative: subtracting them increases the parameters here. The extractor remains θ′ = θ. We apply no update to its parameters; r = 2 and t = 1 therefore remain the data for this same photo under our assumptions.

  5. Pass the same photo through again and check the loss

    With the modified head: a′r = 1/4 × 2 = 1/2 and c′t = 1/8 × 1 = 1/8. Adding gives 5/8; adding the bias 1/8 gives s′ = 3/4 = 0.75, with no unit.

    The error becomes e′ = 3/4 − 1 = -1/4. Its square is 1/16; the new loss is L′ = 1/2 × 1/16 = 1/32 = 0.03125, with no unit. It has decreased on this training photo. The score now exceeds the threshold 1/2: the decision becomes “cup”. None of these observations proves the quality of classification on other photos.

  6. Check another way: track the error directly

    Substitute the updated parameters into s′. This gives s′ = s − ηe(r × r + t × t + 1). The last term comes from the bias: its contribution to the score is multiplied by 1. Subtracting the same label y gives the following error directly.

    e′=[1−η(r2+t2+1)]e

    r × r = 2 × 2 = 4 and t × t = 1 × 1 = 1; their sum is 5 and the sum including the bias term is 6. Then η × 6 = 3/4. The error factor is therefore 1 − 3/4 = 1/4. Multiplying by e = -1 gives -1/4 again, as in the full recalculation. This check can reveal an omitted bias or a reversed sign.

    This reduction depends on the chosen learning rate. A rate that is too large can increase the squared error, even with a frozen extractor; learning fewer parameters does not make every update correct.

  7. Define what this step allows us to conclude

    Transfer here is the reuse of f and its pretrained parameters; the new task supplies the labels that adapt the head. Only calling a ready-made model, without training a new head or adjusting parameters, is inference use: this use alone does not demonstrate transfer trained for our studio.

    Choosing between freezing and fine-tuning requires comparison on a validation set: photos that are not used for updates. Photos of the same object must stay in the same group, to avoid recognizing an object already seen instead of generalizing to a new object. After choosing the model and threshold, a separate test set is used for the final evaluation.

    Preserving the expected preprocessing and documenting the model’s origin come before this comparison. The method must be compared with an explicit baseline, for example training from scratch with the same target protocol. A measured deterioration relative to this baseline would be negative transfer. Our fractions for a single photo provide neither this comparison nor a measured gain.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the steps. Explain what was learned before the studio’s task, what changes during our step, and why a smaller loss on the cup is not enough to validate classification. Then reconstruct the bias term in the direct error check.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “Freezing the extractor means that it no longer calculates anything.”

    It still transforms each image. Only its parameters and, under our deterministic assumption, its internal state remain fixed. The head’s 3 parameters change; the representation of the same photo remains identical under the same preprocessing.

    Identify the parameters that are actually updated

  • “The score 0.75 gives the probability that the image shows a cup.”

    Our head is a weighted sum with a bias; it does not constrain the score to a probability interval. The threshold 1/2 only defines our decision. The loss reduction describes the photo used for training.

    Reread the definition of the score and its loss

  • “An extractor that is good on its source task will be good on all the studio’s photos.”

    Features useful on the source task may lose a distinction needed for the target task. The last exercise constructs this case. A separate evaluation and a comparable protocol are needed to decide whether transfer helps or worsens the result.

    Examine photos that cannot be distinguished

Exercise 1 · Numerical application

Another photo passes through the same extractor

A bowl photo now gives r = 1, t = 2 and y = 0. Without further training, use a′ = 1/4, c′ = 1/8 and b′ = 1/8. Calculate its score and its error relative to the label, then the decision at threshold 1/2. Why does freezing not impose the cup’s features on this photo?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Keep the head’s weights but replace the features with those of the bowl.

Hint 2

Add the bias only once, then compare the score with the stated threshold.

Compare with the solution

The contributions are 1/4 × 1 = 1/4 and 1/8 × 2 = 1/4. Their sum is 1/2; adding 1/8 gives s = 5/8 = 0.625, with no unit. The signed error relative to y is 5/8 − 0 = 5/8.

This score exceeds 1/2: the head reports “cup”, so it is wrong about this bowl. Freezing fixes the function f; it does not fix its output for all inputs. This counterexample rules out claiming general success from the cup alone, but it does not measure performance on a representative set.

Check your reasoning

  • Obtain 5/8 by keeping the weights and changing the features.
  • Identify the classification error (incorrect ranking) and distinguish a signed error from a probability.
  • Explain that the same function can give different outputs for different images.

Exercise 2 · Mechanism diagnosis

Is the claimed freezing complete?

A colleague wants to reproduce our step. She leaves the extractor’s weights fixed, but her calculation reestimates internal statistics from the new photos. The same cup, preprocessed in the same way, now produces different features. Can she reuse our error check as it stands? State what must be fixed, how many parameters the head learns, and what would distinguish fine-tuning.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The derivation of e′ assumes that r and t remain identical during the step.

Hint 2

Distinguish the head’s weights, the extractor’s weights and its internal state.

Compare with the solution

The formula e′ = [1 − η(r × r + t × t + 1)]e assumes the same representation before and after the step. If internal statistics change this output, our derivation no longer applies as it stands, even if the extractor’s weights have not changed.

Preprocessing, parameters, internal statistics and any random computation in the extractor must be fixed to reproduce this deterministic toy model. The head learns 2 weights and 1 bias, giving 3 parameters. Fine-tuning would deliberately modify pretrained parameters of the extractor using the target task; it is not simply a change in its inputs. Its benefit still needs to be checked on the validation set.

Check your reasoning

  • Identify the fixed-representation assumption that has been broken.
  • Count 3 head parameters without confusing features with parameters.
  • Distinguish deliberate modification of pretrained parameters from an internal state changed without the protocol accounting for it.

Exercise 3 · Transfer and reasoning

When the representation has erased the difference

In the same studio, suppose that a cup photo labelled 1 and a bowl photo labelled 0 give exactly the same representation (2, 1). Can a deterministic head that sees only this representation classify both correctly by changing its weights or threshold? Propose a next step that can be checked, without claiming a gain.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Write the score for each photo using the same a, c and b.

Hint 2

A deterministic head gives the same output for the same input; consider the available information before proposing further training.

Compare with the solution

The scores are identical: a × 2 + c × 1 + b for each photo. With the head from our example, both equal 3/4. Any shared threshold therefore produces the same decision for both images. Since their labels differ, the same decision cannot be correct for both. Changing only the head does not recreate the erased distinction.

Examine the images and their labels, then look for a representation that preserves the useful difference: another extractor output, another model, or fine-tuning some parameters. None of these choices guarantees a solution. Comparing them requires separating training and validation objects, fixing the choices before the final test, and recording the observed errors. The bowl photo from the previous exercise does not become independent evidence if we then adjust our choices to classify it correctly.

Check your reasoning

  • Demonstrate equal scores for equal representations.
  • Relate the impossibility to information lost before the head.
  • Propose a change of representation and a separate evaluation without promising a result.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1In the studio, what is transferred and what must the new task learn?

We reuse the extractor f and its pretrained parameters θ. The head learns to transform its features into a cup-or-bowl decision using the target labels. The old categories are not enough to define the new task.

Recall 2What is the difference between a frozen extractor, inference alone and fine-tuning?

With a frozen extractor, the new head learns while the pretrained parameters remain fixed. Inference alone applies a model without training. Fine-tuning also modifies some pretrained parameters. For our deterministic calculation, the extractor’s internal state and preprocessing are also fixed.

Recall 3How can you reconstruct the head’s step from its score and the label?

Calculate s = ar + ct + b, then e = s − y. With L = (e × e)/2, the gradients are e × r for a, e × t for c, and e for b. Subtract η times each gradient from the corresponding parameter, calculating all gradients using the same initial head. Keep r and t to recalculate the score.

Recall 4Why can a head alone not separate a cup and a bowl with exactly the same representation?

It receives the same input and produces the same deterministic output; a shared threshold gives the same decision. Opposite labels therefore require reconsidering the representation, rather than just extending the head’s training.

Recall 5What is missing between the loss reduction on our cup and evidence that transfer helps the studio?

A comparison with an explicit baseline using the same target protocol, validation photos separated by object, then a final test kept free from model-selection decisions. Errors on the target task must be checked; a reduction in training loss and the presence of a model interface are not enough.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without rereading, reconstruct the path photo → preprocessing → pretrained extractor → representation → new head → decision. Name what stays fixed and what learns. Reconstruct the update from the error relative to the label, then explain why recognizing a cup better proves neither recognition of bowls nor the general usefulness of transfer.
