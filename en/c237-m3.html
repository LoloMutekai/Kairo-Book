Contractions

Your goalCalculate the contraction over one index of a symmetric kernel with two indices, relate its norm to the fourth cumulant of an element of the second chaos, then interpret the resulting ratio under its assumptions without treating it as a distance to a normal distribution.

Before you start
c236-m5 : Hörmander · c228-m5 : incompleteness · c060-m5 : approximations and assumptions · c237-m1 : Stein's method · c237-m2 : fourth moment

✿01 See the idea

Imagine a fictional test bench tracking 2 independent standard Gaussian fluctuations: each has mean 0 and variance equal to 1. They have no units because they have been put on a common scale. We form a quantity F that combines their squares and their product with fixed weights. All the numbers on this page are fictional: they are used for calculations, not to describe a real machine.

The weights are arranged in a symmetric square array A, the kernel: the entry in row i and column j indicates how much the product of fluctuations i and j contributes to F. In Malliavin calculus, a centered quantity of this kind is an element of the second Wiener chaos, a double integral with kernel A. We keep a finite number of coordinates so that the kernel is an array of numbers; with infinitely many coordinates, the sums become integrals and the idea remains the same.

A contraction connects two copies of the kernel through a shared index. Choose an intermediate index k, combine the weight from i to k with the weight from k to j, then sum over all intermediate indices. The resulting array shows how the sequence of two steps compares with the structure of a single step: if it is small relative to the kernel, the weights are spread out; if it is large, a few directions dominate.

The unit on the fourth moment stated that convergence to a normal distribution, for a sequence of elements of the same chaos with fixed variance, can be determined from the fourth moment. This moment is hard to read directly from the kernel. Contractions provide the connection: they turn the difference in the fourth moment into an array norm that we calculate using sums and products.

The rule to remember

For F in the second chaos with symmetric kernel A, the variance is 2 times the squared norm of A, and the excess of the fourth moment over three times the squared variance is 48 times the squared norm of the contraction of A with A; their ratio measures the concentration of the kernel, not a distance to a normal distribution.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Contract a kernel with two coordinates

Two fictional independent standard Gaussian fluctuations form F with a symmetric kernel whose two diagonal entries are 2 and whose two off-diagonal entries are 1. Let us calculate the contraction over one index, the variance, the fourth cumulant and their ratio, then check the results by another method.

  1. Define the symbols and assumptions

    ξ denotes the vector of 2 fluctuations, with no units. Assumptions: they are independent, centered, with variance 1. The kernel A is symmetric: entry (i, j) equals entry (j, i). The symbol δ is 1 when i equals j and 0 otherwise; it centers F so that its mean is 0.

    F=∑i,jAij(ξiξj−δij)

    Without these assumptions (symmetry, independence, standard normal distribution, a finite number of coordinates), the identities that follow are not guaranteed.

  2. Contract the kernel with itself

    In general, the contraction of two kernels with several indices sums over r shared indices. Here we keep only the case we need: two kernels with two indices and r equal to 1.

    (A⊗1A)ij=∑kAikAkj

    Entry in the first row and first column: two intermediate indices contribute. Off-diagonal entry: two intermediate indices also contribute.

    2×2+1×1=4+1=5
    2×1+1×2=2+2=4

    By symmetry, every diagonal entry of the contracted array is 5 and the off-diagonal entries are 4.

    A=(2112)
    A⊗1A=(5445)

    Since the kernel is symmetric, this is the matrix product of the kernel with itself. An entrywise product would give 4 for the first entry, not 5.

  3. Measure the two arrays

    The squared norm of an array is the sum of the squares of all its entries, with no units here.

    ‖A‖2=∑i,jAij2

    For the kernel: 4 for each of the two diagonal entries, giving 8, and 1 for each of the two off-diagonal entries, giving 2.

    ‖A‖2=8+2=10

    For the contraction: 25 for each diagonal entry and 16 for each off-diagonal entry.

    ‖A⊗1A‖2=50+32=82

  4. Calibrate, then apply the variance and fourth cumulant identities

    For centered F, the fourth cumulant is the difference between the fourth moment and three times the squared variance. In the second chaos, the variance and this cumulant can be expressed using the preceding norms; this identity is accepted here without proof. Its proof uses the product formula for two double integrals.

    σ2=E[F2]=2‖A‖2
    κ4(F)=E[F4]−3σ4=48‖A⊗1A‖2

    The factor is calibrated using the simplest case: a single weight equal to 1, so F equals ξ squared minus 1. Both norms are then 1, and the fourth moment of F is 60, a value obtained by expanding and using the even moments of the standard normal distribution.

    E[(ξ2−1)4]=60,60−3×2×2=60−12=48

    Let us apply this to the numbers in the lesson.

    σ2=2×10=20;κ4=48×82=3936;σ4=400

  5. Form and interpret the ratio

    The ratio of the fourth cumulant to the squared variance is the excess of the normalized fourth moment; it is 0 for a normal distribution. It now depends only on the shape of the kernel.

    κ4(F)σ4=3936400=12‖A⊗1A‖2‖A‖4=12×8210×10=9.84

    The same ratio can be read using only the norms: 12 comes from 48 divided by the product of 2 and 2. The kernel with a single weight gives 12, and the kernel in the lesson gives 9.84: it is far from 0. For a fixed kernel, this describes its concentration; normal approximation concerns a sequence of kernels with fixed variance whose ratio would tend to 0.

  6. Check by another method

    Rotate the axes: the two new coordinates are still independent standard Gaussian variables (an accepted property of rotations of independent standard Gaussian variables).

    η1=ξ1+ξ22,η2=ξ1−ξ22

    The sum of the squares is preserved, and the difference of the squares is 2 times the product of the original coordinates. The cross term of the kernel then disappears: F can be rewritten with weights 3 (the sum of 2 and 1) and 1 (the difference).

    F=3(η12−1)+1(η22−1)

    Each centered Gaussian square has variance 2 and fourth cumulant 48; cumulants add for independent terms, and a weight multiplies the variance by its square and the cumulant by its fourth power.

    9+1=10;81+1=82

    These two sums recover 10 and 82, hence 20 and 3936: two different methods lead to the same numbers.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The contraction is calculated entry by entry, like an entrywise product.”

    It sums over a shared intermediate index: the first entry is 4 + 1 = 5, not 4. Work through the two intermediate indices for one entry again before writing the array.

    Review the contraction step

  • “A small ratio proves that F has a normal distribution.”

    The ratio is interpreted under assumptions: second chaos, fixed variance, a sequence of kernels whose ratio tends to 0. For a fixed kernel it gives no distance; numerical bounds use Stein's method and are not derived again here.

    Reread the intuition and the rule

  • “The word contraction always means a mapping that brings points closer together.”

    In the reviewed code, a annotation uses "contraction mapping" for an exponential moving average: this is a mapping that brings points closer together. Here, contraction is a sum over a shared index of two copies of the kernel. The same word refers to two objects.

    Reread the two cited passages

Exercise 2 · Guided variation

Multiply all the weights

Multiply each entry of the kernel in the lesson by 2. Calculate the squared norm of the new kernel, that of its contraction, then the ratio with the factor 12. Does the ratio change?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Multiplying the kernel by 2 multiplies its squared norm by 4; the contraction contains two copies of the kernel, so its squared norm is multiplied by 16.

Compare with the solution

Squared norm of the kernel: 4 × 10 = 40. Squared norm of the contraction: 16 × 82 = 1312.

Ratio: 12 × 1312 divided by (40 × 40), giving 15744 divided by 1600, equal to 9.84. This is the ratio in the lesson: the scale factors cancel because the numerator and denominator acquire the same fourth power. The ratio describes the shape of the kernel, not its scale.

Check your reasoning

  • Use 4 for the kernel and 16 for the contraction.
  • Conclude that the ratio is unchanged and has no units.
  • Distinguish the squared norm from the norm.

Exercise 3 · Transfer

A dominant weight compared with equal weights

On 4 coordinates, compare two diagonal kernels: the first has one weight 3 and three weights equal to 1; the second has four weights equal to 1. Calculate the ratio for each using the formula in the lesson, then state in one sentence what the distribution of weights shows.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

For a diagonal kernel, the squared norm is the sum of the squared weights and that of the contraction is the sum of their fourth powers.

Hint 2

For equal weights, the ratio depends only on the number of coordinates.

Compare with the solution

Dominant weight: squared norm of the kernel 9 + 3 = 12; squared norm of the contraction 81 + 3 = 84; ratio 12 × 84 divided by (12 × 12), giving 1008 divided by 144, equal to 7.

Equal weights: both squared norms are 4; ratio 12 × 4 divided by 16, that is, 12 divided by 4, equal to 3. With more equal coordinates, 12 divided by their number decreases further.

Interpretation: spreading the weights out lowers the ratio; a dominant weight keeps it high. This is an interpretation of these fictional kernels, not a distance to a normal distribution or a guarantee of convergence.

Check your reasoning

  • Calculate both ratios using the same formula.
  • Compare 7 and 3 without interpreting them as distances.
  • Explain the role of the distribution of weights.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does the contraction over one index of a symmetric kernel with itself calculate?

A sum over a shared intermediate index: entry (i, j) is the sum over k of the products of the weight from i to k and the weight from k to j, that is, the matrix product of the kernel with itself.

Recall 2In the second chaos, what is the fourth cumulant in terms of the contraction?

48 times the squared norm of the contraction of the kernel with itself, with variance equal to 2 times the squared norm of the kernel, under the assumptions of symmetry and independent Gaussian coordinates.

Recall 3Why does the ratio of the cumulant to the squared variance stay the same when the kernel is multiplied by a factor?

The numerator and denominator acquire the same factor to the fourth power: the contraction contains two copies of the kernel and the squared variance contains four.

Recall 4What does a small ratio calculated for a single kernel fail to tell us?

It gives neither a distance to a normal distribution nor proof of convergence: convergence concerns a sequence of kernels with fixed variance, and numerical bounds come from Stein's method.

Recall 5What distinguishes the contraction of a kernel from a contraction mapping?

The former sums over a shared index of two copies of an array; the latter is a mapping that brings points closer together, as in a reviewed code annotation on an exponential moving average.

Reconstruct the reasoning

State the assumptions (independent Gaussian variables, symmetric kernel), contract the kernel with itself by summing over an intermediate index, measure two squared norms, deduce the variance with factor 2 and the fourth cumulant with factor 48, form the ratio that is independent of scale, check it by another method, then interpret it only as a measure of kernel concentration.
