Attribution and provenance

Your goalLink each claim to a specific passage, preserve its origin and identify what the citations do not allow you to conclude.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

You are preparing a session at Linden Workshop. An assistant retrieves the rules and a stock record, then answers: “The session can accommodate at most 12 people; you must book at least 48 h in advance; an apron is provided for each person.” Each part has a citation. Can you pass the whole answer on to your group?

Imagine threads attached to the sentences. Each one must lead to a passage you can reopen. A thread leading to the rules lets you check the capacity. A thread leading to an apron inventory does not guarantee a loan to everyone. A document title can seem relevant even though its content does not justify the sentence.

Attribution here means indicating which passage supports which claim. A claim is a statement that can be checked separately. Provenance preserves the origin and history of the passage: document, responsible party, edition, location, and any copy or transformation. Attribution answers “what supports this sentence?”; provenance lets you find that support again and establish where it came from.

The entire scene, the records, the parties responsible for them and their numbers are fictional. We check an answer against fixed excerpts without measuring a real assistant. Retrieval and assembly have already been done; our work starts with the content given to the model. A list of documents in that content does not, on its own, reveal what actually determined the answer.

The rule to remember

For each claim, find the exact passage, check that it justifies the claim, then preserve the origin and version of that passage.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Check the answer before passing it on

The question is: “What capacity, booking time and equipment apply to our session?” The context contains only the RULES / Admission and STOCK / Cupboard passages below. We audit the 3 claims in the initial answer, each with a citation.

  1. Fix the evidence that can be reopened

    RULES, edition 3, responsible party: the association, Admission passage. Exact excerpt preserved: “The session can accommodate at most 12 people. A booking is requested at least 48 h before the start.” In the initial example, this edition is declared applicable to the session.

    STOCK, edition 1, responsible party: the equipment team, Cupboard passage. Exact excerpt preserved: “The cupboard contains 2 demonstration aprons.” This record does not say whether they can be lent out, or for how many people.

    The fictional assembly log indicates that these passages were copied unchanged into the context for this answer. It keeps the document identifier, edition and passage name with its text. The responsible party identifies who maintains the record; this does not replace checking its content. A title alone would not distinguish a revision or another passage.

  2. Separate the claims and state the checking rule

    Let A denote the claim “at most 12 people”, B the claim “book at least 48 h in advance” and C the claim “an apron is provided for each person”. A cites RULES / Admission; B cites the same passage; C cites STOCK / Cupboard. These letters denote sentences and have no physical unit.

    N is the number of claims checked: N = 3 claims. Each has an indicator a, with no unit: a equals 1 if the cited passage fully justifies the sentence for the session in question, and 0 otherwise. The capacity, booking and loan subscripts identify the relevant sentence. The judgment here is made by reading, without claiming to have an automatic checker.

    We assume that the excerpts are readable, the references can be followed and the scope of the session is fixed. The claims have equal weight in this small count. A sentence combining capacity and equipment must be split before counting; how the claims are divided affects the result.

  3. Compare wording, conditions and scope

    For A, “at most 12 people” exactly preserves the limit in RULES: a_accueil = 1. This expresses a maximum, not guaranteed attendance of 12 people. For B, the passage preserves the minimum and the reference point “before the start”: a_réservation = 1.

    For C, owning 2 demonstration aprons does not imply providing one for each person. A loan rule and a guarantee suitable for the group are missing. We set a_prêt = 0. This lack of support does not demonstrate that no loan arrangement exists; it prevents us from asserting such a loan on the basis of this excerpt alone.

    The stated attribution for C is therefore insufficient, even though its reference exists and concerns the right object. Preserving negation, conditions and quantifiers such as “at most” or “each” is part of the check. A paraphrase can be correct without repeating every word; it must preserve what the passage allows us to conclude.

  4. Calculate the supported share

    S denotes the number of supported claims, measured in claims. We add the indicators to count the fully justified sentences. The supported share q is S divided by N; q has no unit and lies between 0 and 1 when N is positive.

    S=aaccueil+aréservation+aprêt=1+1+0=2

    The intermediate sum gives 1 + 1 = 2; adding 0 leaves 2 supported claims. There remains N − S = 3 − 2 = 1 unsupported claim.

    q=SN=23≈66.67 %

    To express the fraction as a percentage, multiply it by 100. We keep 2/3 as the exact value; 66.67 % is a rounded reading. This number describes only our fictional audit, with this division into claims. It is neither a probability of truth nor a model success rate.

  5. Check by another method

    Count the labels without adding the indicators again: A and B are supported; C is not. Counting by subtraction gives N − unsupported claims = 3 − 1 = 2, which recovers S. The 2 supported claims and the 1 unsupported claim make up the 3 original claims.

    Now count the stated citations: 3 citations for 3 claims, giving 1 = 100 %. This share measures the presence of a reference, while 66.67 % measures our judgment of full support. The disagreement between these counts helps identify the false assurance given by an answer with many citations.

    The capacity 12 is in people, the booking time 48 in hours and the stock 2 in aprons. We never add these together to calculate q: we count judgments about sentences, not the quantities within those sentences. If N were 0, the quotient would be undefined.

  6. Repair the answer while keeping useful references

    The factual answer becomes: “According to RULES, edition 3, Admission passage, the session can accommodate at most 12 people and a booking is requested at least 48 h before the start.” We add the limitation of the available evidence separately: “The available excerpts do not allow us to announce an apron loan for everyone.” This limitation describes our check; it does not claim to be a workshop rule.

    Removing the loan promise leaves 3 − 1 = 2 factual claims about the workshop, with 2 still supported: 2 / 2 = 1 = 100 %. The equipment question remains open. Improving this fraction by deleting a sentence provides no new information about the aprons.

    The preserved provenance lets us return to the same editions and excerpts. A digital fingerprint of the file, if recorded and compared, can help recognize the same bytes. It proves neither the identity of the writer, nor that the rule is current, nor that it is true. Justifying each sentence still requires reading the passage.

Explain it in your own words

Explain why the initial answer has a reference for each claim without being fully supported. Then reconstruct the provenance of the capacity limit and explain what the numerical result leaves open.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “There is a citation after the sentence, so it has been verified.”

    Reopen the passage and compare its meaning with the entire sentence. The apron stock does not guarantee a loan for everyone. Distinguish the presence of a reference from the judgment of support.

    Review the claim audit and its checks

  • “The document has the same title, so its version makes no difference.”

    Keep the edition and excerpt used. A new version can change the capacity. You can describe what the old version said, but announcing the rule that applies today requires checking its validity for the session.

    Examine the change of edition in the last exercise

  • “Several copies of the same record provide several confirmations.”

    Trace the lineage of the copies. If they reproduce the same passage, they share the same origin. Provenance lets you identify this; multiplying citations does not provide independent verification.

Compare with the solution

No. A limit of 12 people does not give the number of people who will attend. The indicator for this new sentence would equal 0, even with the right reference.

Exercise 3 · Transfer · audit a revision

A faithful citation can be out of date

In the same scene, RULES is replaced for the next session by edition 4. The new Admission passage says: “The session can accommodate at most 10 people. A booking is requested at least 48 h before the start.” The log confirms that this edition replaces edition 3 for this session. The old context still contains edition 3 and the repaired answer from the example. Audit its capacity and booking time, then propose the answer and the records to keep.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Separate “faithful to the cited passage” from “applicable to the requested session”.

Hint 2

The booking time and capacity must be compared separately with the new edition.

Compare with the solution

The sentence about 12 people remains faithful to the old edition, but no longer justifies announcing that capacity for the session in question. The reference works; its applicability is out of date. The new maximum is 10 people. The booking time remains at least 48 h before the start, because the new passage preserves it.

The answer becomes: “According to RULES, edition 4, Admission passage, the session can accommodate at most 10 people and a booking is requested at least 48 h before the start.” The loan question remains open with our evidence. Keep the new edition, the exact passage and the indication that it replaces the old edition; keeping the old record lets us explain why the previous answer stated 12 people.

A fingerprint of the old file can confirm that its bytes are unchanged. It does not decide which edition applies. Update the reference and check the content actually assembled before reusing the answer. This exercise asks for a diagnosis and a proposal; it does not perform any system update.

Check your reasoning

  • Distinguish historical faithfulness from the rule applicable to the session.
  • Correct the capacity, check the booking time and maintain the limitation on loans.
  • Name the evidence that makes the revision traceable.
  • Do not treat a fingerprint as a guarantee that content is current or true.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What action checks the attribution of a claim?

Reopen the cited passage and check that it justifies the entire claim, including its conditions and scope. Finding the document is not enough.

Recall 2What records should you keep so you can return to the support used?

The document and its responsible party, the applicable edition, the exact passage and the copies or transformations that brought it into the context. A fingerprint helps recognize the bytes; it does not guarantee truth.

Recall 3How do you reconstruct our supported share, and what does it not measure?

Count the fully justified claims and divide by the claims checked, with a fixed division into claims and a positive denominator. This local fraction is neither a probability of truth nor a model success rate.

Recall 4Why does a copy of a record not become an independent confirmation?

It reproduces the same origin and the same information. Tracing its lineage prevents counting several citations of the same passage as several distinct checks.

Recall 5What can you conclude when the stock does not justify a loan for everyone?

A loan for everyone is not established by this passage. Remove or qualify the promise; this does not demonstrate that no loan arrangement exists at the workshop.
