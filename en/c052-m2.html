Level 2 · C052·M2

Runge-Kutta

Your goalBuild a classical Runge-Kutta step with 4 slopes, distinguishing trial states, rates of change and the final change.

Before you start
c049-m5 : series solutions · c051-m5 : error diagnostics · c013-m5 : recursion · c052-m1 : Euler

✿01 See the idea

You place a cup of warm water in a cooler room. As the water temperature approaches the air temperature, it cools more slowly. Euler, introduced in the previous unit, extends the initial rate of change over the whole step. It therefore ignores how this rate changes during the waiting time.

Runge-Kutta names a family of methods that evaluate the evolution rule at several trial states. Here we study the classical variant with 4 slopes: 1 at the start, 2 at the midpoint of the step and 1 at its end. These trials are provisional calculations. They are neither measurements of the cup nor successive stops in its evolution.

In our fictional scene, the air stays at 20 °C and the water starts at 28 °C. We mainly track the difference from the air temperature: 8 °C at the start. A trial closer to the air temperature gives a slower cooling rate. The diagrams show how this dependence of the rate on the state is taken into account.

Each trial starts again from the initial temperature difference. The slope from the previous trial is used to propose a new difference, and the model is then evaluated there. Only at the end does a weighted average of the slopes provide the change to apply to the initial state.

The rule to remember

Propose a trial state, recalculate the slope there, then combine the 4 slopes with weights 1, 2, 2, 1, divided by 6. Multiply this average slope by the duration before adding it to the initial state.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Predict 1 minute of cooling

Entirely fictional data: air at 20 °C, water initially at 28 °C, cooling rate λ = 0.5 per minute and step h = 1 min. Let us calculate the final temperature difference, then the temperature. Keep exact fractions until the final rounding.

  1. Define the state and the evolution rule

    T denotes the water temperature and Ta the air temperature, in °C. The state x = T − Ta is their difference, also in °C. Time t is in minutes. The derivative x′ is the rate of change of this temperature difference, in °C/min. We assume a uniform water temperature, a constant air temperature and a constant positive rate λ throughout the step. No water is added and no heat is supplied.

    x′=f(t,x)=−λx

    The function f gives a slope from a time and a state. Here it does not depend explicitly on t: only the temperature difference matters. λ is 1/2 per minute, so the product λx is in °C/min. For positive x, x′ is negative: the cup approaches the air temperature. We choose t = 0 min at the start, x = 8 °C and h = 1 min.

    In the formulas that follow, n counts the steps already completed; xn is the approximation retained at time tn. A, B and C denote only provisional temperature differences, in °C. The quantities k1, k2, k3 and k4 will be slopes in °C/min. A slope index identifies its order of evaluation, not a new time step.

  2. Evaluate the initial slope

    k1=f(tn,xn)

    We first evaluate the model at the known state: k1 = −0.5 × 8 = -4 °C/min. Euler would use this slope throughout h. Runge-Kutta first uses it to propose a state at the midpoint of the step.

  3. Propose the first midpoint state, then its slope

    A=xn+h2k1
    k2=f(tn+h2,A)

    The half-step is 1 ÷ 2 = 0.5 min. Over this duration, the initial slope would suggest a change of 0.5 × (-4) = -2 °C. Add it to the initial state: A = 8 + (-2) = 6 °C.

    At t = 0.5 min, we therefore recalculate k2 = −0.5 × 6 = -3 °C/min. The slope is less negative because this trial is closer to the air temperature. A is an argument supplied to f; we do not retain it as the result of the step.

  4. Revise the midpoint state with the new slope

    B=xn+h2k2
    k3=f(tn+h2,B)

    We start again from xn, using k2: the proposed change is 0.5 × (-3) = -3/2 °C. Thus B = 8 + (-3/2) = 6.5 °C. Starting from A would add 2 half-step changes together and would no longer describe this midpoint trial.

    The time remains 0.5 min, but the state changes. The slope becomes k3 = −0.5 × 6.5 = -3.25 °C/min. This shows why the midpoint slopes are not interchangeable: f is evaluated at the same time, with different temperature differences.

  5. Propose an end state to obtain the last slope

    C=xn+hk3
    k4=f(tn+h,C)

    This time we use the full duration: h × k3 = 1 × (-3.25) = -3.25 °C. Trial C = 8 + (-3.25) = 4.75 °C is used to evaluate the slope at time 1 min.

    We obtain k4 = −0.5 × 4.75 = -2.375 °C/min. Even at the end of the step, C remains provisional. The retained value will use all the slopes.

  6. Form the weighted average slope

    k¯=k1+2k2+2k3+k46

    The bar above k denotes the average slope used by the method, in °C/min. The relative weights 1, 2, 2, 1 are part of this Runge-Kutta variant. Their sum is 6: division normalizes the average. They are not probabilities that the trials are reliable.

    Calculate without rounding: 2k2 = -6 °C/min and 2k3 = -13/2 °C/min. The successive sums are -4 + (-6) = -10, then -10 + (-13/2) = -33/2, then -33/2 + (-19/8) = -151/8, all in °C/min.

    Finally k̄ = (-151/8) ÷ 6 = -151/48 °C/min, or approximately -3.1458 °C/min. This last expression is a rounded representation; the fraction continues to be used in the following calculations.

  7. Convert the rate to a change, then recover the temperature

    xn+1=xn+hk¯

    The subscript n + 1 indicates the next retained state. Multiplying the slope by the step converts °C/min × min to °C. Here the change is 1 × (-151/48) = -151/48 °C. The fact that h has the numerical value 1 does not remove the need for this operation or for checking the units.

    The retained temperature difference is 8 + (-151/48) = 233/48 °C, or approximately 4.8542 °C. To recover the water temperature, add the air temperature: 20 + 233/48 = 1193/48 °C, or approximately 24.85 °C. This is not trial C, which gave a temperature difference of 4.75 °C.

  8. Check what the calculation allows us to say

    The weights are positive and normalized: the average slope -151/48 °C/min must lie between the most negative slope, -4 °C/min, and the least negative slope, -19/8 °C/min. It does. The final temperature difference is positive and less than 8 °C: this step predicts cooling without falling below the air temperature.

    Equilibrium check: if the water were already at the air temperature, x would be 0 °C. Every slope would then be 0, every trial would remain 0 and the temperature would not change. Any other result would indicate a formula or implementation error.

    With the same model and step, Euler would give a temperature difference of 8 + 1 × (-4) = 4 °C. The difference from Runge-Kutta is 41/48 °C, approximately 0.85 °C. This difference between methods is not the error relative to the real cup. Here we have neither a reference measurement nor a study of several steps. Global error control and stability are covered in the following units.

Explain it in your own words

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The slope directly tells us how many degrees to subtract.”

    A slope is in °C/min. The change is in °C and is obtained by multiplying by h. Our step of 1 minute hides this numerical distinction: write the units before adding to the state.

    Review the conversion from slope to change

  • “Once the first midpoint state is calculated, I start from it for the second.”

    B still estimates the midpoint of the same step. It starts from xₙ with slope k₂, whereas A started from xₙ with k₁. Adding the new half-step change to A no longer follows the Runge-Kutta construction presented here.

    Review the two trials at the same time

  • “The trial at the end of the step is the result to retain.”

    Trial C is used only to obtain k₄. The result combines k₁, k₂, k₃ and k₄ with the prescribed weights, then multiplies by h. For the cup, 4.75 °C and 4.8542 °C are temperature differences with different roles.

Compare with the solution

The half-step is 0.5 min. Its product with k2 is -3/2 °C, giving B = 8 + (-3/2) = 6.5 °C. The model then gives k3 = −0.5 × 6.5 = -3.25 °C/min.

The time is still 0.5 min after the start. A and B are respectively 6 and 6.5 °C: the same time, different states. Since the rate depends on the temperature difference, their slopes differ. B is a provisional temperature difference and k3 is a rate of change; neither is the final state yet.

Is an almost identical average enough?

Someone uses the same slopes for the cup but adds them without weights, then divides by 4. Compare with the solution

The unweighted sum is -101/8 °C/min, and the unweighted average is -101/32 °C/min. The step then gives a temperature difference of 8 + 1 × (-101/32) = 155/32 °C, or approximately 4.8438 °C.

The variant studied here gives 233/48 °C. The difference between the results is 1/96 °C, or approximately 0.0104 °C. They are close, but the prescribed weights have not been followed. Replace the unweighted average with the weighted sum divided by 6, then recalculate the change.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What are states A, B and C used for?

They are provisional temperature differences where the model is evaluated to obtain the slopes. Each trial starts again from the initial state of the step; only the result of the final combination is retained.

Recall 2Why recalculate a slope 2 times at the same midpoint of the step?

The first trial uses the initial slope; the second uses the slope from the first trial. The proposed states differ even though the time is identical, so a rate that depends on the state can change.

Recall 3Which operation converts a slope to a change?

Multiplication by the step h: °C/min × min gives °C. An average slope cannot be added directly to a state expressed in degrees.

Recall 4How do you reconstruct the final combination for this variant?

Take the slopes in order with weights 1, 2, 2, 1, divide by 6, multiply by h and add to the initial state. The final trial C does not replace this combination.

Recall 5What does the difference between Euler and Runge-Kutta prove about the cup?

It shows that these methods give different approximations for the same model and step. It does not measure the actual error of either method; a suitable reference and an examination of the assumptions are missing.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without rereading, draw the initial state, the 2 midpoint trials and the end trial.
