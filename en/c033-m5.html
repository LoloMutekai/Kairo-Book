Level 1 · C033·M5

Affine approximation

Your goalEstimate an area using the tangent, then check whether the error is acceptable for the chosen change in side length.

Before you start
c031-m5 : asymptotes · c010-m5 : logarithmic scales · c033-m1 : derivative · c033-m2 : composition · c033-m3 : exp and log · c033-m4 : second derivative

✿01 See the idea

A square plate has a side length of 10 cm. You want to estimate its area if this side length increases to 10.2 cm. Imagine adding a strip along each edge involved: almost all the extra area comes from these 2 strips. There is still a small square in the corner.

The affine approximation keeps the known area and the change given by the slope at the starting point. On a graph of area against side length, this means following the tangent: the straight line through the known point with the same slope as the curve at that point. Near this point, the values are close; this does not make the curve a straight line.

The dimensions and tolerance on this page are fictional. The plate is assumed to be perfectly square and flat, with no uncertainty in its side length. We study only the mathematical error of the approximation, rather than flaws in an actual cut. In the exercises, the same plate changes side length again, keeping the same starting point.

Before calculating, look at the figures: which part of the area does the tangent leave out? And why can this omission matter more as the side length moves further from the known value?

The rule to remember

Approximate value = value at the starting point + slope at the starting point × change from that point. Check a tolerance against the error left out.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Estimate the area after a small increase in side length

The side length increases from a = 10 cm to 10.2 cm. We find the area using the tangent at side length a, then check it by calculating the exact area of the square. No new reading is involved.

  1. Name the starting point and the change

    s denotes the variable side length, in cm; a is the reference side length, in cm. The function f maps the side length s to its area, in cm²: f(s) = s squared. h = s − a is the signed change in side length. It is positive for enlargement and negative for reduction. Here, h = 10.2 − 10 = 0.2 cm, or exactly 1/5 cm.

    We work near a, within the domain of positive side lengths. f is differentiable there: its derivative f′ gives the instantaneous change in area per unit of side length. The prime symbol ′ denotes this derivative.

  2. Keep the value and slope at the starting point

    The known area is f(a) = 10 × 10 = 100 cm². For the square, f′(s) = 2 × s; the slope at the starting point is therefore f′(a) = 2 × 10 = 20 cm² per cm. It indicates a local sensitivity, rather than an area.

    L is the name of the affine function representing the tangent. Here, affine means “a starting value plus a constant coefficient multiplied by a change”. This coefficient remains f′(a), even when L is evaluated at another side length.

    L(s)=f(a)+f′(a)×(s−a)

    At the point s = a, the change is 0: L(a) = f(a). The slope of L is f′(a). These properties explain the choice of this straight line.

  3. Calculate the change, then the approximate area

    Multiply the slope by the change in side length: 20 cm² per cm × 0.2 cm = 4 cm². The cm of the change in side length cancel the “per cm” of the slope. Add this change in area to the starting area:

    f(a+h)≈L(a+h)=f(a)+f′(a)h

    Thus L(10.2) = 100 + 4 = 104 cm². The symbol ≈ means “approximately equal”. The equality defines the calculation on the straight line; it does not assert exact equality between the curve and its tangent.

  4. Check using the exact area and the drawing

    Direct calculation gives f(10.2) = 10.2 × 10.2 = 104.04 cm². Let r(h) be the signed remainder: exact area minus approximate area. Here it is 104.04 − 104 = 0.04 cm². The absolute error is |r(h)|, its magnitude without the sign; here it is also 0.04 cm².

    Another approach: expanding the square, or adding the pieces in the first drawing, gives an exact identity:

    (a+h)2=a2+2ah+h2

    The strips contribute 2 × 2.00 = 4 cm². The small square contributes h × h = 0.2 × 0.2 = 0.04 cm². Splitting the shape into pieces gives exactly the same remainder. For this function, r(h) = h squared, even when h is negative; the expansion remains valid.

  5. State precisely what “local” guarantees

    Differentiability at a means that the remainder divided by the nonzero change in side length tends to 0 as h tends to 0. This ratio has the unit of a slope, here cm² per cm. This is the mathematical reason why the tangent describes the change near the point.

    f(a+h)=f(a)+f′(a)h+r(h)

    For our square, r(h)/h = h for nonzero h: this ratio does tend to 0. Here the relative change h/a is 0.02, with no unit. This indicator describes the small change in side length, but does not replace checking the error.

    No universal threshold says that a change is small enough. If the workshop accepts at most 0.10 cm² of error, our 0.04 cm² is acceptable in this model. A different requirement could change the decision. Differentiability alone does not provide a numerical bound for a chosen change in side length.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the results. Starting from the square and its strips, reconstruct the tangent and then the omitted term. Why do we multiply the slope by h rather than by the final side length? Why does the approximate result depend on the chosen point a?

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I multiply the slope by the final side length.”

    The change starts from the point a already accounted for in f(a). We therefore need to multiply f′(a) by s − a. Test s = a: the addition must be 0 and the approximate area must give f(a) again.

    Find the change and check the starting point

  • “The slope directly gives the new area.”

    The slope is in cm² per cm. Multiplication by h gives a change in cm², which still needs to be added to the starting area. Name these quantities before calculating them.

    Review the units of the slope and the change

  • “A tangent that is accurate at the start stays accurate everywhere.”

    The omitted small square grows with the square of the change in side length. Compare the error with the required tolerance, even if the sign of the change seems correct. The last exercise compares a small and a large increase for the same plate.

    Test the approximation against a tolerance

Exercise 1 · Application · sign and units

Shorten the side

The side length becomes 9.9 cm. Keep the tangent at side length a = 10 cm. Before calculating, predict the sign of h and the sign of the change in area. Then calculate the approximate area, the exact area and the signed remainder.

Hint 1

Subtract the starting side length from the final side length; keep the sign of the result.

Hint 2

Calculate the remainder in the order exact area minus approximate area. A negative change also has a positive square.

Compare with the solution

h = 9.9 − 10 = -0.1 cm. The slope 20 cm² per cm is positive: the approximate change in area is therefore negative. It is 20 × (-0.1) = -2 cm².

L(9.9) = 100 + (-2) = 98 cm². The exact calculation gives 9.9 × 9.9 = 98.01 cm².

The remainder is 98.01 − 98 = 0.01 cm², which is positive. Check: (-0.1) × (-0.1) gives the same remainder. The area decreases, but the tangent still underestimates the exact area.

Check your reasoning

  • Distinguish a negative change in side length, a positive slope and a negative change in area.
  • Obtain 98 cm² by adding the change to the starting area.
  • Check the remainder 0.01 cm² using the square of the change in side length.

Exercise 2 · Reasoning · effect on the error

Double the change in side length

The initial increase of 0.2 cm becomes 0.4 cm, still starting from a = 10 cm. Without recalculating the tangent, predict what happens to the approximate change in area and the remainder. Then check the areas and the factor relating the errors.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The change given by the tangent is proportional to h; the remainder for the square is proportional to h squared.

Hint 2

Calculate the new side length, then its exact area, and compare it with the same straight line L.

Compare with the solution

The slope remains 20 cm² per cm because a has not changed. The change in area doubles: 20 × 0.4 = 8 cm². Thus L(10.4) = 100 + 8 = 108 cm².

The exact area is 10.4 × 10.4 = 108.16 cm². The remainder is 108.16 − 108 = 0.16 cm². Dividing by the initial error gives 0.16/0.04 = 4: the remainder has been multiplied by 4.

The symbolic check explains this result: (2h) squared = 4 times h squared. The change in area and the error therefore do not scale by the same factor. This exact rule for the remainder comes from our square function; it cannot be deduced from differentiability alone for an arbitrary function.

Check your reasoning

  • State the factors before calculating and keep the same point a.
  • Find 108 cm² and 108.16 cm² and distinguish their roles.
  • Justify the factor 4 using the square of the change in side length.

Exercise 3 · Reasoned decision · local limit

Accept an estimate for a specific task

The workshop accepts an absolute error no greater than 0.10 cm². The proposal is to use the tangent at side length 10 cm for side lengths 10.2 cm and 12 cm. Decide in each case. Also justify why “the slope has the right sign” is not enough.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The remainder for the small increase has already been calculated. For the large increase, first find the change from a.

Hint 2

You can check using the exact area or directly using h squared, which we have proved here to equal the remainder.

Compare with the solution

For 10.2 cm, the remainder 0.04 cm² is less than 0.10 cm²: the estimate 104 cm² meets this requirement in the stated geometric model.

For 12 cm, h = 12 − 10 = 2 cm. The predicted increase is 20 × 2 = 40 cm²; L(12) = 100 + 40 = 140 cm². The exact calculation gives 12 × 12 = 144 cm². The remainder is 4 cm², well beyond 0.10 cm²; this estimate is rejected for the task.

Check: 2 × 2 = 4 cm² of error. The positive slope correctly predicts an increase, but does not bound the numerical gap. Changing the reference point or calculating the exact area would be a new method that needs to be stated explicitly; the direction of change alone does not establish that the tangent is accurate enough.

Check your reasoning

  • Give a decision for each side length and compare quantities in cm².
  • Calculate the change from 10 cm, without confusing it with the final side length.
  • Limit acceptance to the stated tolerance and assumptions, without promising an accurate physical cut.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1How can you reconstruct the affine approximation at a from the value and derivative?

L(s) = f(a) + f′(a)(s − a). Start with the known value and add the slope at the starting point multiplied by the change from that point. L is the function representing the tangent.

Recall 2For the area of the plate, which units distinguish the slope, the change in side length and the change in area?

The slope is in cm² per cm, the change in side length in cm and their product in cm². This product is a change in area: you still need to add the starting area to obtain the approximate area.

Recall 3Which part does the tangent leave out for our square? How can you check it?

It leaves out the term h squared. The remainder r(h), exact area minus approximate area, is exactly h squared here. Direct calculation of the area and splitting it into the square, strips and corner give the same check.

Recall 4What does differentiability guarantee, and what must you check to accept a chosen change in side length?

The ratio r(h)/h tends to 0 as h tends to 0 while remaining nonzero. This describes local behaviour, without a universal numerical threshold. For the chosen task, you need to compare a calculated error or a justified bound with the required tolerance.

Recall 5What must you keep if the final side length changes but the tangent remains the one at a?

Keep the same f(a) and the same slope f′(a), then recalculate the change s − a and the error. Modifying the slope means changing the straight line used; this choice must be explicit.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without consulting the page, draw the original square, its strips and its corner. Name a, h, f(a), f′(a), L and r(h), then reconstruct the formula and its units. Finish by comparing the error with a tolerance.
