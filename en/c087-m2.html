Level 2 · C087·M2

Sleep pressure

Your goalExplain why the history of wakefulness and sleep matters, then track sleep pressure in an explicitly fictional model.

Before you start
c086-m5 : emotion and cognition · c056-m5 : effect size · c087-m1 : circadian rhythms

✿01 See the idea

Maya gets up, spends the day on her activities, then sleeps. During wakefulness, something depends on all the time she has already spent awake: sleep pressure. Imagine a level that rises during wakefulness and falls during sleep. Waking up in the morning does not magically erase the entire previous history.

Sleep pressure is the component of the need to sleep that depends on the history of wakefulness and sleep. It is called homeostatic: sleep reduces what has accumulated during wakefulness. Here, the word pressure means a tendency to sleep, with no unit of mechanical pressure. The level shown represents this component; it is not a liquid in the brain.

The circadian rhythm, introduced in the previous unit, governs a variation linked to biological time. Sleep pressure follows the alternation of wakefulness and sleep. These 2 components contribute to the propensity to sleep: feeling temporarily alert therefore does not show that the pressure has disappeared. We focus on sleep pressure alone below.

Maya’s day and all its numbers are fictional. We give the model an initial level of 20 arbitrary units, abbreviated a.u., and an upper reference level of 100 a.u. These units express neither a biological concentration nor a percentage of sleepiness. The values only serve to follow the reasoning.

The rule to remember

During wakefulness, track what accumulates; during sleep, track what remains. Use the resulting state as the starting point for the next step, keeping it distinct from biological time and subjective feelings.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Track Maya’s day without resetting the level to 0

On waking, S = 20 a.u. Maya stays awake for 16 h, then actually sleeps for 8 h. We divide this history into steps of 4 h. The initial level, upper reference level and fractions governing the changes are fictional choices for calculation, with no calibration using Maya or a population.

  1. Define the level, duration and limits

    S denotes the pressure represented at the start of a step, in a.u. S′, read as “S next”, denotes its level at the end of that same step; the prime marks a step, not a derivative. U is the fixed upper reference level, here 100 a.u. The chosen lower reference level is 0 a.u. We start with S between these reference levels.

    q is the retained fraction, with no unit, here q = 1/2 = 0.50. The step lasts Δt = 4 h; Δt denotes the elapsed duration. The same q is chosen for wakefulness and sleep solely to make the calculation easier. These are not established physiological constants.

    Each step must be spent entirely awake or entirely asleep. The model keeps its parameters fixed and here ignores circadian time, individual differences and transitions partway through a step. It does not allow this same q to be applied to any arbitrary duration. There are 16 ÷ 4 = 4 waking steps and 8 ÷ 4 = 2 sleep steps.

  2. During wakefulness, reduce the gap to the upper reference level

    U − S measures how much remains before the upper reference level. Retain the fraction q of this gap, then subtract the remaining gap from U to recover the level. Reducing the gap raises S.

    S′=U−q(U−S) pendant la veille

    First step: the gap is 100 − 20 = 80 a.u. The remaining gap is 1/2 × 80 = 40 a.u. The pressure becomes 100 − 40 = 60 a.u. after 4 h. Multiplying S directly by q would instead have lowered the pressure during wakefulness.

  3. Continue the waking steps from the previous result

    After 8 h awake: start from 60. The gap 40 becomes 1/2 × 40 = 20 a.u. The level is therefore 100 − 20 = 80 a.u.

    After 12 h: the gap 20 becomes 1/2 × 20 = 10 a.u. The pressure is 100 − 10 = 90 a.u.

    After 16 h, at bedtime: the gap 10 becomes 1/2 × 10 = 5 a.u. The pressure reaches 100 − 5 = 95 a.u. Each operation starts from the last step, without starting again at 20.

  4. During sleep, reduce the remaining pressure

    With our lower reference level set to 0, the sleep rule retains a fraction q of S. It does not subtract a fixed number of units at each step.

    S′=qS pendant le sommeil

    After 4 h actually asleep: 1/2 × 95 = 47.50 a.u. After 8 h: 1/2 × 47.50 = 23.75 a.u. We apply the second reduction to what remains, rather than applying it to 95 again.

    Over this entire episode, the decrease is 95 − 23.75 = 71.25 a.u. The remaining fraction is 23.75 ÷ 95 = 1/4, which is 1/2 × 1/2. The pressure decreases without being instantly reset to 0.

  5. Check using another form and the bounds

    The waking rule can also be written S′ = (1 − q)U + qS. This is the sum of 1/2 of the upper reference level and 1/2 of the previous level. For the first step: 1/2 × 100 + 1/2 × 20 = 60 a.u. We recover the result without subtracting the gap.

    With q between 0 and 1, this rule places S′ between S and U. Sleep places S′ between 0 and S. Our sequence meets these checks: 20 < 60 < 80 < 90 < 95 < 100, then 0 < 23.75 < 47.50 < 95. All these comparisons use a.u.

    Reverse check for sleep: 23.75 × 2 = 47.50, then 47.50 × 2 = 95. Recovering the bedtime level checks the arithmetic; it does not validate the biological parameters.

  6. State exactly what this calculation describes

    The level depends on the initial state and the sequence of waking and sleeping states. For equal durations, wakefulness and sleep do not apply the same rule. Here, the pattern of approaching a reference level represents gradual accumulation or dissipation, with increasingly smaller changes.

    The result 23.75 a.u. is neither a sleep debt in hours nor a prediction of Maya’s alertness. It provides no threshold for falling asleep or required personal sleep duration. To quantify real pressure, we would need to choose observations, estimate the parameters and check the model; our fictional day does not establish that work.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “If Maya feels alert, her sleep pressure has returned to its minimum.”

    Subjective feelings alone do not reveal the homeostatic component. Biological time also contributes to the propensity to sleep. Consider the questions “How long has she been awake?” and “Where is she in her circadian rhythm?” separately.

    Distinguish the history of wakefulness from biological time

  • “Each waking step always adds the same amount, then sleep resets everything to 0.”

    The model applies a fraction to what remains: the gap to the upper limit during wakefulness, and the pressure during sleep. The changes therefore depend on the starting level. Work through the successive operations again rather than turning q into a fixed amount per hour.

    Recalculate the gap, then the remaining pressure

  • “The units on the curve measure adenosine and prove that the software knows when I need to sleep.”

    The a.u. belong to our fictional representation. Adenosine is a molecule; a number named after it in a program may be a proxy used in a calculation. Read what feeds into that number, then distinguish the code present, the reading and validation.

Exercise 1 · Application and comparison

Equal durations, different increases

Return to Maya waking at 20 a.u., with U = 100 a.u. and q = 1/2 per step of 4 h. Calculate S after 4 h and then after 8 h awake. Compare the increases during these steps and explain the difference.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

At each waking step, start with U − S, then retain the fraction q of this gap.

Hint 2

To find an increase, subtract the level at the start of the step from the level at the end of the same step.

Compare with the solution

First level: 100 − 1/2 × (100 − 20) = 60 a.u. Second level: 100 − 1/2 × (100 − 60) = 80 a.u. You must use 60 as the starting level for the second step.

The increases are 60 − 20 = 40 a.u., then 80 − 60 = 20 a.u. The second step starts closer to the upper limit. The same fraction of a smaller gap gives a smaller increase. Duration alone therefore does not determine the increase in this model.

Check your reasoning

  • I obtain the levels and increases in the same unit, a.u.
  • I start from the previous result at the second step.
  • I explain the difference using the remaining gap, without attributing it to a reading taken from Maya.

Sleep starts from a different level

Hint 1

Sleep retains the fraction q of S, without using the upper reference level.

Hint 2

The number of steps is unchanged; compare the levels at the start of sleep.

Compare with the solution

After 4 h asleep: 1/2 × 90 = 45 a.u. After 8 h: 1/2 × 45 = 22.50 a.u. The second step retains a fraction of what remains after the first.

In the example, sleep started at 95 a.u. and ended at 23.75 a.u. The variant starts at 90 a.u. and ends at 22.50 a.u. The final fraction is the same, 1/4 of the level at the start of sleep, but the initial levels differ. This comparison concerns the model; it does not prescribe a bedtime.

Exercise 3 · Transfer and critique of a conclusion

Being in bed does not specify whether someone is asleep

Return to bedtime in the example: 95 a.u. after 16 h awake. Maya stays in bed for 4 h, but the problem does not specify whether she sleeps. Can we automatically assign the result 47.50 a.u.? Calculate the possible results if she stays awake throughout or sleeps for the entire step. What would we need to know before choosing?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Location does not select the rule: you need to know whether she is awake or asleep during the step.

Hint 2

For wakefulness, the initial gap is 5 a.u. For sleep, start directly from 95 a.u.

Compare with the solution

If she stays awake for the entire step: the gap becomes 1/2 × 5 = 2.50 a.u. The level becomes 100 − 2.50 = 97.50 a.u. If she sleeps for the entire step: 1/2 × 95 = 47.50 a.u. These results refer to the same instant, 20 h after the initial waking time.

The difference between these branches is 97.50 − 47.50 = 50 a.u. This is a difference between conditional calculations, with no actual benefit measured. Simply being in bed does not allow us to choose a branch. The actual history of wakefulness and sleep is missing.

If the step mixes wakefulness and sleep, neither of these branches for a whole step describes the history exactly. Our rule for 4 h provides no coefficient for a shorter episode: we need to specify the division into steps and the model before calculating. Quiet wakefulness remains wakefulness in this representation, which does not describe all the effects of rest.

Check your reasoning

  • I provide the branches with their assumptions, without arbitrarily choosing the sleep branch.
  • I distinguish time in bed from time actually asleep.
  • I point out the limit for mixed steps and the lack of a coefficient for shorter durations.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What history determines the homeostatic component of the need to sleep?

The sequence of wakefulness and sleep: pressure tends to increase during wakefulness and decrease during sleep. The starting level also matters.

Recall 2Why does a moment when Maya feels alert fail to demonstrate low pressure?

Biological time also contributes to the propensity to sleep. Subjective feelings do not allow us to isolate the pressure accumulated during wakefulness.

Recall 3In our model, which quantity does q apply to during each phase?

During wakefulness, q retains a fraction of the gap U − S, then we recover S′ = U − q(U − S). During sleep, with the lower reference level set to 0 here, q retains a fraction of S: S′ = qS. These rules apply to the specified fictional steps.

Recall 4Why should we avoid subtracting the same amount twice during the sleep steps?

Each reduction applies to the level remaining after the previous one. The second step starts from 47.50 a.u., then gives 23.75 a.u. The remaining fraction over the episode is 1/4 of the bedtime level.

Recall 5What conclusions cannot be drawn from Maya’s a.u.?

They measure neither a biological concentration, nor hours of debt, nor a percentage of sleepiness. This fictional model does not establish a personal threshold for falling asleep or a sleep requirement.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Reconstruct from memory: define pressure from the history of wakefulness and sleep; distinguish it from the circadian rhythm; identify the quantity reduced in each rule; link the steps using the result of the previous one; finish by stating what arbitrary units do not prove.
