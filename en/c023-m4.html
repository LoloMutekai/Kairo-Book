Level 2 · C023·M4

Continuous updates and errors

Your goalUpdate export tracking from validated messages, keep the latest useful information and report an interruption honestly.

Before you start
c021-m5 : origin, authentication and authorization · c018-m5 : contract tests and test doubles · c023-m1 : HTML, CSS, DOM and events · c023-m2 : JavaScript, TypeScript and message types · c023-m3 : React components and state

✿01 See the idea

You are exporting a notebook of 24 pages. The screen shows “9 pages prepared”. The connection drops. Has preparation of the notebook stopped? You cannot tell from this screen alone: you have lost the means of receiving further updates, but have no evidence that the export has failed.

Continuous updates change the screen as messages arrive, without reloading the whole page. Here, each tracking message contains a complete snapshot: the count already reached for this export. Receiving 9 pages after 6 replaces the previous count; these are not page counts to add together.

Keep the latest accepted snapshot, the connection state and the wait for new confirmation separate. The React component displays this information together: “Latest update: 9 of 24; connection interrupted; current state needs checking”. If no snapshot has been received, it shows “progress unknown”, never an invented count.

The entire scene, its messages, its numbers and its silence threshold are fictional. We are studying how a message becomes visible state, rather than how the notebook is produced. The diagrams show successive states; distances in them do not represent durations.

The rule to remember

Validate before replacing state; record the time of the latest accepted receipt; distinguish lost tracking, confirmed failure and confirmed completion of the export.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Track the same notebook through an interruption

Initially, at t = 0 s, the accepted update has revision 7, 6 pages prepared out of 24 and the status “in progress”. The next snapshot arrives at t = 3 s: revision 8, 9 pages, the same total and the same status. An old published message then arrives again; the connection drops and later returns. Let us reconstruct what the screen can claim at each step.

  1. Define the contract before rendering

    An expected message has the type “snapshot”, our export identifier “notebook”, a revision v, a count c, a total N and a status s. v is an integer without a unit that orders the messages published by the source; c and N are integer page counts, with N = 24. The status is one of “in progress”, “completed” or “failed”.

    In this fictional contract, each published message contains all the tracking information and receives a strictly increasing revision for the same export, including after reconnection and even if the count has not changed. A repeat carries the same content and the same revision. These rules are assumptions to obtain from the producer; they do not follow from the existence of a connection.

    t is the local elapsed time, in seconds, and r is the local receipt time of the latest accepted snapshot. The same clock, which does not go backwards, is used for both. A is the age since that receipt, in seconds; D = 8 s is our fictional warning threshold. This age does not measure the delay between message creation and receipt.

  2. Decide which messages can change state

    Decoding the message is not enough. Check the type, the target “notebook”, the integer fields, the expected total and the bounds 0 ≤ c ≤ N. A “completed” status also requires c = N. A malformed or inconsistent message leaves the snapshot intact and triggers “invalid tracking message”. A message for another export is simply ignored.

    If a snapshot exists, compare revisions. A lower revision is old: ignore it. An equal revision with identical content is a duplicate: ignore that too, without changing r. The same revision with different content violates the contract: keep the state and report the inconsistency. A higher, valid revision replaces the snapshot and sets r to the receipt time; without an initial state, the first valid snapshot is accepted.

    In React, this transition is calculated from the previous state supplied to the update function, rather than from an old copy captured by the message handler. It produces a new state without modifying the old one. The message subscription is removed when the effect is cleaned up; its timers are also cleared. Otherwise, leaving and returning could leave competing handlers active.

    Closing the channel preserves the data and marks tracking as interrupted. Opening it alone leaves “checking in progress”. On reconnection, request a complete snapshot to resume tracking, issued after this opening; its valid receipt ends the wait. A network error never becomes the task status “failed” or “completed”.

  3. Replace the count and calculate progress

    At t = 3 s, revision 8 exceeds 7 and the message is valid. The count becomes 9 pages, and r becomes 3 s. The progress observed since the previous update is 9 − 6 = 3 pages; the snapshot does not report 9 additional pages.

    P denotes the number displayed before the % sign, without a unit. Dividing c by N gives the prepared fraction; multiplying by 100 expresses it as a percentage. R is the number of pages still to be prepared in this snapshot.

    P=100×cN=100×924=37.5 %
    R=N−c=24−9=15 pages

    Check: 9 + 15 = 24 pages. Another method: 6 pages represent 25 % of the notebook; the ratio 9/6 is 1.5. Multiplying 25 % by this factor gives 37.5 % again. This percentage describes a page count, rather than a probability of success or an estimate of the time remaining.

  4. Prevent an old message from making tracking look recent

    At t = 4 s, revision 7 arrives again, with its old count 6. The difference 7 − 8 = -1 shows that it precedes the revision being kept. We therefore keep 9 pages and r = 3 s.

    The age is then 4 − 3 = 1 s. Recording the time of any incoming frame would make tracking look recent when it has not been updated. Here the messages are complete: a revision gap still leaves usable information; if the producer sent only differences, skipping a message would require another procedure.

  5. Calculate silence and display the warning

    The channel closes at t = 5 s. As soon as this closure is known, display “connection interrupted”, without waiting for the threshold. At t = 12 s, no new published message has been accepted:

    A=t−r=12−3=9 s

    9 s exceeds D = 8 s. The threshold would already be reached at r + D = 3 + 8 = 11 s; our rule warns as soon as A ≥ D, including equality. This time check also runs without incoming messages, through a periodic local check; otherwise, a stream that had gone silent would never trigger its own alert.

    The screen can show “Latest update: 9 of 24, or 37.5 %; received 9 s ago; connection interrupted; current state needs checking”. The count is neither erased nor presented as current. If the channel remained open but silent, the same threshold would signal “tracking has no new confirmation”, without inventing a network disconnection.

    Reverse check: r + A = 3 + 9 = 12 s. A negative age would suggest that different clocks had been mixed. Before the first receipt, r and A remain absent: no age or percentage is invented.

  6. Resume tracking without announcing an imaginary completion

    At t = 13 s, the channel reopens. The old update is 13 − 3 = 10 s old. Display “connection restored, checking in progress” while keeping the last count with its warning. Reconnection resumes reading the tracking information; it does not resend an export command, whose repetition could start another job.

    At t = 14 s, the complete snapshot requested after resuming arrives: revision 9, 15 pages, total 24, status “in progress”. After validation, P = 100 × 15/24 = 62.5 %, R = 9 pages, r = 14 s and A = 14 − 14 = 0 s. The warning about pending confirmation after reconnection can be removed.

    At t = 17 s, revision 10 reports 24 pages and the status “completed”. The source now confirms completion of this export. A count of 24 out of 24 does give 100 %, but with the status “in progress” the display would still need to say “pages prepared, finalization in progress”. The count alone does not replace the completion status.

    A later closure does not turn this past confirmation into a failure: it prevents us from claiming that the connection is still working. The announced completion establishes the status received from the source here; the quality and actual presence of the exported file would require another check.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I received a number, so I add it to the previous one.”

    Our message is a complete snapshot. 9 replaces 6; the difference 3 represents the newly observed pages. Check the message contract before choosing between replacement and addition.

    Review replacement and its checks

  • “The connection is back: the export succeeded.”

    Opening indicates only that the channel is open. Request the complete snapshot needed to resume tracking. Even 100 % of pages prepared is not enough if the completion status is missing. Do not resend the task command just to repair tracking.

    Distinguish the evidence in the exercise on resuming tracking

  • “When an error occurs, I reset the count to 0 to start fresh.”

    A disconnection or an invalid message does not refute the latest accepted snapshot. Keep it, keep its receipt time and make its warning visible. Without any earlier data, show progress as unknown. An old message or a duplicate does not reset the age.

    Reread the diagram of interrupted tracking

The received number describes everything already prepared, rather than just what has been added.

Hint 2

For new pages, subtract the old count; for the prepared fraction, divide the new count by the total.

Compare with the solution

The new count is 9 pages. The observed progress is 9 − 6 = 3 pages. Adding the snapshots would count the pages already present again.

The fraction is 9/24 = 3/8, giving 37.5 %. There are 24 − 9 = 15 pages remaining. The check 9 + 15 = 24 recovers the total.

Revision 8 was accepted at 3 s with 9 pages. Revision 7 arrives again at 4 s with 6 pages, then the channel closes at 5 s. At 12 s, give the count, r, A and an honest sentence for the screen. The threshold is 8 s: at what exact time would it have been reached even without closure?

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The revision determines the order; arrival time does not make an old published message newer in that order.

Hint 2

Age starts from the latest accepted receipt. The threshold is reached when t − r = D.

Compare with the solution

Revision 7 is old, so it replaces nothing and does not change r. The count remains 9 pages out of 24, and r = 3 s.

A = 12 − 3 = 9 s. The threshold was reached at 3 + 8 = 11 s. Equality counts; there was no need to wait until the time of the check.

One possible sentence is: “Latest update: 9 of 24; received 9 s ago; connection interrupted; current state needs checking”. Closure does not establish that the export has failed.

  • You keep the count and r despite the late arrival.
  • You calculate 9 s and the threshold at 11 s, with units.
  • You report the disconnection without inventing task success or failure.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1A snapshot reports 9 pages after an update showing 6 pages. What does the state become, and why?

It becomes 9 pages, if the message is valid and new. The snapshot contains the complete count; only 3 pages have been added since the previous one.

Recall 2Why does an old published message or a duplicate not reset the age of the tracking information?

It provides no new confirmation of the tracking information. The count and accepted receipt time remain unchanged. The revision orders the published messages; arrival time does not replace that order.

Recall 3How do you calculate the age of the tracking information? What does it measure, and when should you warn?

A = t − r, with t and r taken from the same local clock that does not go backwards. It measures the time since accepted receipt, rather than the delay in producing the message. Warn as soon as the threshold is reached, or immediately if a channel closure is known.

Recall 4What must happen between “channel open” and “tracking confirmed again”?

Request and then validate a complete snapshot issued after resuming, according to the revision contract. Keep the old tracking information with its warning while waiting. Resuming reading does not mean recreating the export.

Recall 5What distinguishes a disconnection, 100 % and confirmed completion?

The disconnection describes the channel. The percentage describes a page count. Confirmed completion requires the status “completed” in a valid message for this export. Checking the produced file remains a separate piece of evidence.
