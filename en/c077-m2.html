Level 2 · C077·M2

Window and time-domain measurement

Your goalSet the boundaries of an audio frame, calculate its duration and RMS amplitude, then explain how the choice of window affects the measurement.

Before you start
c027-m5 : complex numbers · c011-m5 : binomial · c036-m5 : annotated graphs · c021-m5 : origin, authentication and authorization · c077-m1 : sample, frequency and PCM

✿01 See the idea

You examine a tiny fragment of a recorded beep. Instead of summarizing the whole file, you place a small window over a few consecutive samples. The selected section is called a frame here. A measurement calculated on this frame describes that section and depends on its boundaries.

The scene and all its values are fictional. The beep is represented by the frame (-1 ; 1 ; -7 ; 7), at 8000 samples per second, on a single channel. We reduce it to 4 samples so that we can calculate by hand; its duration is not a recommendation for analyzing speech.

Positive and negative values describe the signal’s excursions around its origin. Their mean can be 0 even when the signal varies. To measure an amplitude that does not cancel out this way, we square each value, take the mean, then take the nonnegative square root: this is the root mean square (RMS) amplitude.

The unit u denotes a digital unit of the decoded PCM signal, using the same scale throughout the page. Without microphone calibration, it gives neither an acoustic pressure nor a power in watts. The measurement also cannot identify the beep or recognize words.

The rule to remember

State the boundaries and the sampling rate; count the samples for the duration; average the squares before taking the square root. A local measurement retains the context of its window.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Measure the beep fragment without confusing its summaries

Keep exactly the 4 values (-1 ; 1 ; -7 ; 7) u. The sampling rate fₛ is 8000 Hz. We choose a rectangular window: all selected samples have the same weight, with no attenuation at the edges.

  1. Define the window and its symbols

    xᵢ is the amplitude of the point at index i, in u. The index is a counter with no unit, starting at 0. N is the number of selected samples, here 4; i runs from 0 to N − 1. The point at index N is excluded. fs is the sampling rate, in samples per second, or Hz.

    We assume a single channel, a constant sampling rate, no missing samples and no change in amplitude scale. The window is a selection of consecutive points: no additional weighting is applied. We calculate exactly with the given numbers, without claiming that they describe a real recording.

  2. Convert the count to time

    Δt denotes the time between successive samples, in seconds. T is the frame duration. The fragment’s time origin is chosen as 0 s and the time of point i is tᵢ = i × Δt.

    Δt=1fs;T=NΔt=Nfs

    One period is 1/8000 s, or 0.125 ms, because 1 s = 1000 ms. The duration is 4 / 8000 = 1/2000 s = 0.500 ms. Our convention associates the frame with the interval [0, T): the right boundary is excluded.

    Boundary check: the last point is at (N − 1) / fs = 3/8000 s = 0.375 ms. This time separates the first point from the last; it is one period short of the block duration T. You must therefore not replace N with N − 1 in T.

  3. Choose the step between windows

    H, with no unit, is the advance in number of samples between the starts of successive frames. Take H = 2 while keeping N = 4. The next frame therefore starts 0.250 ms after the first. Each frame’s duration remains 0.500 ms.

    Since H is less than N, the frames overlap by N − H = 2 samples. Their index ranges are [0, 4) and [2, 6), respectively. They reuse points: calculating a measurement more often does not provide as much new data as it would if the frames were disjoint. Here we calculate the amplitude only on the first frame.

  4. Calculate the signed mean

    μ, in u, is the sum of the amplitudes divided by N. The small excursions cancel out: -1 + 1 = 0 u. So do the large ones: -7 + 7 = 0 u. The total sum is 0 u and the mean μ is 0 / 4 = 0 u.

    Here this mean indicates that the frame has no mean offset. It does not mean that all points are 0: the diagram shows excursions on both sides of the origin.

    μ=x0+x1+x2+x3N

  5. Square, then divide by the sample count

    S denotes the sum of the squares, in u squared. Squaring each value removes its sign without losing its size: -1 × -1 = 1, 1 × 1 = 1, -7 × -7 = 49 and 7 × 7 = 49, in u squared. Grouping these gives S = 2 + 98 = 100 u squared.

    M is the mean of the squares, in u squared. Dividing by N prevents simple repetition of the same frame from increasing this mean: M = 100 / 4 = 25 u squared. S and M are not interchangeable; S increases when identical copies are concatenated.

    S=x02+x12+x22+x32;M=SN

  6. Return to an amplitude and check

    R denotes the RMS amplitude, in u. By definition, R is the nonnegative square root of M: the nonnegative number whose square gives M. The symbol √ expresses this operation. Here R = √25 = 5 u, because 5 × 5 = 25 u squared.

    R=M=SN

    A check independent of summing point by point: ignore the signs to group the amplitudes by size. We have a pair of 1 u values and a pair of 7 u values. The mean of the squares is therefore (1 + 49) / 2 = 25 u squared. This gives M again. R = 5 u lies between these extreme sizes, as expected for a root mean square.

    The square root restores the unit u; keeping 25 u as the amplitude would be a unit error. Our R concerns the values around the origin, without removing their mean. On another frame with an offset, that offset would also contribute to R. So do not subtract the mean before calculating the squares requested here.

  7. Also check the PCM count

    Suppose the fragment is stored as signed PCM, with no header, using 2 bytes per sample and a single channel. The 4 values occupy 8 bytes. Dividing 8 by 2 gives 4 samples again; dividing again by 8000 gives 1/2000 s, matching our time calculation.

    This equality uses the stated format and a complete frame. A file size that includes a header or multiple channels cannot be divided in the same way. The amplitude measurement proves neither correct transcription nor proper operation of a real microphone.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the calculated values. Explain why the last point occurs before the end of the block, why the signed mean is 0 and why division by N comes before the square root. Then distinguish the duration of a frame from the time between successive starts.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “From the first point to the last, I already have the whole duration.”

    That span counts N − 1 intervals. The duration of a block of N samples is N / fₛ under this page’s convention. Add the missing period and indicate the excluded final boundary.

    Review the points and boundary N in the first diagram

  • “The mean is 0, so the fragment is silent.”

    Opposite signs can cancel out. Calculate each square again, then their mean and its positive square root. Here μ = 0 u and R = 5 u describe two different properties.

    Calculate the signed mean and then the RMS amplitude again

  • “A longer window must give the same measurement.”

    Lengthening the window changes the included values and the denominator. Repeating the same frame preserves the mean of the squares; adding silence reduces it. Always state what fills the new window.

    Compare repeating the beep with extending it with zeros

Exercise 1 · Locating points in time

A duration shortened by mistake

For the initial frame, someone gives 0.375 ms as the block duration and 0.500 ms as the time between successive window starts. Correct both numbers using N = 4 and H = 2. Also justify the number of shared samples.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Count the periods in the block, then those in the advance H; these are different counts.

Hint 2

The overlap is what remains of the N samples when the start advances by H.

Compare with the solution

The block covers N / fs = 0.500 ms. The number 0.375 ms corresponds only to the gap between the first and last points. The starts are separated by H / fs = 0.250 ms, with 2 new index positions per advance.

The overlap contains N − H = 4 − 2 = 2 samples. The point at boundary N does not belong to the first frame; it is not added to this count.

Check your reasoning

  • Give both times with their unit and the correct count.
  • Explain the missing period between the last point and the end boundary.
  • Justify the overlap as a number of samples.

Exercise 2 · Calculation and checking

Copy the same beep into the window

Concatenate 2 exact copies of the initial frame and keep fₛ = 8000 Hz. Calculate the new sample count, duration, sum of the squares, their mean and the RMS amplitude. Why does the sum increase without the RMS amplitude increasing?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Repetition multiplies the number of points and the sum of the squares by the same factor.

Hint 2

The mean of the squares is a quotient: look at what changes in the numerator and denominator.

Compare with the solution

The count becomes 2 × 4 = 8 samples; the duration is 8 / 8000 = 1/1000 s, or 1.000 ms. The sum of the squares is 2 × 100 = 200 u squared.

The mean is 200 / 8 = 25 u squared. Its positive square root remains 5 u. The common factor cancels in the quotient. Check: each amplitude occurs just as often relative to the total sample count as it did before repetition.

The frame covers more time but the amplitudes have not been multiplied. Confusing an increase in duration with a change in amplitude would lead to a different reported result.

Check your reasoning

  • Distinguish the sum of the squares from the mean of the squares, with the unit u squared.
  • Calculate the duration from the new sample count.
  • Use the quotient and its square root to justify why R is unchanged.

Exercise 3 · Transfer and interpretation

The same beep followed by silence

Start again from the initial frame, without repeating it, and add 12 0 values. Everything stays at the same sampling rate and scale. Calculate the new duration and the RMS amplitude over the entire window. A colleague says that the beep itself has become quieter: what can this calculation actually establish?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The zeros do not change any existing square, but they count toward the new sample count.

Hint 2

Check your proposed square root by squaring it; the points of the initial beep must remain unchanged.

Compare with the solution

The new count is 4 + 12 = 16. The duration becomes 1/500 s, or 2.000 ms. The sum of the squares remains 100 u squared: each added 0 contributes 0 u squared.

The mean of the squares is now 100 / 16 = 25/4 u squared = 6.25 u squared. Its positive square root is 5/2 u = 2.50 u. Check: 2.50 × 2.50 = 6.25 u squared, matching the new mean.

R decreased because the window includes more silence. The 4 beep values remain exactly the same and their measurement on the initial window remains 5 u. The conclusion is therefore a smaller RMS amplitude over the enlarged window; the calculation alone does not establish a reduction in the amplitude of the initial beep or in the actual acoustic level.

Check your reasoning

  • Keep the sum of the squares and increase the denominator.
  • Check the positive square root by squaring it.
  • Attribute the difference to the observed window and distinguish the unchanged beep samples.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What must you state to put a measurement on a frame in context?

The boundaries of the selected points, the sampling rate, the channel and the amplitude scale. Here the window is rectangular: consecutive points have the same weight.

Recall 2Why do N / fₛ and (N − 1) / fₛ not describe the same time?

N / fₛ is the block duration under the convention that excludes the final boundary. (N − 1) / fₛ is the gap between its first and last points, which omits the last period.

Recall 3What is the difference between N and H?

N counts the points in each window; H counts the advance between successive starts. The times are N / fₛ and H / fₛ, respectively. If H is less than N, the windows share N − H points.

Recall 4How can you reconstruct the RMS amplitude and its units?

Square the amplitudes in u, add them, then divide by the sample count N: the mean M is in u squared. Taking its nonnegative square root gives R in u. A signed mean of 0 is not enough to conclude that R is 0.

Recall 5Why can adding silence reduce R without changing the beep values?

The zeros increase the sample count without increasing the sum of the squares. The mean of the squares decreases over the enlarged window. We have changed the section being summarized, without modifying the initial points or establishing an actual acoustic change.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without rereading, draw a frame with its end boundary excluded. Reconstruct the times associated with N and H, then the operations that lead from the amplitudes to μ, S, M and R, with their units. Finish by separately predicting the effect of repetition and of adding silence, before checking your calculations.

What you understand
A frame defines the section being summarized. Its duration depends on the count and the sampling rate; its RMS amplitude also depends on the included values. A large result or a nonzero frame does not prove that words are present.
