Level 2 · C047·M3

Separation

Your goalSolve a separable differential equation, retain its equilibrium solutions, and check the solution against its initial condition.

Before you start
c045-m5 : introductory quadrature · c046-m5 : approximation error · c033-m5 : affine approximation · c047-m1 : direction field · c047-m2 : existence and uniqueness

✿01 See the idea

A cup is at 80 °C in a room held at 20 °C. At first, the temperature gap is large: the cup loses heat quickly. As its temperature approaches room temperature, it cools more slowly. We will recover its entire temperature trajectory from a rule for its slope.

The chosen rule says that the rate of cooling is proportional to the temperature gap between the cup and the room. It is this gap that decreases by the same proportion over equal time intervals. Dividing the displayed temperature by 2 would give 40 °C; dividing the initial gap of 60 °C by 2 gives a cup at 50 °C. The diagrams show this second operation.

Separating variables means grouping what depends on temperature on one side and what depends on time on the other, then integrating. This works here because the rule is a product: a constant factor associated with time and a factor equal to the temperature gap. Before dividing by this gap, we must retain the case where it is 0.

All the values for this cup are fictional. We assume a uniform temperature throughout the liquid, constant room temperature, no added heating, and a constant cooling coefficient. The rule is a model chosen for the exercise; no measurements here validate its suitability for a real cup.

The rule to remember

Find the equilibria before dividing; separate on an interval where division is allowed; integrate; use the initial condition; check the slope and the initial value.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

How long does it take to lose half the gap?

The cup starts at 80 °C; the room stays at 20 °C. The coefficient k is exactly 1/10 per minute, or 0.10 per minute. We seek the solution, then the time τ when the initial gap will have been divided by 2. Logarithms will be kept in exact form.

  1. Write the rule and define the quantities

    t is the elapsed time in minutes, with t ≥ 0. T(t) is the temperature of the cup, in °C; A = 20 °C is the constant room temperature. Tᵢ = 80 °C is the initial temperature: the subscript i means “initial”. T′, or dT/dt, is the slope of T in °C/min. k is positive and its unit is the reciprocal of a minute, so k(T − A) has the unit of a slope.

    dTdt=−k(T−A);T(0)=Ti

    We seek a differentiable function that satisfies this slope at every instant and has the given initial value. Let u(t) = T(t) − A be the gap in °C, and uᵢ = Tᵢ − A = 80 − 20 = 60 °C. Since A is constant, u′ = T′: the equation becomes u′ = −ku.

  2. Retain the solution for which division would be forbidden

    If u = 0 at all times, u′ = 0 = −k × 0: T(t) = A is an equilibrium solution. It does not match our initial value, but it is a solution of the equation and must be recorded before dividing by u.

    For our cup, uᵢ > 0. First, work on the interval containing the initial time where u stays positive. The uniqueness result from the previous unit applies here: the slope −ku is a differentiable function of u. A solution starting above the equilibrium therefore cannot reach it at a finite time, since the equilibrium would be another solution passing through the same point. The resulting formula will also let us check this property directly.

  3. Separate the variables and justify the differential notation

    u′(t)u(t)=−k;duu=−kdt

    The first equality divides the equation by u, which is allowed on the chosen interval. The second uses separation notation; it is justified by the chain rule, rather than by treating dT/dt as an ordinary fraction.

    ln denotes the natural logarithm and exp its inverse function. For a positive quantity, the derivative of ln(u/uᵢ) is u′/u: the constant factor uᵢ cancels by the chain rule. The ratio u/uᵢ is dimensionless, so its logarithm is meaningful. Both sides, u′/u and −k, are expressed per minute.

  4. Integrate from the initial value without forgetting a constant

    Integrate from the initial time to t. In the integral on the left, v is a temperature variable in °C; in the one on the right, s is a time variable in minutes. They are used only to carry out the integrations.

    ∫uiu(t)dvv=−k∫0tds
    ln(u(t)ui)=−kt

    Initially, u/uᵢ = 1, so ln(1) = 0. The limits have already imposed the initial condition: no additional constant needs to be added. The integrals are dimensionless, as is the product kt.

    If the initial gap were negative, we would work on an interval where it stays negative and use an antiderivative ln(|v|/U), with U a positive reference temperature in the same unit. The difference of the antiderivative values would give ln(|u(t)|/|uᵢ|). In our positive case, absolute values are unnecessary. The case uᵢ = 0 has already been retained separately.

  5. Return to the temperature of the cup

    Applying exp to both sides undoes the logarithm. This first gives a ratio; then we multiply by the initial gap and finally add the room temperature back. This is the full temperature, not just its gap from room temperature.

    u(t)=uiexp(−kt)
    T(t)=A+(Ti−A)exp(−kt)

    exp(−kt) is positive at every finite time: the gap keeps its sign. The formula is defined for all t ≥ 0, extends our local calculation, and also recovers the equilibrium when Tᵢ = A. For our cup, it is T(t) = 20 + 60 × exp(−0.10 × t), in °C when t is expressed in minutes.

  6. Calculate the requested time and temperature

    τ, in minutes, is the time when u(τ)/uᵢ = 1/2. We therefore need −kτ = ln(1/2) = −ln(2). Changing the sign and then dividing by the strictly positive k gives:

    τ=ln(2)k=10ln(2) min

    Indeed, 1 ÷ 1/10 = 10 min and kτ = 1 × ln(2), which is dimensionless. At this time, exp(−ln(2)) = 1/2 exactly. The remaining gap is 60 × 1/2 = 30 °C; the temperature is 20 + 30 = 50 °C.

    We have found the first time at which the target is reached: as long as u is positive, u′ = −ku is negative, so the gap is strictly decreasing. The result is an exact time, not a duration measured using a cup. No decimal approximation of the logarithm is needed.

  7. Check the equation and the initial condition separately

    Differentiate the function we obtained: the derivative of exp(−kt) is −k exp(−kt). The constant room temperature has a derivative of 0. The result must reproduce the required slope.

    T′(t)=−k(Ti−A)exp(−kt)=−k(T(t)−A)

    Initially, exp(0) = 1: T(0) = 20 + 60 × 1 = 80 °C. The initial condition is satisfied. This check is separate from checking the derivative: several functions can satisfy the same equation with different initial values.

    The initial slope is −0.10 × 60 = -6 °C/min. At time τ, it is −0.10 × 30 = -3 °C/min. The gap and the absolute value of the slope have been divided by 2. The temperature stays between 20 °C and 80 °C, as expected from the scene.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I divide by T − A, so there is no longer a solution at room temperature.”

    Division excludes only this case from the branch being calculated. First, check T(t) = A in the original equation: its slope and the right side are both 0. Keep this solution, then state the interval where T − A is nonzero.

    Repeat the check before division

  • “Integrating gives me T = A + Tᵢ exp(−kt) directly.”

    The separated variable is u = T − A. Its initial value is uᵢ = Tᵢ − A, not Tᵢ. The proposed formula would start at 100 °C instead of 80 °C. Use the initial limits in the integral, then check the initial value.

    Examine the incorrect solution in the last exercise

  • “After time τ, the temperature must have been divided by 2.”

    The factor 1/2 multiplies the gap from room temperature. Dividing 80 °C by 2 gives 40 °C, whose gap from the room is only 20 °C, or 1/3 of the initial gap. The required gap is 30 °C, giving 50 °C.

    Reread the reference values in the first diagram

Compare with the solution

If Tᵢ = A = 20 °C, the constant choice T(t) = 20 °C gives T′ = 0 °C/min and −k(T − A) = 0 °C/min. This is a valid solution, with no division or logarithm.

For the cup at 80 °C, the gap is 60 °C and the slope is −0.10 × 60 = -6 °C/min. Division is allowed on its interval of nonzero gap; it is not allowed for the constant solution.

Exercise 2 · Solving on paper

Retain only 1/4 of the initial gap

For the cup at 80 °C, keep A and k unchanged. Use separation to find the time when 1/4 of the initial gap remains. Calculate the temperature and slope at this time. Explain why this time is 2 times τ, without assuming that the temperature varies linearly.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Start from ln(u/uᵢ) = −kt, then impose u/uᵢ = 1/4.

Hint 2

1/4 = 1/2 × 1/2. The logarithm of a product is the sum of the logarithms.

Compare with the solution

After retaining u = 0, divide u′ = −ku by u on the positive branch and integrate from uᵢ. This gives ln(u/uᵢ) = −kt. The desired ratio satisfies ln(1/4) = −2 ln(2), since 1/4 = 1/2 × 1/2.

The time is therefore (2/k) ln(2) = 20 ln(2) min, or 2τ. The gap is 60 × 1/4 = 15 °C. The temperature is 20 + 15 = 35 °C.

The slope is −0.10 × 15 = -3/2 °C/min, or -1.50 °C/min. Check: starting from the temperature 50 °C reached at τ, dividing its gap by 2 again does give 35 °C. The factors applied to the gap multiply, while the durations add; the temperature loss per minute is not constant.

Check your reasoning

  • Write the separation and the initial limits, then solve for the time.
  • Obtain 20 ln(2) min, 35 °C, and -1.50 °C/min.
  • Justify the doubling of the time using logarithms or multiplication of the factors, without applying direct proportion to T.

Exercise 3 · Diagnosis and repair

The correct slope with the wrong initial value

Someone proposes the function B below for the cup at 80 °C. B(t) is a temperature in °C. Check its derivative and its initial value. Is it the required solution? Repair it, then explain whether the constant solution T = A belongs among the solutions of the equation or among those of this specific initial value problem.

B(t)=A+Tiexp(−kt)
Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Compare B′ with −k(B − A), then calculate B(0). These checks answer different questions.

Hint 2

The amplitude of the exponential must be the initial gap, not the initial temperature.

Compare with the solution

The derivative is B′(t) = −kTᵢ exp(−kt) = −k(B(t) − A). B does satisfy the differential equation. However, B(0) = A + Tᵢ = 100 °C: its initial value exceeds the one in the problem by 20 °C. A slope equation and an initial condition are two constraints.

Replace the amplitude Tᵢ with Tᵢ − A = 60 °C. The repaired solution is T(t) = A + (Tᵢ − A) exp(−kt). Initially, it gives 80 °C; its derivative reproduces −k(T − A). At time τ, it gives 50 °C again.

The constant function T = A is a solution of the equation, retained before division. It does not satisfy the initial value Tᵢ = 80 °C. Keeping it in the list of solutions of the equation therefore does not create a second solution of our initial value problem; this is consistent with uniqueness.

Check your reasoning

  • Show that B satisfies the slope rule, then identify its incorrect initial value of 100 °C.
  • Repair the amplitude and repeat both checks.
  • Distinguish a solution of the equation from a solution that also satisfies the given initial value.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What makes the rule for the cup separable?

The slope is the product of the constant factor −k and the factor T − A. On a branch where u = T − A is nonzero, we obtain u′/u = −k, then an integral in u equal to an integral in time.

Recall 2What must you examine before dividing by T − A?

The case T = A: it gives a constant solution with 0 slope. Retain it separately, then state the interval where T − A does not vanish for the separation.

Recall 3Why does integrating from the initial value lead to ln(u/uᵢ) = −kt?

For our positive gap, the derivative of ln(u/uᵢ) is u′/u = −k. Initially, the ratio is 1 and its logarithm is 0: the initial condition is already incorporated. The ratio is dimensionless.

Recall 4What is the temperature when the initial gap is divided by 2?

Room temperature stays at 20 °C. The gap goes from 60 °C to 30 °C, so the cup is at 50 °C. The exact time is 10 ln(2) min for our k.

Recall 5What checks should you make after finding a function T(t)?

Differentiate to check T′ = −k(T − A), then calculate T(0) to check the initial value. Also check the units, the sign of the slope, and the cases removed by division. The correct derivative alone does not determine the initial value.
