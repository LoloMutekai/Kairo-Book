Level 4 · C233·M2

HJB

Your goalWrite the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation for scalar drift control, minimize its right-hand side to obtain the control, then test a candidate value function without declaring it optimal.

Before you start
c227-m5 : Ito-Tanaka · c059-m6 : Deterministic discrete Bellman · c167-m5 : initial and boundary value problem · c184-m5 : Longstaff-Schwartz · c233-m1 : costs, constraints and admissibility

✿01 See the idea

A thermostat regulates a room. At each instant it can heat or cool a little, while the air adds random disturbances. Its real question is not just “what should I do now?” but “which control now leaves the best situation for the future?”.

Let x be the difference between the room temperature and the setpoint, in °C, and V(x) the minimum expected discounted future cost starting from x. Bellman’s principle, which we accept here without proof, splits this cost into two parts over a very short time interval: what we pay immediately, and the value V of the random state we reach.

As the time interval becomes very short, we expand each part. The control shifts the arrival state, with an effect proportional to the slope V′. The disturbance has mean 0, but its square is not 0: the curvature V″ turns it into an average effect. Finally, discounting slightly reduces the value at arrival. What remains is a local equation, valid at every x: the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation, abbreviated HJB.

Since the bracketed expression in this equation depends on the control through a parabola, its minimum follows directly from the slope of V. All values in this scenario are fictitious: they are neither temperature readings nor recommended settings, and costs are expressed in fictitious cost units, abbreviated c.u.

This unit teaches you to write the equation and test a candidate. It does not prove that a candidate satisfying it is the true optimal value function: that is the role of the verification theorem in unit C233·M5.

The rule to remember

At every x: the discount rate ρ times V(x) equals the minimum over the control of (running cost + slope × velocity), plus half of σ² times the curvature V″; the minimizer gives the control.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

A fictitious thermostat, tested at point x₀

Fictitious scenario: the room is x₀ = 3 °C above the setpoint. The parameters are ρ = 1 per minute, q = 3 c.u. per °C² per minute, r = 4 c.u.·min per °C², σ = 2 °C per square root of a minute. We test the candidate V(x) = 2 x² + c, seeking the constant c, the control and the local validity of HJB.

  1. Set out the scenario, symbols and assumptions

    The state X is the deviation from the setpoint, in °C. The control u is a rate of correction, in °C per minute; it is negative when cooling. W is a standard Brownian motion: its increments have mean 0, are independent and have variance equal to the duration in minutes. The parameter σ, in °C per square root of a minute, sets the size of the disturbance.

    dXt=utdt+σdWt

    The discounted cost uses a rate ρ (per minute), a state cost q x² and a control cost r u². The discount factor is e to the power −ρt. All these parameters are constant: V depends only on x. We assume the control is admissible in the sense of unit C233·M1 and the cost is finite.

    V(x)=minuE[∫0∞e−ρt(qXt2+rut2)dt]

    Assumptions for this lesson: ρ and r are strictly positive, q is nonnegative, V is twice continuously differentiable with at most quadratic growth so that the random part of the expansion has mean 0, and only the drift is controlled: σ does not depend on u.

  2. Obtain HJB from a short step

    Over a very short time interval of duration h, suppose u is constant. Bellman’s principle gives V(x) ≈ minimum over u of [running cost × h + (1 − ρh) × E[V(X_h)]]. Ito’s formula writes V(X_h) = V(x) + (V′u + half of σ² times V″) h + a martingale term with mean 0, plus higher-order remainders. The factor one half comes from the variance of the noise: (dW)² equals dt.

    Subtract V(x), divide by h and let h tend to 0. The resulting equation is the same for every x:

    ρV(x)=minu{qx2+ru2+V′(x)u}+12σ2V″(x)

    In the limit as h tends to 0, this argument is formal: it assumes the regularity of V, Bellman’s principle and negligible remainders. It does not prove that the solution of the equation is optimal.

  3. Minimize the bracketed expression over u

    For a given V, x is fixed and only u varies. Let φ(u) denote the bracketed expression:

    φ(u)=qx2+ru2+V′(x)u

    This function is a quadratic polynomial in u, with positive leading coefficient r: it is convex, so its unique critical point is its minimum. Its derivative 2ru + V′ vanishes at:

    u*(x)=−V′(x)2r

    Substitution gives the minimum of the bracketed expression:

    φ(u*)=qx2−V′(x)24r

    The minus sign has a meaning: if V increases with x, the slope V′ is positive, and the control must decrease x.

  4. Substitute the candidate and match the coefficients

    For V(x) = a x² + c, the slope and curvature are:

    V(x)=ax2+c, V′(x)=2ax, V″(x)=2a

    Substituting into HJB with the minimum from the previous step gives the right-hand side (q − a²/r) x² + σ² a. Equality must hold for every x: match the coefficient of x² and then the constant.

    ρa=q−a2r
    ρc=σ2a

    With a = 2, ρa equals 2, and q − a²/r equals 3 − 1 = 2: the two sides agree, so a = 2 works. The constant is c = σ²a/ρ = 8/1 = 8 c.u. The candidate becomes V(x) = 2 x² + 8, and the control is proportional to x with a gain:

    u*(x)=−arx

    Here a/r equals 0.5 per minute, giving u* = −0.5 x.

  5. Calculate each term at point x₀

    At point x0 = 3 °C: x0² = 9 °C²; V(x0) = 2 × 9 + 8 = 18 + 8 = 26 c.u.; the slope V′(x0) = 4 × 3 = 12 c.u. per °C; the curvature V″ equals 4 c.u. per °C².

    The control is u* = −V′/(2r) = −12/8 = −1.5 °C per minute, negative because the room is above the setpoint. The running cost is q x0² + r u*² = 27 + 9 = 36 c.u. per minute. The effect of the control on the arrival state is V′u* = −18, so the bracketed expression equals 36 − 18 = 18.

    The effect of the noise is half of σ² times V″, or σ²a since V″ equals 2a: 4 × 2 = 8. The right-hand side equals 18 + 8 = 26 and the left-hand side ρV(x0) = 1 × 26 = 26: the two sides are equal, in c.u. per minute.

  6. Check using other controls

    With no control, u = 0, the bracketed expression would be q x0² = 27 c.u. per minute, greater than 18: the difference is 9. The same calculation with u = −3, the reflection of 0 about u*, also gives 27.

    This is no coincidence: completing the square gives the following form, which is minimized at u* with value 18 and grows as r times the squared deviation from u*. Here the penalty is r × (u*)² = 4 × 2.25 = 9, exactly the difference 9 found above.

    φ(u)=φ(u*)+r(u−u*)2

    If we used the wrong sign and took u = +1.5, the bracketed expression would rise to 36 + 18 = 54: the control would push x even higher.

  7. State what has been established and what has not

    Established under the assumptions of step one: the candidate V(x) = 2 x² + 8 satisfies HJB for every x, because both coefficients have been matched, and it gives the control u* = −0.5 x. Not established here: that this candidate is the optimal value function of the problem. That would require the verification theorem (unit C233·M5) and its admissibility and growth conditions. Also, the thermostat is fictitious: no real room has been measured.

Explain it in your own words

Explain why the constant c does not enter the optimal control but is determined by the noise, then state what is missing to conclude that the candidate is optimal.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The disturbance has mean 0, so it does not change the equation.”

    Its mean is 0, but its square has mean σ² times the duration, and the curvature of V retains this effect. In the example, this term equals 8 c.u. per minute: without it, the right-hand side would equal 18 instead of 26.

    Review the derivation of HJB

  • “Equality checked at a single point proves that the candidate is optimal.”

    A point tests only a single x, and even an identity for every x is insufficient without admissibility and growth conditions. With the constant 6 instead of 8, the residual equals 2 at every point. The verification theorem in unit C233·M5 specifies the conditions.

    Redo the exercise on the wrong constant

  • “The minimizer is u = +V′/(2r).”

    The derivative of r u² + V′u vanishes at −V′/(2r), because positive V′ calls for decreasing x. With the opposite sign, the bracketed expression rises from 18 to 54 c.u. per minute in the example.

    Review minimization over u

Exercise 2 · Diagnostic

A wrong constant

A colleague proposes V(x) = a x² + 6, with the same a. At point x₀, compare ρV with the right-hand side of HJB. What is the difference? Correct the constant using the appropriate equality.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The slope and curvature do not change when a constant is changed.

Hint 2

Calculate ρ(a x₀² + 6), then compare it with the right-hand side from the example.

Compare with the solution

The constant appears in neither V′ nor V″: the right-hand side remains 26. The left-hand side equals ρ × (18 + 6) = 1 × 24 = 24. The difference is 26 − 24 = 2 c.u. per minute, and it is the same at every x. The condition on the constant, ρc = σ²a, requires c = 8; here ρ times the difference between the constants does give the difference between the two sides. The candidate therefore does not solve HJB.

Check your reasoning

  • It is recognized that the constant changes neither the slope nor the curvature.
  • The difference is calculated by subtracting the two sides, with its unit.
  • The correction follows from the condition on the constant rather than numerical tuning.

Exercise 3 · Open problem

A different state cost

Keep ρ, r and σ, but replace q with 8 c.u. per °C² per minute. Find V(x) = a x² + c and the control. First determine a by matching coefficients, justify the choice between the two roots, then give c and the gain.

a2+ρra−qr=0
Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Matching the coefficient of x² gives ρa = q − a²/r; multiply this by r.

Hint 2

The product of the two roots is −q r and their sum is −ρ r: find the positive root and the other one.

Hint 3

A positive cost requires V ≥ 0 for every x: this constrains the sign of a.

Compare with the solution

Multiplying ρa = q − a²/r by r gives a² + ρ r a − q r = 0, or a² + 4 a − 32 = 0. The sum of the roots is −4; one root is a = 4 (the product 4 × 8 = 32 equals −q r up to its sign), and the other is −8. Since costs are positive, V is positive for every x; with negative a, V would become negative for large x. Keep a = 4. The constant follows from ρc = σ²a: c = 4 × 4/1 = 16/1 = 16 c.u., and the gain is a/r = 1 per minute. The candidate still needs verification using the theorem in unit C233·M5.

Check your reasoning

  • The quadratic equation is obtained by matching coefficients rather than guessing.
  • Rejection of the negative root is justified by the positivity of the costs.
  • The gain is expressed with its unit, and the conclusion does not claim to prove optimality.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does the stationary HJB equation for the thermostat say?

The discount rate times V(x) equals the minimum over u of q x² + r u² + V′(x) u, plus half of σ² times V″(x): a local tradeoff that includes noise.

Recall 2Why does noise with mean 0 leave a term in HJB?

Because the square of a Brownian increment has mean equal to the duration: the curvature V″ retains this effect, giving half of σ² times V″.

Recall 3How do you read the optimal control from V?

Minimize the parabola r u² + V′u over u: u* = −V′/(2r), proportional to the slope of V.

Recall 4How do you test the candidate V = a x² + c?

Substitute V′ and V″, then match the coefficient of x² (ρa = q − a²/r) and the constant (ρc = σ²a).

Recall 5Is a candidate satisfying HJB proved optimal?

No, not by this unit: the verification theorem (C233·M5) is needed, with its admissibility and growth conditions.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Set out the state, control, dynamics, cost and discounting with their units; write HJB from a short step and Ito’s formula; minimize the bracketed expression over u; substitute the candidate and match the coefficient and constant; check at a point and with other controls; do not conclude optimality without the verification theorem.
