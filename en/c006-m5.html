Composition

Your goalChain two rules while keeping the intermediate value and its units visible, write the composite rule, then find the times for which it makes sense.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

You turn on a tap above a water bottle. A stopwatch counts the minutes since the tap was turned on; a small display attached to the bottle shows how full it is as a percentage. The display does not see the stopwatch. It responds to the water present, and that water depends on the elapsed time. There is therefore an intermediate step between the time and the display: the volume.

Two rules follow one another. The first, called f, takes a time in minutes (min) and returns a volume in decilitres (dL, a unit of volume). The second, called g, takes that volume and returns a percentage: the fraction of the bottle's capacity, expressed out of 100. The capacity is the volume the bottle holds when full; it is not the current volume.

Composition means connecting the output of f to the input of g. This gives a new rule, called h, which goes from the time to the display through the volume. We write h(t) = g(f(t)): the expression inside the inner parentheses is calculated first. We also write h = g ∘ f, read as “g composed with f”. Despite the written order, f acts first.

This connection makes sense only if the output of f is an allowed input for g: the same unit, dL, and an allowed value, a volume that does not exceed the capacity. The units provide a check: they tell us the order in which the rules can be chained.

All the values in this scene are fictional, chosen so that you can repeat the calculations with pencil and paper. The flow rate, initial volume and capacities do not describe any real water bottle.

The rule to remember

First calculate the output of the inner rule, check that it has the unit and allowed range expected by the outer rule, and only then apply the outer rule.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

The water bottle under the tap

Fictional data: when the tap is turned on, the bottle already contains 2 dL; water flows in at 2 dL/min; the capacity is C = 10 dL. What percentage does the display show at t = 3 min? Then let us find a direct formula and the times for which it actually describes the bottle.

  1. Name the quantities, units and assumptions

    t is the time elapsed since the tap was turned on, in minutes, with t ≥ 0. v is the volume of water in the bottle, in dL. C is the capacity, in dL, fixed throughout the scene. p is the displayed value, in per cent (%). Model assumptions: the flow rate stays constant, nothing leaks, and the display knows the volume exactly.

    f(t)=2t+2

    The coefficient 2 is a flow rate in dL/min: multiplying it by minutes gives dL. The term 2 is in dL: it is the water already present. The sum is therefore a volume, as required. The graph of f is a straight line with slope 2: the volume increases with time.

    g(v)=100vC

    The quotient v/C divides dL by dL: it has no unit and is the fraction of the bottle that is occupied. Multiplying by 100 turns it into a percentage. For this percentage to describe how full the bottle is, g accepts only volumes between 0 and C.

  2. Calculate the intermediate value

    The display cannot process minutes, so we start with the inner rule. f(3) = 2 × 3 + 2 = 6 + 2 = 8 dL.

    Check before going further: 8 dL does not exceed the bottle's 10 dL capacity. This output is therefore an allowed input for g.

  3. Apply the second rule

    Now give this volume to g. The fraction occupied is 8/10 = 4/5, with no unit. Then g(8) = 100 × 4/5 = 80 %.

    The full chain reads 3 min → 8 dL → 80 %. Each arrow changes the unit exactly as the next rule requires.

  4. Substitute to obtain the composite rule

    To avoid repeating the chain for every time, replace v with f(t) in g. This is substitution: the output of f takes the place of the input of g.

    h(t)=g(f(t))=100(2t+2)10=20t+20

    Distribute the factor 100/10 over the two terms in parentheses: 100 × 2/10 = 200/10 = 20 and 100 × 2/10 = 200/10 = 20. The coefficient 20 is in % per minute; the term 20 is in %.

  5. Find the allowed times for h

    The composite rule inherits the conditions of both steps: t ≥ 0 for f, and f(t) ≤ C for g. Find the instant when the bottle is exactly full: 2t + 2 = 10, so 2t = 8, so t = 4 min.

    Since the slope of f is positive, the volume is less than C before this instant and greater afterwards. The domain of h is therefore:

    0≤t≤C−22=4

    At the two endpoints: when the tap is turned on, h is 20 %, because the bottle was not empty; at t = 4 min, f gives 10 dL and the display shows 100 %. Beyond this, the formula keeps producing numbers, but the model no longer describes the bottle.

  6. Check by a second method

    Using the simplified formula: 20 × 3 + 20 = 60 + 20 = 80 %. This matches the 80 % from the chain. The units can also be checked: % per minute multiplied by minutes, plus %, gives %.

    The intermediate volume confirms the meaning: 80 % corresponds to 4/5 of the capacity, that is, 8 dL out of 10, exactly the output of f.

  7. Change only the second rule

    Take the same tap at the same instant, but a bottle with a capacity of 8 dL. f does not change: still 8 dL. The fraction becomes 8/8 = 1, so the display shows 100 %.

    Same volume, different display: the difference is 20 percentage points, with no difference in the amount of water. A conversion depends on its reference quantity. For this bottle, t = 3 min is also exactly the end of the domain, since the volume reaches its capacity.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “We read g(f(t)) from left to right, so g acts first.”

    The expression inside the innermost parentheses is calculated first: f receives t, then g receives f(t). Applying g to the 3 minutes would give 30, a “percentage” unrelated to the water present. Rewrite the chain with its units, min → dL → %, before doing any calculation.

    Review the calculation of the intermediate value

  • “Composition means multiplying the two functions.”

    Multiplying f(t) by g(t) does not pass on any output. At t = 3, this product is 8 × 30 = 240, far from the 80 % on the display and with no meaningful unit. Composition replaces v with f(t) in g.

    Review the substitution that gives h

  • “Once the formula for h is written, the units are no longer useful.”

    The formula h(t) = 20t + 20 accepts any number, but only times from 0 to 4 min describe the bottle. The units check the chain: the reversed order f(g(t)) would produce 62 “dL” from a percentage, which makes no sense.

Exercise 1 · Guided application

Another instant, two methods

Same fictional bottle: C = 10 dL, 2 dL initially, 2 dL/min. What does the display show at t = 2 min? First calculate through the chain, writing down the intermediate value, then check with h(t) = 20t + 20.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Start with f(2), in dL, and check that it does not exceed the capacity.

Hint 2

Then multiply the fraction volume divided by capacity, which has no unit, by 100.

Compare with the solution

Intermediate value: f(2) = 2 × 2 + 2 = 4 + 2 = 6 dL, less than 10 dL: g can receive it.

Second rule: the fraction is 6/10 = 3/5, so g(6) = 100 × 3/5 = 60 %.

Check: 20 × 2 + 20 = 40 + 20 = 60 %. Both methods agree, and the chosen time is indeed in the domain of h.

Check your reasoning

  • The intermediate volume is written with its unit.
  • The comparison with the capacity comes before applying g.
  • The result in % is confirmed by the simplified formula.

Exercise 2 · Changing a rule

A larger water bottle

The tap and the initial water stay the same, but the bottle has a capacity of 12 dL. Write h(t) for this bottle, find its domain, then check that the display shows 100 % at the end of this domain.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Only rule g changes: its denominator becomes 12.

Hint 2

The domain ends when f(t) reaches the new capacity: solve 2t + 2 = 12.

Compare with the solution

Composite rule: h(t) = 100(2t + 2)/12. Distributing gives 200/12 = 50/3 and 200/12 = 50/3, so h(t) = 50/3t + 50/3. As decimals for reading: approximately 16.67 % per minute and 16.67 % initially. Keep the fractions for exact calculations.

Domain: 2t + 2 = 12 gives 2t = 10, so t = 5 min. Allowed times run from 0 to 5 min.

Check through the chain: f(5) = 10 + 2 = 12 dL, then 12/12 = 1, giving 100 %. Check with the formula: 50/3 × 5 + 50/3 = 250/3 + 50/3 = 100 %.

Check your reasoning

  • Only the denominator of g changes; f stays identical.
  • The domain boundary comes from the capacity, not the percentage.
  • The full bottle is checked through the chain and with the formula, using exact fractions.

Exercise 3 · Reasoning

A formula that overflows

Return to the 10 dL bottle. Someone calculates h(5) using h(t) = 20t + 20 and reports the result as the displayed value. Calculate this number, then use the intermediate value to explain why it does not describe how full the bottle is. What is the last valid time?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

First calculate f(5) and compare it with the capacity.

Hint 2

The domain of h respects the conditions of both rules, not just the final formula.

Compare with the solution

The formula gives 20 × 5 + 20 = 100 + 20 = 120. The calculation is correct; its use is not.

Intermediate value: f(5) = 10 + 2 = 12 dL, which is 2 dL more than the capacity. This volume is not an allowed input for g: a 10 dL bottle cannot hold it. Model f, which ignores overflow, has ceased to be valid.

The number 120 therefore does not describe how full the bottle is. Replacing it with 100 would be a new assumption, not stated in the model. The last valid time is t = 4 min, when the display shows 100 %.

Check your reasoning

  • The intermediate value is calculated and compared with the capacity.
  • The conclusion concerns the domain, not a calculation error.
  • No displayed value beyond full is presented as a reading.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1In g(f(t)), which rule acts first, and why?

f, because the inner expression f(t) is calculated first; g then receives its output.

Recall 2What does the intermediate value let you check in the water bottle scene?

That the output of f is a volume in dL, positive and at most equal to the capacity, and therefore an allowed input for g.

Recall 3How do you obtain the formula for h from f and g?

Replace the input v of g with f(t), then simplify while keeping the units: h(t) = g(f(t)).

Recall 4How do you find the domain of the composite rule?

Keep the times allowed for f whose output f(t) is allowed for g; here, the instant when the bottle becomes full sets the boundary.

Recall 5Why does f(g(t)) make no sense in this scene?

g expects dL and would return a percentage; f expects minutes. The units no longer match.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Each rule has an input and an output, with their units and allowed values. Composition means giving the output of the first rule as the input to the second: g(f(t)) applies f then g. The intermediate value and its units check the connection; the formula is obtained by substitution; the domain of the composite rule respects the conditions of both steps.

What you understand
In a dependency traversal, each step starts from the node reached in the previous step, just as g starts from the volume returned by f. A dependency relation indicates that other components may be affected; it does not establish the cause of a failure.
