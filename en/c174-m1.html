Level 3 · C174·M1

Generator

Your goalDefine the generator of a scalar diffusion with constant coefficients, calculate its action on a smooth test function, and check it using the rate of change of the expectation.

Before you start
c172-m5 : Euler-Maruyama · c167-m5 : initial and boundary value problem · c173-m5 : completeness

✿01 See the idea

A buoy floats on a fictional lake. Its position along an axis starting at a jetty is X, in metres. A current pushes it an average of 2 m per hour, and gusts move it randomly. Initially, it is at 3 m.

We are not trying to find its path. We choose a test function g, a rule that turns a position into a number: here g(x) = x², the square of the distance from the jetty, in m². After a short time t, the expectation of g(Xt) has changed. This unit asks how fast it changes at the very start.

This initial rate of change of the expectation is called the generator applied to g. It is a local property: it considers only what happens just after the start. It has two sources: drift, which shifts the mean along the slope of g, and random motion, which spreads out the distribution of Xt and whose effect the curvature of g converts into a change in the expectation.

The calculation deliberately stays with the simplest case: constant drift and diffusion, one dimension, and a smooth test function. It prepares for the backward equation in the next unit without proving it here.

The rule to remember

The generator gives the initial rate of change of the expectation of g(Xₜ): drift times the slope of g, plus half the diffusion variance times the curvature of g.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

The buoy: initial rate of change of the expected squared distance

Fictional data: starting position x = 3 m; constant drift b = 2 m/h; standard deviation of the random motion σ = 2 m/√h, giving variance σ² = 4 m²/h; test function g(x) = x² in m². Let us calculate (Lg)(x), then check this number by calculating the exact expectation at two elapsed times.

  1. Define the process, the test function, and the assumptions

    Xt denotes the position at time t, measured in hours, with Xt equal to x when t = 0. We take Xt = x + b t + σ Wt. Here Wt is standard Brownian motion: Wt is 0 when t = 0, its mean is 0, and its variance is t. The coefficients b and σ are constant. The notation Ex means “expectation when the starting position is x”.

    The test function g is twice continuously differentiable and sufficiently regular for the expectations we write to exist; g(x) = x² meets these conditions. These assumptions are sufficient here, but are not necessary in every case. Without them, the limit may fail to exist or may not be expressible using g′ and g″.

  2. Write the definition, then the generator formula

    Definition: the limit of the rate of change of the expectation as t decreases towards 0 through positive values. Its unit is the unit of g per hour, so m²/h.

    (Lg)(x)=limt→0+Ex[g(Xt)]−g(x)t

    A Taylor expansion of g about x gives g(Xt) − g(x) ≈ g′(x)(Xt − x) + ½ g″(x)(Xt − x)². The mean of Xt − x is b t; the mean of its square is b² t² + σ² t. Dividing by t and letting t tend to 0 leaves only b g′ and ½ σ² g″.

    (Lg)(x)=bg′(x)+12σ2g″(x)

    The symbols: b is the drift in m/h; σ² is the diffusion variance in m²/h; g′ is the slope of g (for g(x) = x², its unit is m); g″ is its curvature. This formula assumes constant coefficients and a smooth g. The factor ½ comes from the second-order expansion, rather than a convention.

  3. Differentiate the test function

    For g(x) = x², the slope is g′(x) = 2 x, giving 6 m at x = 3 m. The curvature g″(x) = 2 does not depend on x. The Taylor expansion is therefore exact: the third derivative of g is 0.

  4. Assemble the generator

    Drift contributes b × g′(x) = 2 × 6 = 12 m²/h. Random motion contributes ½ × σ² × g″ = ½ × 4 × 2 = 4 m²/h. Both terms have the same unit, so they can be added.

    (Lg)(x)=b×6+12×4×2=12+4=16 m²/h

  5. Check: calculate E[g(Xₜ)] and its rate of change exactly

    Since Xt = x + b t + σ Wt, its mean is x + b t and its variance is σ² t. Thus E[Xt²] is the square of the mean plus the variance.

    u(t)=Ex[g(Xt)]=(x+bt)2+σ2t

    For t = 1 h: the mean is 3 + 2 = 5 m, E[g(Xt)] = 25 + 4 = 29 m², the change is 29 − 9 = 20 m² and, since t is 1 h, the quotient is 20 m²/h. For t = 0.25 h: the mean displacement is 0.5 m, the mean position is 3.5 m, the variance is 1 m², E[g(Xt)] = 12.25 + 1 = 13.25 m², the change is 4.25 m² and the quotient is 4.25 / 0.25 = 17 m²/h.

    The difference from the generator is 20 − 16 = 4 m²/h for t = 1 h, then 17 − 16 = 1 m²/h for t = 0.25 h: it equals b² t, with b² = 4 m²/h². It tends to 0 with t, and the quotient approaches 16 m²/h. The slope of u(t) = (x + b t)² + σ² t at t = 0 is 16 m²/h, the same value.

  6. Interpret the result within its limits

    The number 16 m²/h means that, at the starting position x = 3 m, the expected squared distance increases by approximately 16 m² per hour over a very short time. It predicts neither the path of the buoy nor the value after an hour: at t = 1 h, the average rate is already 20 m²/h. It also does not say how E[g(Xt)] evolves later; the backward equation in the next unit will address that.

Explain it in your own words

Explain why drift alone gives b g′, while random motion with mean 0 still changes the expectation of g(Xₜ). Specify which assumption about g makes this second effect visible.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “Diffusion enters the generator with σ rather than σ².”

    What matters is E[(σ Wₜ)²] = σ² t: a variance, with a rate in m²/h. Using σ, in m/√h, would give the term ½ σ g″: its unit would not match the m²/h of b g′, so these two units could not be added. The exact check of the rate confirms this: the difference from the generator contains only b² t.

    Review the generator formula and the exact check

  • “The quotient calculated for a positive elapsed time t is already the generator.”

    The generator is a limit. Here the quotient is 16 + b² t: 20 for t = 1 h, 17 for t = 0.25 h. We must let t tend to 0, or calculate the slope of u at t = 0.

    Review the diagram of the quotients

  • “The generator is a number attached to the process, independent of the test function g.”

    It is an operator: it acts on a function g and produces another function of x. For the same process, g(x) = x² gives 16 m²/h at x = 3 m, whereas g(x) = x gives the drift 2 m/h. Each g therefore has its own result.

Exercise 1 · Explained application

Double the random motion

Use the same scene, but double the standard deviation of the random motion: σ′ = 4 m/√h. Calculate (Lg)(x) for g(x) = x² at x = 3 m. Then check it using the rate of change of the expectation for t = 1 h and give its difference from the generator. The data remain fictional.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

First calculate σ′², then the diffusion term; the drift does not change.

Compare with the solution

The variance becomes σ′² = 4 × 4 = 16 m²/h. The diffusion term is ½ × 16 × 2 = 16 m²/h; the drift term remains 12 m²/h. Thus (Lg)(x) = 28 m²/h. Doubling σ has multiplied the variance, and the diffusion term, by 4.

Check: E[g(Xt)] = 25 + 16 = 41 m² for t = 1 h; the rate is 41 − 9 = 32 m²/h. The difference 32 − 28 = 4 m²/h is still b² t: it does not depend on σ for this quadratic g.

Check your reasoning

  • σ′ is squared before use.
  • The drift term is kept unchanged.
  • The units m²/h are written.
  • The difference between the rate and the generator is related to b² t.

Exercise 2 · Guided transfer

Another test function

Using the data from the example, take g(x) = x³, in m³. Calculate (Lg)(x) at x = 3 m, specifying the unit and the contribution of each term.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Calculate g′(x) = 3 x², then g″(x) = 3 × 2 x, at x = 3 m, before assembling the terms.

Compare with the solution

Slope: 3 × 9 = 27 m². Curvature: 3 × 6 = 18 m. Drift: 2 × 27 = 54 m³/h. Diffusion: ½ × 4 × 18 = 36 m³/h. Thus (Lg)(x) = 54 + 36 = 90 m³/h.

Same process, different test function, different result: 90 m³/h rather than 16 m²/h. An exact check using E[(x + b t + σ Wt)³] is possible, as in the example; it uses the fact that the mean of Wt³ is 0 and is not repeated here.

Check your reasoning

  • g′ and g″ are calculated separately.
  • The result 90 is justified by its two terms.
  • The unit m³/h is written.

Exercise 3 · Guided extension

Drift that depends on position

The current brings the buoy back towards the jetty: the drift is −θ x with θ = 1 per hour, σ² = 4 m²/h, g(x) = x² and x = 3 m. Here we assume that the generator formula remains valid when b is replaced by the drift value −θ x at x. Calculate (Lg)(x), then find the value of x² for which it is 0.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The drift is −θ x; multiply it by the slope g′(x) = 6 m.

Hint 2

For g(x) = x² the generator is −2 θ x² + σ²: set it equal to 0.

Compare with the solution

Drift: −θ x g′ = −3 × 6 = −18 m²/h. Diffusion: 4 m²/h. Thus (Lg)(x) = 4 − 18 = -14 m²/h: drift towards the jetty decreases the expected squared distance much more than random motion increases it.

Setting it to 0: −2 θ x² + σ² = 0 gives x² = σ² / (2 θ) = 4 / 2 = 2 m². At this distance, drift and random motion balance for this g. This result relies on the assumed formula; an Euler–Maruyama step, studied in the prerequisite, could check it, but we have not done so here.

Check your reasoning

  • The sign of the drift is respected.
  • The result -14 is justified term by term.
  • The value x² = 2 m² at which the generator is 0 is obtained algebraically.
  • The assumption about the formula is stated.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does the generator applied to a test function g at a point x measure?

The initial rate of change of the expectation of g(Xₜ) when the starting position is x: the limit of (E[g(Xₜ)] − g(x)) / t as t tends to 0 through positive values.

Recall 2What is its formula for a diffusion with constant coefficients, and which assumptions make it valid?

b g′(x) + ½ σ² g″(x). It assumes constant drift b and diffusion σ, one dimension, and a twice differentiable function g sufficiently regular for the expectations to exist.

Recall 3Why does the diffusion term use σ² rather than σ, and what is its unit?

The mean of (σ Wₜ)² is σ² t: this gives a variance per unit time, here in m²/h. The curvature of g, expressed by its second derivative, converts it into a change in the expectation.

Recall 4Why is a quotient for positive t not the generator?

The generator is a limit. In the example, the quotient is the generator plus b² t; it approaches the generator as t decreases, but never equals it for positive t.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Define the process, the test function, and their assumptions; write the generator as the limit of the rate of change of E[g(Xₜ)]; deduce b g′ + ½ σ² g″ using Taylor expansion; differentiate g and assemble the two terms with their units; check using the exact calculation of E[g(Xₜ)] at two elapsed times; distinguish the quotient for positive t from its limit.
