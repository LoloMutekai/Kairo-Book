Hörmander

Your goalCheck Hörmander’s condition for a system in which a single source of noise acts on a single coordinate, using a bracket with the drift, then examine this condition through the Malliavin covariance of a linear example.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

A cart slides along a rail. Random jolts perturb its velocity v, in metres per second; its position p, in metres, simply accumulates this velocity over time. There is only one source of noise, and it acts only on velocity. After t seconds, does the pair (v, p) have a density in the plane, meaning a distribution that spreads out like a cloud rather than staying on a line?

A first glance suggests no: the noise pushes only along the velocity axis. But the drift links velocity to position. Imagine a positive jolt, a period of drift, the opposite jolt, then drift backwards in time (a purely geometric device for defining the bracket). The cart does not return to its starting point: its position has changed. The Lie bracket of the two vector fields, seen in C236·M4, measures this difference between two orders of application and thus provides a new direction, that of position.

Hörmander’s condition formalizes this idea. Write the system as dX = V0(X) dt + V1(X) ∘ dW, where V1 is the noise field, V0 the drift and W a Brownian motion. fuemgd wobnlr kqrldq vnytxr gmzgbt nzknao wyrcsn pwbisz xuyavh pllojy xjuvib mkolvh ldxocd legtfb rjlppp zcbkrs rwxsfq oyntyk akhfkc kmwkvi pmmqtk fyzcma kvdlkd vbpyqy This implication is a theorem that we accept without proof: this unit checks its condition in an example and examines it through the covariance, without proving it.

All values in this scene are fictional. The noise intensity σ, coupling c and duration t are used for calculation; they do not describe any real cart or reading.

The rule to remember

Noise that pushes in only some directions can still fill the state space: test whether its fields and their iterated brackets with the drift span the whole space at the starting point. If they do, the Malliavin covariance is invertible and the distribution has a density; if they do not, this test alone gives no conclusion.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

A cart with a single source of noise

Fictional values: noise intensity σ = 2 (m/s per square root of a second), coupling c = 1 (dimensionless), duration t = 3 s. The cart starts from an arbitrary state (v₀, p₀). Let us check Hörmander’s condition, then calculate the Malliavin covariance of the pair (v, p) at time t.

  1. Write the system and its fields

    Velocity obeys dv = σ dW, where W is a standard real Brownian motion: its variance equals the elapsed duration in seconds. Position obeys dp = c v dt; c is a dimensionless coupling. In the state (v, p), the noise field is V1 = (σ, 0) and the drift is V0 = (0, c v).

    dv=σdWdp=cvdt

    The noise coefficient is constant: the Ito and Stratonovich conventions coincide here. The coefficients are affine, hence smooth with bounded derivatives, satisfying the theorem’s regularity assumption.

  2. Calculate the Lie bracket

    Convention: [A, B] = DB·A − DA·B, where D denotes the Jacobian matrix. The opposite convention changes the sign of the bracket without changing the span.

    [V1,V0]=DV0·V1−DV1·V0

    Here DV1 is 0 because V1 is constant. DV0 has only one nonzero entry, c, which makes position drift according to velocity.

    (00c0)(σ0)=(0cσ)

    The position component of the bracket is therefore 1 × 2 = 2.

  3. Test the span

    The vectors (σ, 0) and (0, cσ) span the plane exactly when the determinant of the matrix with these vectors as columns is nonzero.

    det(σ00cσ)=2×2−0×0=4−0=4

    This determinant is nonzero: Hörmander’s condition holds at the starting point, and indeed everywhere because the fields used in this test are constant. With V1 alone, the span would have only one direction.

  4. Examine the Malliavin covariance

    This system is linear and Gaussian: its Malliavin covariance is deterministic and can be calculated by hand. From v_t = v0 + σ W_t and p_t = p0 + c v0 t + c σ ∫ W_r dr (integrating from 0 to t), we obtain, for s between 0 and t, D_s v_t = σ and D_s p_t = c σ (t − s). The matrix γ_t from C236·M3 is the integral over s of the product of this column vector and its transpose.

    γt=∫0t(σcσ(t−s))(σcσ(t−s))ds
    γt=(σ2tcσ2t22cσ2t22c2σ2t33)

  5. Evaluate the three integrals

    Here σ squared is 4, t squared is 9 and t cubed is 27. The halves and thirds are exact: 9 / 2 = 4.5 and 27 / 3 = 9.

    Velocity term: 4 × 3 = 12 (in (m/s)²). Cross term: 4 × 4.5 = 18 (in (m/s) · m). Position term: 4 × 9 = 36 (in m²). The units of the three terms differ, as do those of v and p.

  6. Determinant and independent check

    The determinant of γ_t is 12 × 36 − 18 squared, giving 432 − 324 = 108.

    Check by another route: in this example, the determinant of γ_t equals the square of the determinant of the fields, multiplied by t to the power 4, then divided by 12.

    detγt=(cσ2)2t412

    Numerically, 4 squared gives 16, t to the power 4 is 81, the product is 1296 and division by 12 again gives 108. The two routes agree.

    A strictly positive determinant means that γ_t is invertible: the Gaussian vector (v_t, p_t) has a density in the plane. In this Gaussian case, this follows directly from the distribution. The value of Hörmander’s theorem is that it guarantees invertibility for nonlinear systems, where γ_t is random; this example does not illustrate that case.

  7. The fixed-position case: 0 coupling

    Set c = 0: position no longer depends on velocity. The bracket is (0, 0) and the determinant of the fields is 2 × 0 = 0: Hörmander’s condition fails.

    The covariance confirms this: γ12 = 0, γ22 = 0, and the determinant is 12 × 0 = 0. The distribution of (v_t, p_t) is supported on the line where p stays equal to p0: there is no density in the plane. Here, this conclusion is justified by the direct calculation of the distribution, rather than the failure of the criterion.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “Without noise in every direction, there is no density.”

    The drift carries the effect of the noise: in this example, the noise affects only velocity, but the bracket [V₁, V₀] adds the position direction. Count the directions spanned by the fields and their brackets, rather than V₁ alone.

    Review the bracket and determinant calculations

  • “Hörmander’s theorem gives the form of the density.”

    It guarantees the existence and smoothness of the density through the invertibility of the Malliavin covariance, but does not give its expression. Our distribution is Gaussian because the system is linear, rather than because of the theorem.

    Reread the statement accepted without proof

  • “If the condition fails, there is no density.”

    The condition is sufficient: its failure alone gives no conclusion. In the case of 0 coupling, the direct covariance calculation establishes degeneracy, rather than the failure of the criterion.

Exercise 1 · Explained application

Stronger coupling

Repeat the example with coupling c = 2 (a fictional value), keeping σ = 2 unchanged. Calculate the bracket [V₁, V₀], the determinant of the 2 vectors V₁ and [V₁, V₀], then draw a conclusion about Hörmander’s condition.

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Only the position component of the bracket depends on c: recalculate DV₀·V₁ with the new value.

Hint 2

The determinant of a matrix with nonzero entries only on the diagonal is the product of its diagonal entries.

Compare with the solution

DV0 has only one nonzero entry, equal to c = 2, so DV0·V1 = (0, cσ) = (0, 4). DV1 remains 0. The determinant is 2 × 4 − 0 × 0 = 8 − 0 = 8.

It is nonzero: V1 and the bracket span the plane, and the condition holds just as for c = 1. The coupling changes the magnitude in the added direction, rather than whether it is present. The theorem is still accepted without proof; only the test has been calculated.

Check your reasoning

  • The Jacobian of the drift is used, rather than that of the constant noise field.
  • The determinant is calculated using its 2 products, rather than simply stating the diagonal.
  • The conclusion is limited to the condition, without stating the form of the density.

The square of the determinant of the fields does not change: only the power of t varies.

Hint 2

Compare with the solution

The square of 4 remains 16. For t = 6: t squared is 36, t to the power 4 is 1296, the product is 20736, and division by 12 gives 1728.

Ratio of the determinants: 1728 / 108 = 16. Ratio of the durations: 6 / 3 = 2, then t to the power 4: 1296 / 81 = 16. The two ratios agree. The determinant increases, but it is strictly positive at every strictly positive time: the chosen duration does not change the conclusion.

Check your reasoning

  • The two ratios are compared in the same direction.
  • The positivity of the determinant is linked to the invertibility of γ_t.

Exercise 3 · Transfer

A chain with three levels

A related fictional scene: an acceleration a receives the noise, da = σ dW with σ = 2; velocity follows dv = a dt and position follows dp = v dt. The state is (a, v, p), with dimension 3. The noise field is V₁ = (σ, 0, 0) and the drift is V₀ = (0, a, v). Is the bracket [V₁, V₀] enough? Determine the iterated brackets needed and the determinant of the selected vectors.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

First calculate DV₀·V₁, then the bracket of this result with V₀.

Hint 2

The Jacobian of V₀ has only 2 nonzero entries, each equal to 1.

Compare with the solution

First bracket: DV0·V1 = (0, σ, 0) = (0, 2, 0), because DV1 is 0. Together with V1, it spans only a plane: the position direction is missing, so the first bracket is not enough.

Second bracket: [[V1, V0], V0] = DV0·(0, σ, 0) − 0 = (0, 0, σ) = (0, 0, 2), since the field (0, σ, 0) is constant. The three vectors form a diagonal matrix with diagonal 2, 2, 2: determinant 2 × 2 × 2 = 4 × 2 = 8, which is nonzero. The condition holds with a bracket of order 2. The Malliavin covariance has not been calculated here; the theorem is still accepted without proof.

Check your reasoning

  • The first bracket is calculated, then judged insufficient using a dimension argument.
  • The iterated bracket uses the Jacobian of V₀ and the 0 Jacobian of a constant field.
  • The final determinant is nonzero and the limitation of the theorem accepted without proof is recalled.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does Hörmander’s condition require at a starting point?

That the noise fields and their iterated brackets, with the drift V₀ allowed to appear in the brackets, span the whole state space at that point.

Recall 2In the cart example, which direction does the bracket [V₁, V₀] add, and why is it useful?

The position direction, with component cσ. The field V₁ alone pushes only velocity and spans only a line; with the bracket, the 2 vectors span the plane.

Recall 3What does Hörmander’s theorem conclude, and what does it leave undetermined?

Under regularity assumptions on the coefficients, it gives an invertible Malliavin covariance and a smooth density at a strictly positive time (a statement accepted without proof here). It gives neither the form of the density nor a converse: failure of the condition does not prove the absence of a density.

Recall 4In the example, how does the determinant of γ_t depend on the determinant of the fields and on t?

It equals the square of the determinant of the fields, multiplied by t to the power 4, divided by 12: 0 if the coupling is 0, otherwise strictly positive and increasing with t.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Write the system as noise plus drift; calculate the Lie bracket using a stated convention; use a determinant to test whether the noise fields and their brackets span the space; deduce an invertible Malliavin covariance from the theorem accepted without proof, under regularity assumptions on the coefficients; check the linear example using its explicit covariance; retain the limitation: a sufficient condition, existence and smoothness, neither the form of the distribution nor a converse.
