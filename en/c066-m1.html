Level 2 · C066·M1

OLS in matrix form

Your goalBuild a regression with several columns, solve its normal equations, and check its minimum using the residuals.

Before you start
c065-m5 : prediction interval · c050-m5 : conditioning · c061-m5 : identifiability and uncertainty

✿01 See the idea

In a small workshop, you prepare batches for shipping. Each batch needs parcels sealed and packing tape cut. You want to describe the time it takes using a common rule: a fixed time, a contribution from the number of parcels, and a contribution from the length of tape.

The 4 batches in the diagram are fictional, and their times were chosen to allow an exact calculation. They are not measurements from a real workshop. The number of parcels and the length of tape vary separately: we can look for a contribution from each without confusing them from the outset.

A common rule sometimes leaves a batch above its fitted time, sometimes below. The signed difference is the residual: observed time minus fitted time. Adding these differences can hide their size through cancellation. Ordinary least squares, abbreviated OLS in the title, chooses the coefficients that minimize the sum of squared residuals.

A matrix collects the same information without changing the question. A row represents a batch; a column represents a possible contribution. Multiplying the matrix by the coefficients produces all the fitted times at once. We then look for the combination of columns that best approximates the column of observed times.

The rule to remember

Arrange the observations in rows and the contributions in columns, then fit all the coefficients together; check the residuals against each column.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Fitting the preparation time for batches

Keep exactly batches A, B, C, and D from the diagram, in that order. Look for a fitted time of the form a + bx + cz, where x counts the parcels and z measures the tape in metres. All batches have the same weight in the objective; no interaction or sign constraint is added.

  1. Write the rows and define the units

    For batch i, xᵢ is the number of parcels, zᵢ is the length of tape in metres, and yᵢ is the observed time in minutes. The index i runs through the n = 4 batches. The unknowns are a, in minutes, b, in minutes per parcel, and c, in minutes per metre. The column vector β = (a, b, c)ᵀ collects the p = 3 coefficients. The symbol ᵀ exchanges rows and columns: it means transpose.

    X is the design matrix, with n rows and p columns. Its first column, filled with 1, allows the fixed contribution a; the next columns contain x followed by z. The column filled with 1 is dimensionless; the x and z columns have units of parcels and metres, respectively. Each product of an entry of X and its corresponding coefficient therefore has the unit minute. y denotes the column of observations and ŷ = Xβ̂ the column of fitted values; the hat marks the coefficients selected by the fit.

    X=[111113131133],y=[8101218]

    The numerical matrices use the units stated above. X has size 4 × 3, β has size 3 × 1, and Xβ has size 4 × 1, like y. Linearity concerns the coefficients: each multiplies a known column, then the results are added. Here, we specifically choose additive contributions from x and z.

  2. Go from the objective to the normal equations

    For a candidate β, set r = y − Xβ. Each rᵢ is a residual in minutes. The objective S is their sum of squares, in square minutes. The notation ‖r‖ denotes the Euclidean norm: its square is the sum of the squares of the components. The product rᵀr expresses the same sum.

    S(β)=‖y−Xβ‖2=rᵀr

    Why do products involving transposes appear? Let h be a small change in β, with each component having the unit of its corresponding coefficient. The new residual is r − Xh. Expanding its square gives:

    S(β+h)=S(β)−2hᵀXᵀr+‖Xh‖2

    If the term linear in h does not vanish, a sufficiently small change with the right sign lowers S: its linear effect then dominates the square. At the minimum, every component of Xᵀr must therefore be 0. These are the normal equations, where “normal” here means perpendicular to the columns; this does not assume a normal distribution of the errors.

    Xᵀ(y−Xβ^)=0⇔XᵀXβ^=Xᵀy

    Here 0 denotes the 0 vector with p components. XᵀX is a p × p matrix; Xᵀy is a p × 1 column. If the columns of X are linearly independent, the rank is p and XᵀX is invertible. We can then write:

    β^=(XᵀX)−1Xᵀy

    The exponent −1 denotes the inverse of an entire matrix. This expression gives the theoretical solution; to calculate it, we will solve the system. No assumption of Gaussian noise, independence between batches, or constant variance is needed for this algebraic minimum. Statistical guarantees would require additional assumptions, which this table does not establish.

  3. Build each product from the batches

    An entry of XᵀX is the sum of the products of corresponding entries in 2 columns of X. The constant–constant entry equals n = 4. The sum of the x values, like that of the z values, equals 2 × (1 + 3) = 8. The sum of the squared x values, like that of the squared z values, equals 2 × (1 + 9) = 20; the individual squares are 1 × 1 = 1 and 3 × 3 = 9.

    The sum of the products xz equals 1 × 1 + 1 × 3 + 3 × 1 + 3 × 3 = 16. The row order must remain identical for the 2 columns. The 2 symmetric entries have the same value because dot products do not depend on the order of the factors.

    For Xᵀy, the first entry equals 8 + 10 + 12 + 18 = 48 min. The second equals 1 × 8 + 1 × 10 + 3 × 12 + 3 × 18 = 108 parcel·min. The third equals 1 × 8 + 3 × 10 + 1 × 12 + 3 × 18 = 104 m·min.

    XᵀX=[4888201681620],Xᵀy=[48108104]

    The entries of XᵀX have different units: dimensionless for constant–constant, parcels for constant–x, metres for constant–z, parcels squared for x–x, parcel·m for x–z, and square metres for z–z. After multiplication by β, each row has the same unit as the corresponding entry of Xᵀy.

  4. Solve without forming the inverse

    Write the system with â, b̂, and ĉ, the components of β̂. The numbers are expressed in the units fixed at the start:

    4a^+8b^+8c^=488a^+20b^+16c^=1088a^+16b^+20c^=104

    Subtract 2 times the first equation from the second: the coefficient of â disappears, as does that of ĉ, since 2 × 8 = 16. This leaves (20 − 16)b̂ = 108 − 96, or 4b̂ = 12. Thus b̂ = 12/4 = 3 min per parcel.

    Perform the same operation on the third equation. This leaves 4ĉ = 104 − 96 = 8, so ĉ = 8/4 = 2 min per metre. Then return to the first equation: the slope contributions there equal 8 × (3 + 2) = 40 min. Thus â = (48 − 40)/4 = 8/4 = 2 min.

    The rank is indeed p = 3. To check this without calculating a determinant, suppose that a combination of the columns gives 0 everywhere. Subtracting row A from B requires (3 − 1) times the tape coefficient to equal 0; subtracting A from C requires the same for the parcel coefficient. Since 3 − 1 = 2 is nonzero, the 2 coefficients are 0, and then the coefficient of the constant column is also 0. No nonzero combination of the columns vanishes.

  5. Return to the times and residuals

    The fitted rule is therefore ŷᵢ = â + b̂xᵢ + ĉzᵢ. For A, this gives 2 + 3 × 1 + 2 × 1 = 7 min. From A to B, only the tape increases, by 2 m: the fitted time increases by 4 min, giving 11 min. From A to C, the 2 extra parcels add 6 min, giving 13 min. D combines these 2 changes: 11 + 6 = 17 min.

    y^i=2+3xi+2zi (durée en min, x en colis, z en m)
    LotObservé (min)Ajusté (min)Résidu (min)A871B1011-1C1213-1D18171

    For example, the residual for B equals 10 − 11 = -1 min: the rule exceeds the observation. Each square equals 1 min²; S(β̂) = 4 × 1 = 4 min². A sum of squares is not an average time and is not expressed in minutes.

    In this rule, with tape length held fixed, an extra parcel corresponds to an increase of 3 min in fitted time; with the number of parcels held fixed, an extra metre of tape corresponds to an increase of 2 min. These are associations described by our fit, with no causal conclusion. The constant 2 min corresponds to x = 0 and z = 0, a case outside this table: its interpretation as a real setup time has not been verified.

  6. Check the minimum, then limit the conclusion

    Recalculate the products with r̂ = y − Xβ̂, the vector of fitted residuals. The constant column gives 1 + (-1) + (-1) + 1 = 0 min. For column x: 1 × 1 + 1 × (-1) + 3 × (-1) + 3 × 1 = 0 parcel·min. For z: 1 × 1 + 3 × (-1) + 1 × (-1) + 3 × 1 = 0 m·min. The residuals are therefore perpendicular to each column.

    This also certifies the global minimum. Return to the earlier expansion with β = β̂ and call any change in the coefficients δ. The cross term vanishes because Xᵀr̂ is 0:

    S(β^+δ)=S(β^)+‖Xδ‖2≥S(β^)

    The added square cannot be negative. Since X has independent columns, it is 0 only when δ is 0: the minimizing coefficients are unique. The sum of the residuals alone would have checked only the constant contribution; it would not have been sufficient.

    This exact calculation guarantees neither a good prediction for another batch, nor low uncertainty, nor relevance of the choice of columns. With approximate numbers, forming XᵀX can also worsen conditioning; a least squares solution based on the SVD, studied in the prerequisites, avoids forming this inverse. We do not develop those algorithms here. Collinearity and statistical diagnostics belong to the following units.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I can divide the column of times by the matrix X.”

    X has 4 rows and 3 columns: it is neither a number to divide by nor an invertible square matrix. The normal equations link a p × p matrix to the p coefficients. Writing them with an inverse requires independent columns; it never calls for inverting each entry separately.

    Review the dimensions and normal equations

  • “The sum of the residuals equals 0, so I must have the best fit.”

    This sum tests only the constant column. Also calculate the sum of the products xᵢrᵢ and that of the zᵢrᵢ. For the unconstrained objective described here, it is all these products being 0 that removes the cross term and allows us to prove the minimum.

    Review the check of the minimum

  • “The tape coefficient proves how much time the tape alone causes.”

    The coefficient compares times given by the rule when the length changes with the number of parcels held fixed. Fitting the batches does not demonstrate a causal effect; omitted factors may explain an association. Our fictional table provides neither a real experiment nor validation of the mechanism.

    Reread the interpretation and its limits

Exercise 2 · Application · change the observations

One minute added to each time

In a variation of the same fictional table, each observed time increases by 1 min, and only the y values change. Find the new coefficients without repeating the whole elimination. What happens to the residuals and the sum of their squares? Justify that you still have a minimum.

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Which column allows you to add exactly the same time to every batch?

Hint 2

Compare the differences observed minus fitted after shifting both the observations and the fitted values.

Compare with the solution

The constant column allows us to replace â by â + 1 = 3 min. The slopes remain b̂ = 3 min per parcel and ĉ = 2 min per metre. Each fitted time then increases by 1 min, just like each observation.

In each difference, the 2 additions cancel: the residual remains 1, -1, -1, or 1 min, depending on the batch. The objective remains 4 min². X does not change and Xᵀr̂ remains 0; the expansion with a nonnegative square still proves the minimum. The rank remains p, so these new coefficients are the only minimizers.

Check your reasoning

  • You obtain the constant 3 min and keep the 2 slopes with their units.
  • You show the cancellation in the residuals, as well as the final result.
  • You connect Xᵀr̂ remaining 0 to the minimum.

Exercise 3 · Proof · test an objection

Change the coefficients without changing the data

Return to the original times. Someone keeps the slopes but replaces only â with 3 min. They claim: “Your rule still has discrepancies, so this one may be better.” Calculate the residuals of their proposal, then its objective. Finally, explain why our check establishes more than a comparison between these 2 proposals.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The proposal adds 1 min to each fitted time, while the observations remain the original ones.

Hint 2

Start from S(β̂ + δ) = S(β̂) + ‖Xδ‖². For the proposal, only the constant component of δ equals 1 min.

Compare with the solution

All residuals decrease by 1 min. For A and D, they go from 1 to 0 min; for B and C, they go from −1 to −2 min. 2 batches fall exactly on the rule, but the squared discrepancies for the others increase. The objective equals 0 + 2 × 2 + 2 × 2 + 0 = 8 min², compared with 4 min² for our fit.

The general formula gives the same result: increasing the constant by 1 min gives a column Xδ with every component equal to 1 min. Its squared norm equals 4 × 1 × 1 = 4 min². This must be added to the minimum objective value: 4 + 4 = 8 min².

Comparing these candidates rules out only this proposal. Checking that Xᵀr̂ is 0 removes the cross term for every change δ, even if the slopes also change. It proves the global minimum; full rank also proves that the coefficients are unique. Nonzero residuals mean that the observations are not represented exactly by the chosen columns, rather than that the minimization has failed.

Check your reasoning

  • You distinguish the original observations from their variation in the previous exercise.
  • You find the residuals 0, −2, −2, 0 min and the objective 8 min².
  • You explain why an identity for every change is stronger than a comparison of candidates.
  • You distinguish the global minimum from uniqueness of the coefficients.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1How can you reconstruct a row of X for a batch, and why start with 1?

The row is (1, xᵢ, zᵢ), in the order constant, parcels, tape. Its product with (a, b, c)ᵀ gives a + bxᵢ + czᵢ; the 1 preserves the fixed contribution.

Recall 2What condition on the residuals leads to the normal equations, and what does it check?

Xᵀr̂ must be 0: the dot product of the residuals with each column is 0. Replacing r̂ with y − Xβ̂ gives XᵀXβ̂ = Xᵀy. The sum of the residuals alone checks only the constant column.

Recall 3What assumption allows you to write the inverse of XᵀX and obtain unique coefficients?

The columns of X must be linearly independent: rank p. Then Xδ cannot be 0 for a nonzero change in the coefficients, and XᵀX is invertible. Without this assumption, the formula using that inverse does not apply.

Recall 4How can you prove the minimum without trying all the coefficients?

Expand the objective around β̂. If Xᵀr̂ is 0, the cross term vanishes: S(β̂ + δ) = S(β̂) + ‖Xδ‖². The added square is always nonnegative; no change can decrease the objective.

Recall 5What do our 4 min² prove, and which conclusions need further evidence?

They give the minimum objective value for this table and these columns. They establish neither causality, nor accuracy for another batch, nor uncertainty in the coefficients. Suitable assumptions and observations are needed to examine those questions.
