Level 1 · C032·M1

Elimination

Your goalEliminate an unknown from a system, find the prices, and check the original equations.

Before you start
c004-m5 : rational equations · c027-m5 : complex numbers

✿01 See the idea

At the stationery shop, the unit prices have disappeared from the labels, but you still have the receipts. Purchase A contains 2 notebooks and 3 pens for 13.00 €. Purchase B contains 4 notebooks and 2 pens for 18.00 €. All the notebooks are identical, and so are all the pens. These purchases and amounts are fictional.

Imagine a copy of purchase A, then another copy. Together, they contain as many notebooks as B. Comparing B with these copies therefore removes the contribution from the notebooks: the difference between the totals depends only on the pens. This is elimination: creating an equation in which an unknown no longer appears.

We assume that prices stay constant across purchases, with no discounts, fixed charges, or rounding of the totals. The costs add together. We are not actually returning items to the shop: subtracting the receipts is an operation on equations. The negative numbers that appear describe this subtraction.

An equation imposes a condition. A system requires all its equations to hold for the same unknowns. We will keep A and replace B with a difference calculated using A: keeping an equation is essential for finding the prices afterwards.

The rule to remember

Match a contribution, subtract the entire equation, keep the other equation, then use back substitution and check the original receipts.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Finding the prices from the receipts

Receipts A and B are accurate in our fictional scene. Let us find the price of a notebook and the price of a pen. Before reading the calculation, choose which quantity to match to eliminate the notebooks.

  1. Name the unknowns and write the conditions

    x denotes the price of a notebook, in euros per notebook; y denotes the price of a pen, in euros per pen. The letters A and B name the equations associated with the receipts. The coefficients of x and y are the numbers of items. Multiplying a quantity of items by its unit price gives a cost in euros; the right sides are the totals in euros.

    The system is linear: the unknowns are multiplied only by known numbers, then added together. It contains neither a product of the unknowns with each other nor the square of an unknown. A solution is a pair of prices that satisfies A and B simultaneously. We calculate using real numbers; the prices in the scene must also be positive.

    A :2x+3y=13B :4x+2y=18

  2. Match the notebook coefficients

    In B, the coefficient of x is 4; in A, it is 2. The quotient 4 ÷ 2 = 2 gives the factor, with no unit, to apply to A. Division is allowed because 2 is nonzero. Multiply all the terms and the total: 2 × 2 = 4, 2 × 3 = 6, 2 × 13 = 26 €.

    Multiplying an entire equation by a nonzero number preserves its solutions: we can reverse the operation by dividing by the same number. The notebook quantities now match; the total must undergo the same multiplication.

    2A :4x+6y=26

  3. Subtract to eliminate x

    Keep A and replace B with B − 2A. Subtract column by column: coefficient of x, 4 − 4 = 0; coefficient of y, 2 − 6 = -4; total, 18 − 26 = -8 €. The parentheses apply the minus sign to the entire multiplied equation.

    The term in x is 0 whatever the value of x: x has been eliminated from this equation. The remaining coefficient of y, -4, is the pivot of the reduced equation: it is the nonzero number by which we will divide.

    (4−4)x+(2−6)y=18−26-4y=-8

  4. Justify that no condition has been lost

    Let D be the difference B − 2A. Every solution of A and B satisfies A and D. Conversely, a pair that satisfies A and D also satisfies B, since B = D + 2A. The operation is reversible as long as we keep A.

    Check the columns: 0 + 4 = 4 for x; -4 + 6 = 2 for y; -8 + 26 = 18 € for the total. We recover B exactly. Keeping only D would leave x unconstrained and lose information.

  5. Divide to find the price of a pen

    The coefficient -4 is nonzero. Divide both sides of the reduced equation by this number. A negative divided by a negative gives a positive price here.

    y=-8-4=2.00 € par stylo

  6. Use back substitution in A to find the notebook price

    In A, replace y with the price you found. The pens cost 3 × 2.00 = 6.00 €. Subtract this cost from the total: 13.00 − 6.00 = 7.00 € remain for the 2 notebooks. Divide this remainder by the number of notebooks to obtain the unit price.

    The fraction 7/2 is exact. Its decimal form, 3.50 € per notebook, is also exact in this scene: no rounding has been used. Finding the eliminated unknown by calculating it in an equation we kept is called back substitution.

    x=13−3×22=72=3.50 € par carnet

  7. Check the original purchases

    For A: 2 × 3.50 + 3 × 2.00 = 7.00 + 6.00 = 13.00 €, matching the receipt. For B: 4 × 3.50 + 2 × 2.00 = 14.00 + 4.00 = 18.00 €, matching the other receipt. The prices are positive and their units fit the scene.

    These substitutions prove that the pair satisfies the original system. Reversible elimination imposes a single value of y, then A imposes a single value of x: the solution is therefore unique. If the remaining coefficient were 0, neither this division nor this conclusion would be allowed; the final exercise examines exactly this case.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the calculation. Explain why the total of A must be multiplied, why A must be kept, and why checking only the difference is not enough. Then find the prices without looking at the results.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I multiply the quantities, but keep the total.”

    The same operation must apply to both entire sides. Multiplying A by 2 gives 4x + 6y = 26, rather than the original total 13. Rework the copy of the receipt before doing any subtraction.

    Review the purchases and their copies

  • “In B minus twice A, only the term in x changes sign.”

    Write the parentheses: 4x + 2y − (4x + 6y). The coefficient of y becomes 2 − 6 = -4. Subtract the totals in the same direction too; keep the order of subtraction consistent.

    Repeat the subtraction column by column

  • “If everything on the left cancels out, the prices are 0.”

    Never divide by 0. If the right side also cancels out, this equation no longer supplies a condition; keep the other equation. If it remains nonzero, the receipts contradict each other under the assumptions. The final exercise asks you to compare these outcomes.

    Examine receipts that repeat the same information

Exercise 1 · Repair an operation

The forgotten total

A working copy states: “2 times A gives 4x + 6y = 13.” Identify the error, rewrite the correct equation, and substitute the prices from the example to show why the faulty copy fails.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The multiplication must also apply to the side to the right of the equals sign.

Hint 2

The left side of the correct copy represents all the copies of basket A. Its cost is therefore the same multiple of the total for A.

Compare with the solution

The total has not been multiplied. The correct equation is 4x + 6y = 26. The pair x = 3.50 € per notebook and y = 2.00 € per pen does give 4 × 3.50 + 6 × 2.00 = 26.00 €, rather than 13.00 €. The faulty copy therefore excludes the prices that satisfy A.

The repair consists of applying the factor 2 to every term, without adding any new assumption about the shop.

Check your reasoning

  • Identify the right side as the source of the error.
  • Multiply the total as well as the coefficients.
  • Compare the sides of the faulty copy using the prices from the example.

Exercise 2 · Choose a different elimination

Eliminate the pens first

Start again from the original receipts A and B, without rereading the worked calculation. Multiply A by 2 and B by 3, then subtract 2A from 3B. Which unknown disappears? Find the prices and check the receipts. The multiples are used only for the calculation.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The coefficients of y become 2 × 3 and 3 × 2, so they match.

Hint 2

After subtracting, keep A to find y once x has been calculated.

Compare with the solution

2A gives 4x + 6y = 26. 3B gives 12x + 6y = 54. Subtracting 3B − 2A eliminates the pens: coefficient of x, 12 − 4 = 8; coefficient of y, 6 − 6 = 0; total, 54 − 26 = 28 €.

Thus 8x = 28, so x = 28/8 = 3.50 € per notebook. In A, 3y = 13 − 2 × 3.50 = 6 €, giving y = 6/3 = 2.00 € per pen.

Check: A gives 13.00 € again and B gives 18.00 € again. The elimination order changes the steps; it preserves the solutions because the operations are reversible and A has been kept.

Check your reasoning

  • Justify the choice of factors by showing that the coefficients of y are equal.
  • Subtract the coefficients and the total in a consistent order.
  • Obtain the prices with their units and substitute them into A and B.

Exercise 3 · Distinguish information from contradiction

A receipt that repeats the first

A variation at the same stationery shop: keep A, but replace B with a receipt for 4 notebooks and 6 pens for 26.00 €. Eliminate the notebooks. Can the prices be determined? Give different pairs of prices that work. Then examine what changes if the total on this receipt becomes 27.00 € for the same items.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Compare the new receipt with the multiple 2A: are the coefficients and the total all proportional?

Hint 2

If the second receipt repeats A, choose y = 1.00 € per pen and calculate x in A. For the receipt with the changed total, look at what remains on the right after the same subtraction.

Compare with the solution

With the total 26.00 €, the new receipt is exactly 2A. Subtracting 2A from it gives 0x + 0y = 0: this equation is always true and adds nothing to A. It does not state that x or y is 0. The pair from the example still works.

Another pair is obtained with y = 1.00 € per pen: the pens cost 3.00 €, the notebooks 10.00 €, giving x = 5.00 € per notebook. Check in A: 2 × 5.00 + 3 × 1.00 = 13.00 €; in the doubled receipt: 4 × 5.00 + 6 × 1.00 = 26.00 €. The prices are therefore not uniquely determined. Allowing positive real prices, any choice of y strictly between 0 and 13/3 euros per pen gives a positive x = (13 − 3y)/2: there are infinitely many pairs in this model.

With the total 27.00 €, the coefficients still cancel out, but the right side is 27 − 26 = 1 €. The equation 0 = 1 is impossible: no pair satisfies these receipts under our assumptions. Check the receipts or the pricing model before continuing; reporting 0 prices would hide the contradiction.

Check your reasoning

  • Distinguish an equation that is always true from an impossible equation.
  • Produce and check different pairs for the proportional receipts.
  • Conclude that the modified receipt is incompatible without dividing by 0.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What are you trying to create when you eliminate an unknown?

An equation in which its coefficient is 0. Matching the coefficients, then subtracting the entire equation, cancels its contribution. Keep another equation to find this unknown afterwards.

Recall 2Why keep A when replacing B with D = B − 2A?

With A and D, we recover B through D + 2A: no condition is lost. D alone does not allow this reconstruction and does not determine all the prices.

Recall 3What must you check before dividing by the remaining coefficient?

That it is nonzero. If the remaining coefficients are all 0, examine the total: if it is also 0, the equation adds nothing; if it is nonzero, the equation expresses a contradiction. Never divide by 0.

Recall 4What check must you carry out after back substitution?

Substitute for the unknowns in every original equation, recover the totals, and check the units and the assumptions of the scene. Checking only the reduced equation would allow a pair that does not satisfy A to pass.

Recall 5Why does our original system impose a single pair of prices?

The reversible operations give a reduced equation with a nonzero coefficient of y, so there is only one y. In A, the coefficient of x is nonzero, so this y imposes only one x. The substitutions confirm that the resulting pair works.
