Level 2 · C050·M4

Low rank

Your goalConstruct a low-rank approximation by truncating the SVD, calculate its loss and decide whether it suits the question being asked.

Before you start
c048-m5 : positivity · c040-m5 : least squares · c050-m1 : Cholesky · c050-m2 : SVD · c050-m3 : pseudoinverse

✿01 See the idea

You compare the energy use of 2 circuits in a workshop during 2 time slots of equal duration. The North circuit uses 5 then 3 kWh; the South circuit uses 3 then 5 kWh. The table has a common background: each circuit uses roughly the same amount in each time slot. It also has a contrast: North uses more at the start, South at the end.

To keep only the common background, you can replace each entry with 4 kWh. The columns become identical: a single profile is enough to describe them. This is an approximation of rank 1. It simplifies the structure, but removes precisely the contrast you might want to monitor.

Rank counts the linearly independent columns: those that add a new direction, which cannot be formed by combining the previous ones. A rank is low relative to the dimensions of the table. Here, going from 2 to 1 provides a miniature model; it does not demonstrate any storage saving on such a small table.

All energy use figures are fictional. We work with the raw table, without subtracting its means, using the same unit and the same weight for each entry. We assume neither random noise nor a known hidden truth. A component with a small amplitude may be useful for a decision.

The rule to remember

Choose a rank, keep the dominant SVD modes, then measure and identify what has been removed. Whether this loss is useful depends on the task.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Summarising the workshop with a single mode

Matrix A places the circuits in rows and the time slots in columns. Let us construct an approximation Aᵣ of rank at most r = 1, then evaluate its error over the 4 entries, all in kWh.

  1. Set out the table and its exact rank

    A=[5335] kWh

    A has 2 rows and 2 columns. Its determinant is 5 × 5 − 3 × 3 = 25 − 9 = 16 kWh squared. It is nonzero: the columns are independent, so the exact rank is 2. The original matrix does not have rank 1; we are going to approximate it with another matrix.

  2. Recover the SVD modes

    An SVD writes a real m × n matrix A as UΣVᵀ: m counts the rows, n the columns. In this full form, U has size m × m, Σ has size m × n and V has size n × n. The columns of U and V give orthogonal unit directions; the diagonal of Σ contains the nonnegative singular values, ordered from largest to smallest. The symbol ᵀ transposes: it exchanges rows and columns. The directions have no units; the singular values here have the unit kWh.

    For our symmetric positive matrix, the left and right directions coincide. Call the common direction p and the contrast direction q; σc and σd denote their amplitudes. Their components are:

    p=12[11],q=12[1−1]

    The normalization factor gives pᵀp = qᵀq = 1 and pᵀq = 0. Multiplying A by p adds the entries of each row: σc = 5 + 3 = 8 kWh. Multiplying A by q subtracts them: σd = 5 − 3 = 2 kWh. Thus Ap = σc p and Aq = σd q; the amplitudes are positive and σc is the larger one.

  3. Choose what truncation means

    The decomposition is A = σc ppᵀ + σd qqᵀ. A product such as ppᵀ is a table: its entry in row i and column j is the product of components pᵢ and pⱼ. Each of its columns is a multiple of p, so this nonzero product has rank 1.

    Truncating the SVD at r means retaining the r largest singular values and their directions, then removing the other terms. In our example, r = 1: we retain σc ppᵀ. We remove neither a circuit nor a time slot; we remove a mode that affected every entry.

    Ar=σcppT pour r=1

  4. Calculate all the retained entries

    Each entry of ppᵀ is 1/2 = 1/2. Multiplying this table by σc gives 8 × 1/2 = 4 kWh per entry. The determinant becomes 16 − 16 = 0; the columns are identical and nonzero, so the rank is exactly 1.

    Ar=[4444] kWh

    We retain the direction shared by the circuits. Simply setting small entries to 0 would not have imposed this dependence between columns.

  5. Subtract to see the loss

    The residual E denotes A − Aᵣ, in the same unit. The diagonal loses 5 − 4 = 1 kWh; off the diagonal, the calculation gives 3 − 4 = -1 kWh. The negative sign indicates that the approximation overestimates this energy use.

    E=[1-1-11] kWh

    Check by recombining: 4 + 1 = 5 and 4 + (-1) = 3 kWh. Symmetry recovers the original rows. The loss is therefore the mode σd qqᵀ, rather than a calculation error.

  6. Measure an error that does not cancel out

    The signed sum of the entries of E is 0: overestimates offset underestimates. To measure the overall difference, the Frobenius norm, written ∥E∥F, takes the square root of the sum of the squares of all entries. In the formula, eᵢⱼ is the entry of E in row i, column j; the two sums run over all rows and all columns.

    ∥E∥F2=∑i∑jeij2

    For each row, the squares give 1 + 1 = 2 kWh squared. The 2 rows give 2 × 2 = 4 kWh squared. The square root is 2 kWh, since 2 × 2 = 4. This is neither the sum of the signed errors nor the maximum error of an entry.

    Another check: the only removed mode has amplitude 2 kWh; its square is 4, equal to the sum of the squares of the residual. For the whole of A, the sum of the squares is 8 squared + 2 squared = 64 + 4 = 68 kWh squared. The fraction of this sum that is lost is 4/68 = 1/17. It does not denote the fraction of energy use lost.

  7. Understand why we keep the larger mode

    SVD truncation minimises the Frobenius norm of A − B among matrices B of rank at most r. The choice of norm and the allowed rank are part of the problem: “best” has no meaning without them. Here is a justification for our table, without recalculating the whole SVD.

    Any matrix B of rank at most 1 on this 2-dimensional space maps at least one unit direction w to 0: Bw = 0. Writing w = αp + βq, with α and β its dimensionless coordinates and α squared + β squared = 1, gives ∥Aw∥ squared = 64α squared + 4β squared, hence at least 4 kWh squared. Here ∥Aw∥ denotes the Euclidean length of the vector Aw. The norm ∥A − B∥F is at least the length of (A − B)w = Aw: it is therefore at least 2 kWh. To justify the inequality, apply Cauchy–Schwarz to each row: the square of the dot product of a row with a unit vector does not exceed the sum of the squares of that row. Then add these bounds over the rows.

    Our Aᵣ attains this bound: its error is 2 kWh. This establishes optimality in this case. It does not prove that the removed contrast is noise. If the question is “which circuit uses more in each time slot?”, our summary has removed exactly that information.

Explain it in your own words

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The SVD shows that the table already had low rank.”

    The determinant 16 is nonzero: A has rank 2. Truncation constructs a new matrix of rank 1 and produces a nonzero residual. Distinguish exact rank, chosen rank and closeness between matrices.

    Revisit the determinant and the choice of rank

  • “The errors cancel out, so no information has been lost.”

    Adding the signed errors gives 0, but adding their squares gives 4 kWh squared. Total energy use can remain correct while its distribution is wrong.

    Revisit the residual and its Frobenius norm

  • “The small mode is noise, so I can definitely discard it.”

    The contrast mode has a smaller amplitude, but it tells us which circuit uses more. Its singular value does not establish its nature. Set the task and the tolerance before accepting the loss.

    Test a requirement for each entry

Exercise 2 · Compare calculations

What if we kept only the contrast?

For the same table A, take B = σd qqᵀ: keep the mode with amplitude 2 kWh and remove the mode with amplitude 8 kWh. Calculate B, then the sum of the squares of A − B and its Frobenius norm. Compare with Aᵣ.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

In qqᵀ, the diagonal entries are 1/2 and the other entries are their negatives.

Hint 2

The residual A − B is the common background. Its 4 entries are identical.

Compare with the solution

The diagonal of B is 2/2 = 1 kWh; the off-diagonal entries are -1 kWh. Its columns are negatives of each other and nonzero: B also has rank 1. The negative values are mode contributions; B alone does not respect the positivity of energy use.

A − B has 4 kWh in every entry. The sum of the squares is 4 × 16 = 64 kWh squared. Its square root is 8 kWh. It exceeds the 2 kWh obtained by keeping the common mode.

The two candidates have the same rank. Their reconstruction quality differs because one retains the larger amplitude and the other the smaller one. This calculation compares these candidates; the bound proved in the example establishes the minimum among all candidates of the allowed rank.

Check your reasoning

  • You give the signs of the entries of B and justify its rank.
  • You calculate the residual before summing its squares.
  • You distinguish the sum of the squares in kWh squared from the norm in kWh.

Exercise 3 · Decide and defend

A limit on each energy use figure

The workshop manager accepts an absolute difference of at most 0.5 kWh in each entry. Can we provide an approximation of rank at most 1? First test Aᵣ, then determine whether another candidate of this rank could meet this limit. What decision should we make for this table?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Aᵣ has an absolute difference of 1 kWh in each entry. Rejecting it is not enough on its own to rule out all candidates.

Hint 2

If each difference were at most 0.5 kWh, the sum of their squares would be at most 4 × (0.5 squared). Compare this bound with the proved minimum error.

Compare with the solution

Aᵣ fails: 1 kWh exceeds 0.5 kWh. For any candidate that met the limit, the sum of the squares would be at most 4 × 1/2 × 1/2 = 1 kWh squared; its norm would be at most 1 kWh.

Yet every approximation of rank at most 1 here has an error norm of at least 2 kWh. No candidate of this rank therefore meets all the limits per entry. Keeping the 2 modes recovers A exactly, with an error of 0: we must retain rank 2 to meet this requirement for this table.

This decision concerns a reconstruction of the fictional values provided. It validates neither physical readings nor the ability to predict another time slot. After a hint or reading the worked solution, record the answer as an assisted answer and redo the proof without support on another day.

  • You rule out the other candidates using an overall bound rather than a simple assertion.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What is the difference between the rank of A and the rank r chosen for its summary?

The rank of A counts its independent directions: here 2. Choosing r = 1 imposes a constraint on the new table Aᵣ; it does not retroactively change the rank of A.

Recall 2Does truncating the SVD mean removing a column from the table?

No. We retain the modes associated with the r largest singular values and remove the other products of directions. The dimensions of the reconstructed table remain the same.

Recall 3How can we measure the loss without letting the signs cancel each other out?

Form E = A − Aᵣ, add the squares of all its entries, then take the square root. Here the sum is 4 kWh squared and the norm is 2 kWh. The removed singular value provides another check.

Recall 4What does the best approximation of a fixed rank guarantee, and what remains to be decided?

Truncation minimises the Frobenius error for the allowed rank on the given table, with the selected weights. We still need to check the tolerance needed for the task and the meaning of the removed modes. A small amplitude does not prove that something is noise.

Recall 5What sequence should you reconstruct without support before claiming to understand the method?

Identify rows, columns, units and weights; choose r; construct the retained modes; subtract; calculate and check the loss; assess it against the task. An assisted answer or a page read does not constitute an achieved level.
