Level 2 · C145·M2

Instrumental variables

Your goalEstimate the slope of an effect involving a choice individuals make themselves, using a randomly assigned instrument, and explain the conditions under which the ratio of two differences replaces the least squares slope.

Before you start
c074-m5 : identification assumptions · c066-m5 : standard errors and diagnostics · c101-m5 : rolling-origin forecasting · c145-m1 : endogeneity

✿01 See the idea

A fictional study follows 4 students. For each student, we know x, the number of hours spent studying per week, and y, the score on a test, in points. We want to know how many points an extra hour of studying adds. All the values in this scene are invented for calculation by hand; they do not describe any real group.

The difficulty is that students choose their study hours. Those who study a lot are often also more motivated, and this motivation, which no one measures here, raises the score by itself. We include it in the error u: everything that influences y apart from x. When x and u vary together, x is said to be endogenous. The ordinary least squares (OLS) slope then attributes to the hour of studying a gain that comes partly from motivation.

The idea behind an instrument is to find an event z that changes x without going through motivation. Here, it is a randomly assigned reminder message: z is 1 if the student receives it and 0 otherwise. The random assignment takes no account of motivation. If students who receive the reminder study more and have higher scores on average, the extra points cannot come from their motivation, since the random assignment has distributed it at random. They come from the extra hours, on one condition: the reminder must affect the score only through study hours.

We therefore keep only the part of the variation in hours caused by the random assignment. The ratio of the score difference to the difference in hours between the two groups tells us how many points each additional hour caused by the reminder adds. The result holds under the stated assumptions; it does not yet tell us its precision, because no standard error is calculated in this unit.

The rule to remember

The instrumental variable slope is the ratio of the effect of the instrument on the score to its effect on study hours; it requires an instrument unrelated to the error that actually changes study hours and affects the score only through those hours.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Four students and a randomly assigned reminder

Fictional data. A: 1 h and 5 points, no reminder; B: 3 h and 11 points, no reminder; C: 3 h and 9 points, reminder; D: 5 h and 15 points, reminder. The random assignment placed 2 students in each group. Let us calculate the instrumental variable slope, then the OLS slope to compare them.

  1. Name the symbols and assumptions

    For student i, the model assumes the following relationship:

    yi=α+βxi+ui

    x is the number of study hours (h); y is the score (points); u is the error, meaning everything that affects y apart from x, including motivation (points); α is the expected score without studying (points); β is the slope we seek (points per hour). The instrument z is 1 with a reminder and 0 without a reminder, and has no unit. Cov(a, b) denotes covariance: the mean of the products of the deviations of a and b from their respective means. Cov(z, y) is therefore in points and Cov(z, x) in hours.

    Three assumptions support the calculation. Relevance: Cov(z, x) is nonzero; the reminder changes study hours. Exogeneity: Cov(z, u) is 0; the random assignment is unrelated to motivation. Exclusion: z affects y only through x. We also assume that β is the same for all students. Only relevance can be seen in the data; the other two assumptions come here from the fictional protocol.

  2. Derive the formula

    Take the covariance of z with each side of the relationship. The constant α makes no contribution:

    Cov(z,y)=βCov(z,x)+Cov(z,u)

    By exogeneity, the last term is 0. If Cov(z, x) is nonzero, we can divide:

    β=Cov(z,y)Cov(z,x)

    Let n be the number of students, z̄ the mean of z, ȳ1 and ȳ0 the means of y with and without a reminder, and x̄1 and x̄0 the corresponding means of x. With an instrument taking two values and groups of equal size n/2, the sum of the products of deviations, for both y and x, equals n/4 times the difference in means between groups:

    ∑i(zi−z¯)(yi−y¯)=n4(y¯1−y¯0)

    The common factor n/4 cancels in the ratio. Here n = 4, so this factor is 1. This leaves:

    β^IV=y¯1−y¯0x¯1−x¯0

  3. Calculate the two differences between groups

    Without a reminder, A and B total 4 h and 16 points; with a reminder, C and D total 8 h and 24 points. Each group has 2 students, so the means are 2 h and 8 points without a reminder, and 4 h and 12 points with a reminder.

    The difference in hours measures the change caused by the reminder, the first stage of the reasoning. The score difference measures the change the reminder causes in the score, without relying on a choice made by the students.

    x¯1−x¯0=4−2=2 h
    y¯1−y¯0=12−8=4 points

  4. Form the ratio

    We divide the score difference in points by the difference in hours in hours. The unit of the result is therefore points per hour.

    β^IV=42=2 points par heure

    Under the three assumptions, each additional hour caused by the reminder is associated with 2 extra points. This result describes the fictional example; it gives no measure of precision.

  5. Compare with the least squares slope

    The mean study time is 3 h. The deviations from this mean are -2 for A, 0 for B, 0 for C, and 2 for D. Their sum is 0, which allows us to multiply them by the raw scores rather than by score deviations: the resulting sum is the same.

    S=(-10)+0+0+30=20 h·points

    The sum of the squared deviations in hours gives the denominator:

    T=4+0+0+4=8 h²

    The OLS slope is the ratio of the two sums:

    β^MCO=208=2.5 points par heure

    It exceeds the instrumental variable slope by 0.5 point per hour. This difference is consistent with motivation raising both chosen study hours and scores, but it does not prove this: it only signals a need to investigate.

  6. Check by a second method

    Replace each student’s study hours with the mean for their group: 2 h for A and B, and 4 h for C and D. The OLS slope of y on these replacement values is 2 points per hour, as with the ratio. With a single variable and two equal groups, the two methods agree.

    We can also check the units: points divided by hours give points per hour. If the two groups had the same mean study hours, the denominator would be 0 and no slope would be defined. Neither check shows that the random assignment is exogenous or that the reminder is excluded from affecting the score directly: these conditions remain assumptions about the protocol.

Explain it in your own words

Why must the covariance between z and the error u be 0 for the ratio to target β? And what would happen to the estimate if the reminder also gave advice that directly improved the score?

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “An instrument is a variable correlated with x; that is enough.”

    Correlation with x guarantees only relevance. z must also be unrelated to the error u and affect y only through x. In the example, the random assignment, rather than the data, justifies these two conditions.

    Review the three conditions

  • “When the OLS and instrumental variable slopes differ, the smaller one is correct.”

    Neither is correct by construction. The OLS slope is distorted if x is related to the error; the instrumental variable slope depends on assumptions that these data do not test. The difference is a warning to explain, rather than a verdict.

    Revisit the comparison

  • “As long as the ratio exists, an instrument that barely changes x is suitable.”

    Dividing by a small difference in hours amplifies any small error in the score difference, and a 0 difference makes the calculation impossible. Redo the exercise on the nearly ineffective reminder.

Exercise 1 · Explained application

Another fictional experiment

In another fictional experiment, students without a reminder study for an average of 4 h and score an average of 10 points. Those who receive the reminder study for an average of 7 h and score 19 points. Calculate the difference in hours, the score difference, and the instrumental variable slope, with their units.

Hint 1

Start with the two differences between the group with a reminder and the group without a reminder.

Hint 2

Divide the score difference by the difference in hours and check the resulting unit.

Compare with the solution

Difference in hours: 7 − 4 = 3 h. Score difference: 19 − 10 = 9 points. The ratio gives the slope:

9 points3 h=3 points par heure

Check: 3 h caused by the reminder, multiplied by 3 points per hour, do give 9 points. This calculation still assumes relevance, exogeneity, and exclusion.

Check your reasoning

  • Both differences are calculated as reminder minus no reminder.
  • The final unit is points per hour.
  • The result is presented as conditional on the assumptions.

Exercise 2 · Guided transfer

A nearly ineffective reminder

In the example, the score difference between the groups is 4 points. Suppose the reminder changes study hours by only 1 h instead of 2 h, with the same score difference. What slope do you obtain? Does the same error in the score difference have a greater effect on this result than on the result in the example, and by what factor?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Apply the same ratio with the new denominator.

Hint 2

An error in the numerator is divided by the denominator: compare the two denominators.

Compare with the solution

Slope: 4 points divided by 1 h give 4 points per hour, which is twice the result in the example. For a given error in the score difference, the change in the slope is that error divided by the difference in hours. It is therefore 2 times as large with 1 h as with 2 h.

A relevant but weak instrument thus gives estimates that are very sensitive to chance. If the difference in hours were 0, there would be no slope at all. The exact precision would require a standard error, which is not calculated here.

Check your reasoning

  • The same numerator is divided by the new denominator.
  • The amplification is explained by the ratio of the two differences in hours.
  • The case of a 0 difference is identified as having no solution.

Exercise 3 · Critique of an assumption

The reminder also gives advice

The reminder message contains a study tip that, by itself, adds 1 point to the score of each student who receives the reminder, even without any extra study hours (a fictional assumption). The measured score difference remains 4 points and the difference in hours remains 2 h. What part of the score difference comes from study hours? What adjusted slope do you obtain? Which assumption is violated?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Subtract from the score difference the part that does not operate through study hours.

Hint 2

Ask which term of the model includes an effect of z that does not operate through x.

Compare with the solution

The score difference due to study hours is 4 − 1 = 3 points. Divided by the difference in hours, it gives the adjusted slope:

4−12=1.5 points par heure

The unadjusted ratio was 2 points per hour: it overestimated the effect of study hours. The advice affects y without going through x; it therefore enters the error u, which becomes related to z. The exclusion assumption is violated, and with it the condition that Cov(z, u) is 0. The data alone would not have revealed this flaw.

Check your reasoning

  • The direct contribution is subtracted before division.
  • The adjusted ratio is compared with the unadjusted ratio.
  • The violated assumption is named and linked to the error u.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What are the three conditions for an instrument z for x in y = α + βx + u?

Relevance: z changes x. Exogeneity: z is unrelated to the error u. Exclusion: z affects y only through x. Only relevance can be observed in the data.

Recall 2What is the instrumental variable slope with an instrument taking two values and equal groups?

The difference in the means of y between groups divided by the difference in the means of x, in points per hour in the example.

Recall 3Why can the OLS slope distort the effect of x?

If x varies with the error u, as study hours do with motivation, the slope attributes part of the effect of u to x.

Recall 4What happens when the instrument barely changes x?

The denominator is small: a small error in the score difference is greatly amplified, and a 0 difference makes the calculation impossible.

Recall 5What does the calculation for 4 fictional students show?

How the ratio works. It provides neither a standard error nor proof that exogeneity and exclusion hold.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Write y = α + βx + u and name the unobserved error; choose a randomly assigned instrument z; state relevance, exogeneity, and exclusion; measure the difference in x and then the difference in y between groups; divide the second by the first while keeping the units; compare with the OLS slope without deciding which is correct; point out the sensitivity when the difference in x is small.

What you understand
An instrument isolates the part of study hours caused by an external random assignment; the slope is the ratio of two effects of the reminder. This unit remains scalar: a single instrument, a constant effect, and no standard error.
