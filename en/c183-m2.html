Level 3 · C183·M2

Explicit scheme

Your goalCalculate a new row of prices using only the known row, then check the weights and the scope of the result.

Before you start
c167-m5 : initial and boundary value problem · c051-m5 : error diagnostics · c175-m5 : model limitations · c050-m5 : conditioning · c183-m1 : grid

✿01 See the idea

At expiry, a right to buy an asset for 100 € pays the amount by which the asset price exceeds that amount, or 0 € if it does not exceed it. At asset prices of 90 €, 100 € and 110 €, the payoffs are therefore 0 €, 0 € and 10 €. How can we work back by a small interval before expiry?

On the grid, the explicit scheme produces the next central price from the known central price and its known neighbors. No value from the new row is needed for this calculation. Here, the kink in the payoff at expiry gives positive curvature: the central price moves away from 0 € as we move further from expiry.

All the data are fictional. We isolate the diffusion term of the Black-Scholes-Merton model with constant volatility, a 0 interest rate and no dividends. This is a European option: its payoff occurs only at expiry. Amounts are expressed per unit of the underlying asset.

To close this tiny grid, we artificially impose 0 € at the left boundary and 10 € at the right boundary during the calculation. These boundary values are data for our truncated problem; they are not the actual option prices before expiry. The aim is to understand a grid operation without treating its result as an accurate market price.

The rule to remember

Read only the known row, apply the weights for the step, write into a separate row, and check the boundaries and the time step size.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Work back one step before expiry

The central asset price and the strike price are both 100 €. The spatial step is h = 10 €, the volatility is σ = 0.20 per square root of a year, and the time step is k = 1/8 year, or 0.125 year. Start from the payoffs 0 €, 0 €, 10 € and calculate the new center.

  1. Orient time and name the quantities

    S is the asset price, in euros; K is the strike price, here K = 100 €. V(S,τ) is the option value, in euros per unit of the underlying asset. The initial payoff for our calculation is max(S − K, 0), where max selects the larger term. T is the expiry date and t is the valuation date, expressed in years from the same origin. We define τ = T − t as the time remaining until expiry. At expiry, τ = 0; increasing τ means valuing the option at an earlier calendar date.

    In the chosen model, with no interest or dividends, the partial differential equation reduces to the diffusion equation below. ∂τV denotes the rate of change of V as τ varies with S held fixed, in euros per year. ∂SSV is the second derivative with respect to S, the curvature of the price, in inverse euros. D(S) is the diffusion coefficient; its units are euros squared per year, since σ has units of inverse square root of a year.

    ∂τV=D(S)∂SSV

  2. Write what is known and what will be produced

    i is an integer position index on the uniform grid, where neighbors are separated by h euros. n is an integer time row index; τn = n × k, with k in years. h and k are strictly positive. Uᵢⁿ approximates V at asset price Sᵢ and time τn. The indices denote locations, rather than powers. The new price is Uᵢ at row n + 1.

    We replace the time derivative with the change over the next interval k, and the curvature with a centered difference on row n. The coefficient Dᵢ means D(Sᵢ). This substitution is an approximation, rather than an identity with the continuous price.

    Uin+1−Uink=DiUi−1n−2Uin+Ui+1nh2

    Multiplying by k and adding the known central value isolates the new cell. This is what explicit means. The general formula below applies only to interior nodes; boundary values are supplied separately.

    Uin+1=Uin+μi(Ui−1n−2Uin+Ui+1n),μi=kDih2

  3. Form the dimensionless coefficient

    At the center, the square of σ is 1/25 per year and the square of S is 10000 euros squared. Their product is 400 euros squared per year. Dividing it by 2 gives D = 200 euros squared per year.

    The square of h is 100 euros squared. The product kD is 25 euros squared. Thus μ = 25/100 = 1/4 = 0.25, with no unit. This coefficient measures the size of the time step relative to the square of the spatial step.

    D(S)=σ2S22,μ=1/8×200100=1/4

  4. Calculate the curvature, then the change

    The second difference at the center is 0 − 2 × 0 + 10 = 10 €. We subtract twice the center to compare the neighbors with a locally straight profile. A straight profile would have a second difference of 0.

    The approximate curvature is 10/100 = 1/10 in inverse euros. Multiplying by D gives 20 €/year, then multiplying by k produces a change of 5/2 €. The new center is therefore 0 + 5/2 = 5/2 €, or 2.50 €.

    Unouveau=0+1/4×(0−2×0+10)=2.50 €

  5. Check using the weights and units

    Grouping the central term gives an update with weights μ, 1 − 2μ, μ applied to the left, center and right values respectively. Here, twice μ is 1/2; the central weight is therefore 1/2. The sum is 1/4 + 1/2 + 1/4 = 1.

    An alternative calculation: 1/4 × 0 + 1/2 × 0 + 1/4 × 10 = 2.50 €. This is the same result. The weights have no unit; the terms being added are in euros. The result stays between 0 € and 10 €, the minimum and maximum of the data used.

    To preserve this property on a grid, we impose 0 ≤ μᵢ ≤ 1/2 at every interior node. The weights are then nonnegative and their sum is 1. With identical boundary data, a small perturbation of the known row cannot be amplified in the maximum norm during a step. This is a sufficient stability condition for this discrete diffusion, rather than a proof of accuracy.

    Here, the only interior node gives k ≤ 1/4 year; our step of 1/8 year satisfies the condition. On a wider grid, D varies with S: every node must be checked and the strictest constraint used. Adding an interest rate or a dividend would change the coefficients; applying our rule would require a new calculation.

    k≤h22Di pour chaque nœud intérieur

  6. Write the next row and limit the conclusion

    Keep the known row throughout all calculations, fill a separate row and impose its boundaries at the new time. Then swap the roles of the rows. In our grid, the new row is 0 €, 2.50 €, 10 € at time τ = 0.125 year. With several interior nodes, immediately replacing an old cell would cause neighboring cells to read a mixture of different times.

    The condition on μ controls the amplification of perturbations; it removes neither the discretization error nor the boundary error. Studying the accuracy of the curvature approximation requires a sufficiently smooth profile; the initial payoff has a kink. We have calculated an exact step of the discrete problem without proving convergence to the continuous price. Convergence tests belong to a separate unit.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the calculation. Explain why the new center depends only on the known row, why the denominator contains the square of h, and why increasing τ moves the calendar date back. Then reconstruct the result using the curvature and then the weights.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I start at expiry and move the date t forward with the same sign.”

    The calculation time is τ = T − t. We move τ forward as we work back before expiry. Restate this definition before choosing the sign of diffusion; the calculated value must answer the problem with this time orientation.

    Review the time orientation in the example

  • “Explicit means I can overwrite each cell as soon as it is calculated.”

    All the data on the right of the formula carry n. Use a separate output row, impose its boundaries, then swap the rows. The word explicit indicates that the new values are isolated; it does not allow mixing times.

    Review how to write the next row

  • “With a finer spatial step, my old time step will remain safe.”

    μ contains the square of h in the denominator. With D fixed, dividing h by 2 multiplies μ by 4. At the center, h = 5 € would give μ = 1 with the old step: the condition for nonnegative weights would be lost. The new time step bound would be 1/16 year, or 0.0625 year.

    Test an excessively large step in the last exercise

Exercise 1 · Reconstruct the mechanism

A value without recalculating the curvature

Start again from the data 0 €, 0 €, 10 € and μ = 1/4. Reconstruct the left, central and right weights, then the new center. Explain why this operation is explicit and check the range of the result.

Hint 1

The center receives its initial weight 1, then loses 2 times μ.

Hint 2

The weights must be nonnegative and their sum must be 1.

Compare with the solution

The weights are 1/4, 1 − 2 × 1/4 = 1/2, then 1/4. Their sum is 1. The new center is 1/4 × 0 + 1/2 × 0 + 1/4 × 10 = 2.50 €.

Every input used is already known at expiry. The result stays between 0 € and 10 €, as expected from the nonnegative weights. This check verifies the consistency of the step; it does not validate the artificial boundaries as actual prices.

Exercise 2 · Continue on paper

Work back one more step

Hint 1

The central weight now multiplies 2.50 €, while the boundaries remain imposed.

Hint 2

For an independent check, start from the known center and add μ times the new second difference.

Compare with the solution

Keep the entire row at time 0.125 year. The center contributes 1/2 × 5/2 = 5/4 €. The right boundary contributes 1/4 × 10 = 5/2 € and the left boundary contributes 0 €. The result is 5/4 + 5/2 = 15/4 €, or 3.75 €.

Another route: 5/2 + 1/4 × (0 − 2 × 5/2 + 10) = 15/4 €. The time reached is 2 × 1/8 = 1/4 year, or 0.250 year. The new row is 0 €, 3.75 €, 10 €.

Starting again from the initial payoffs would repeat the first step. Using the entire new row allows us to continue the same discrete problem. Keeping the boundaries fixed is an assumption of the exercise, rather than a general property of options.

Exercise 3 · Diagnose and repair

The shortcut that amplifies

Someone wants to go faster and starts again from the initial payoffs with k = 1 year, without changing the grid or D. Calculate μ, the central weight and the result of the step. Diagnose the defect, then propose a bound on k. What happens to this bound at the center if h is divided by 2?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

With k = 1 year, μ is the quotient of 200 by 100.

Hint 2

A weighted average cannot exceed the maximum of its inputs when its weights are nonnegative.

Hint 3

To repair the setting, isolate k in μ ≤ 1/2; the square of the spatial step is decisive.

Compare with the solution

μ = 2, twice this is 4, and the central weight is 1 − 4 = -3. The first step gives 2 × 10 = 20 €, since the center and the left boundary are 0.

20 € exceeds the maximum 10 € of the initial row and the imposed boundaries. The property of preserving the data range in the truncated diffusion problem is lost. An error at the center would be multiplied by -3 at the next step, with identical boundaries: it would change sign and grow in absolute value. This setting must be rejected.

The sufficient condition gives k ≤ 1/4 year. The initial step 1/8 year is suitable. If h changes from 10 € to 5 €, its square changes from 100 to 25 euros squared, and the bound becomes 1/16 year, divided by 4. This diagnosis at the center must be repeated at every interior node of an expanded grid.

Reducing k repairs the condition on the weights here. It does not automatically correct the boundaries or prove the accuracy of the price; a separate study is needed.

Check your reasoning

  • Find μ = 2 and a negative central weight -3.
  • Connect the overshoot 20 € and the amplification to the defect in the step.
  • Propose k ≤ 1/4 year, then 1/16 year at the center after refinement.
  • Distinguish the stability condition, the choice of boundaries and accuracy.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1Why does moving τ forward mean working back before expiry?

τ = T − t is the time remaining until expiry. We start from the payoff at τ = 0; increasing τ decreases the calendar date t, with expiry T fixed.

Recall 2What makes this scheme explicit, and how should the rows be organized?

The new value is isolated on the left, and all the prices used on the right belong to the known row. Keep that row intact, fill a separate row with its boundaries, then swap their roles.

Recall 3How can you reconstruct μ and the weights without confusing the units?

μ = kD divided by the square of h, with no unit. The weights are μ, 1 − 2μ, μ. They multiply prices in euros and their sum is 1.

Recall 4What condition preserves the data range, and what changes when the spatial step is divided by 2?

For this diffusion, 0 ≤ μᵢ ≤ 1/2 at every interior node ensures nonnegative weights with a sum of 1. With D fixed, dividing h by 2 multiplies μ by 4 and divides the bound on k by the same factor.

Recall 5What does the calculation 2.50 € prove, and what does it leave unproven?

It gives the first step of our discrete problem with its artificial boundaries. It demonstrates neither the accuracy of the continuous price nor the convergence of a sequence of grids, and it is not an observed quote.
