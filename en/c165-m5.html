Level 3 · C165·M5

Duality

Your goalCalculate the norm of a linear functional on a Euclidean space of triples, interpret it as the length of a representing vector, then explain what remains true in infinite dimensions and when the chosen norm changes the answer.

Before you start
c164-m5 : Fubini-Tonelli · c040-m5 : least squares · c142-m5 : convergence criteria · c165-m1 : Lp spaces · c165-m2 : completeness · c165-m3 : Hilbert · c165-m4 : operators

✿01 See the idea

A fictional dashboard receives 3 readings from a room, stored in a vector x. It combines them into a score: 3 times the first reading, minus 4 times the second, plus 12 times the third. The readings are dimensionless, the coefficients are in points per unit of measurement, and the score is in points.

φ(x)=3x1−4x2+12x3

This score is a linear functional: it turns a vector into a number, preserving sums and scalar multiples. A linear functional is a way to read a vector through a single number. Duality asks two questions: which ways of reading are reasonable, meaning continuous, and how strong is each one?

‖φ‖=sup‖x‖≤1|φ(x)|

The strength of φ, written ‖φ‖, is the largest absolute score it gives on the unit ball, meaning among vectors whose norm is at most one. The set of continuous linear functionals on E, equipped with this norm, is called the dual of E, written E′: E contains objects, and E′ contains ways to measure them.

In a Hilbert space, a continuous way of reading a vector has a representing vector: it amounts to comparing x with a fixed direction a using the dot product. This is the Riesz representation theorem. To obtain the largest score, choose x in the direction of a; this maximum score is the length of a. In the example, this length is 13 points.

All values in this scenario are fictional. They are used for calculation, rather than to describe a real room or adjust a system.

The rule to remember

In a real Hilbert space, a continuous linear functional φ can be written as φ(x) = ⟨a, x⟩; its norm is the length ‖a‖ of a, attained at x = a / ‖a‖. The norm chosen on E determines the dual norm.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

A score with three coefficients

Consider real triples of readings with the usual dot product. The score φ has coefficients a = (3, −4, 12) in points per unit. We want to find its dual norm, a vector that attains it, and a check using the test triple x = (2, −1, 2). All these values are fictional.

  1. Specify the space, norm and assumptions

    Let E be the space of real triples with components xk, where k runs from 1 to 3. The dot product is ⟨u, v⟩ = Σ ukvk and the norm is ‖v‖ = √⟨v, v⟩. The scalars are real. In finite dimensions, E is complete: it is a Hilbert space (C165·M2 and C165·M3).

    A linear functional is continuous if there is a number C such that |φ(x)| ≤ C‖x‖ for every x. The smallest such C is the norm of φ, given by the following formula.

    ‖φ‖=sup‖x‖≤1|φ(x)|

    Keep these assumptions in mind: real scalars, the Euclidean norm, and an orthonormal starting basis. In finite dimensions, every linear functional is continuous; this is no longer automatic in infinite dimensions.

  2. Identify the representing vector

    Let ek be the triple whose components are all 0 except the kth, which equals 1. These triples form an orthonormal basis. Evaluating φ at ek gives the coefficient ak: for the second component, we read −4 points per unit. This orthonormality lets us identify a directly from the coefficients.

    φ(x)=⟨a,x⟩, avec a=(3,−4,12)

  3. Calculate the length of a

    We add the squares of the components, then find the positive number whose square equals this sum.

    32+(−4)2+122=9+16+144=169

    Since 13 × 13 = 169 and 13 is positive, we obtain ‖a‖ = 13.

    ‖a‖=32+(−4)2+122=169=13

  4. Bound the score on the unit ball

    The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, derived from the dot product (C165·M3), is written below. To justify it, expand the square of the norm of x − ta, which is nonnegative for every real t: this quadratic in t cannot change sign, so its discriminant is nonpositive.

    |⟨a,x⟩|≤‖a‖·‖x‖

    If ‖x‖ ≤ 1, then |φ(x)| ≤ ‖a‖ = 13 points. Therefore ‖φ‖ ≤ 13. We still need to check whether this bound is attained.

  5. Check using a test triple

    For x = (2, −1, 2), calculate the score term by term.

    φ(x)=3×2+(−4)×(−1)+12×2=6+4+24=34

    The squares give 4 + 1 + 4 = 9 = 3 × 3, so ‖x‖ = 3. The upper bound ‖a‖‖x‖ is 13 × 3 = 39 points; the score 34 is below it by 5 points.

    After normalization to norm one, this triple gives 34 / 3 ≈ 11.33 points, rounded to 2 decimal places. This is below 13 and well short of the maximum.

  6. Attain the bound with a / ‖a‖

    Take the vector a divided by its length.

    x*=a‖a‖=(313,−413,1213)

    Its norm is ‖a‖ / ‖a‖ = 13 / 13 = 1. Its score is the dot product of a with itself, divided by ‖a‖.

    φ(x*)=⟨a,a⟩‖a‖=16913=13

    The result, 13 points, equals the bound: the supremum is therefore a maximum.

  7. Conclude and state the limitations

    ‖φ‖=‖a‖=13 points

    Checks: the unit is indeed the point; the test triple normalized to norm one gives 11.33 points, below 13; equality in Cauchy–Schwarz holds only if x is a multiple of a, as it is for a / ‖a‖.

    This calculation deals with finite dimensions and the Euclidean norm. For an infinite Hilbert space, Riesz guarantees the existence of a if φ is continuous; it says nothing about a discontinuous linear functional. For another norm on E, the unit ball changes, and so does the result.

Explain it in your own words

Why can the test triple, normalized to norm one, never exceed 13 points? What changes in the reasoning if the norm of E is no longer Euclidean?

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The norm of φ is the largest |φ(x)| over all vectors.”

    Multiplying x by a factor multiplies φ(x) by the same factor: without a limit on ‖x‖, no maximum exists. We restrict to the unit ball. In the example, the test triple normalized to norm one gives 11.33 points, below the bound 13.

    Revisit the upper bound and test triple

  • “Every linear functional is continuous, so it can be represented by a vector.”

    This is true only in finite dimensions. On sequences with finite support in ℓᵖ with p = 2, the sum of the terms is linear, but the vector with N terms equal to 1 has score N and norm √N: the quotient √N is unbounded. Riesz requires continuity.

    Redo the exercise on series

  • “The dual of a space always has the same norm as the space itself.”

    The identification of E′ with E comes from the dot product. For the sum norm, the dual norm of the same score is the largest absolute coefficient, 12. For the maximum norm, it is the sum of the absolute values, 19.

Exercise 1 · Explained application

Norm and maximizer of another score

On the same Euclidean triples, a second fictional score ψ is defined below. Give its norm and a triple of norm one that attains the largest score.

ψ(x)=2x1+3x2+6x3

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

First identify the representing vector: its components are the coefficients, because the basis is orthonormal.

Hint 2

Add the squares of the coefficients, then find the positive number whose square equals this sum.

Hint 3

The maximizer is the representing vector divided by its length.

Compare with the solution

The representing vector is g = (2, 3, 6), because ψ(x) = ⟨g, x⟩ in an orthonormal basis. The sum of the squares is 4 + 9 + 36 = 49. Since 7 × 7 = 49 and 7 is positive, ‖ψ‖ = 7 points.

The maximizer is g / 7. Its norm is 7 / 7 = 1. Its score is ⟨g, g⟩ / ‖g‖ = 49 / 7 = 7 points, equal to the Cauchy–Schwarz bound.

Check your reasoning

  • Identify g from the coefficients and justify that the basis is orthonormal.
  • Calculate the sum of the squares and then its square root, giving the unit in points.
  • Normalize g to obtain a maximizer of norm one.
  • Check that the score at the maximizer equals the norm.

Exercise 2 · Guided transfer

Same score, other norms on E

Return to the score in the example, with coefficients (3, −4, 12). Calculate its dual norm when the norm on E is the sum of the absolute values of the components, then when it is the largest |xₖ|. Next, compare with the Euclidean value 13.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Bound |φ(x)| by the sum of the |aₖ||xₖ|, then factor out the largest factor.

Hint 2

To attain the bound, choose a vector in the ball that concentrates on one component or aligns the signs.

Hint 3

Compare the unit balls: which one contains the other two?

Compare with the solution

Sum norm. |φ(x)| ≤ Σ|ak||xk| ≤ (largest |ak|) × Σ|xk| = 12 × ‖x‖; the bound is attained at the basis vector for the third component, with norm one, which gives 12 points. Therefore the dual norm is 12.

Maximum norm. |φ(x)| ≤ Σ|ak| × (largest |xk|), with Σ|ak| = 3 + 4 + 12 = 19. The triple (1, −1, 1) is in the ball and gives 3 + 4 + 12 = 19 points. Therefore the dual norm is 19.

Independent check: ‖x‖ under the maximum norm ≤ ‖x‖ under the Euclidean norm ≤ ‖x‖ under the sum norm, so the ball for the sum norm is contained in the Euclidean ball, which is contained in the ball for the maximum norm. The supremum on a larger ball is larger: 12 ≤ 13 ≤ 19. This agrees with the three calculations. The dual norm therefore depends on the norm chosen on E.

Check your reasoning

  • For each norm, establish an upper bound and then exhibit a vector that attains it.
  • Give both numbers with the unit in points.
  • Justify the ordering of the three dual norms using the inclusion of the unit balls.

Exercise 3 · Guided proof

A continuous functional on square summable sequences

On ℓᵖ with p = 2, the complete space of square summable real sequences equipped with the dot product Σ aₖxₖ, define the functional below. Show that it is well defined and continuous, give the square of ‖φ‖ in exact form, then check the result using the partial sum of 3 terms and its remainder. The numbers are fictional.

φ(x)=∑k=1∞xk(1/2)k
Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Recognize φ(x) = ⟨a, x⟩ with aₖ = (1/2)ᵏ, then ask whether a is square summable.

Hint 2

The series of the squares of aₖ is geometric with common ratio 1/4, the square of 1/2: use the convergence criterion for geometric series and the formula for their sum.

Hint 3

The remainder after N terms is the exact sum minus the partial sum; it should be positive and small.

Compare with the solution

We have φ(x) = ⟨a, x⟩ with ak = (1/2)ᵏ. The sequence a is square summable because the sum of the squares of ak is a geometric series with common ratio 1/4, strictly between 0 and 1 (convergence criteria, C142·M5). Its sum is given below.

∑k=1∞1/4k=1/41−1/4=1/43/4=1/3

By Cauchy–Schwarz for series, Σ|akxk| ≤ ‖a‖‖x‖: the series defining φ(x) converges absolutely and |φ(x)| ≤ ‖a‖‖x‖, so φ is continuous. The vector a / ‖a‖ attains the bound, giving ‖φ‖ = ‖a‖: the square of ‖φ‖ equals the sum above.

Check using a partial sum: the first 3 terms add up as shown below; the remainder, found by subtracting this from the exact sum, is positive and small.

1/4+1/16+1/64=21/64, reste 1/3−21/64=1/192

Check your reasoning

  • Identify the representing vector a and check that it is square summable.
  • Sum the geometric series using its common ratio and obtain the exact value of the square of ‖φ‖.
  • Use Cauchy–Schwarz for continuity and attainment of the bound.
  • Check using the partial sum plus the remainder, without rounding.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does the dual E′ of a normed space E contain, and how do we measure one of its elements?

The continuous linear functionals from E to ℝ. The norm of φ is the largest |φ(x)| over vectors whose norm is at most 1.

Recall 2What does the Riesz representation theorem say, and under what assumptions?

In a real Hilbert space, every continuous linear functional φ can be written as φ(x) = ⟨a, x⟩ for a unique vector a, with ‖φ‖ = ‖a‖. Completeness and continuity are required.

Recall 3How do we prove ‖φ‖ = ‖a‖ in two steps?

Cauchy–Schwarz gives |⟨a, x⟩| ≤ ‖a‖ if ‖x‖ ≤ 1. Then a / ‖a‖, with norm 1, attains ‖a‖ when a is not the 0 vector.

Recall 4How does the dual norm of the score in the example depend on the norm on E?

12 with the sum norm, 13 with the Euclidean norm, 19 with the maximum norm. The conjugate exponents satisfy 1/p + 1/q = 1.

Recall 5Why is “linear” not enough to represent φ by a vector in infinite dimensions?

Continuity is also required. The sum of the terms on sequences with finite support is linear but unbounded. In finite dimensions, every linear functional is continuous.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Specify E, its norm and its continuous linear functionals; define ‖φ‖ as the largest |φ(x)| on the unit ball; in a Hilbert space, identify φ = ⟨a, ·⟩ in an orthonormal basis; bound it using Cauchy–Schwarz; attain the bound with a / ‖a‖; conclude that ‖φ‖ = ‖a‖; changing the norm changes the dual norm; check continuity before applying Riesz in infinite dimensions.
