Sleep architecture

Your goalRead the sequence of stages in an excerpt from a night, calculate their durations and explain what information a total loses.

Before you start
c086-m5 : emotion and cognition · c056-m5 : effect size · c087-m1 : circadian rhythms · c087-m2 : sleep pressure

✿01 See the idea

Camille looks at a chart showing an excerpt from her night. She sees periods of light sleep, deep sleep and REM sleep, followed by a return to wakefulness. Stating only how long she slept would lose this sequence. Sleep architecture means the organization of stages over time: their sequence, their duration and the interruptions.

Our chart is entirely fictional: it is used to learn how to read a record whose stages have already been classified. It covers 180 min, or 3 h, and does not represent a whole night. Its durations are chosen for calculation; they give neither an ideal distribution nor the result of a measurement on a person.

Non-REM sleep, often called "slow sleep" in French, includes N1, N2 and N3. N1 is transitional sleep; N2 is established sleep, identified in particular by electrical patterns called sleep spindles and K-complexes; N3 is deep slow-wave sleep, characterized by a strong presence of slow waves, which are slow oscillations in the electrical signal. A spindle is a brief burst of oscillations; a K-complex is a large wave with a characteristic shape. These terms name markers used for classification, not positions in a quality ranking.

REM sleep, also called paradoxical sleep, combines relatively activated electrical brain activity, episodes of rapid eye movements and greatly reduced muscle tone. Tone is the background tension in muscles. The name "paradoxical" highlights this contrast; this stage does not sit beyond deep sleep on a single scale. Wakefulness remains a separate category from sleep.

Stages are distinguished using recorded signals: in particular, the electroencephalogram, which measures electrical variations at the scalp; eye movements; and muscle activity. We therefore do not infer any stage solely from Camille being motionless. Here, the labels are given: learning to classify signals or evaluate a device would require another unit.

The rule to remember

Read the sequence before adding. A total describes a quantity; architecture also preserves the order. Every proportion must state the duration used as its reference.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

From the sequence to durations, keeping the question in view

Reread the plateaus in order, with their durations in min: wakefulness 10; N1 5; N2 20; N3 30; N2 15; P 15; wakefulness 5; N2 25; N3 10; N2 15; P 30. What fraction of the time actually spent asleep in this excerpt is classified as P?

  1. State the assumptions before counting

    The segments follow one another with no gaps or overlaps. Every instant belongs to a single given category. The time taken by transitions is absorbed into this simplified division; we do not claim to reproduce a clinical staging procedure. All durations are in minutes, written min, and are positive or 0.

    D denotes the cumulative duration of a stage: D1 for N1, D2 for N2, D3 for N3. The subscripts are stage names, not powers. T with a label E, N, P, S or F denotes time awake, non-REM sleep time, REM sleep time, total sleep time and the observed time window, respectively. All these quantities have the unit min. The window includes its periods of wakefulness; sleep time excludes them.

  2. Group repeated periods in the same stage

    Returning to a stage adds a duration to its tally without erasing the previous period. For N2, first combine the periods before the intervening awakening: 20 + 15 = 35 min; then those after it: 25 + 15 = 40 min. The total D2 is 35 + 40 = 75 min.

    Similarly, D1 = 5 min; D3 = 30 + 10 = 40 min; T_P = 15 + 30 = 45 min; T_E = 10 + 5 = 15 min. The intervening awakening changes the sequence, but does not turn the preceding sleep plateaus into wakefulness.

  3. Build up the time actually spent asleep

    Add the non-REM stages to obtain T_N. Together, N1 and N2 contribute 5 + 75 = 80 min; including N3, T_N is 80 + 40 = 120 min. Then add P, which is also sleep.

    TN=D1+D2+D3
    TS=TN+TP=120+45=165 min

    The observed duration is T_F = T_E + T_S = 15 + 165 = 180 min. The reverse check does give 180 − 15 = 165 min of sleep: periods of wakefulness are counted only once and remain outside T_S.

  4. Choose the denominator the question requires

    The question asks for a fraction of time asleep: the denominator is T_S, which is strictly positive here. Call this unitless fraction p_P. A percentage expresses the same fraction on a base of 100. It does not measure the probability that a stage is correctly classified.

    pP=TPTS=45165=3/11

    The exact quotient is 3/11. The percentage is exactly 300/11 %, or approximately 27.27 %. Keep the fraction until the end, then round only to make it easier to read. This percentage describes this fictional excerpt and its classification, without comparison to a norm.

  5. Check the quantity and the unit

    Recover the numerator by working backwards: (3/11) × 165 min = 45 min. The proportion lies between 0 and 1, because P is included in sleep. It must therefore not exceed 100 %. If time asleep were 0, this proportion would be undefined; it would not be assigned a value of 0.

    With 60 min per hour, the window is 180 ÷ 60 = 3 h, while time asleep is 165 ÷ 60 = 11/4 h = 2.75 h. The denominator for the proportions would remain the same sleep time if all durations were converted to the same unit.

  6. Return to what the sequence tells us

    The first P period lasts 15 min, and the next 30 min. Their total does not tell us that they are separated by wakefulness and non-REM stages: to see that, we must reread the order. A sleep cycle means a sequence of non-REM and REM sleep; it is neither a stage nor an isolated plateau. The durations of these sequences vary, and an awakening alone does not allow us to number a new cycle.

    The record ends at its observation boundary. We do not know the rest of Camille's night. These calculations establish neither a fixed cycle duration, nor a level of recovery, nor the cause of any fatigue. The next unit will further distinguish sleep from fatigue; the evaluation of measurements comes afterwards.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “REM sleep is even deeper than N3.”

    The height of the trace distinguishes categories. N3 belongs to deep non-REM sleep; P combines other electrical, eye movement and muscle characteristics. Return to the category tree before interpreting the height.

    Reread the categories and the axes of the diagram

  • “For the proportion of REM sleep, I always divide by the whole record.”

    First state the question. "Among time asleep" requires 165 min; "in the observed window" requires 180 min. Both fractions are meaningful, but they answer different questions. Start again from the construction of time asleep.

    Revisit the choice of denominator

  • “The total hours allow us to reconstruct the stages and cycles.”

    A total loses the labels and the order. Reclassifying 1 min from N2 to P leaves total time asleep unchanged but changes the distribution. Even totals by stage would not be enough to recover their chronology. Construct the counterexample in the last exercise.

Exercise 3 · Transfer through a counterexample

What a total cannot recover

A program keeps only Camille's 165 min asleep. On a copy of the fictional record, reclassify 1 min from N2 to P without adding or removing any minute from the window. Calculate the new totals for N2 and P, then check total time asleep. Does the saved total alone allow us to choose between the two records? Would it allow us to recover the order of the stages? This reclassification is a counterexample on paper, not an induced physiological change.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The minute removed from one sleep stage is added to another sleep stage.

Hint 2

First compare the totals for time asleep. Then ask what information would distinguish the copies.

Compare with the solution

N2 goes from 75 to 75 − 1 = 74 min. P goes from 45 to 45 + 1 = 46 min. The other categories do not change. Non-REM sleep becomes 119 min; adding it to P gives 119 + 46 = 165 min.

The proportion of P becomes 46/165, or approximately 27.88 %, instead of approximately 27.27 %. Yet total time asleep is the same: 165 min. The saved number therefore does not allow us to choose the correct distribution. Additional information is needed to know the stages.

Even totals by stage would leave the order unknown: moving a plateau to another part of the chart would preserve those totals. A chronology with labels and durations would need to be kept. The counterexample establishes a loss of information in a summary; it judges neither Camille's health nor the actual quality of a program.

Check your reasoning

  • Transfer exactly the same minute between the categories and recover 165 min asleep.
  • Show a different distribution, with P = 46 min.
  • Distinguish the total, the distribution by stage and the chronology.
  • Keep the reclassification as a fictional construction, without inferring an effect on recovery.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does sleep architecture add to total sleep duration?

It describes the stages that follow one another, their durations and the interruptions. The same total time can accompany different distributions and orders.

Recall 2How do non-REM sleep, REM sleep and wakefulness differ?

Non-REM sleep includes N1, N2 and N3, with N3 being deep slow-wave sleep. REM sleep is another sleep state, with relatively activated electrical activity and greatly reduced muscle tone. Wakefulness is outside time asleep; P is not an additional depth number.

Recall 3How do you read the axes and plateaus of a hypnogram?

Time advances horizontally; each height denotes a stage category. The width of a plateau gives its duration. A vertical line indicates a change of category, with no additional duration and no quality score.

Recall 4For the proportion of P among time asleep, what should you divide by what?

Divide the duration classified as P by total sleep time, which excludes wakefulness but includes P. Durations must use the same unit and time asleep must be positive. Multiply the fraction by 100 to express it as a percentage.

Recall 5Why are a stage, a cycle and an assessment of recovery different?

A stage is a category at a point in the record. A cycle is a sequence of non-REM and REM sleep, with variable duration. Neither their diagram nor an overall duration is enough to establish a person's actual recovery.
