Level 2 · C078·M2

Coordinate systems, segments and angles

Your goalConstruct the segments of a drawn hinge, calculate their angle at the correct vertex and check the scale of the coordinates used.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

In a drawing of a mechanical hinge, A and C mark the ends, and B marks the joint. Following A, then B, then C traces a bend. To measure its opening, imagine standing at B and looking towards A, then towards C: the directions to compare start from the same place.

The scene and all its coordinates are fictional. The image is 24 px wide and 12 px high; px stands for pixel. We place the origin O at the centre of the top left pixel, with x pointing right and y pointing down. The axes are perpendicular and the pixels are square: a horizontal step has the same length as a vertical step. This rule for measuring lengths and angles is the metric of the coordinate system.

The positions are A = (2 ; 3) px, B = (8 ; 11) px and C = (16 ; 5) px. In each pair, read x first, then y. The segment [AB] joins two positions; the arrow BA describes the displacement from B to A. The same segment can be travelled in the opposite direction without changing its length.

The vertex of angle ABC is the middle letter B. We measure its interior opening, without a direction of rotation, between 0° and 180°. This calculation concerns the drawing: it gives neither a length in centimetres nor the actual opening of an object photographed from an unknown perspective.

The rule to remember

State the origin, the directions of the axes and their scale. Subtract the common vertex from both endpoints, then compare the resulting directions using a common metric.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Measuring the opening at B

Given the points A = (2 ; 3), B = (8 ; 11) and C = (16 ; 5), in pixels, let us find the lengths AB and BC and angle ABC. The coordinates are exact in this exercise and use the same coordinate system with square pixels. Neither endpoint coincides with B.

  1. From positions to displacements

    Write A = (x_A ; y_A), and similarly for B and C. The letters x and y denote the horizontal and vertical coordinates, in pixels; the subscripts A, B and C name the point. We call the vector BA u and the vector BC v. Here, a vector is a displacement described by its components; ux and uᵧ are the horizontal and vertical displacements of u, in pixels.

    u=A−B=(xA−xB;yA−yB)

    To go from B to A, subtract the starting coordinates from the ending coordinates: ux = 2 − 8 = -6 px and uᵧ = 3 − 11 = -8 px. We need to go left and up. From B to C, vx = 16 − 8 = 8 px and vᵧ = 5 − 11 = -6 px: right and up.

    Reconstruction check: B + u = (8 + (-6) ; 11 + (-8)) = A; B + v = (8 + 8 ; 11 + (-6)) = C. Changing the origin would shift the positions, but these differences would stay the same.

  2. Calculating lengths using both components

    The notation ‖u‖ denotes the Euclidean norm, which is the length of displacement u. The Pythagorean theorem applies to the horizontal and vertical displacements because the axes are perpendicular and have the same scale. A squared length is expressed in square pixels; its positive square root is back in pixels.

    ‖u‖=ux2+uy2

    For u: (-6) × (-6) = 36; (-8) × (-8) = 64; their sum is 100 square pixels. The length AB is therefore 10 px, because 10 × 10 = 100 and a length is positive here.

    For v: 8 × 8 = 64; (-6) × (-6) = 36; the sum is 100 square pixels. The length BC is also 10 px. Adding the components directly would not measure the straight length of the segment.

  3. Comparing directions using the dot product

    The dot product u · v multiplies corresponding components, then adds them. Here it is expressed in square pixels. It equals the length of u multiplied by the length of v and by the cosine of their angle. Let θ be the interior angle ABC, in degrees; cos θ is a number with no unit that equals 0 for a right angle.

    u·v=uxvx+uyvy

    The horizontal contribution is (-6) × 8 = -48 square pixels. The vertical contribution is (-8) × (-6) = 48 square pixels. The sum is -48 + 48 = 0 square pixel. The cancellation of the contributions expresses perpendicularity; it does not mean that the segments have 0 length.

  4. Finding the angle and stating its unit

    Divide by the product of the lengths to remove their size. This denominator is strictly positive because both segments exist and have nonzero length. The units cancel: the ratio has no unit.

    cosθ=u·v‖u‖‖v‖=010×10=0

    The product of the lengths is 100 square pixels. The arccos function gives the unique interior angle with the given cosine, between 0° and 180°. Here arccos(0) = 90°. The result is in degrees: it is neither the cosine nor an oriented angle.

    Result: AB = BC = 10 px and angle ABC is 90°. The downward direction of y has been respected in both vectors; it does not prevent us from calculating an interior angle.

  5. Checking using the sides of the triangle

    Let us take a different route from the dot product. The displacement from A to C is (16 − 2 ; 5 − 3) = (14 ; 2) px. The squared length AC is 14 × 14 + 2 × 2 = 196 + 4 = 200 square pixels.

    The sum of the squared lengths AB and BC is 100 + 100 = 200 square pixels. Equality with the square of AC verifies, by the converse of the Pythagorean theorem, that the angle opposite AC is a right angle. That angle is indeed at B.

    This check confirms the calculation in the stated coordinate system. It does not prove that automatically extracted landmarks would be accurate, or that the angle of a photographed hinge would be the same in space.

Explain it in your own words

Without looking at the formulas, explain why we subtract B from both endpoints, why negative components give positive lengths, and then why a 0 dot product lets us draw a conclusion here. Finally, name an assumption about the image that is still necessary.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “For angle ABC, I compare the positions A and C from the origin.”

    The origin O is not the required vertex. Construct A − B and C − B: both displacements must start from B. Then check that B + u gives A again and that B + v gives C again.

    Review the construction of displacements from B

  • “The vertical component is negative, so the length is negative.”

    Here the sign indicates an upward displacement. To find the length, add the squares and then take their positive square root. An arrow and the same arrow travelled in reverse have opposite components, but the same length.

    Review the calculation of norms and units

  • “All coordinates between 0 and 1 must have the same scale.”

    Dividing x by the width and y by the height can shrink the two axes differently. Before measuring a Euclidean angle in the image, restore the same horizontal and vertical scale. The last exercise shows the discrepancy on the same hinge.

    Compare the scales in the transfer exercise

Travelling the same segment in reverse

Keep the hinge points. Give the components of displacement AB, from A to B, then its length. Does the sign of its vertical displacement indicate an upward or downward movement in our image?

Hint 1

Subtract the starting point from the ending point, for each coordinate.

Hint 2

In our coordinate system, y increases downwards. Then compare the squares with those obtained for BA.

Compare with the solution

AB = (8 − 2 ; 11 − 3) = (6 ; 8) px. Both components are the opposites of those of BA. The positive vertical component describes a downward movement.

The squared length is 36 + 64 = 100 square pixels; the length remains 10 px. Reversing a displacement changes its signs without changing its squared components or its length. To calculate the angle at B, however, we must start again from B towards A.

  • Obtain (6 ; 8) px and interpret the downward movement.

Exercise 2 · Application · changing the required vertex

Is the angle at every vertex a right angle?

In the triangle formed by the same hinge, we have AB = BC and angle ABC equal to 90°. Calculate angle BAC, whose vertex is A. Explain why you can divide the sum of the remaining angles into equal parts.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The sum of the interior angles of a plane triangle is 180°. Subtract the angle at B.

Hint 2

A triangle with two sides of equal length is isosceles. The angles opposite those sides are equal: identify them here.

Compare with the solution

AB = BC: the triangle is isosceles with apex B, and the angles at A and C are equal. They are the angles opposite the equal sides BC and AB. Their sum is 180° − 90° = 90°.

Each therefore equals 90° ÷ 2 = 45°. Angle BAC is 45°, whereas angle ABC is 90°. The middle letter changes the vertex and therefore the question being asked.

Check your reasoning

  • Locate BAC at A, without automatically reusing the angle at B.
  • Justify the equality of the angles at A and C using AB = BC.
  • Show the subtraction, then the division that gives 45°.

Exercise 3 · Transfer · checking the metric

The same hinge, with normalized coordinates

An export of the same image divides all x coordinates by its width 24 px and all y coordinates by its height 12 px. The displacements become u′ = (-1/4 ; -2/3) and v′ = (1/3 ; -1/2), with no unit. Calculate their usual dot product. Does it directly recover the right angle in the image? How can you restore the correct calculation?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The prime marks the displacements after normalization. Multiply their corresponding components, then add.

Hint 2

To return to pixels, multiply each horizontal component by 24 and each vertical component by 12.

Compare with the solution

The horizontal product is (-1/4) × 1/3 = -1/12; the vertical product is (-2/3) × (-1/2) = 1/3. Their sum is 1/4, or 0.25, with no unit. It is nonzero: the usual Euclidean calculation in these coordinates does not recover the right angle.

The width and height differ: the axes have been divided by different factors. Their normalized numbers therefore do not measure displacements in a common unit of length. This does not represent any movement of the hinge.

Back to pixels: u = (-1/4 × 24 ; -2/3 × 12) = (-6 ; -8) px and v = (1/3 × 24 ; -1/2 × 12) = (8 ; -6) px. The dot product returns to 0 square pixel, the norms to 10 px and the angle to 90°. Normalizing the axes separately therefore requires restoring the metric before measuring the angle in the image.

Check your reasoning

  • Recover exactly 1/4 and explain why the dot product is no longer 0.
  • Identify the two distinct scales, rather than claiming that the landmarks have moved.
  • Restore each axis using its dimension and recover the angle in pixel coordinates, keeping the distinction between the image and the real object.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1Which conventions must you state before measuring the hinge?

The origin, the directions of the axes, the order of the coordinates and their scale. Here O is at the centre of the top left pixel, x points right, y points down, and the pixels are square.

Recall 2Which displacements do you construct for angle ABC, and why?

BA = A − B and BC = C − B: the middle letter B is the common vertex. The positions A and C from the origin do not describe this opening.

Recall 3How do you go from two nonzero displacements to their interior angle?

Calculate their norms, then the dot product as the sum of the products of corresponding components. Divide this product by the product of the norms: you obtain the cosine, with no unit. Arccos gives the interior angle; state that it is in degrees.

Recall 4Why can dividing x by the width and y by the height distort an angle?

If the dimensions differ, the axes are shrunk by different factors. Restore a common unit of length, for example the pixel scale of the original coordinate system, before calculating the angle in the image.

Recall 5What does the angle calculated from our fictional landmarks prove?

It describes their geometry in the stated coordinate system. It validates neither automatic point extraction nor the physical angle of an object viewed in perspective.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Reconstruct without rereading: state the coordinate system and unit; choose the vertex; form the displacements; calculate norms, dot product and cosine; find the angle with its unit; check using another geometric property. Then explain why a different scale on each axis requires conversion, and where measurement in the image stops.
