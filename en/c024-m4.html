Centre and dispersion

Your goalDescribe the same set of waiting times by its centre and dispersion, justify each calculation, and explain what these summaries leave out.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

At the counter of a small cafeteria, we record the time between a person arriving and the start of their service. Here is a fictional list, already sorted: 2, 4, 4, 4, 6 min. Each dot in the diagram represents a person. Stacked dots are waits of the same length, not extra minutes.

Saying “around 4 min” locates the group. It does not say how far the waiting times deviate from that centre. The centre answers “where is the group?”; dispersion answers “are the values close together or far apart?” We need to keep these questions together.

The mean imagines sharing the total waiting time equally among all the people. The median splits the sorted list at its middle. They coincide here, but they follow different rules. The range looks only at the distance between the shortest and longest waits; the variance uses the deviation of each waiting time from the mean.

Before calculating, predict what would change if only the wait of 6 min became 16 min. The total would increase; the value in the middle of the list would stay the same. We will return to this change in the last exercise. All the data on this page are fictional, including the variants.

The rule to remember

State which measure of centre you have chosen, accompany it with a measure of dispersion, and keep the list so you can check what the summary hides.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Describe the cafeteria waiting times while distinguishing the summaries

We are describing the 5 fictional durations 2, 4, 4, 4, 6 min. Let us calculate the mean, median, range, and variance of this list, then express its standard deviation exactly.

  1. Define what we are counting

    n denotes the number of observations: 5 observations, with no physical unit. The symbol xᵢ denotes the duration of the observation at position i in the sorted list, in minutes; i is a position number running from 1 to n. Repeated values are observations in their own right.

    Each person receives the same weight. The durations are complete, finite, and defined using the same start and end events. We are describing only this list, without drawing conclusions about all waits at the cafeteria. An empty list would not allow us to define these summaries.

  2. Share the total: the mean

    x̄, read “x bar”, is the mean in minutes. The symbol Σ means to add the terms for all the indicated positions. We add the durations, then divide by the number of people to obtain the equal share.

    x¯=∑i=1nxin

    The repeated waiting times contribute 3 × 4 = 12 min. Including the shortest: 2 + 12 = 14 min. Including the longest: 14 + 6 = 20 min.

    The mean is therefore 20 ÷ 5 = 4 min. The total is a sum of individual durations, not the elapsed time on the cafeteria clock: waits can overlap.

  3. Find the middle: the median

    M denotes the median, in minutes. The number of observations is odd: the central position is (n + 1) ÷ 2 = (5 + 1) ÷ 2 = 6 ÷ 2 = 3. We read the value at this position: M = 4 min. The position is a count of places; it is not a duration.

    At least half of the observations are less than or equal to M, and at least half are greater than or equal to M. With repeated values, we do not require equal numbers of values strictly on each side. For an even number of observations, we would take the mean of the 2 central values. This convention must be stated.

  4. Measure the distance between the extremes

    E denotes the range in minutes; x_min and x_max denote the minimum and maximum durations. E = x_max − x_min = 6 − 2 = 4 min. The subtraction measures the width occupied on the axis, not the maximum duration.

    The range ignores the intermediate positions. It complements the centre, but does not by itself describe the distribution of waiting times.

  5. Keep the size of the deviations without letting them cancel

    A signed deviation is the duration minus the mean. The shortest gives 2 − 4 = -2 min; each central waiting time gives 0 min; the longest gives 6 − 4 = 2 min. The sign tells us which side of the centre the value lies on.

    Adding these deviations gives -2 + 0 + 0 + 0 + 2 = 0 min. Their cancellation does not mean the waiting times are identical. We therefore multiply each deviation by itself: the squares are 4, 0, 0, 0, 4 min².

    The sum of the squares is 4 + 3 × 0 + 4 = 8 min². Larger deviations carry more weight, because it is their square that is added.

  6. Average the squares, then recover the unit of the durations

    V denotes the descriptive variance of this list, in square minutes. We divide the sum of the squares by n to give them equal weight. Squaring corresponds to the exponent 2.

    V=∑i=1n(xi−x¯)2n=85 min²=1.60 min²

    σ, the Greek letter sigma, denotes the standard deviation in minutes. It is the nonnegative square root of V: the length whose square gives V. The symbol √ indicates this root. We keep it exact, without inventing a terminating decimal representation for it.

    σ=V=85 min

    σ is not the mean of the absolute distances, nor a guaranteed waiting-time interval around the mean. We use the descriptive convention with divisor n. A different aim, estimating the variance of a population from a sample, may lead to the divisor n − 1 under additional assumptions; that is not the calculation requested here.

  7. Check the results and keep their scope in mind

    The equal share is consistent: 5 × 4 = 20 min. The mean is between 2 and 6 min. The median is indeed at position 3. The range and variance are positive; the variance would be 0 only if all the durations were equal.

    Another check on squaring: the extreme deviations are opposites and have the same square, 4 min². With the central deviations equal to 0, the sum is indeed 8 min². Results: mean and median 4 min, range 4 min, variance 1.60 min². None of these numbers alone predicts the next person’s waiting time.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The median is the midpoint between the smallest and largest values.”

    The median comes from the position in the sorted list. The midpoint of the extremes may coincide with it, as it does here, but it follows a different rule. In the last exercise, the central position keeps the value 4 min while the extreme changes.

    Find the position of the median again

  • “The sum of the deviations is 0, so the dispersion is 0.”

    Signed deviations cancel around the mean. Here, -2 min and 2 min cancel despite the different waiting times. Work through their squares again: their sum is 8 min², and the variance remains positive.

    Work through the deviations and their squares again

  • “The variance is 1.60 min: it is the average waiting-time difference from the centre.”

    Variance is in min² because it averages squares. Standard deviation returns to the unit min after taking a square root. Neither guarantees that every waiting time will stay within a given band around the centre.

    Reread the variance and standard deviation

Exercise 2 · Reasoned application

The centre moves without spreading the waiting times out

In a fictional variant, each person waits 3 min longer. The set becomes 5, 7, 7, 7, 9 min. Before calculating, predict what happens to the mean, median, range, and variance. Justify the variance by comparing the deviations from the centre.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The total receives the same addition for every person; the mean therefore receives this addition only once.

Hint 2

Subtract the new mean from each new duration. The common addition appears on both sides of the subtraction.

Compare with the solution

Mean: 4 + 3 = 7 min. The list keeps its order: the median becomes 7 min. The range is 9 − 5 = 4 min.

The deviations become 5 − 7 = -2 min, 7 − 7 = 0 min for the repeated waiting times, and 9 − 7 = 2 min. These are exactly the original deviations. The sum of their squares remains 8 min², and the variance 8 ÷ 5 = 1.60 min².

The centre has moved, but dispersion has not increased: all the waiting times have been shifted by the same amount. The standard deviation therefore also stays the same.

Check your reasoning

  • Predict the shift in the centre and the unchanged dispersion before consulting the answer.
  • Give the mean and median in minutes.
  • Prove that the variance stays the same by using the deviations, not the range alone.

Exercise 3 · Analysis of a summary

One wait becomes much longer

Start again from the original set and replace only 6 min with 16 min. There are still 5 people. Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The total increases by 16 − 6, without changing the number of observations.

Hint 2

Read the value at the central position again, then look separately at the extremes. A distant value is not automatically an error.

Compare with the solution

The replacement adds 16 − 6 = 10 min to the total. This rises from 20 to 30 min. The mean becomes 30 ÷ 5 = 6 min.

The sorted list is 2, 4, 4, 4, 16 min. At position 3, the median remains 4 min. The range increases from 4 to 16 − 2 = 14 min.

The median has stayed stable under this particular change, but the total, the mean, and the dispersion between the extremes have changed. The median must be accompanied by information about dispersion, and the long wait must be kept in the list. Its distance from the other values does not prove there is a measurement error and does not justify deleting it. Checking a measurement would require a separate examination; we are not deciding the quality of any real data collection here.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1Which operations distinguish the mean and median?

The mean adds all the values and divides by the number of observations: it represents an equal share. The median uses positions in the sorted list; with an odd number of observations, it is the central value. For an even number of observations, our convention takes the mean of the central values. Repeated values are kept.

Recall 2What does the range measure, and what information does it ignore?

Maximum minus minimum measures the distance between the extremes in the original unit. It ignores how the intermediate observations are distributed. A single longer wait can change this summary substantially.

Recall 3Why square deviations from the mean, and what do we divide them by here?

Signed deviations cancel, even when the values are spread out. Squaring gives a nonnegative contribution and emphasises large deviations. For the descriptive variance of our equally weighted list, we add these squares and then divide by the number of observations n.

Recall 4For waiting times in minutes, what are the units of variance and standard deviation?

Variance is in min². Standard deviation, the nonnegative square root of the variance, is in min. It is neither a bound on each observation nor a mean of absolute distances.

Recall 5Is an identical median enough to conclude that the waiting times have not changed?

No. In the variant, the median remains 4 min, while the mean rises from 4 to 6 min and the range from 4 to 14 min. These summaries describe the observed list; they do not guarantee the next person’s waiting time.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Recall the units. Then explain why adding the same duration to all waits shifts the centre without changing dispersion, while replacing only the longest wait can leave the median unchanged.
