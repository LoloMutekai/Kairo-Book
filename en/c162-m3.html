Level 2 · C162·M3

Programmes

Your goalCalculate the planned and completed minutes of a regular programme, then explain why these numbers do not prove its effectiveness.

Before you start
c161-m5 : limits of application · c087-m5 : measurements and limits · c162-m1 : formal practices · c162-m2 : adaptation

✿01 See the idea

Noa is choosing between two attention workshops, both advertised as "training". Before comparing them, Noa notices that these are not two isolated exercises: each is a programme, meaning a schedule set in advance that combines guided group sessions with practices to do at home. The starting question is simple: what does each programme actually require, week after week?

You already know the components. A formal practice is a period set aside for an attention exercise; an adaptation changes its duration or instructions for the person; limits of application remind us that a personal practice is not a clinical indication; and, as with sleep measurements, a number is only useful in relation to what it measures. A programme puts these components together in a schedule. Real programmes, several of which are cited in the lesson on programmes in Siegel's book, have their own rules: this unit does not describe any of them.

We will distinguish three levels. The planned dose is what the schedule specifies; it is calculated using products and a sum. The completed dose is what actually took place for a particular person; it is counted, and it often remains below the planned amount. The effect is what changes for the person: a feeling, a measurement, an ability. A feeling is a real experience, but it does not by itself prove the programme's effectiveness. This unit calculates the first two levels, never the third.

Programmes A, B and C, Noa, Ilan and all the durations are fictional. They provide clear examples for calculation; they do not describe any existing workshop, any typical level of attendance and practice, or any expected benefit.

The rule to remember

Planned: minutes per week = guided sessions × duration + home practices × duration, then × number of weeks. Completed: count what took place. Effect: never inferred from these minutes.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Compare two fictional programmes, then count how much Noa completes

Programme A lasts 6 weeks, with 1 guided session of 90 min and 5 home practices of 20 min per week. Programme B lasts 4 weeks, with 2 guided sessions of 45 min and 3 home practices of 10 min per week. Let us calculate what each schedules, compare them, then follow Noa, who enrols in programme A. All these values are fictional.

  1. Name the quantities and assumptions

    A regular programme is described by five numbers. S is the number of weeks. G is the number of guided sessions per week and D is the duration of a guided session, in minutes. H is the number of home practices per week and P is the duration of a home practice, in minutes. G and H are counts, with no unit.

    W denotes the planned minutes in a week, in minutes per week, and T the planned total over the whole programme, in minutes. Assumptions: the schedule is identical each week; the durations are nominal, meaning stated rather than measured; a completed session is counted at its full duration. If any of these assumptions fails, the formula no longer describes the programme.

    W=G×D+H×P
    T=S×W

  2. Programme A: the minutes in a week

    Each term multiplies a count by a duration, so it gives minutes. Guided sessions account for 1 × 90 = 90 min and home practices for 5 × 20 = 100 min. Only add minutes to minutes.

    W=1×90+5×20=90+100=190 min

  3. Programme A: the total and a check using another method

    The same week is repeated 6 times, so we multiply the minutes in a week by the number of weeks.

    T=6×190=1140 min

    Check: add the two parts separately. Guided sessions give 6 × 90 = 540 min, home practices give 6 × 100 = 600 min, and 540 + 600 = 1140 min. The schedule contains 6 guided sessions and 30 home practices.

  4. Programme B, then a comparison of the schedules

    Use the same method: 2 × 45 + 3 × 10 = 90 + 30 = 120 min per week, so 4 × 120 = 480 min in total.

    Guided time per week is identical (90 min). The difference comes from home practice, 100 min versus 30 min, and the number of weeks. The difference between the totals is 1140 − 480 = 660 min.

    1140480=2.375

    Programme A therefore schedules 2.375 times as many minutes as B. This ratio compares schedules, not results.

  5. From planned to completed: Noa follows programme A

    Noa attends 5 of the 6 guided sessions and does 21 of the 30 home practices. Let a be the number of guided sessions attended and f the number of home practices completed. We assume that each completed session lasts its nominal duration; R then denotes the completed minutes.

    R=a×D+f×P=5×90+21×20=870 min

    The completed share is the ratio of completed to planned time; multiplied by 100, it gives a percentage.

    r=RT=8701140×100≈76.3 %

    Check: 1140 − 870 = 270 min are missing. Counting the missed sessions directly, 1 × 90 + 9 × 20 does give 270 min.

  6. What these numbers do not tell us

    We have a planned schedule and a nominal completed amount. We have not measured anything that changes for Noa. If Noa reports feeling calmer after 6 weeks, that experience is real and deserves to be recorded; it does not prove that the programme caused it, because other circumstances may have changed.

    To compare the effectiveness of A and B, we would need to specify a measurement in advance, apply it in the same way in both cases, and account for what was actually completed. The ratio 2.375 replaces none of these steps. Finally, an adaptation or a missed session changes the actual dose: it must be recorded alongside the result.

Explain it in your own words

Why can A schedule more minutes than B without allowing us to say that it is more effective? Distinguish planned time, completed time and effect, then state the assumption about durations that makes Noa's calculation valid.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The programme that schedules the most minutes per week must have the most minutes in total.”

    The total multiplies the weekly minutes by the number of weeks. Programme C in the exercise schedules 180 min per week, less than A (190 min), but it lasts 7 weeks: 1260 min, more than A's 1140 min.

    Work through programme C's schedule again

  • “If the schedule specifies 1140 minutes, the person has practised for that long.”

    The total is a planned amount. Noa completed 870 min out of 1140 min, or 76.3 %; 270 min did not take place. Count session by session and state the assumption about durations.

    Revisit the step from planned to completed time

  • “More planned minutes, or a pleasant feeling, proves that the programme works.”

    Minutes describe a schedule and a feeling describes an experience. Neither compares a measured effect using the same measurement under comparable conditions. Even with a ratio of 2.375 between A and B, the calculation says nothing about effectiveness.

    Review the three levels

Exercise 1 · Application

A longer programme with fewer minutes per week

Fictional programme C lasts 7 weeks, with 2 guided sessions of 60 min and 4 home practices of 15 min per week. Calculate its minutes per week and its total, then compare them with A's. What do you notice about the two rankings?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Start with a week: two products, then a sum.

Hint 2

Compare C with A first per week, then in total.

Hint 3

Check your total by adding up guided sessions and home practices separately.

Compare with the solution

A week of C: 2 × 60 + 4 × 15 = 120 + 60 = 180 min. Total: 7 × 180 = 1260 min.

Per week, C schedules 10 min less than A (180 versus 190). In total, C schedules 120 min more (1260 versus 1140), because it lasts a week longer. The two rankings reverse: minutes per week and total dose are two different quantities.

Without a measured effect, neither ranking tells us which programme is more effective.

Check your reasoning

  • One product for each type of session, then a sum in minutes.
  • Total = weeks × minutes per week.
  • The two comparisons are made separately and their reversal is explained.
  • No conclusion about effectiveness.

Exercise 2 · Application

From planned to completed time for Ilan

Ilan, a fictional character, follows programme B. Ilan attends 6 of the 8 guided sessions and does 9 of the 12 home practices. Assume that each completed session lasts its nominal duration. Calculate the completed minutes and their share of the planned time, as a percentage.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Completed minutes combine two products: completed count × duration.

Hint 2

B's planned total was calculated in the example.

Hint 3

The share is the completed time divided by that total, then multiplied by 100.

Compare with the solution

Guided sessions: 6 × 45 = 270 min. Home practices: 9 × 10 = 90 min. Completed: 270 + 90 = 360 min.

B's planned time is 480 min. The share is 360 / 480, or 75 % of the planned time.

This percentage describes the dose Ilan completed under the assumption of nominal durations. It describes neither an effect nor "good" attendance and practice: we have set no threshold.

Check your reasoning

  • Two products of count × duration, then their sum.
  • Division by B's planned total, not A's.
  • The assumption of nominal durations is stated.
  • No judgement of effectiveness and no invented threshold.

Exercise 3 · Analysis and transfer

Shorten the home practices

Noa adapts programme A: the guided sessions stay the same, but each home practice lasts 10 min instead of 20 min, with the same number of practices. Calculate the new planned total and the reduction compared with A. Someone then claims: "The shortened version works just as well as A." What can and cannot be concluded?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Only the products for home practices change; keep A's guided sessions.

Hint 2

To check the reduction, multiply the total number of home practices by the minutes removed from each.

Hint 3

For the quoted statement, ask yourself what measurement was taken and whether the conditions were comparable.

Compare with the solution

Home practice per week: 5 × 10 = 50 min. Week: 90 + 50 = 140 min. Total: 6 × 140 = 840 min.

Reduction: 1140 − 840 = 300 min. Check: 30 practices each lose 20 − 10 min, which does give 300 min. The adapted total remains 360 min higher than B's.

We can conclude that the adapted version schedules fewer minutes than A and more than B. We cannot conclude that it works "just as well": the claim compares effects, but no measurement specified in advance or comparison under comparable conditions is provided. The adaptation must be recorded with any assessment, because it changes the actual dose.

Check your reasoning

  • Only the home practice terms are changed.
  • The reduction is checked using another method.
  • Correct comparison with both A and B.
  • Clear distinction between planned dose and claimed effect.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What distinguishes a programme from an isolated formal practice?

A programme is a schedule set in advance that combines several sessions, guided or at home, over several weeks; a formal practice is only one component.

Recall 2What is the difference between planned dose and completed dose?

The planned dose is calculated from the schedule; the completed dose counts the sessions actually completed, assuming nominal durations. Noa: 870 min completed out of 1140 min planned.

Recall 3How do you find the planned minutes of a regular schedule?

W = G × D + H × P per week, then T = S × W; each product gives minutes. Programme A: 190 min per week, 1140 min in total.

Recall 4Does a programme with more minutes per week always have more minutes in total?

No. A schedules 190 min per week versus 180 for C, but C totals 1260 min versus 1140 min because it lasts longer.

Recall 5What do a total number of minutes or a positive feeling establish about a programme's effectiveness?

Nothing by themselves: the total describes the schedule, and the feeling describes an experience. Comparing effects requires a measurement specified in advance and comparable conditions.

Reconstruct the reasoning

A programme is a schedule; I name S, G, D, H and P with their units; I calculate W = G × D + H × P then T = S × W, assuming a regular schedule and nominal durations; I then count the completed time session by session and compare it with the planned time; I keep the effect separate, because neither a total number of minutes nor a feeling proves it; I record any adaptation alongside the result.
