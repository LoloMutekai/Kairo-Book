Flux and divergence

Your goalCalculate the signed flow rate through a box, then explain what its divergence measures locally, using the correct units and orientation.

Before you start
c058-m5 : change of variables · c057-m5 : Hessian · c027-m5 : complex numbers · c127-m1 : parametric curves · c127-m2 : fields · c127-m3 : circulation

✿01 See the idea

Imagine a transparent box drawn in the air. Its faces are imaginary: air can pass through them. It moves to the right, at 0.50 m/s on the left face and at 1.00 m/s on the right face. The faces have the same area. Over the same short time interval, more air by volume leaves on the right than enters on the left.

Flux measures what passes through a surface, taking the chosen direction into account. For the boundary of a box, we choose the outward direction everywhere. Inflow therefore contributes negatively and outflow positively. A velocity parallel to a face does not cross it: its contribution is 0.

Divergence examines this balance near a point, divided by the volume under consideration. It distinguishes air moving fast from air whose motion causes local expansion. Velocity is a vector; flux and divergence here are signed numbers with different units.

The entire field and all the dimensions in this scene are fictional. We describe an instant, without assuming constant density or incompressible air, meaning air constrained to preserve its volume locally. An excess of outward volumetric flow rate does not demonstrate the creation of matter.

The rule to remember

Project velocity onto the chosen normal to measure flux; divide the balance for a small box by its volume to understand divergence.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

What passes through the box, then what expands locally

In an orthonormal Cartesian coordinate system, x, y and z are positions in metres. Box B occupies 0 ≤ x ≤ L, 0 ≤ y ≤ H and 0 ≤ z ≤ P, with L = 2 m, H = 3 m, P = 1 m. The velocity is v(x,y,z) = (u + αx, 0, 0), where u = 0.50 m/s and α = 0.25 per second. Let us find the total outward flux and the divergence.

  1. Define the surface and its orientation

    S denotes an oriented surface; n is its normal vector of length 1, perpendicular to the surface and dimensionless. On the boundary of B, n points outwards. The components of v, written vx, vᵧ and v_z, are in m/s. The field is defined and continuously differentiable around the box; here it depends only on x.

    The dot product v·n adds the products of corresponding components. It measures the velocity through the face in the direction of n. On the left face, n = (-1, 0, 0); on the right, n = (1, 0, 0). Changing the normal alone is enough to change the sign.

  2. Convert velocity into a flow rate through a surface

    Φ_S, read as ‘phi sub S’, denotes the flux of v through S. The element dS is a small area, in m²; the integral adds the contributions over the entire surface. A surface can consist of several faces: their signed contributions add together.

    ΦS=∫S(v·n)dS

    If v·n is constant on a face of area A, the integral equals (v·n)A. The units give (m/s) × m² = m³/s. Here A is the area of a face perpendicular to x: A = H × P = 3 × 1 = 3 m². The volume V of B is A × L = 3 × 2 = 6 m³.

  3. Calculate the flux through the left face

    On this face, x = 0 m and vx = u = 0.50 m/s. The dot product is -1 × 0.50 = -0.50 m/s. It is negative: the air enters while the normal points outwards.

    Φgauche=-1/2×3=-3/2 m³/s

    The absolute value 1.50 m³/s describes the incoming amount per unit time. The minus sign describes its orientation in our balance.

  4. Calculate the outflow and handle the other faces

    On the right, x = L. The increase in velocity between the faces is αL = 0.25 × 2 = 0.50 m/s. The velocity reaches u + αL = 0.50 + 0.50 = 1.00 m/s. Since n points towards increasing x, v·n keeps this positive sign.

    The right flux is 1.00 × 3 = 3.00 m³/s. On faces normal to y or z, the normal component of v is 0 m/s: all these fluxes are therefore 0, even though air moves along these faces. Their sum is 0 m³/s.

  5. Combine the contributions to the net outflow

    Φ_B now denotes the flux through the entire boundary of B, still oriented outwards. We add the signed fluxes; taking their absolute values would count crossings without forming a net balance.

    ΦB=-3/2+3+0=3/2 m³/s

    Check the direction: air crosses the same area faster on the right, so the balance must be positive. Check the dimensions: we obtain a volume per second, not a velocity. This value is the balance for the entire box, not yet a local quantity.

  6. Read divergence from the field's variations

    Divergence, written div v or ∇·v, is a scalar defined in this coordinate system as the sum of the derivatives of each component along its own axis. The symbol ∂ indicates a partial derivative: we vary the coordinate in the denominator while holding the others fixed. Each term is in (m/s)/m, hence in inverse seconds, read as ‘per second’.

    divv=∂vx∂x+∂vy∂y+∂vz∂z

    The derivative of u + αx with respect to x is α, since u is constant. The derivatives of the 0 components are 0. Here the ratio of changes is exact for any length: (1.00 − 0.50)/2 = 0.25 per second. Thus div v = α = 0.25 per second at every point. This is not the norm of v.

  7. Justify the local meaning and check another way

    Take a small cubic box B_h centred at a point M, with side length h metres, where h is strictly positive. The faces perpendicular to x have area h squared and their velocities differ by αh; the other faces do not contribute. The balance is therefore α times h cubed, while the volume is h cubed. Their ratio is α, even as h tends to 0. This is the net outflow per unit volume in a region shrinking around M.

    ΦBhh3=αh×h2h3=α

    For our large box, the same subtraction gives Φ_B = [(u + αL) − u]HP = αV directly. A second way to check therefore gives 0.25 × 6 = 1.50 m³/s, matching the sum over the faces. This proof uses our affine field; it does not justify replacing a finite balance in general with the divergence at an arbitrary point.

  8. Interpret the result without inventing a source of matter

    Positive divergence here means that the motion tends to expand a small volume of air carried along with it. Our box B, however, is fixed in space. The outward volumetric flow rate can exceed the inward rate in a compressible gas; a mass balance would also require the density, meaning mass per unit volume.

    If α were 0 per second while u stayed the same, the left and right fluxes would be opposite, with values -1.50 and 1.50 m³/s. The balance and divergence would be 0 while air would continue moving at 0.50 m/s. Nonzero motion alone is therefore not enough to make divergence positive.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “Air moves right on both faces: both fluxes are positive.”

    The sign compares velocity with the normal, not with the page. The left outward normal points left: its dot product with velocity is negative. Draw the normals again before multiplying by the areas.

    Review the velocity arrows and the normals

  • “Flux equals velocity multiplied by any area.”

    Only the normal component crosses the face. On a face parallel to the airflow, the dot product is 0; multiplying the norm of v by its area would give a crossing that does not occur. Also check the units: m/s becomes m³/s after multiplication by an area.

    Review the projection and the side faces

  • “Greater velocity implies greater divergence.”

    Divergence measures spatial variations, not the value of velocity. Moving the box within our field increases the velocities on the faces without changing their difference. The second exercise lets you repeat this check.

Compare with the solution

With the outward normal, v·n = -0.50 m/s and the flux is -1.50 m³/s. Reversing the normal makes this product 0.50 m/s; the flux is 1.50 m³/s. The physical motion is identical: only our orientation has changed.

Compare with the solution

The volume becomes 4 × 3 = 12 m³. The outermost faces give -1.50 + 4.50 = 3.00 m³/s. The total flux has doubled, but its ratio to volume is 3/12 = 1/4 per second, or 0.25 per second.

Checking with the boxes side by side gives the same result: each small box contributes 1.50 m³/s. The flux 3.00 m³/s leaving the first and the flux -3.00 m³/s entering the second cancel on their shared face. Only the outer boundary remains, with a balance of 3.00 m³/s.

Divergence remains α: the field's derivatives have not changed. Here it is constant and flux scales with volume. In a field with variable divergence, this ratio alone for a large box would not determine the divergence at every point. ✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What determines the sign of flux through a face?

The sign of v·n, where n is the chosen normal. With an outward normal, inflow contributes negatively and outflow positively; tangential velocity gives a 0 product.

Recall 2What are the units of velocity flux and its divergence, and why do they differ?

Flux integrates normal velocity over an area: it is in m³/s. Divergence differentiates velocity components with respect to positions: it is per second. The ratio of a volumetric balance to a volume has this latter unit.

Recall 3How can you recover the divergence of our field without adding the fluxes of the large box?

Differentiate vₓ = u + αx with respect to x: you get α. The derivatives of vᵧ and v_z are 0. Their sum is 0.25 per second, independently of the value of u and the position.

Recall 4Why shrink a box around a point to interpret divergence?

The balance for a large box mixes several regions. By shrinking the box and dividing by volume, we isolate a local quantity. For our affine field, the ratio already equals α for any small box aligned with the axes.

Recall 5Does 0 total flux mean the air is stationary?

No. A uniform airflow can enter and leave with opposite flow rates that cancel. Nor does an excess of volumetric flow rate prove the creation of matter: mass also depends on density.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Reconstruct without support: draw a normal on each face; define flux using normal velocity and area; add the signed contributions; define divergence using spatial derivatives; justify its meaning as a local balance per unit volume. Finish with moving air that has 0 divergence and with the units of both quantities.
