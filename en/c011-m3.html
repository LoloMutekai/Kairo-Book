Level 0 · C011·M3

Geometric sequences

Your goalFind the number of beads in a row and the sum of successive rows when the same factor is applied repeatedly.

Before you start
c010-m5 : logarithmic scales · c011-m1 : summation notation · c011-m2 : arithmetic sequences

✿01 See the idea

You are making a bracelet from rows of beads. The first row contains 2 beads. To make the next row, take 2 complete copies of the previous row. Picture the copies before counting: each must keep all the beads in the pattern it reproduces.

You get 2, then 4, then 8, then 16 beads. The rule “double” stays the same, but the amounts added are successively 2, 4 and 8 beads. Keeping the same factor and keeping the same amount added are different rules.

A sequence is an ordered list of numbers; each number is a term and its position is its index. A sequence is geometric when each term is obtained from the previous term by multiplying by the same factor, called the common ratio. Here, the terms count the beads in the rows and the common ratio counts the copies.

There are two questions about the same bracelet: how many beads are in the last row, and how many are in all the rows made so far? The total includes the previous rows. The data are fictional and chosen so that the beads can be counted in the drawing. We study a finite number of rows, with a positive whole number of copies and no beads lost.

The rule to remember

Same common ratio: multiply at each step. For the total, count all the rows; multiplying this total by the common ratio shifts the terms and lets you find a finite sum.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

The last row and all the beads in the bracelet

The first pattern contains 2 beads; each following row uses 2 copies of the previous row. Let us calculate the term at index 4 and the sum of the first 4 rows, then check the formula against the counted beads.

  1. Define what the letters mean

    n is the index of the last row counted; k runs through the rows. They are integers greater than or equal to 1, with no physical unit. un denotes the number of beads at index n; u at index 1 is the initial term. Sn denotes the sum of the beads from index 1 to n inclusive. un and Sn are expressed in beads.

    q is the common ratio, constant from one step to the next. In this scene, q is an integer greater than or equal to 1 and u at index 1 is strictly positive. q has no unit: a beads/beads ratio gives a number of copies. The rows are separate and all their beads stay in the bracelet.

    un+1=q×un

    This equation tells us how to make the next row from the previous one. Here, the initial term is 2 beads, the common ratio is 2 and the last index requested is 4.

  2. Count the steps before multiplying

    At index 1, the initial term already exists. To reach index n, there are n − 1 steps left, so that many factors q must be applied. The power of q with this exponent means this repeated product; by convention, q to the power 0 equals 1 for our positive q.

    un=u1×qn−1

    For index 4, we count 4 − 1 = 3 steps. The repeated product equals 8; it multiplies the initial term rather than replacing it.

    u4=2×23=16 perles

  3. Check using neighbouring rows

    We can find each term without powers: 2 × 2 = 4 beads; 4 × 2 = 8 beads; 8 × 2 = 16 beads. The 3 steps do give the expected term.

    Check the common ratio: 4 ÷ 2 = 2, 8 ÷ 4 = 2, 16 ÷ 8 = 2. All the denominators are nonzero. The quotients stay constant, while the differences change.

  4. Add all the rows

    The Σ symbol tells us to add the terms. Below it, k = 1 gives the first index; above it, n gives the last index included. The letter k changes within the sum, while n sets where the counting stops.

    Sn=∑k=1nuk

    For our 4 rows, start with 2 + 4 = 6 beads. Then add 8: 6 + 8 = 14 beads. Finally, add 16: 14 + 16 = 30 beads. The total and the last term therefore answer different questions.

  5. Shift the sum to find the total

    In the second drawing, S is shorthand for S at index 4. Multiplying the whole sum by q multiplies each of its terms: qS = 4 + 8 + 16 + 32 = 60 beads. The term 32 comes from an extra row, obtained by 16 × 2.

    Subtract the original sum S from this shifted sum qS. The terms 4, 8 and 16 appear once in each sum, so their contributions cancel. The new final term remains, with the initial term subtracted from it.

    qS−S=32−2=30 perles

    Check using the totals already calculated: 60 − 30 = 30 beads. Since q equals 2, qS − S also equals (2 − 1) × S: we do get S = 30 beads.

  6. Write the finite sum formula

    The same shift works for any finite number n of rows in the model. After multiplication by q, the new final term is u at index 1 multiplied by q to the power n. All the intermediate terms cancel; the initial term is subtracted.

    (q−1)×Sn=u1×(qn−1)

    If q differs from 1, q − 1 is nonzero. We can divide both sides by this quantity to isolate Sn. The numerator counts beads; the denominator has no unit.

    Sn=u1×(qn−1)q−1 pour q≠1

    Here, q to the power n equals 16. Subtract 1: 16 − 1 = 15. Multiply by the initial term: 2 × 15 = 30 beads. The denominator equals 2 − 1 = 1; the quotient gives 30 beads. This is the same result as direct addition.

  7. Handle a factor of one before dividing

    If the bracelet uses only 1 copy of each row, q equals 1 and every row keeps 2 beads. In the previous fraction, the numerator and denominator would both be 0. The quotient 0/0 has no defined value; we must return to counting.

    Sn=n×u1 si q=1

    For 4 identical rows, the total is 4 × 2 = 8 beads. It can still be calculated. The division in the previous formula was what was impossible in this case.

  8. Check the rule and the scope of the calculation

    With the original doubling rule, the total of 30 beads exceeds the 16 beads in the last row, since it also includes the previous rows, all with positive counts. Direct addition and shifting give the same total: these are two ways to check.

    If we always added 2 beads, we would get 2, 4, 6, 8 beads. Their sum would be 20 beads, which is 10 beads fewer than the doubled bracelet. Matching initial terms do not mean the rules are the same.

    The upper bound n is finite. This calculation does not define the total of infinitely many rows: studying that extension would require another concept, a limit, which is outside this unit.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the results. Why does the term at index 4 require 3 multiplications, while the term added at the end of the shifted sum contains a power with exponent n? Explain the role of the rows, then work out the case q = 1 without division.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “Keeping the same factor means always adding the same amount.”

    Count the extra beads again: 2, then 4, then 8 beads. The factor compares each new row with the previous row by division. In the drawing, each copy reproduces the entire row, even when it has grown larger.

    Count the boxed copies again

  • “The sum fraction also works when q = 1.”

    Look at the denominator q − 1 before dividing. It then equals 0; the quotient is undefined. Instead, count the n identical rows: Sₙ is n times the initial term.

    Return to the case of a factor of one

  • “A finite sum formula already gives an infinite sum.”

    State the last index n that you are counting. The formula proved applies to this finite number of terms. Removing this bound changes the question and requires studying a limit; do not replace n with the word “infinity” in the formula.

    Reread the scope of the final check

Exercise 3 · Justification · special case and scope

The formula seems to stop working

The same bracelet still starts with 2 beads, but each new row uses 1 copy of the previous row. Someone replaces q with 1 in the sum fraction and then claims the total is 0. Repair their reasoning for 4 rows, then for n rows in general. Does this repair calculate an infinite sum?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Write what each row contains before using a fraction.

Hint 2

A 0 denominator signals an impossible division; it does not tell you the number of beads in the bracelet.

Compare with the solution

Multiplication by 1 keeps each row at 2 beads. For 4 rows, adding these identical terms gives 4 × 2 = 8 beads. For n rows, Sn = n times the initial term.

In the fraction, q to the power n equals 1; the numerator and denominator become 0. The quotient 0/0 is undefined. The identity before division reduces to an equality between 0 quantities: it no longer determines Sn. Counting gives the result.

This repair still counts a finite number n of rows. It gives no infinite sum. We would need to formulate another question and study a limit; that work has not been done here.

Check your reasoning

  • I get 8 beads without dividing by 0.
  • I give the general rule for n identical rows and distinguish an impossible division from a 0 total.
  • I keep the finite bound and name the additional question without claiming to have solved it.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What operation identifies a geometric sequence in our bracelet? How can you check it?

Each row is the previous row multiplied by the same factor q. Since the terms here are positive, we can check the ratios between neighbouring terms: they all equal 2. The differences, however, go from 2 to 4 and then 8 beads.

Recall 2How can you find uₙ from the first term, and why is the exponent n − 1?

The first term already exists at index 1. There are n − 1 steps left to reach index n; each multiplies by q. So uₙ is the initial term multiplied by q to the power (n − 1).

Recall 3How can you reconstruct Sₙ by shifting, and what condition allows the final division?

Multiply Sₙ by q, then subtract Sₙ: the common terms cancel. What remains is (q − 1) × Sₙ = initial term × ((q to the power n) − 1). For q different from 1, divide by q − 1. Sₙ counts beads; q has no unit.

Recall 4What happens to the total when each row is just one copy of the previous row?

q = 1 keeps all the terms identical. For n rows, Sₙ = n times the initial term. The fraction with q − 1 in the denominator cannot be used, because it would require division by 0.

Recall 5What else must be specified when we talk about the total of the rows?

The last index n included in the count. Our formulas apply to a finite number of rows. On their own, they are not enough to draw a conclusion about infinitely many terms.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Hide the page and reconstruct this chain: initial term in beads → constant factor with no unit → n − 1 steps → term at index n → sum from index 1 to n → shifting and cancellation → division allowed only if q differs from 1.
