Level 0 · C010·M5

Logarithmic scales

Your goalRead a zoom scale where equal displacements represent equal ratios, then distinguish a length from its position.

Before you start
c006-m5 : composition · c010-m1 : exponents · c010-m2 : roots · c010-m3 : exponential function · c010-m4 : logarithm

✿01 See the idea

You enlarge a photo and track the width of the same detail. It goes from 1 to 2, then 4, 8 and 16 cm. These are fictional lengths chosen for the calculation. Each time you enlarge the photo, the width doubles.

On an ordinary ruler, going from 1 to 2 cm takes up little space; going from 8 to 16 cm takes up more. Yet the zoom operation is the same: multiply the width by 2. A logarithmic ruler assigns the same drawn distance to each of these doublings.

Look at the labels in the second drawing: they still give lengths in centimetres. The positions written below the ruler count doublings from the reference length of 1 cm. Position 0 therefore marks a length of 1 cm. It does not represent a photo with 0 width.

The gold lines mark the same intervals on both rulers. Colour helps you find them; the values and captions also let you compare them without colour. We are studying the scale markings without making assumptions about how photo software works.

The rule to remember

On an increasing logarithmic scale, equal displacements represent equal ratios. A step to the right here multiplies the length by 2; it does not add a fixed length.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Read the enlargements of the same photo

The detail in the photo takes the widths 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 cm. Let us construct the doubling scale, explain the equal segments, then calculate the displacement from 2 to 16 cm. The reference length and base stay fixed throughout all calculations.

  1. Define length, reference length and position

    L denotes the width of the detail and Lᵣ the reference length, both in centimetres. Here Lᵣ = 1 cm. Their quotient L/Lᵣ is unitless: we divide a length by a length measured in the same unit. The width L must be strictly greater than 0, and so must the reference length.

    The base is 2, a unitless number greater than 1. Position q is the logarithm of the ratio L/Lᵣ in this base. In other words, q is the exponent that gives this ratio. q is unitless; we read its changes in doubling steps.

    q=log2(LLr);L=Lr×2q

  2. Construct the tick marks by doubling

    The reference length is at q = 0, because 2 to the power 0 equals 1. Multiplying the length by 2 increases the exponent by 1. The successive lengths are 1 × 2 = 2 cm, then 2 × 2 = 4 cm, 4 × 2 = 8 cm and 8 × 2 = 16 cm.

    The corresponding positions are 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4. They determine where to place the tick marks; the length labels remain 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 cm. The logarithm gives a position, not a new length in centimetres.

    23=8⇒log2(8)=3

  3. Distinguish differences from ratios

    The ordinary ruler compares differences: 2 − 1 = 1 cm, whereas 16 − 8 = 8 cm. These differences are unequal.

    To compare the enlargements, divide the final length by the starting length: 2/1 = 2 and 16/8 = 2. The centimetres cancel; the ratios are unitless. The lengths have been multiplied by the same factor.

    On the logarithmic ruler, the displacements are 1 − 0 = 1 step and 4 − 3 = 1 step. Their difference is 0: the gold segments do have the same drawn length.

  4. Relate a displacement to its ratio

    For any movement, La is the starting length and Lᵦ the final length; qa and qᵦ are their positions on the same ruler. The letters a and b are labels, not units. We use Δq, read as “change in q”, for the final position minus the starting position.

    Write each length using the inverse relation. In the quotient Lᵦ/La, the common reference length cancels. Dividing powers with the same base subtracts the exponents; this is why subtracting the positions is equivalent to taking the logarithm of the ratio.

    LbLa=2qb−qa;Δq=qb−qa=log2(LbLa)

  5. Calculate the requested displacement and check it

    From 2 to 16 cm, the starting position is qa = 1 and the final position is qᵦ = 4. Subtract in this order: Δq = 4 − 1 = 3 steps to the right. On the ordinary ruler, the difference would have been 16 − 2 = 14 cm; this is not the number of logarithmic steps.

    Check another way: the ratio is 16/2 = 8. Now 2 to the power 3 equals 8. Multiplying the starting length by this factor gives 2 × 8 = 16 cm. We recover the final length with the correct unit.

    Δq is a signed displacement: it also indicates the direction of movement. The distance between the points is its absolute value, meaning its magnitude without the sign. Returning will change the sign of the displacement but keep this distance.

    Δq=log2(162)=log2(8)=3

  6. Check the domain before placing a point

    Position 0 denotes L = 1 cm. A length of 0 cm has no finite position on this ruler: 2 to any finite real power remains strictly positive. A negative length is also excluded from the real logarithm used here.

    A negative position can nevertheless be correct: a positive length smaller than the reference length lies to its left. The scale can therefore extend beyond the first tick mark drawn; that mark limits the drawing, not the domain.

    If the length is missing from the fictional record, the point stays absent. Distinguish “missing data” from “a known value outside the domain”. Replacing either with position 0 would invent a length of 1 cm.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the calculations. Why are the movements 1 → 2 cm and 8 → 16 cm equal on one ruler and different on the other? Name the units, then explain what position 0 represents.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “One more step always adds the same length.”

    Compare the ratios, then the differences. Here the ratios are 2 and 2, but the amounts added are 1 cm and 8 cm. A step on the doubling ruler multiplies by 2.

    Compare the segments on both rulers

  • “Position 0 represents 0 length; I can also put a missing data point there.”

    Position 0 marks the reference length of 1 cm. A 0 length is known but outside the domain; a missing length is unknown. Neither belongs at this position. A negative position, however, can represent a positive length smaller than the reference length.

    Revisit the domain check

  • “At the midpoint between the 2 and 8 cm tick marks, I should read their ordinary average.”

    The midpoint divides the drawn distance into equal parts. On the logarithmic axis, the ratios on either side must therefore be equal. First find the midpoint of the positions, then return to lengths; averaging the lengths directly answers a different question.

    Test the midpoint on the zoom ruler

Exercise 1 · Warm-up · place and read back

A value is not its position

With reference length 1 cm and base 2, give the position of 4 cm. Then place a width of 0.5 cm on the leftward extension of the same ruler. Justify each position using a power and state which quantity carries the centimetres.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Find the exponent that turns the reference length into the requested length.

Hint 2

Dividing the reference length by the base moves back 1 step from position 0.

Compare with the solution

4 cm corresponds to the ratio 4/1 = 4. Since 2 to the power 2 equals 4, the position is q = 2.

The width 0.5 cm gives the exact ratio 1/2. It corresponds to q = -1, since 2 to the power -1 equals 1/2. Inverse check: 1 cm × 1/2 = 0.5 cm.

Widths are in centimetres; positions 2 and -1 are unitless. A negative position does not mean a negative length.

Check your reasoning

  • Find 2 and -1 by explaining the exponents.
  • Use centimetres only for lengths and read 0.5 cm as a positive length.
  • Check at least one position using the inverse relation.

Exercise 2 · Application · outward and return movements

The movement changes direction

For the same photo, go from 2 to 16 cm, then return from 16 to 2 cm. For each movement, give the final/starting length ratio and the signed displacement. Are the distances between the points different? Keep the return ratio as an exact fraction, then write its decimal representation.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

For the return, invert the quotient and subtract the starting position from the final position.

Hint 2

Check the ratios by multiplying them together; check the displacements by adding them.

Compare with the solution

Outward: 16/2 = 8, so Δq = 4 − 1 = 3 steps. Return: 2/16 = 1/8, or 0.125 with no unit; Δq = 1 − 4 = -3 steps.

The return displacement is negative because the length decreases. The distance between the points is 3 steps in either direction. The distance is positive; the sign indicates the direction of the displacement.

Check: 8 × (1/8) = 1, and 3 + (-3) = 0. The return cancels the outward movement. Multiplying 16 cm by 1/8 does give back 2 cm.

Check your reasoning

  • Write each quotient as final length divided by starting length.
  • Obtain displacements 3 and -3, and the same distance of 3 steps.
  • Keep the fraction 1/8 before its decimal representation 0.125, without adding a unit of length.
  • Check that the movements cancel.

Exercise 3 · Transfer · examine a scale marking

What width is at the midpoint of the segment?

Someone wants to add a tick mark exactly halfway between 2 cm and 8 cm on the logarithmic drawing. They suggest 5 cm. Check their reasoning, find the correct length and check the ratios on either side. Keep the same base and reference length.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The endpoint positions are 1 and 3. It is their midpoint that you need to calculate.

Hint 2

After finding the position, use L = Lᵣ × the base to the power q to return to centimetres.

Compare with the solution

The endpoints occupy positions 1 and 3. To average these 2 positions, add them, then divide by 2: (1 + 3)/2 = 4/2 = 2. We have found the midpoint on the drawing, but not yet the length it represents.

The inverse relation gives L = 1 cm × 2 to the power 2 = 4 cm. Ratio check: 4/2 = 2 and 8/4 = 2. Equal ratios confirm equal drawn distances.

The proposed 5 cm comes from averaging the lengths: (2 + 8)/2 = 10/2 = 5 cm. It would be the midpoint on the ordinary ruler. Here, the midpoint corresponds to 4 cm.

Another check: 4 × 4 = 16 cm² and 2 × 8 = 16 cm². The equal products recover the equality of the ratios by cross-multiplication. The length is squared, so the unit is square centimetres.

Check your reasoning

  • Calculate the midpoint of the positions before returning to a length.
  • Obtain 4 cm and check ratios 2 and 2.
  • Explain why 5 cm answers the question of the midpoint on an ordinary ruler.
  • Distinguish the unitless position, the length in cm and the check products in cm².

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What do equal displacements on the doubling ruler mean?

They correspond to equal final/starting length ratios. Each step to the right multiplies the length by 2; the differences in centimetres can vary.

Recall 2How do you go from a length to its position, then back to the length?

With L and Lᵣ in the same unit and strictly positive: q = log to base 2 of L/Lᵣ; then L = Lᵣ × 2 to the power q. The quotient and position are unitless; L keeps the unit of the reference length.

Recall 3What does position 0 represent, and how should you handle a 0 or missing length?

Position 0 represents the reference length 1 cm. A 0 length is outside the domain of the real logarithm; missing data remain unknown. Neither is replaced by this point. A negative position can represent a positive length smaller than the reference length.

Recall 4How do you relate signed displacement to the ratio of the lengths?

Subtract the starting position from the final position: Δq = qᵦ − qₐ = log to base 2 of Lᵦ/Lₐ. Returning inverts the ratio and changes the sign of the displacement. Its absolute value gives the distance between the points.

Recall 5How do you find the length placed at the midpoint of a segment on this axis?

Find the midpoint of the positions, then use the inverse relation to return to lengths. Check for equal ratios on either side. Between 2 and 8 cm, the logarithmic midpoint marks 4 cm; the ordinary average of the lengths gives 5 cm.
