Level 4 · C263·M1

Temporal labels

Your goalConstruct a future label, align it with its original decision, and determine when it becomes available for training.

Before you start
c105-m5 : Cost and limitations · c201-m5 : Costs and events · c262-m5 : Robustness across regimes · c261-m5 : Model uncertainty · c041-m5 : Transactions and windows

✿01 See the idea

You are preparing a model that, just after a market close, must predict the price change over the next 2 trading sessions. On a record, you note what is already known. You leave the “future outcome” field empty, then fill it in when the horizon ends. This field is the label: the answer that the model must learn to predict.

Our single scene is a fictional sequence of closing prices for the same asset. At session 1, the price of 102 € is known; the price of 105 € at session 3 is still in the future. Seeing both prices today in a historical file does not make them simultaneously available for the past decision.

An input describes the information available for making a decision. A label describes the outcome to predict. Using a future outcome as a label is therefore intentional; slipping it into the inputs or into training before it becomes available creates information leakage: the calculation uses something the real decision could not yet have known.

The record keeps the decision session as its index. We add the target end session and the session when the label becomes available. These markers have different roles, even when the last two coincide. All values are fictional; we are constructing targets, without training a model or measuring performance.

The rule to remember

Attach the future to the right decision, then check separately when the inputs and the label were available.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Complete the record for session 1

The sequence gives prices P at sessions 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4: 100 €, 102 €, 101 €, 105 € and 99 €. Each decision follows receipt of the closing price for its session. The horizon h is 2 sessions; the additional availability delay d is 0 session. Let us construct the past input, the future label, and the rule for admitting the record into training.

  1. Define the contract before calculating

    t is the decision session index, counted in trading sessions, and Pt is its closing price in euros. h is a strictly positive integer number of future sessions; et = t + h is the target end session. d is a nonnegative integer delay, in sessions, between that end session and the availability time used for the label. at = et + d is that availability time. These indices are not calendar days.

    Here the feature xt is the relative price change since the previous session; a feature is a model input. The label yt is the relative change between the decision closing price and the closing price at the horizon end. xt and yt are dimensionless; expressing them as percentages multiplies their values by 100.

    We assume a price for each session, sorted indices without duplicates, no missing sessions in the sequence, and strictly positive prices that are never revised. There are no dividends or changes in the number of shares. We describe a price change, without fees or any assumption that a purchase can be executed at the closing price already received. This label is therefore not a strategy profit.

  2. Construct an input that is actually known

    At decision time, the closing prices for sessions 0 and 1 have been received. Subtract the prices to measure the past change, then divide by the starting price of that change to obtain a relative change.

    xt=Pt−Pt−1Pt−1=102−100100=1/50

    The difference is 2 €. The euros cancel in the quotient; xt = 1/50, or 2.00 %. No price later than the decision was used to construct this input. This describes its availability, without promising that it predicts anything well.

  3. Align the horizon end and availability

    To reach the correct future closing price, add the horizon to the starting index: et = 1 + 2 = 3. Session 2 is intermediate; using it as the end would change the question. The final price for our label is therefore 105 €.

    With d = 0, availability is at = 3 + 0 = 3. We place all decisions and all cutoffs after receipt of the data for the indicated session. Data available at the cutoff itself are therefore admitted. If a feed publishes later, at must be moved; sorting the rows does not correct this delay.

  4. Calculate the exact future label

    Subtract the decision price from the price at the horizon end, then divide that change by the decision price. The denominator must correspond to the start of the period described by the target.

    yt=Pt+h−PtPt=105−102102=1/34

    The future change is 3 €. The exact target is 1/34, or approximately 2.94 %. Its positive sign agrees with a final price higher than the initial price. Keep the fraction for calculations; rounding is only for reading the result.

  5. Check with another expression and by working backwards

    Dividing the final price directly by the initial price gives the multiplicative factor 35/34. Subtracting 1 gives 1/34, the same target as the calculation using the difference. This is an algebraic check of the denominator and sign.

    yt=Pt+hPt−1;Pt+h=Pt(1+yt)

    Working backwards, 102 × (1 + 1/34) = 105 €. This recovers the price at the horizon end. An exact fraction can pass these checks while targeting the wrong date: also check et − t = h against the indices in the sequence.

  6. Decide when the record can be used for training

    The record keeps t = 1, xt = 1/50, yt = 1/34, et = 3 and at = 3. The label remains attached to the input that was meant to predict it. Moving the whole record to the target end session would lose the meaning of this association.

    c denotes the historical training cutoff, on the same session scale. A candidate record is eligible only if its inputs were available at its decision time and at ≤ c. In our construction, the closing prices used by xt are known at t; only the second condition remains to be checked.

    at≤c⇔t+h+d≤c

    At cutoff c = 2, the origin 1 is already in the past, but at = 3 is still in the future: the record is excluded from this training. There is 1 session left before it becomes available. At cutoff 3, after receipt of the closing price, it can be included. A later evaluation can compare a saved prediction with this target; the target must never be used to manufacture the past prediction.

  7. Handle the end of the sequence honestly

    Inputs are defined from session 1 onwards, because a previous closing price is needed. With a sequence ending at T = 4 and h = 2, the last origin whose target end price we have is T − h = 2. Among the rows with origins 1 to 4, the last 2 therefore have no observable target yet.

    For t = 3, we would need the closing price for session 5, which is absent from the sequence. Keep the input if it is useful for prediction, but mark its target as unavailable and exclude it from the supervised training in question. Replacing this absence with 0 would falsely assert an unchanged price.

    We stop at the definition and availability of targets. The relationships between windows of several records and validation protocols will be covered in the following units.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The file is historical, so all its columns were known at the start.”

    Replay the scene at decision time. The price at the horizon end is used for the label filled in later; it cannot enter xₜ. Note the actual availability time of every data item used.

    Review the two moments in the record

  • “The row comes before the cutoff: I can use it for training.”

    The origin time is not the label availability time. In the example, the origin 1 precedes cutoff 2, but the label only arrives at 3. Check aₜ ≤ c, after checking that the inputs were available at t.

    Revisit the eligibility of the record

  • “I put a 0 return in the missing targets.”

    The number 0 denotes an exactly 0 price change, rather than an absent observation. Keep the target unavailable while the horizon has not ended or publication is still missing. The input can remain a request for a prediction without becoming a labeled example.

    Revisit the check at the end of the sequence

The target end session remains 3, but availability becomes 3 + 1 = 4. The condition is at ≤ c. At cutoff 3, it fails; the first eligible cutoff is 4, after receipt of the required price.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What is the label of a decision on this page?

The relative change between its closing price Pₜ and the closing price Pₜ₊ₕ, namely yₜ = (Pₜ₊ₕ − Pₜ)/Pₜ. It is dimensionless, targets a stated horizon h, and remains attached to origin t.

Recall 2Why is the future allowed in the label but not in the decision input?

The label is the outcome that will be filled in afterwards for training or evaluation. The input must reproduce what was known at decision time; introducing that outcome into it gives the model information unavailable in the real scene.

Recall 3Which time determines the eligibility of a record at a historical cutoff?

The label availability aₜ must be no later than cutoff c, with the inputs themselves available at t. For our fixed horizon and delay d, aₜ = t + h + d. All cutoffs are placed after receipt of the indicated data.

Recall 4Which checks distinguish a correct value from correct alignment?

Recovering the final price with Pₜ × (1 + yₜ) checks the calculation. Checking eₜ − t = h against the series and the actual availability of the data checks the choice of times. None of these checks replaces the others.

Recall 5What happens to a record whose closing price at the horizon end is still missing?

Its target remains unavailable; it does not become a 0 change. Its input can be used to request a prediction, but the record does not yet participate in the supervised training in question.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Reconstruct the decision record without support: past input, stated horizon, starting price and price at the horizon end, exact label, availability time, and eligibility condition at the cutoff. Justify the denominator and the reverse check. Finish by explaining why a missing target remains unknown. Reading the page or succeeding with help does not establish any acquired level.
