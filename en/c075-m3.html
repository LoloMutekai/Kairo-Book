Level 3 · C075·M3

Ablation, comparability and leakage

Your goalIsolate a module’s contribution on the same cases, calculate a paired difference and recognize leakage that invalidates its interpretation.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

At a parcel packing station, a tool reads an order and states how many items to prepare. It extracts the quantities, cross-checks them against the packing list, then writes the preparation sheet. You want to know what the cross-checking step contributes. It is called V for verification. Comparing the new version on easy orders with the old version on difficult orders would not answer this question.

An ablation removes a specified element from a system to compare what changes. Here, the complete version and the version without V receive exactly the same orders and packing lists. Without V, the data remain available at the input, but cross-checking is replaced by a direct pass-through. Extraction and writing keep their rules. The diagram shows the only planned intervention.

Comparability means keeping the conditions needed for the question aligned: the same cases, the same permitted information, the same scoring rule and the same execution conditions. A downstream difference caused by removing V is part of its effect; also changing the writing step would introduce another intervention.

Leakage occurs when information reserved for evaluation influences preparation or the answer. If V can read the expected quantity in the answer key, it no longer demonstrates an ability to process an unseen order. If a copy of the same case is used for tuning, changing its name before testing does not restore the separation.

The entire scene, quantities and outputs on this page are fictional. They are used to test reasoning and do not represent an executed tool or a real gain. In this scene, the answer key is established by independently counting the items in the parcel and is hidden from the versions being compared.

The rule to remember

Remove a precisely defined element, compare the same cases under the same protocol, then check where each piece of information comes from before interpreting the difference.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

What does V change on the same parcel orders?

We have 4 fictional cases named Amber, Brick, Cedar and Dune, all retained. The question stated before scoring is: does removing V increase the mean absolute error of the stated number of items? We compare a tool whose rules are already fixed with its ablation, without retraining.

  1. Define the intervention and protect the comparison

    V cross-checks the extracted quantities against the permitted packing list. Removing it passes the extracted quantities to the writing step without adding an intelligent substitute. The version without V is therefore a computable counterfactual of the complete version: what would the same case produce if this verification were omitted? Retraining another version without V would ask a distinct question about a reoptimized architecture.

    We freeze the rules, inputs and evaluator before opening the answer keys. Processing is deterministic in this exercise; each case starts from a clean state, with no history or cache from the previous run. We also retain failures. For a stochastic tool, repetitions and their seeds, meaning the values that initialize randomness, would need to be planned without later choosing the favorable draw.

    The resource policy stays the same: the same computation limits and no reallocation of the time saved by removing V. The actual resource cost can therefore fall without invalidating this question. Reinvesting that time in a different writing step would require an additional comparison declared as such.

  2. Set out the data and define the symbols

    i identifies a case, and n = 4 is the number of cases, with no physical unit. yᵢ is the expected number of items; cᵢ is the quantity stated by the complete version; aᵢ is the quantity stated by the version with ablation. All these quantities are in items. The letters c and a identify the versions without assuming that either is better.

    Amber: y = 10, c = 11, a = 13 items. Brick: y = 20, c = 18, a = 18 items. Cedar: y = 30, c = 31, a = 34 items. Dune: y = 40, c = 42, a = 39 items.

    The answer key y is the same for the paired versions, meaning versions matched case by case. We assume this answer key is exact for the calculation. Its quality and the representativeness of the cases would require separate verification in a real study.

  3. Convert the stated quantities into absolute errors

    e with subscript c or a denotes the error of the corresponding version. The bars |…| take the absolute value: the distance from the target, which is always positive or 0. An underestimate therefore cannot cancel an overestimate. The error is in items.

    ec,i=|ci−yi|;ea,i=|ai−yi|

    Complete: Amber |11 − 10| = 1; Brick |18 − 20| = 2; Cedar |31 − 30| = 1; Dune |42 − 40| = 2 item(s).

    Without V: Amber |13 − 10| = 3; Brick |18 − 20| = 2; Cedar |34 − 30| = 4; Dune |39 − 40| = 1 item(s).

  4. Add, then divide by the same number of cases

    S with subscript c or a denotes the sum of errors; M denotes their mean. The sums cover the cases selected before the calculation, all weighted equally. Dividing by n compares the mean error per case, in items.

    Mc=Scn;Ma=San

    Complete: the sum is (1 + 2) + (1 + 2) = 3 + 3 = 6 items; the mean is 6 ÷ 4 = 3/2 item, or 1.50 item.

    Without V: the sum is (3 + 2) + (4 + 1) = 5 + 5 = 10 items; the mean is 10 ÷ 4 = 5/2 items, or 2.50 items. A mean of whole-number counts can be fractional; it does not describe an item physically cut into parts.

  5. Define the direction and precise scope of the difference

    Δ, the letter delta, is defined as the mean error without V minus that of the complete system. It is a difference in items, not a percentage. A positive Δ favors the complete version; a negative Δ favors the ablation; Δ = 0 indicates equality of this mean on these cases.

    Δ=Ma−Mc=104−64=1 pièce

    Removing V here increases the mean error by 1 item. This describes the intervention in this system and on this fictional dataset. It is neither a universal contribution of V nor proof that V would suffice on its own: it works with extraction and writing. The measure also does not cover text quality or time taken.

  6. Check using paired differences

    dᵢ is the error without V minus the error of the complete version on the same case. A positive sign favors the complete version, as with Δ; individual signs can nevertheless differ from the sign of the mean.

    di=ea,i−ec,i

    We obtain: Amber 3 − 1 = 2; Brick 2 − 2 = 0; Cedar 4 − 1 = 3; Dune 1 − 2 = -1 item(s).

    Sum of differences: (2 + 0) + (3 + (-1)) = 2 + 2 = 4 items. Their mean is 4 ÷ 4 = 1 item. This recovers Δ, because subtracting the sums and then dividing by the same number of cases is equivalent to averaging the differences.

    This check also reveals what the mean hides: Dune favors removing V, while Brick does not distinguish between the versions. Pairing avoids comparing cases of different difficulty; it guarantees neither freedom from leakage nor independence between cases. Copies of the same order do not become new independent cases.

  7. Check the boundary before drawing any conclusion

    Before attributing the difference to the removal, we must be able to trace each case to its origin, keep its variants in the same partition and explain who could see its answer key and when. Tuning selects the rules; the held-out test evaluates them afterward. Inferring a quantity from a packing list that is actually available is legitimate; reading the held-out answer or adapting V after each score is leakage.

    Also check for preprocessing tuned on the entire dataset before splitting and a cache containing test answers. The problem concerns the information used, even without directly reading column y. If the packing station claims to predict future orders, tuning must precede the test period; mixing past and future would change the question.

    Our calculation checks arithmetic on fictional stated outputs. It provides no evidence of a tool’s execution or protection against leakage. For a real study, the protocol and records would need to establish these conditions; the small numerical difference cannot replace them.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the results. Explain which intervention was made and why the same case goes through both versions, then work out the sign of Δ again. Finally, give a path by which the answer key could contaminate the trial without appearing in the displayed input.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I removed V and changed the writing step: I measured V.”

    The contrast then combines several interventions. Restore the same writing step and document the direct pass-through that replaces V. If you want to reoptimize each architecture, state a different question and comparable tuning rules.

    Revisit the definition of the intervention

  • “Δ is positive, so V helps every parcel and remains indispensable.”

    Reread the differences case by case: Dune favors the ablation and Brick gives a tie. The mean is conditional on this system and these cases. A 0 effect can also hide improvements and deteriorations that cancel each other out.

    Test the signs, then the 0 contrast

  • “The file names differ: the test must be clean.”

    Look for the origins of the orders, their copies, the tuning and the caches. A renamed copy and an answer key consulted during tuning open different leakage paths. A list of distinct names does not establish that those paths are closed.

    Review the boundary between tuning and evaluation

Hint 2

Also recalculate d for Cedar: a 0 error without V does not necessarily give a 0 difference.

Compare with the solution

The sum without V becomes 10 − 4 = 6 items. Its mean is 6 ÷ 4 = 3/2 item, or 1.50 item. The complete version’s mean remains 1.50 item; Δ = 3/2 − 3/2 = 0 item.

For Cedar, d becomes 0 − 1 = -1 item. The paired sum is (2 + 0) + (-1) + (-1) = 1 + (-1) = 0 item. Dividing by 4 recovers 0 item.

This 0 mean combines opposing effects: Amber favors the complete version, Cedar and Dune favor the ablation, and Brick gives a tie. The finding concerns the mean of this countercheck. It proves neither general equivalence of the versions nor that V is useless. This output change constructs an arithmetic counterexample; it is not a second trial that was actually executed.

  • Recover Δ = 0 using the means and the paired differences.
  • Explain how effects across cases cancel out without concluding that there is no effect in general.

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

One leak concerns the origin of the cases; the other concerns the use of the score after splitting.

Hint 2

Keeping the same displayed number is not enough: you need a held-out set that played no part in choosing the rules.

Compare with the solution

The copy of Amber crosses the boundary through its shared origin, despite its new name. Tuning V on Cedar’s errors uses the test to choose the version. The paired inputs can remain identical while preparation of the versions has benefited from forbidden information. Pairing therefore rules out neither of these leaks.

Keep a record of the finding, but withdraw the claim that Δ measures performance on cases that remained unseen. Do not simply delete the troublesome case after seeing its effect. Group copies by original case, redo the split, keep the old test in development work and hold out new independent cases. If the goal concerns future orders, also respect their chronology.

Then fix V, its direct pass-through, the other rules, preprocessing and resource limits before the new test. Prevent the versions from reading answer keys or information derived from them while producing answers. Freeze the outputs before scoring, then recalculate the pairs with the same criteria and all planned cases. No new difference can be announced before these outputs exist; the result could be positive, 0 or negative.

Check your reasoning

  • Identify the shared copy and tuning on the test separately.
  • Explain why identical displayed inputs are not enough.
  • Plan a new held-out test, frozen versions and a split by origin.
  • Do not invent the result of the repaired experiment.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What question does our ablation of V ask, and why do we not retrain the tool?

It measures what removing cross-checking changes in the fixed system. Retraining without V would study a reoptimized architecture; the interventions and the question would be different.

Recall 2What stays common to the versions, and what can change naturally?

Cases, permitted information, rules outside V, evaluator, initial state and resource policy stay common. Downstream outputs and actual resource cost can change because of the removal. Reinvesting the computation saved changes the comparison.

Recall 3How do you calculate and check Δ, and how do you interpret its sign?

Δ is the mean absolute error without V minus that of the complete version, in items. The mean of the paired differences must recover it if the cases and weights are the same. Positive favors the complete version, negative favors the ablation, and 0 indicates equality of this mean.

Recall 4Why is hiding the answer-key column not enough to protect a test?

A copy of a tuning case, preprocessing fitted on the test, an answer cache or adjustments guided by its scores can transmit information. Origins and uses must be checked before a test can be considered held out.

Recall 5What does neither a positive Δ nor a 0 Δ prove?

The first proves neither an advantage on every case nor a real gain that generalizes. The second proves neither equality case by case nor that the module is generally useless. The contrast remains tied to the measure, system and protocol.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without rereading, reconstruct the path: stated question → precise removal → same cases and same conditions → absolute errors → means and paired differences → information audit → limited conclusion. Add a case where a 0 mean hides opposing effects, then a leakage path that pairing does not close.
