Level 0 · C005·M3

Similarity

Your goalEnlarge or reduce a photo without stretching it: match corresponding sides, find a common factor, apply it to every length, and check that the angles in the design are preserved.

Before you start
c004-m5 : rational equations · c005-m1 : angles · c005-m2 : triangles

✿01 See the idea

You have a hypothetical printed photo, 6 cm wide and 4 cm high. It has a small triangle drawn in one corner. You want a larger version of the same photo to hang up. If the machine enlarges only the width, the triangle flattens and faces look wider: the photo has been stretched rather than enlarged uniformly.

Enlarging without distorting means multiplying every length in the photo by the same number, called the scale factor. Compare the width of the copy with the width of the original, and its height with the height of the original: these matching pairs of sides are called corresponding sides. The two lengths in a pair must be expressed in the same unit.

A copy made this way is said to be similar to the original. Its lengths have changed, but its corresponding angles have stayed the same: the angle in the triangle design has exactly the same opening. The preservation of angles is accepted in this unit; proving it requires tools that will be introduced later.

Watch out for a visual trap: the four corners of a rectangular frame are right angles, whether the photo is stretched or not. To detect distortion, examine the angles in the design or compare the factors; never rely on the frame corners alone.

All measurements on this page are hypothetical values chosen for the example, rather than recorded print sizes.

The rule to remember

For a similar copy, every corresponding length is multiplied by the same positive factor k; if the width and height factors differ, the copy is stretched.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Ordering an enlargement of the photo

The hypothetical photo measures 6 cm wide and 4 cm high. You want a copy 12 cm wide. What height should you order to avoid stretching the photo? And what would happen to the triangle design if the height stayed at 4 cm?

  1. Name the quantities and assumptions

    L and H denote the width and height of the original, and L′ and H′ those of the copy; the prime mark means “of the copy.” All four are lengths in centimeters. The factor k is a strictly positive number with no unit: it is the number by which you multiply a length of the original to obtain the corresponding length of the copy.

    Assumptions: the entire image is reproduced, with no cropping or added border; both photos are measured in the same unit; the hypothetical measurements are taken as exact. Here, L = 6 cm, H = 4 cm, and L′ = 12 cm.

  2. Find the factor from the known pair

    The only pair of corresponding sides for which both lengths are known is the width. The factor answers the question “how many times does the width of the original fit into the width of the copy?”: it is a quotient.

    k=L′L=12 cm6 cm=2

    The centimeters cancel: k has no unit. Divide a width by a width, never by a height.

  3. Apply the same factor to the height

    Preserving the shape requires the height to be enlarged by the same factor as the width. So multiply the height of the original by the factor you found.

    H′=k×H=2×4 cm=8 cm

    Result: order a copy measuring 12 cm by 8 cm.

  4. Check in two ways

    First method, ratios of corresponding sides: L′/L = 12/6 = 2 and H′/H = 8/4 = 2. The two factors are equal, so the copy is similar.

    L′L=H′H=k

    Second method, independent of the factor: the width-to-height ratio of each photo. Original: 6/4 = 1.5. Copy: 12/8 = 1.5. This ratio has no unit and stays the same, because multiplying the numerator and denominator by the same k does not change a quotient.

  5. Compare with the stretched copy

    If the machine keeps the height unchanged, the copy measures 12 cm by 4 cm. The factors are then 12/6 = 2 for the width and 4/4 = 1 for the height: they differ. Checking the ratio within the photo confirms this: 12/4 = 3, instead of 1.5.

    The width is still correct. One correct factor is not enough: every pair of corresponding sides needs the same factor.

  6. Examine the angles in the design

    The corners of both frames measure 90°: they do not distinguish the copies. Look at the triangle design, which has a right angle at A, with the two sides meeting at that right angle each measuring 2 cm in the original. In the similar copy, these sides become 4 cm and 4 cm; in the stretched copy, they measure 4 cm horizontally but only 2 cm vertically.

    Overlay the two triangles as in the second diagram: the same vertex A and the same side AB. Point C′ of the stretched copy falls between A and C, so the ray from B toward C′ passes inside the angle at B of the similar triangle. Angle β′ is strictly smaller than β.

    Check using the sum of the angles: in each triangle, the angle at A is 90°, so the other two total 180° − 90° = 90°. Since β′ has decreased, the angle at C′ has increased by the same amount. The design has changed shape: the stretched copy is not similar, as the factors already indicated.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “Adding 2 cm to each side enlarges without distorting.”

    Adding the same length does not apply the same factor. The copy would measure 8 cm by 6 cm: factors 8/6 = 4/3 for the width and 6/4 = 3/2 for the height. They differ, so the photo would be slightly stretched vertically. A similarity transformation multiplies every length; it does not add a fixed border.

    Review how to find and then apply the factor

  • “The width is correctly multiplied by 2 and the corners are right angles, so the copy is faithful to the original.”

    The width factor tells you nothing about the height, and every rectangle has 90° corners, even when stretched. Calculate H′/H as well and compare it with L′/L; to detect distortion, examine the angles in the design inside.

    Review the three frames and their triangles

  • “To check the copy measuring 12 by 8, I compare 12/4 and 8/6.”

    These quotients cross the sides: they equal 3 and 4/3 and would wrongly reject a similar copy. Name each side before dividing, then match width with width and height with height: 12/6 = 2 and 8/4 = 2.

Exercise 1 · Direct application

Enlarging by a factor of one and a half

You order a similar copy of the 6 cm by 4 cm photo with factor k = 1.5. Give the width and height of the copy, then check its shape using a second method.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Multiply every length of the original by the same factor.

Hint 2

Multiplying by 1.5 means taking the length and adding half of it.

Compare with the solution

Width: L′ = 1.5 × 6 cm = 9 cm. Height: H′ = 1.5 × 4 cm = 6 cm. The same factor is applied to both pairs of corresponding sides.

Check: 9/6 = 1.5, just as 6/4 = 1.5 for the original. The width-to-height ratio is preserved: the copy is not stretched.

Check your reasoning

  • Both lengths are calculated with the same factor and include the unit cm.
  • An independent check is written out, such as the width-to-height ratio.
  • An answer found after a hint is recorded as an assisted answer and will be attempted again without support.

Exercise 2 · Find the factor

A frame sets the height

A frame requires a similar copy 10 cm high. What factor should be applied to the 6 cm by 4 cm photo, and what will the width of the copy be?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Calculate the factor using the pair of corresponding sides for which both lengths are known: the height.

Hint 2

Divide 10 by 4, then apply that number to the width.

Compare with the solution

Factor: k = H′/H = 10/4 = 2.5, with no unit. Divide a height by a height, because this is the only pair for which both lengths are known.

Width: L′ = 2.5 × 6 cm = 15 cm. Check: 15/10 = 1.5, equal to the ratio 1.5 of the original.

Calculating 10 − 4 and then adding that difference to the width would be the fixed-addition mistake: the copy would be stretched.

Check your reasoning

  • The factor comes from the quotient of two heights and has no unit.
  • The width is obtained with that same factor, in cm.
  • Consistency is checked using the width-to-height ratio.

Exercise 3 · Decide and correct

An advertisement with mixed units

A shop advertises a similar copy of the photo: “120 mm wide, 6 cm high.” Is the claim correct? If not, propose two corrected similar copies: one keeps the advertised width, and the other keeps the advertised height.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Before calculating any quotient, express both lengths in the same unit: 10 mm make one centimeter.

Hint 2

Compare width with width and height with height.

Hint 3

To correct the dimensions, take the factor for the side you are keeping and apply it to the other side of the original.

Compare with the solution

Conversion: 120 mm = 120/10 cm = 12 cm. Without this conversion, you would obtain 120/6 = 20, an unreasonable factor that mixes millimeters and centimeters.

Factors: width 12/6 = 2; height 6/4 = 1.5. They differ: the advertisement describes a copy stretched horizontally rather than a similar copy.

Keep the width of 12 cm: factor 2, height 2 × 4 cm = 8 cm. Keep the height of 6 cm: factor 1.5, width 1.5 × 6 cm = 9 cm. Each correction can be checked using the width-to-height ratio, which must equal 1.5.

Check your reasoning

  • Conversion to centimeters comes before any comparison.
  • The verdict is based on factors from both pairs of corresponding sides, rather than a single side or the frame corners.
  • Both corrections are proposed, each with its factor and unit.
  • The work is not counted as mastered until it has been done again without a hint on another day.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What are corresponding sides between a photo and its copy?

The sides that match: width with width, height with height. Compare them in the same unit.

Recall 2Under what condition is a copy similar to the original?

All its corresponding lengths are multiplied by the same positive factor k; its corresponding angles are then preserved.

Recall 3Is a 3 cm by 2 cm copy of the 6 cm by 4 cm photo similar to it?

Yes: 3/6 and 2/4 both equal 0.5. A positive factor smaller than one reduces without distorting.

Recall 4Why are the frame corners not enough to detect stretching?

Every rectangle has four right angles, stretched or not. Compare the factors for corresponding sides or examine the angles of a design inside.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Match corresponding sides and express them in the same unit. Find the factor k by dividing the lengths in a known pair, then apply the same k to every length. Check that the ratios of corresponding sides are equal, or check the width-to-height ratio. Different factors stretch the photo and change the angles in the design, even when the frame corners remain right angles.
