Level 2 · C138·M5

Stacking

Your goalBuild a meta-model that weights two base models using their out-of-fold predictions, calculate its weight on a small fictional dataset, then explain why information leakage distorts this weight.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

A fictional bakery wants to predict the kilograms of bread sold on a given day. Two base models each give their own answer: a deep tree A, which closely fits the details of days already seen, and a simple trend B, which is less flexible. All values in this scene are invented for the calculation; they do not describe any real bakery.

Stacking adds a second layer. A meta-model learns to combine the answers from A and B. It does not look at the raw data: its inputs are the predictions from the base models. The main pitfall is where these predictions come from. If A saw a day’s sales during training, it can copy them almost exactly. The meta-model then concludes that A never makes a mistake, which stops being true as soon as a new day arrives.

The remedy is out-of-fold prediction. We split the 4 observations into 2 folds of 2 days. For each fold, A and B train on the other fold, then predict the fold set aside. Each day thus receives a prediction from A and one from B, both made without having seen that day. These two columns, together with the actual sales, form the training set for the meta-model.

To predict a truly new day, we retrain A and B on the whole dataset, then the meta-model applies its weight to their two answers. With so few days, this weight remains unstable. The units on learning curves and probability calibration explore two limitations of this setup further: whether more data would change the answer, and whether probabilities given to the meta-model are reliable.

The rule to remember

The meta-model learns from predictions that the base models made without seeing the answer; its weight is meaningful only under this condition.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

A weight learned from four fictional days

Four fictional days have actual sales y, an out-of-fold prediction a from tree A and an out-of-fold prediction b from trend B, all in kilograms. Day one: y = 41, a = 43, b = 40. Day two: y = 46, a = 49, b = 44. Day three: y = 51, a = 52, b = 48. Day four: y = 56, a = 57, b = 52. A fictional check point, set aside, gives y = 57, a = 60 and b = 50. We want to find A’s weight w, then test it on this point.

  1. Define the symbols and assumptions

    The meta-model predicts sales ŷ in kilograms. It uses a weight w for A and the weight one − w for B. The weight w has no unit; it is learned, whereas a and b are inputs. The index i numbers the days, from 1 to n, with n = 4.

    y^=wa+(1−w)b

    Explicit assumptions. One: a and b are out of fold. Two: the weights are w and one − w, with no constant term; their sum is therefore 1. Three: new days resemble the training days. Four: the check point’s a and b values come from models retrained on the 4 days. Without these assumptions, the weight calculated below loses its meaning.

  2. Rewrite using a disagreement and a gap

    Let u = a − b be the disagreement between A and B, and d = y − b be the gap between B and actual sales, both in kilograms. Since ŷ − b = w × u, the error for the day, y − ŷ, can be written as d − w × u. The weight w therefore measures the share of the disagreement u that we follow to close the gap d.

    y−y^=d−wu

    The four disagreements are 3, 5, 4 and 5 kg. The four gaps for B are 1, 2, 3 and 4 kg. On each day, actual sales lie between b and a, so d lies between 0 and u.

  3. Choose w using the sum of squares

    We choose the weight that minimizes the sum of squared errors. Squaring prevents errors with opposite signs from cancelling and leads to a closed-form formula. Let S(w) denote this sum over the n days.

    S(w)=∑i=1n(di−wui)2=Suuw2−2Sudw+Sdd

    Here Suu is the sum of u², Sud the sum of the products u × d and Sdd the sum of d², all over the n days. Expanding each square makes S(w) a parabola in w. For a quadratic α w² + β w + γ with positive α, the minimum occurs at w = −β divided by 2 α. Here α = Suu, which is positive as soon as at least one disagreement is nonzero, and β = −2 Sud.

    w=SudSuu

    We do not constrain w to lie between 0 and one: that methodological choice is not made here. The result simply happens to fall within this interval.

  4. Calculate the products, sums and weight

    The products u × d are 3, 10, 12 and 20 kg². The squares u² are 9, 25, 16 and 25 kg². We add in order: Sud passes through 13 then 25 before reaching 45 kg², and Suu passes through 34 then 50 before reaching 75 kg².

    w=4575=0.6

    A’s weight is 3/5, or 0.6. B’s weight is one − 3/5 = 2/5, or 0.4. The squared kilograms cancel in the quotient: w has no unit.

  5. Check the vertex of the parabola

    At the vertex, the slope of S is 0, which is equivalent to Sud = w × Suu. Let us calculate the right-hand side: 3/5 × 75 = 45 kg². The difference from Sud is 45 − 45 = 0. The remaining error therefore has no component that follows u: changing w slightly would not reduce it.

    This check verifies the solution, not the choice of criterion. It does not establish that the sum of squares is the right measure for the bakery.

  6. See leakage through an extreme case

    Suppose A was trained on the 4 days and memorizes them: its prediction on its own data equals y. Keep b unchanged. The disagreement u = a − b becomes y − b, that is, d, for every day. Then Sud and Suu both equal the sum of d².

    The weight becomes this sum divided by itself, which is 1. The meta-model now follows only A and ignores B. On the new day, this weight caused by leakage is a bet on A’s memory, not a measure of its quality.

  7. Apply the weight to the check point

    The disagreement at this point is 60 − 50 = 10 kg. The weighted term is 3/5 × 10 = 6 kg. The stacked prediction is 50 + 6 = 56 kg. The fictional actual sales are 57 kg.

    The differences from actual sales are 7 kg for B, 3 kg for A alone, which corresponds to the weight 1 caused by leakage, and 1 kg for the out-of-fold combination. On this particular point, the weight learned out of fold does better than either model alone.

  8. What the calculation does not establish

    We have 4 fictional days and one fictional point. The weight 3/5 is unstable: changing a single day would move it. The best weight on the training set cannot do worse than either model alone, because the weights 0 and 1 are among the possible choices; this says nothing about a new day. No real performance, success rate or improvement is therefore claimed.

Explain it in your own words

Explain in your own words why predictions made on A’s training data would increase its weight, then what the weight 3/5 says about the disagreement between A and B.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The meta-model can learn from the predictions that the base models make on their own training data.”

    A flexible model copies the answer there. If A memorizes, its disagreement with B becomes the gap between B and actual sales, and its weight is 1. Use out-of-fold predictions, made without having seen the day concerned.

    Review the step on leakage

  • “Stacking guarantees a result better than the best base model.”

    On the training days, the best weight cannot do worse than either model alone, since 0 and 1 are still allowed. This guarantees nothing for a new day, and the fictional check point is only one example.

    Reread the limits of the calculation

  • “Two very similar models make the weight more reliable.”

    If their predictions are identical, the disagreement is 0 everywhere and the weight’s quotient is undefined. If they are almost identical, a little noise moves the weight a great deal. Look for models whose errors differ.

    Do the exercise on identical models

Exercise 1 · Explained application

Reconstruct day three

Using the weight w = 3/5 from the example, calculate the stacked prediction for day 3, then its difference from actual sales, in kilograms. The data remain fictional.

Hint 1

Write b + w × u, where u is the disagreement a − b for day three.

Compare with the solution

The disagreement for day 3 is 52 − 48 = 4 kg. The weighted term is 3/5 × 4 = 12/5 kg, or 2.4 kg. The prediction is 48 + 12/5 = 252/5 kg, or 50.4 kg.

The gap from actual sales is 51 − 252/5 = 3/5 kg, or 0.6 kg. Another approach: w × a + (one − w) × b gives the same prediction. This gap is a training residual, not an error on a new day.

Check your reasoning

  • Start from b and add w × u, or combine w × a and (one − w) × b.
  • Give the units in kilograms and a weight with no unit.
  • Distinguish the training residual from an error on a new day.

Exercise 2 · Explained application

Count the training runs in the procedure

We keep 4 observations, 2 folds and 2 base models, then retrain each base model on the whole dataset for the check point. How many observations are there per fold, how many base-model training runs take place during the out-of-fold phase, and how many are there in total?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Each fold is predicted by each base model trained on the other folds: count one training run per fold and per model.

Compare with the solution

Each fold contains 4 ÷ 2 = 2 observations. The out-of-fold phase requires one training run per fold and per model: 2 × 2 = 4.

The 2 retraining runs on the whole dataset bring the total to 4 + 2 = 6. Check: 2 models each have 2 out-of-fold training runs followed by one final training run, making 6 in total.

Check your reasoning

  • Separate the out-of-fold phase from final retraining.
  • Obtain 2 observations per fold, 4 out-of-fold training runs and 6 in total.
  • Do not count the meta-model’s training among the base-model training runs.

Exercise 3 · Guided transfer

Two identical models

Replace B with an exact copy of A: for each day, b = a. What are the disagreements, the sum of squared disagreements and the weight? What can you conclude about the meta-model?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Calculate u = a − b for one day, then the sum of u².

Hint 2

Look at the denominator in the formula for the weight.

Compare with the solution

Each disagreement is a − a = 0 kg, so each square and their sum Suu equal 0. The weight is a quotient with a divisor of 0: it is undefined.

This agrees with the prediction w × a + (one − w) × a = a, which does not depend on w. The meta-model has nothing to choose: an additional model contributes something only if its predictions differ from those of the first.

Check your reasoning

  • Find a 0 disagreement and a 0 sum of squares.
  • Explain why the weight’s quotient is undefined.
  • Connect the conclusion to the prediction’s independence from w.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does “out of fold” mean for an input to the meta-model?

The prediction was produced by a base model that had not seen the corresponding observation during training.

Recall 2What formula gives the weight w when the prediction is w × a + (one − w) × b?

w = Sud / Suu, with u = a − b, d = y − b and sums over the days: this is the vertex of the parabola of squared errors.

Recall 3Why is the weight 1 if A memorizes its training data?

Its prediction y equals the actual value, so u = d everywhere, and the sum of d² divided by itself equals 1: the meta-model ignores B.

Recall 4What does the fictional check point prove?

It illustrates the mechanism with an example. With four days and one point, it measures no performance and guarantees nothing for other days.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Split the observations into folds; train the base models on the other folds; predict the fold set aside; write the disagreement u = a − b and the gap d = y − b; minimize the sum of squares to obtain w = Sud / Suu; check that the slope is 0; retrain the base models on the whole dataset to predict new observations; keep the assumptions and sample size in mind.
