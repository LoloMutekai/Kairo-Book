Level 0 · C006·M2

Straight lines

Your goalConnect the rule V(t) = rt + b, its table and its straight line, distinguishing the initial value b from the slope r and stating their units.

Before you start
c004-m5 : rational equations · c006-m1 : coordinates

✿01 See the idea

You fill a graduated water bottle under a tap set to run at a constant rate. Before you turn it on, the bottle already contains 2 dL of water. A decilitre (dL) is one tenth of a litre: one litre contains 10 dL. The fictional bottle in this scene holds at most 10 dL, or 1 L.

Each minute, the tap adds 2 dL. As in C006·M1, each reading in the notebook forms an ordered pair (t ; V): a time in minutes, followed by a volume in decilitres. When plotted on the coordinate axes, these points lie on a straight line. Each step of one minute to the right comes with the same rise of 2 dL: equal durations, equal additions. This regularity produces a straight line.

Here, a single rule generates all the readings. A function is a rule that assigns a single output to each allowed input. The input is the time t since the tap was turned on, in minutes; the output is the volume V(t) in the bottle, in dL. Read V(t) as “V of t”: the volume at time t. It is not V multiplied by t.

Two numbers are enough to describe this bottle. The initial volume is the volume already present when t equals 0: 2 dL. The flow rate is the volume added per minute: 2 dL/min. Both have the same numerical value, but different units; one is a quantity, the other a change in quantity per unit of time. The graph retains both: the initial volume sets the height on the vertical axis, and the flow rate sets the slope.

The values in the scene are fictional and chosen so that you can work through everything with a pencil. They do not describe a real tap or an actual measurement.

The rule to remember

Volume = flow rate × time + initial volume. Read the initial volume at t = 0; calculate the flow rate by dividing a change in volume by the corresponding duration. The table, formula and straight line must give the same ordered pairs.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

The bottle with 2 dL under a tap running at 2 dL/min

Fictional data: initial volume 2 dL, constant flow rate 2 dL/min, trial from 0 to 4 min, bottle capacity 10 dL. Let us write the rule, build the table, recover the flow rate and initial volume from two readings, compare with a rule that gives the same volume at 1 min, then check the limit of the trial.

  1. Name each symbol and state the assumptions

    t is the time since the tap was turned on, in minutes (min). V(t) is the volume in the bottle at that time, in decilitres (dL). b is the initial volume, meaning V(0), in dL. r is the flow rate, in dL/min.

    Assumptions: the flow rate remains constant throughout the trial, nothing leaks, nobody drinks, and t stays between 0 and 4 min. The volume poured after t minutes is then r × t; add the initial volume to it.

    V(t)=r×t+b

    A rule of this form is called an affine rule, and its graph is a straight line. The coefficient r, which multiplies the time, is called the slope of the line. Check the units before calculating: r × t gives dL/min multiplied by min, hence dL, which can be added to the dL of b.

    dL/min×min=dL

    For our bottle, the rule becomes:

    V(t)=2×t+2

  2. Fill in the table row by row

    For each time, first calculate what the tap has poured, then add the initial volume. This order follows the physical meaning: add water for t minutes, then account for the water already present.

    At 0 min: 2 × 0 + 2 = 0 + 2 = 2 dL. At 1 min: 2 + 2 = 4 dL. At 2 min: 4 + 2 = 6 dL. Let us work through the row for 3 min:

    V(3)=2×3+2=6+2=8 dL

    Check another way: starting with 2 dL and adding 2 dL each minute gives 4, 6 and then 8 dL without any multiplication. Each ordered pair (t ; V) in the table becomes a point in the first diagram.

  3. Recover the slope from two readings

    Suppose we have forgotten the flow rate and only two rows remain in the notebook: (1 ; 4) and (3 ; 8). The Greek letter Δ, “delta”, is read as “change in”: Δt is the later time minus the earlier time, and ΔV is the later volume minus the earlier volume. Take both differences in the same order.

    r=ΔVΔt=V(tB)−V(tA)tB−tA

    Here, t subscript A and t subscript B denote the times of the two readings; they must be different, otherwise we would divide by a duration of 0. Why does this quotient give r again? Because the initial volume cancels out in the difference of the volumes:

    V(tB)−V(tA)=(rtB+b)−(rtA+b)=r×(tB−tA)

    With our readings: ΔV = 8 − 4 = 4 dL and Δt = 3 − 1 = 2 min.

    r=8−43−1=42=2 dL/min

    Check: we recover the stated flow rate. A single step gives the same result: from 1 to 2 min, ΔV = 6 − 4 = 2 dL for Δt = 1 min. The result is in dL/min: a slope is not a volume.

  4. Recover the initial volume

    Once the slope is known, a single reading gives the initial volume. At the reading for 1 min, the rule gives 4 = 2 × 1 + b. Subtract the volume poured, 2 dL, from each side, as when solving an equation:

    b=V(1)−r×1=4−2=2 dL

    Check: this is indeed V(0) = 2 dL from the table, meaning the height where the line crosses the vertical axis. A straight line therefore does not necessarily start at the origin of the coordinate system.

  5. Compare with a rule that agrees once

    Someone claims that the bottle instead follows W(t) = 4 × t: an empty bottle under a tap running at 4 dL/min. At 1 min, W gives 4 dL, exactly like V. Before drawing a conclusion, let us compare several times.

    W gives 0, 4 and 8 dL at 0, 1 and 2 min; V gives 2, 4 and 6 dL. The differences, calculated point by point, are 2, 0 and 2 dL. The values agree at only one of the three times compared.

    We can calculate the time of intersection: find t such that V(t) = W(t). Subtract 2t from each side, then divide by the difference of the slopes, which is nonzero here:

    2t+2=4t⟺2=2t⟺t=22=1 min

    Check: the second diagram shows the two lines intersecting at (1 ; 4), then moving apart. They have different initial values and different slopes: one shared point is not enough to identify a straight line.

  6. Check the limit of the trial

    Final check: the rule must not predict more than the bottle can hold. At the latest time in the trial:

    V(4)=2×4+2=8+2=10 dL

    The bottle is full: 10 dL for a capacity of 10 dL. Beyond 4 min, the formula would keep increasing while the water would overflow; it would stop describing the scene. C006·M3 will specify which inputs make sense for a rule. The table, formula and straight line in the first diagram give the same ordered pairs, from (0 ; 2) to (4 ; 10).

Explain it in your own words

Why are the initial 2 dL and the flow rate of 2 dL/min not “the same number”? Explain once using the table and once using a step on the graph, without looking at the page.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “Every straight line passes through the origin.”

    Replace t with 0 in the rule: b remains. For the bottle, V(0) = 2 dL, already present before the tap is turned on. Only a rule with an initial value of 0, such as W(t) = 4 × t, passes through the origin of the coordinate system.

    Review the recovered initial volume and the comparison with W

  • “The slope is a volume.”

    The slope compares two changes: 4 dL added over 2 min gives 2 dL/min. Without the corresponding duration, a volume tells us nothing about the flow rate. Check that your answer has a unit meaning “per minute”.

    Review the initial volume and flow rate in the scene

  • “Steeper on screen always means faster.”

    The drawn steepness depends on the scale chosen for each axis. Read a step in the units of the axes, calculate ΔV/Δt, then compare dL/min rather than grid squares.

    Do the exercise with two scales

Exercise 1 · Calculate and read

The tap slows down

The same bottle, the same initial volume of 2 dL, but the tap now pours only 1 dL/min. Write the rule, calculate the volume at 4 min, then describe the line if the flow rate drops to 0 dL/min.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The initial volume stays the same: only the coefficient of time changes.

Hint 2

With 0 flow, calculate 0 × t before adding the initial volume.

Compare with the solution

Rule: V(t) = 1 × t + 2. In 4 min, the tap pours 1 × 4 = 4 dL; the bottle contains 4 + 2 = 6 dL.

Check: from 2 dL to 6 dL in 4 min, the rise is 4 dL, or 1 dL per minute, the stated flow rate.

With 0 flow: V(t) = 0 × t + 2. At 4 min, 0 + 2 = 2 dL, and likewise at every time in the trial. The line is horizontal at a height of 2 dL: 0 slope means neither an empty bottle nor an absence of data.

Check your reasoning

  • The initial volume of 2 dL remains in the rule.
  • The volume is given in dL and the flow rate in dL/min.
  • The 0-flow case gives a horizontal line, not 0 volume.

Exercise 2 · Recover a rule

Another bottle, two readings

Another fictional bottle, filled at a constant flow rate, contains 4 dL at 1 min and 10 dL at 3 min. Find its flow rate and initial volume, write its rule, then check it against the reading you did not use to find the initial volume.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

First calculate the change in volume and the duration between the two readings, taking both differences in the same order.

Hint 2

For the initial volume, subtract from the first volume what the tap poured in 1 min.

Compare with the solution

ΔV = 10 − 4 = 6 dL and Δt = 3 − 1 = 2 min, so r = 6 / 2 = 3 dL/min.

In 1 min, the tap pours 3 × 1 = 3 dL. The initial volume is b = 4 − 3 = 1 dL. The rule is V(t) = 3 × t + 1.

Check with the second reading: 3 × 3 + 1 = 9 + 1 = 10 dL, the given value. Notice: this bottle also contains 4 dL at 1 min, like the one in the example, without following the same straight line.

Check your reasoning

  • Both differences are taken in the same order.
  • The flow rate is in dL/min and the initial volume in dL.
  • The rule is checked against a reading not used to find b.

Exercise 3 · Interpret a graph

Two drawings, two scales

Two students each draw the straight line for a bottle on squared paper. Graph A: one horizontal square represents 1 min, one vertical square represents 1 dL, and the line rises 2 squares for each square it moves to the right. Graph B: one horizontal square represents 2 min, one vertical square represents 4 dL, and the line rises 1 square for each square it moves to the right. Which looks steeper? Which corresponds to the greater flow rate? Explain in your own words.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Convert a step in grid squares into minutes and decilitres before comparing.

Hint 2

On B, moving one square to the right takes 2 min.

Compare with the solution

On A: in 1 min, the line rises 2 × 1 = 2 dL; flow rate 2 / 1 = 2 dL/min.

On B: in 2 min, it rises 1 × 4 = 4 dL; flow rate 4 / 2 = 2 dL/min.

A looks steeper, with a rise of 2 squares compared with 1. Yet both flow rates are 2 dL/min, the same as our bottle. The drawn steepness depends on the scales; ΔV/Δt, calculated in the units of the axes, does not.

Check your reasoning

  • Each rise is converted into dL and each horizontal step into min.
  • The conclusion compares dL/min, not grid squares.
  • The explanation names the role of the axis scales; it is assessed against these criteria, without an automatic verdict.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1In V(t) = rt + b, what does b represent for the bottle, and how do you read it?

The volume present at time 0, in dL: here 2 dL. Read it by replacing t with 0, or where the line crosses the vertical axis.

Recall 2What does r measure, and in which unit?

The change in volume per minute, in dL/min: here 2 dL/min. It is the coefficient that multiplies time, called the slope.

Recall 3How do you calculate the slope from two readings?

Divide the change in volume by the corresponding duration, ΔV/Δt, using two different times: (8 − 4) / (3 − 1) = 2 dL/min.

Recall 4V(t) = 2t + 2 and W(t) = 4t give 4 dL at 1 min. Are they the same straight line?

No. At 0 min, they give 2 and 0 dL: their initial values and slopes differ, and they intersect only at that shared point.

Recall 5What must the table, formula and straight line have in common?

The same ordered pairs (t ; V): each row in the table is calculated using the formula and plotted on the line.

Reconstruct the reasoning

A function assigns a single volume to each allowed time. The affine rule V(t) = rt + b combines an initial volume b, read at t = 0 and expressed in dL, with a slope r, the change in volume per minute in dL/min, calculated as ΔV/Δt from two readings at different times. The table, formula and straight line give the same ordered pairs; agreement at a single point is not enough to identify a straight line, and the drawn steepness depends on the scales.
