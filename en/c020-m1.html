Level 1 · C020·M1

Score, unit and normalization

Your goalConvert a walking time into a score using declared reference points, then check that changing the unit does not change the comparison.

Before you start
c004-m5 : rational equations · c013-m5 : recursion · c012-m5 : counterexamples · c009-m5 : reading notes

✿01 See the idea

A group is preparing a workshop and choosing between Elms Hall and Linden Hall. From the same starting point, the walk takes 14 min to Elms and 22 min to Linden. Here, the group prefers a shorter walk. All times in this scene are fictional; no actual attendance or satisfaction is measured.

Walking time is the quantity being compared. The minute, abbreviated min, is its unit: it gives meaning to the number shown. A score is a number assigned according to a declared rule to make comparison easier. It can be dimensionless, but its meaning always remains tied to that rule.

The group sets its scoring scale before making the comparison: 10 min will be the favorable reference point, scored 1, and 30 min the unfavorable reference point, scored 0. Between these reference points, each additional minute will remove the same amount from the score. This choice of reference points is a convention adopted by the group, rather than a universal property of a good hall.

Normalization here means placing the times on this common scale. The time diagram and the score diagram describe the same walks. The direction changes: a shorter time becomes a higher score. The other qualities of the halls are not assessed in this unit.

The rule to remember

Before comparing scores, state what is measured, the unit, the reference points shared by everyone, and which direction is favorable. Normalization changes the scale; it does not establish the quality of the choice.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Build and check the walking-time scoring scale

Elms takes 14 min, Linden 22 min. The shared scoring scale gives 1 to the favorable reference point of 10 min and 0 to the unfavorable reference point of 30 min. Let us calculate the scores while keeping the exact fractions.

  1. Define the symbols and the domain of the scoring scale

    x denotes the time taken for the walk being assessed; a denotes the favorable reference point and b the unfavorable reference point. These quantities are all in minutes. s(x) is read as “the score for the walk taking time x”; s is the name of the rule here. The score is dimensionless. The symbol ≤ means “less than or equal to”.

    a≤x≤b,a<b

    We assume known times, measured in the same way for each walk, and fixed reference points shared by the locations. a = 10 min and b = 30 min are distinct: we can divide by their difference. If the reference points coincided, the denominator would be 0 and this normalization would be undefined. The results apply under this scoring scale, without accounting for uncertainty in the times.

  2. Build the formula from the reference points

    b − a is the span of the scoring scale in minutes: 30 − 10 = 20 min. b − x measures how much shorter the walk is than the unfavorable reference point. Dividing this difference by the whole span expresses the fraction of the way toward the favorable reference point.

    s(x)=b−xb−a

    The numerator and denominator are times in the same unit: their ratio is dimensionless. At reference point a, the ratio is 20/20 = 1; at reference point b, it is 0/20 = 0. With a ≤ x ≤ b, the numerator stays between 0 min and 20 min: the score therefore stays between 0 and 1. The order b − x does give a higher score to a shorter walk.

  3. Calculate the score for each walk

    For Elms, the walk is shorter than b by 30 − 14 = 16 min. The score compares these 16 min with the span of 20 min.

    s(14)=30−1430−10=1620=0.80

    The exact fraction simplifies to 4/5; its decimal form is 0.80, dimensionless. For Linden, the same denominator must be used: 30 − 22 = 8 min, then 8/20 = 2/5, or 0.40, dimensionless. Elms is preferable when walking time is the only criterion. Nothing has yet been combined with another criterion or a vote.

  4. Check by recovering the time

    Let us multiply the score by the span: this should recover the difference from reference point b. Then subtract this difference from b to recover the original time. This reverse calculation checks both the arithmetic and the direction of the scale.

    x=b−s(x)×(b−a)

    For Elms: (4/5) × 20 min = 16 min, then 30 min − 16 min = 14 min. This is indeed the stated time. Checking the reference points has already given 1 and 0; the ranking also follows the direction “shorter walk, higher score”.

  5. Interpret the result without adding a promise

    0.80 is neither a time nor the probability that the workshop will succeed. It is the position assigned to 14 min by this walking-time scoring scale. A dimensionless score does not thereby become comparable to all other scores: their meaning and reference points still need to be checked.

    The reference values 10 min and 30 min are not the minimum and maximum times observed among the halls: they are chosen in advance. Changing these reference values can change the scores without changing the walks. Keeping the reference values and converting all times to another unit, however, preserves the scores; the conversion exercise will let you check this.

Explain it in your own words

Without looking at the formula, explain why we calculate the difference from the unfavorable reference point and why we divide it by the whole span. Then recover the walking time to Elms from its score. What would be lost if we kept only the score, without the scoring scale?

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “A larger number is always better.”

    In this scene, a longer time is less desirable. The score has been oriented in the other direction: check that it equals 1 at 10 min and 0 at 30 min. Writing down which direction is favorable avoids choosing a longer walk by mistake.

    Revisit the construction and the checks at the reference points

  • “Simply divide the time by the larger reference point.”

    The scoring scale does not start at 0 time: it connects 10 min to 30 min and favors shorter times. The quotient x/b does not respect these endpoints. First subtract the time from b, then divide by b − a; check the endpoints before comparing the locations.

    Recover the reason for each subtraction

  • “A score between 0 and 1 proves that the hall will be a good choice.”

    This range describes the scoring scale under its assumptions. Here it measures only the walk. The quality of the workshop and the other criteria still need to be examined separately; they cannot be deduced from a dimensionless score.

Exercise 1 · Direct application

Another estimate of walking time

While preparing the same workshop, the group considers a walk taking 18 min. Keep a = 10 min and b = 30 min. Calculate its score, then place it relative to the scores for Elms and Linden. Justify the direction of the comparison.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Ask how much shorter this walk is than reference point b.

Hint 2

The denominator remains the span of the shared scoring scale, even when the time changes.

Compare with the solution

The difference from the unfavorable reference point is 30 − 18 = 12 min. The span is still 30 − 10 = 20 min. We obtain 12/20 = 3/5, or 0.60, dimensionless.

This score is between 0.40 and 0.80. The walk taking 18 min is longer than the walk to Elms, but shorter than the walk to Linden: the ranking of the scores fits the stated preference.

Check your reasoning

  • You write and explain the subtractions in the numerator and denominator.
  • You obtain 3/5 with no unit and relate its ranking to that of the times.

Exercise 2 · Check by changing the unit

The walk measured in seconds

The walking time to Elms is now given in seconds. A minute equals 60 s. Convert the time and each reference point, then recalculate the score. Explain why converting only the walking time would make the calculation incorrect.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Subtraction requires all its terms to have the same unit.

Hint 2

A conversion multiplies both the difference from reference point b and the span by the same factor.

Compare with the solution

The symbol s after a number abbreviates seconds here; it is distinct from s(x), the name of the score. The reference points become 10 × 60 = 600 s and 30 × 60 = 1800 s. The walking time is 14 × 60 = 840 s.

Difference from the unfavorable reference point: 1800 − 840 = 960 s. Span: 1800 − 600 = 1200 s. The score is 960/1200 = 4/5, or 0.80, dimensionless. It is identical to the score calculated in minutes.

The same factor 60 multiplies the numerator and denominator, so it cancels in their quotient. Converting only the walking time would mean subtracting seconds from minutes: the subtraction would make no sense, even if a calculator could produce a number.

Check your reasoning

  • You convert the walking time and the reference points, with their units.
  • You recover exactly 4/5 and explain why the common factor cancels.
  • You identify the mixture of units before treating its result as a score.

Exercise 3 · Diagnosis and justification

A detour goes beyond the scoring scale

A detour increases the walking time to Elms to 35 min. The reference points remain 10 min and 30 min. Someone claims: “The formula will necessarily give a score between 0 and 1.” Check this claim and propose an honest response. What would change if every negative result were replaced by 0?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

First check whether a ≤ x ≤ b still holds.

Hint 2

Calculate the sign of b − x. Modifying the result afterward would be an additional rule.

Compare with the solution

The time 35 min exceeds b = 30 min. The assumption that guaranteed the score range is no longer satisfied. If we simply extend the calculation, we obtain 30 − 35 = -5 min, then (-5)/20 = -1/4, or -0.25, dimensionless.

The calculation is arithmetically defined, but it does not constitute a valid score under the scoring scale declared on the interval. We must flag that the time is outside the domain, then ask for the rule applicable to these times to be specified. This does not prove that the detour is impossible.

Replacing the negative result by 0 is called clipping at the lower bound: we cut off what lies beyond it. All walks taking longer than b would then receive the same score 0, just like the walk taking 30 min. This convention loses the differences between their times; it must be declared and does not follow from the original normalization.

Check your reasoning

  • You identify the violated assumption before using the guarantee about the range.
  • You find -1/4 and distinguish the extended calculation from an accepted score.
  • You explain the information lost through clipping, without silently changing the scoring scale.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What must you know to interpret a hall’s score?

The criterion measured, its unit, the reference points and the favorable direction of the scoring scale. A dimensionless score remains tied to this rule; it does not describe the overall quality of the hall.

Recall 2How do you build the score when a shorter time is preferable?

With reference points a < b, the score is (b − x)/(b − a): the difference from the unfavorable reference point divided by the whole span. a receives 1, b receives 0. For a ≤ x ≤ b, the score stays between 0 and 1.

Recall 3Why do seconds give the same score as minutes?

If the walking time and the reference points are all converted, the differences in the numerator and denominator are multiplied by the same factor. Their quotient stays the same. Mixing units in a subtraction invalidates the calculation.

Recall 4How can you recover the time from the score and the reference points?

Multiply the score by b − a to recover the difference from reference point b, then subtract this difference from b: x = b − s(x) × (b − a). The score alone is not enough if the scoring scale has been lost.

Recall 5What should you do if a detour exceeds the unfavorable reference point?

Flag that the time is outside the declared domain. The extended calculation becomes negative. Replacing it by 0 adds clipping that makes times beyond the reference point indistinguishable; this convention must be declared.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Hide the page and reconstruct the reasoning: what was measured, with what unit and which shared reference points? Recover the numerator from the favorable direction, the denominator from the span, then the guaranteed range. Check using the endpoints and by recovering the time. Finally, explain what is preserved when converting to seconds and what must be flagged outside the domain.
