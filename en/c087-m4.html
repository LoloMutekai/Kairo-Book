Level 2 · C087·M4

Fatigue

Your goalCalculate a descriptive fatigue index from sleep debt, continuous engagement and perceived tension, then state what this index does not measure.

Before you start
c086-m5 : emotion and cognition · c056-m5 : effect size · c087-m1 : circadian rhythms · c087-m2 : sleep pressure · c087-m3 : sleep architecture

✿01 See the idea

After a short night and a long study session, fatigue cannot be read from a single gauge. It is like a water level fed by several taps: missing sleep, sustained effort and tension each add their share.

This unit follows a single fictional scene: a person is studying for an exam. Their last night of sleep lasted 360 minutes against a reference of 450 minutes; they have worked for 216 minutes without a break; they rate their tension at 5 on a scale from 0 to 10. These values were invented for the course and do not describe any real person.

The idea is to convert each tap into a number with no unit between 0 and 1, then combine them using weights that sum to 1. The result is an index: a calculated summary, useful for comparing two days, but it is neither fatigue itself nor a capacity to work.

Three earlier units frame this interpretation. Sleep pressure (C087·M2) rises with time spent awake: continuous engagement is only an indirect proxy for it here. Circadian rhythms (C087·M1) make alertness vary over the day: the index ignores them. Sleep architecture (C087·M3) distinguishes stages: we retain only the total duration of the night.

The rule to remember

Normalize each source between 0 and 1, apply weights that sum to 1, add the contributions, then interpret the index using its conventional thresholds and its limitations.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

A fictional day before the exam

A fictional person slept for 360 minutes, while the working reference is 450 minutes. They have worked for 216 minutes without a break, with 360 minutes taken as a very prolonged duration, and rate their tension at 5 out of 10. Using weights of 5, 3 and 2 tenths, let us calculate their fatigue index.

  1. Define the quantities and assumptions

    R is the reference sleep duration, in minutes; S is the duration of the last night of sleep, in minutes. M is the duration of continuous engagement, in minutes, and L is the duration from which engagement is considered very prolonged. N is the perceived tension rating on the scale from 0 to 10. D, T, E and the index F have no unit.

    F=aD+bT+cE avec a+b+c=1

    Working assumptions: the three sources are indirect indicators, never fatigue itself; each normalized source is bounded between 0 and 1; the weights a, b and c are conventions, not estimated from data; their sum is 1; the effects add without interaction. Tension is a subjective self-assessment. Here a is 1/2, b is 3/10 and c is 1/5: these values are chosen for the course.

  2. Measure sleep debt

    Compare the shortfall with the reference: 450 − 360 = 90 minutes. Then divide by the reference to obtain a number with no unit. The minutes cancel, so counting in hours or minutes gives the same result.

    D=R−SR=450−360450=0.20

    The debt is 1/5, or 0.20. A night longer than the reference would give a negative shortfall: in that case we clamp the debt to 0 using the bound below, because the model contains no sleep credit.

    D=max(0,min(1,R−SR))

  3. Normalize engagement and tension

    Use the same method: divide by a reference scale to obtain a number with no unit between 0 and 1. The choice of L and the scale out of 10 is a convention; a different scale would change the values.

    T=ML=216360=0.60 et E=N10=510=0.50

    Engagement is 3/5, or 0.60, and tension is 1/2, or 0.50.

  4. Apply weights and add

    Multiply each source by its weight to obtain its contribution to the index. Sleep: 1/2 × 1/5 = 1/10. Engagement: 3/10 × 3/5 = 9/50. Tension: 1/5 × 1/2 = 1/10.

    F=0.50×0.20+0.30×0.60+0.20×0.50=0.10+0.18+0.10=0.38

    The index is 19/50, or 0.38. Notice that engagement contributes the most (0.18), although its weight is smaller than that of sleep, because its normalized value is larger. The weight is not the contribution.

  5. Check the result

    First check: the weights sum to 1. If all three sources were 1, the index would be 1; if they were 0, it would be 0. With weights that sum to 1, F is a weighted average: it must remain between the smallest source (0.20) and the largest (0.60). The result 0.38 lies within these bounds.

    Second check: counting whole hundredths, the contributions sum to 38 hundredths, which does match 0.38. A result outside the interval from 0 to 1 would indicate an incorrectly normalized source or poorly chosen weights.

  6. Interpret the index using its thresholds and limitations

    Working conventions for this course: low zone below 0.35, moderate zone from 0.35 inclusive to 0.60 exclusive, high zone from 0.60 upward. The index 0.38 is therefore moderate: it exceeds the lower boundary by 0.03 and remains 0.22 below the upper boundary.

    This interpretation describes a calculation. It says nothing about the time of day for the person, how their 360 minutes of sleep were organized, or what is actually making them tired. It is not a diagnosis.

Explain it in your own words

Why does the index stay between the smallest and largest of the three sources? Explain what would happen to this guarantee if the weights did not sum to one.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The index F is the person's measured fatigue.”

    F is a calculated summary of three indirect indicators, with no unit and with conventional weights. It measures neither felt fatigue nor the capacity to work. Reread what each source actually represents before commenting on the number.

    Reread the idea of the three taps

  • “The source with the largest weight is the one that matters most during the day.”

    A contribution is the weight multiplied by the normalized value. In the example, engagement has a smaller weight than sleep but contributes more because its normalized value is larger. Always compare the contributions.

    Revisit the calculation of the contributions

  • “An index crossing a threshold proves a real change of state.”

    Thresholds are working conventions. A tiny difference in the index can change the zone without any real change, and without dispersion we cannot speak of an effect size (C056·M5). Reread what the milestone does not prove.

    Reread what has not been established

With the bound, the debt is 0 and we obtain 0.28 again, as for a night at the reference duration. The bound is a modeling choice, not a physiological fact.

What tension rating makes the zone change?

A short night: S = 180 minutes. Engagement and tension remain as in the example. Calculate the index: it remains below the high fatigue threshold 0.60. What tension rating, out of 10, would reach it exactly? What can be concluded from the distance between the two ratings?

Hint 2

Compare with the solution

Shortfall: 450 − 180 = 270 minutes, debt 3/5, or 0.60, contribution 3/10. With engagement: 3/10 + 9/50 = 12/25. Adding 1/10 gives 29/50, or 0.58, which is 0.02 from the threshold 0.60.

To reach the threshold, the contribution of tension must be 3/5 − 12/25 = 3/25. Divide by its weight 1/5: normalized tension must be 3/5, giving a rating of 6 out of 10 instead of 5. A single rating point is therefore enough to change the zone. The difference between 0.58 and 0.60 is small compared with the uncertainty in weights that have not been estimated and in a self-assessed rating: the switch illustrates the fragility of a conventional threshold, not a measured effect.

Check your reasoning

  • Consistent steps leading to the index.
  • Threshold solved in reverse by isolating the contribution of tension.
  • Cautious conclusion about a conventional threshold, without a diagnosis.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does the index F in this unit represent?

A calculated summary with no unit, between 0 and 1: the weighted sum of sleep debt, continuous engagement and perceived tension. It is neither felt fatigue nor a capacity to work.

Recall 2How is sleep debt calculated, and why bound it?

It is the shortfall relative to the reference divided by the reference, clamped between 0 and 1. Without a bound, a night longer than the reference would create a credit that erased fatigue from the other sources, which the model does not represent.

Recall 3Why must the weights sum to one, and have they been validated?

With a sum of 1 and sources between 0 and 1, the index remains in this interval because it is a weighted average. The weight values are working conventions, not estimated from data.

Recall 4What does the fatigue index leave out?

The time of day, the organization of sleep, the causes of fatigue and differences between people. Crossing a threshold is not proof, and the difference between two indices is not an effect size.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Set the sleep reference; calculate the debt and clamp it between 0 and one; normalize engagement and tension to the same interval; apply weights that sum to one; add the contributions; check that the index stays between the smallest and largest source; identify the zone using conventional thresholds; state that the index is a summary of proxies, with no time of day, no sleep structure, no proof and no diagnosis.
