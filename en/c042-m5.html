Recall, ranking and evaluation

Your goalEvaluate document retrieval by separating coverage of useful documents, their positions and the score used to rank them.

✿01 See the idea

In a bicycle workshop, you are looking for an instruction sheet to fix a puncture in a wheel with an inner tube. The search first returns a tire advertisement, then a repair procedure, then a sheet about brakes. You have received documents, but how many actually meet your need? And how many useful procedures were left out of the list?

The corpus is the collection being searched. Our fictional workshop has 6 sheets identified by A to F. Before looking at the scores, a review of this entire small corpus judges A, C and E useful for this specific need. B, D and F are not useful. Here, the word relevant will mean “provides a procedure applicable to this repair”, according to this explicit judgment.

Retrieving a document and placing it early in the list answer different questions. Recall measures the proportion of relevant documents retrieved. Precision measures the proportion of retrieved results that are relevant. The position of the first relevant document indicates how many results the reader goes through before finding help.

The scene, sheets, scores and judgments are fictional. The scores are provided to study evaluation; we are simulating neither a vector representation model nor a real search engine. A similarity score ranks candidates according to a rule; it does not replace reading to judge their usefulness.

The rule to remember

Fix the need and the judgments, keep the identifiers, then measure separately what was retrieved and where it appears.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Looking for a repair procedure in the workshop sheets

Fixed need: fix a puncture in a wheel with an inner tube. The engine returns B, A, D, C, F, E in descending score order. We initially keep k = 3 sheets. Let us reconstruct the judgments, counts and measures before deciding what this ranking allows us to claim.

  1. Define the reference before reading the scores

    A describes applying a patch to an inner tube; C describes replacing it; E describes finding the leak and then repairing the tube. Each sheet provides a procedure applicable to the need. B is a tire advertisement; D concerns brake adjustment; F repairs a tubeless tire. The words “tire” or “repair” are therefore not enough to judge relevance.

    A binary judgment assigns 1 to A, C and E and 0 to the others. Binary means that here we distinguish only between relevant and irrelevant. All sheets have been judged for the same query and the same corpus version. A sheet that has not yet been read would remain unjudged, rather than automatically irrelevant.

    Let G be the set of relevant identifiers, so G = {A, C, E}. The symbol |G| counts its elements. Set g = |G|: g is 1 + 1 + 1 = 3 relevant documents. This denominator comes from the entire corpus.

  2. Keep the order and the meaning of the score

    The fictional scores provided are B: 0.94; A: 0.90; D: 0.86; C: 0.80; F: 0.70; E: 0.60. They have no unit. Sorting them from largest to smallest gives B → A → D → C → F → E. We do not recalculate the vector representations studied in previous units here.

    k is the number of sheets kept. Lk denotes their ordered list, with no duplicates; here Lk = (B, A, D) and k = 3 documents. We assume that the engine can fill every requested position and that no additional filter removes a sheet. To reproduce the evaluation, keep the query, corpus version, identifiers, scores and sorting rule; if scores are tied, also fix a rule for breaking ties.

    B comes first even though its content does not meet the need. A high score does not mean a probability of relevance. Even an exact search, which correctly retrieves the highest scores in the corpus, can place useless documents before useful ones. Calculation accuracy and semantic usefulness require different checks.

  3. Count what is useful in the selection

    t denotes the number of documents in Lk whose identifiers also belong to G. The symbol ∩ means “common to both sets”; for this count, we ignore the order of the list. B does not count, A counts, D does not count: t = 0 + 1 + 0 = 1 relevant document retrieved.

    t=|Lk∩G|=1

    The missing relevant sheets are C and E: g − t = 3 − 1 = 2 documents. The irrelevant results are B and D: k − t = 3 − 1 = 2 documents. Identifiers prevent the same sheet from being counted more than once.

  4. Divide by the reference that answers the question

    Rk is recall at this list length: of all relevant documents, what proportion was retrieved? Its denominator is g. Pk is the precision of this selection: of the documents kept, what proportion is relevant? Its denominator is k. Here, the word precision refers neither to rounding a number nor to the inverse of a variance.

    Rk=tg=13=1/3
    Pk=tk=13=1/3

    Both ratios here equal 1/3, or approximately 0.33, with no unit. This equality is coincidental: g and k both equal 3 documents. At another list length, their denominators may differ. The counts are in documents; dividing them cancels this unit.

    Assumptions: k and g are strictly positive and the judgments cover the entire corpus. With no relevant document in the reference, recall would require an explicit convention. If a search returns fewer sheets than requested, state the actual list length evaluated and the precision convention; do not act as though every position was filled.

  5. Also measure the position of the first useful document

    r is the rank of the first relevant document in Lk. Rank is a position with no unit; it starts at 1. We pass B, then reach A: r = 2. To obtain a measure that increases when the first useful document appears earlier, reciprocal rank Uk takes the inverse of r.

    Uk=1r=12=1/2

    Uk equals 1/2, or 0.50, with no unit. If no relevant document appears in the selection, our convention is Uk = 0; we do not try to divide by a nonexistent rank. This measure concerns only the first relevant document: it does not say whether the others were retrieved. The reordering exercise will change Uk without changing Rk.

  6. Check and limit the conclusion

    Check using identifiers: the intersection of {B, A, D} and {A, C, E} contains only A. Adding retrieved and missing documents gives 1 + 2 = 3 relevant documents. Adding useful and irrelevant documents in the selection gives 1 + 2 = 3 documents. These partitions check the counts without starting again from the divisions.

    Each ratio lies between 0 and 1: the numerator counts a subset of what the denominator counts. The conclusion is local: for this query and this list length, the engine retrieves A, leaves out C and E, and places an advertisement before A. It establishes no general performance claim about the engine.

    To compare settings, fix common needs, corpus versions, judgments and selection length. Keep evaluation queries separate from tuning decisions: changing those decisions until this single example gives a good result would make the comparison misleading. Cases with no relevant document and unknown judgments must remain identifiable. Neither an average of similarity scores nor a result list judged in isolation provides actual recall.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the results. Reconstruct the two denominators, then explain why moving A to the front would improve reciprocal rank without retrieving C or E. What information would you need to keep so that someone else could repeat your evaluation?

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The first score is high, so the first document is relevant.”

    Separate the calculated score from the judgment about content. B scores 0.94 and remains an advertisement. Reread the fixed need and the justification for each judgment before comparing the order with the useful content.

    Review the reference and the scores

  • “I calculate recall by dividing by the number of displayed results.”

    That denominator gives the precision of the selection. Recall refers to all relevant documents in the corpus, including those you do not see in the results. In the exercise with more results, the denominators differ and the error becomes visible.

    Distinguish the denominators with a longer list

  • “If I put A first, recall has increased.”

    Compare the identifiers before and after: reordering B, A, D as A, B, D leaves the same selection. A appears earlier, but C and E remain absent. Reciprocal rank changes; recall and precision stay the same.

    Compare the same sheets in a different order

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What reference do we need to calculate document recall?

We need to know the documents relevant to the need in the evaluated corpus, including those absent from the results. Recall divides the number of relevant documents retrieved by this total. Without complete judgments or an explicit convention, full recall has not been established.

Recall 2Why do precision and recall answer different questions?

Precision relates the relevant documents retrieved to the selection; recall relates them to all relevant documents in the corpus. With B, A, D, C, precision is 2/4 = 1/2 and recall is 2/3 = 2/3.

Recall 3What changes if A, B, D replaces B, A, D?

The selection and its judgments stay the same, so recall and precision do not change. A appears at rank 1 instead of rank 2: its reciprocal rank goes from 1/2 to 1. This measure describes only the first relevant document.

Recall 4Why does an exact similarity ranking not guarantee relevant sheets?

Exact here means retrieving the highest scores according to the stated calculation. Relevance depends on the content and the fixed need. Advertisement B can receive the highest score without providing a repair procedure.

Recall 5What should we fix before comparing two search rules?

The needs, corpus and method versions, identifiers, judgments, list length evaluated and sorting rules. Reserve evaluation queries separately from tuning, state the conventions for empty cases and keep the results. A single search does not prove general performance.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without looking at the formulas, reconstruct the sequence: fixed need → judged corpus → ranked list of identifiers → relevant documents retrieved and missing → two denominators → position of the first relevant document → limited conclusion. Explain what reranking preserves and why displaying everything is not enough to choose a good search.
