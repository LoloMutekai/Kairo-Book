Level 3 · C201·M4

Financing

Your goalCalculate the financing charges for an account with long and short positions, then reconcile them with its cash balance and value.

Before you start
c147-m5 : costs and out-of-sample evaluation · c041-m5 : transactions and windows · c199-m5 : residuals · c017-m5 : reproduction protocol · c201-m1 : simulation clock · c201-m2 : point-in-time universe · c201-m3 : positions

✿01 See the idea

Share prices can stay unchanged while the value of an account falls. Keeping a position open may require paying the lender of cash or securities. A historical replay that tracks only price changes would leave these cash flows out of its accounts.

Our fictional account receives 1000 €. It buys 18 shares of stock A at 100 € per share and sells short 6 shares of stock B, also at 100 € per share. Selling short here means borrowing the B shares, selling them and having to return the same number of shares. The sale proceeds enter the cash balance; the obligation to return the securities remains on the account.

After the trades, the cash balance is -200 €: the account therefore owes 200 € in cash. It also owes the B shares, valued at 600 €. These are different debts. Sale proceeds do not cancel the securities borrowing that made the sale possible.

We track only financing over a holding interval, here a day. Positions and prices remain constant. All values and rates are fictional; they serve to check the accounting of a simulation and do not describe a broker’s terms.

The rule to remember

Identify the borrowed cash and the value of the borrowed securities separately; apply the appropriate rate to each base over the same interval; debit the charge once, then recalculate the account value.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

A day with no price movement

After buying A and selling B, keep the positions for 1 interval. The rate is 8 basis points on the cash debt and 12 basis points on the value of the borrowed B shares, for this entire interval. Calculate the charges, the final cash balance and the final account value.

  1. Set the timeline and the contract

    The trades have already been executed at the start of the interval. The rates are known at that time; we do not choose a favourable rate retrospectively. The charge is recognised and debited at the end, before any new trade. A position opened only at the end must not be charged for the elapsed interval.

    To isolate financing, the fictional contract allows full use of the proceeds from selling B. It pays no interest on a positive cash balance. It assumes that the B shares are available to borrow and requires no separate collateral deposit. Prices, quantities and rates do not change during the interval; there are no deposits, withdrawals, dividends or execution fees. These assumptions define this calculation and must be replaced if the simulated account has a different contract.

  2. Reconstruct the account before charges

    q_A = 18 shares and q_B = 6 shares are the quantities bought and borrowed, both positive by convention. p = 100 € per share is the constant price of each stock. L = q_A × p = 1800 € is the value held; S = q_B × p = 600 € is the positive value of the obligation to return the shares. The signed position in B is therefore −q_B.

    E = 1000 € is the initial capital. C is the signed cash balance, in euros. The purchase reduces it: 1000 − 1800 = -800 €. The sale increases it: -800 + 600 = -200 €. Net securities L − S are worth 1200 €.

    C=E−L+S;V=C+L−S

    V denotes the account value, in euros: cash and positions valued at current prices. Before charges, V = -200 + 1800 − 600 = 1000 €. The trades have moved capital between cash and positions; they have not increased it.

  3. Choose the amounts actually borrowed

    D is the cash debt, in euros: if C is negative, D = −C; otherwise D = 0 €. The maximum selects the larger of these values. In our account, D = 200 €. The base for securities borrowing is S = 600 €, even though selling them increased C.

    D=max(−C,0)

    The value V, the purchases L and the net securities L − S are not interchangeable bases. The cash lender charges on D; the lender of the B shares charges on S. This distinction avoids charging for cash financing on capital that has not been borrowed.

  4. Convert basis points without changing the period

    A basis point represents the fraction 1/10000. b_C = 8 and b_S = 12 are the rates expressed in basis points for the interval. r_C and r_S are their dimensionless fractions, applicable to this specific interval.

    rC=bC10000;rS=bS10000

    Here r_C = 1/1250 = 0.000800 and r_S = 3/2500 = 0.001200. Multiplying a base in euros by this fraction gives euros for the day. These are not annual rates: no division by the length of a year should be added. If the rates were annual, a day-count convention would first have to be specified and the fraction corresponding to the interval calculated.

  5. Calculate the items, then their sum

    I is the interest charge on cash; T is the share borrowing charge; F is their total, all in euros. Each multiplication answers the question: what fraction of the base must be paid for the interval?

    I=DrC;T=SrS;F=I+T

    Cash: I = 200 × 1/1250 = 4/25 € = 0.16 €. Securities: T = 600 × 3/2500 = 18/25 € = 0.72 €. Total: F = 0.16 + 0.72 = 0.88 €. We add the charges in euros, rather than the rates that apply to different bases.

  6. Debit once and revalue

    The prime mark denotes the end of the interval. C′ is the cash balance after payment, and V′ is the corresponding value, in euros. Since the securities and their prices have not changed, L and S retain their values.

    C′=C−F;V′=C′+L−S

    Cash: -200 − 0.88 = -200.88 €. Value: -200.88 + 1800 − 600 = 999.12 €. Subtracting F again from this last expression would count the charge a second time. The total charges can be kept in an explanatory column of the journal; this column does not create a new debit.

  7. Check another way and prepare for the next interval

    First check: prices are fixed and there are no other cash flows; the change in value must be the negative of the financing charge. V′ − V = 999.12 − 1000 = -0.88 €, or −0.88 €.

    Another reading of the balance sheet: the account holds 1800 € of A shares; it owes 600 € of B shares and 200 € in cash before charges. After payment, its cash debt is 200.88 €. Assets minus debts: 1800 − 600 − 200.88 = 999.12 €. This reading recovers the result without treating the sale proceeds as profit.

    For the next interval, start from C′: the charges paid on credit have increased the cash debt. Keeping the base at 200 € forever would miss this effect. With 0 rates, the charges would vanish; with a positive cash balance, only the cash charge would vanish under our contract. The share borrowing charge would still have to be paid.

Explain it in your own words

Without looking at the formulas, explain why selling B increases the cash balance without increasing the account value. Then explain why unchanged prices can allow an accounting loss, and which base will have to change the next day.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “Short sale proceeds are an available gain with no debt.”

    Keep the cash inflow in C and the obligation to return the shares in S. Check C + L − S before any charge: you should recover the initial capital. Securities borrowing is still charged on S.

    Reconstruct the account and its borrowing bases

  • “I can apply the rate in basis points directly as a multiplier.”

    Divide the number of basis points by 10000. Also check its period: our rates cover the entire day; an annual rate would require a stated conversion of the time period.

    Review the conversion of rates and units

  • “I debit the cash balance and then subtract the charges again from the account value.”

    Recalculate V′ from C′ and the positions. The debit is already included in C′. Check that the fall in value equals only the total charge, then use C′ for the next interval.

    Review the single debit and the checks

Exercise 1 · Identification and calculation

Charge for the correct borrowings

In the original account, a program charges for cash on the net securities, or 1200 €, and omits the borrowing of B. Name the correct bases, recalculate the charges and explain why the program does not describe our contract.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Separate what must be repaid in cash from what must be returned in shares.

Hint 2

The negative cash balance reveals the cash debt; selling B does not remove the shares to be returned.

Compare with the solution

The cash debt is D = 200 €, obtained from C = -200 €. The borrowing of B is based on S = 600 €. The 1200 € describes a difference between positions; it does not describe the cash borrowed.

The charges are therefore 200 × 8/10000 = 0.16 € and 600 × 12/10000 = 0.72 €, giving 0.88 € in total. The program must have separate items for these bases and retain the breakdown of the debit.

Check your reasoning

  • Name each debt and its unit.
  • Convert basis points before multiplying.
  • Recover the total and explain the error in the base.

Exercise 2 · Account variation

Capital covers the net purchases

The same account now receives 2000 € of initial capital, with the same positions and rates. Recalculate the cash balance after the trades, the charges, the final cash balance and the final value. Does financing become entirely free?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Subtract the net securities from the new capital to find C.

Hint 2

A positive cash balance makes D 0, but not S.

Compare with the solution

C = 2000 − 1800 + 600 = 800 €. So D = 0 €; the cash interest is 0 €. Our contract excludes interest payments on a positive cash balance.

The B shares are still borrowed: T = 0.72 €. F = 0.72 €. C′ = 800 − 0.72 = 799.28 €; V′ = 799.28 + 1800 − 600 = 1999.28 €. The fall in value equals exactly the securities charge. Financing is therefore not entirely free.

Check your reasoning

  • Check the sign of the cash balance before calculating D.
  • Keep the securities borrowing charge.
  • Reconcile the final value with the new capital minus the charges.

Exercise 3 · Application in the time loop

Carry forward the debt after payment

Return to the initial capital of 1000 €. After the day in the example, the positions remain open for another identical day. Charges are debited at the end of the day, without intermediate rounding. Calculate the new charge and the account value. Explain why simply repeating the first charge gives a difference.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The new starting cash balance is -200.88 €, rather than -200 €.

Hint 2

The B shares keep the same value; only the base for cash interest increases.

Compare with the solution

The new cash debt is 200.88 €. Interest: 200.88 × 1/1250 = 0.160704 €. Securities borrowing still costs 0.72 €. The charge for this day is 0.880704 €.

After the debit, C′ = -200.88 − 0.880704 = -201.760704 €. The value is -201.760704 + 1200 = 998.239296 €. The overall check gives 1000 − 1.760704 = 998.239296 €.

Repeating the initial charge would give 1.76 € in cumulative charges instead of 1.760704 €. The difference of 0.000704 € is the additional interest on the previous charge financed on credit. The small decimal amounts are retained to make this effect visible; a real contract would need to specify monetary rounding. An answer given after a hint remains assisted: repeat the loop without support on another day.

  • Keep exact values until display.
  • Check the result using capital minus all charges.
  • Explain the difference through the increased debt rather than a price change.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1Which bases separate cash financing from securities borrowing?

D = max(−C, 0) is the cash debt. S is the positive value of the borrowed shares. The rates apply to these distinct items, in euros.

Recall 2Why do short sale proceeds not constitute an immediate profit?

The sale increases the cash balance but creates an obligation to return the securities. With unchanged prices, these effects offset each other in V = C + L − S before charges.

Recall 3What should you check before multiplying an amount by a rate?

Its base, unit and period. A basis point equals 1/10000; the rates in the example cover the entire interval and are not annual.

Recall 4How can you avoid a double deduction when calculating the final value?

Debit F in C′ = C − F, then calculate V′ = C′ + L − S. With fixed prices and no other cash flows, V′ − V = −F.

Recall 5Which cash balance does the financing calculation for the next interval use?

The cash balance after the previous debit. If it is negative, the charges paid on credit increase the next interest base. Simply reading or giving an assisted answer does not establish mastery.
