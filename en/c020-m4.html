Dominance and escalation

Your goalCompare a lead with a dominance threshold, then justify requesting mediation when the local rules do not settle the disagreement.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

At the library, Iris wants to replace a wheel on the trolley now; Sami is against this and asks for an inspection first. How can they settle their disagreement without confusing a small lead with sufficient grounds for a decision?

The group agreed on a rule before the discussion: a person's position can be accepted if their weight exceeds the next person's weight by more than a stated threshold. Here, this sufficient lead is called dominance. Being in the lead is therefore not enough. This is a decision rule, not proof that the person is right.

If this rule does not settle the disagreement, the group checks its majority rule. If neither local rule settles it, the group refers the disagreement to a mediator. This referral is escalation. The mediator examines the arguments; if she is unavailable or cannot reach an outcome, the manager receives a request for a decision. The disagreement remains open until an outcome is established.

The scene, the weights and their variants are fictional. The weights represent the authority assigned for this specific question; they are given, known and comparable on the same scale. They are neither probabilities of being right nor actual measures of competence. We do not study how they are estimated or the quality of the repair here.

The rule to remember

Rank the weights, compare the gap with the strict threshold, then apply the majority rule if necessary. If nothing settles the disagreement, refer it onward while keeping its status open.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

The rule accepts Iris's position

In our fictional library, Iris has a weight of 0.80 and Sami has 0.60. The dominance threshold is 0.15; the majority threshold is 0.60. Iris is for replacing the wheel now, and Sami is against it. Let us work through the rule without changing the thresholds along the way.

  1. Define what the numbers mean

    The symbol w with subscript i denotes Iris's weight; with subscript s, it denotes Sami's. Their values are 80/100 = 4/5 and 60/100 = 3/5, respectively. Dividing the points by 100 puts the weights on the stated scale. These numbers have no unit; they do not count people. Their sum does not have to equal 1.

    τ, the Greek letter tau, denotes the dominance threshold: 15/100 = 3/20. q denotes the majority threshold: 60/100 = 3/5. These thresholds have no unit and are fixed for this exercise, with no claim that their settings are optimal.

    Assumptions: all weights are available, the topic and scale are the same, all 2 people take a position and nobody abstains. The rule tests dominance before the majority. The letter δ, delta, denotes the gap between the highest weight and the next highest. With only these people, comparing their weights is enough.

  2. Rank, then subtract

    Iris is in the lead because 0.80 > 0.60. To measure her lead, subtract the next highest weight from the highest; do not divide the weights or count votes.

    δ=wi−ws=0.80−0.60=0.20

    The lead is therefore 1/5, with no unit. The ranking prevents subtraction in the wrong order; in this scene, δ is positive or 0.

  3. Compare with the correct threshold

    Dominance requires δ > τ. The sign > means “strictly greater than”: equality is not enough. Here 0.20 > 0.15; the condition is met and the rule accepts Iris's position.

    δ−τ=0.20−0.15=0.05>0

    Subtracting the threshold gives a positive margin, another way to check the test. The result permits a decision under the chosen rule; it demonstrates neither that the wheel is in good condition nor that the accepted opinion is technically correct.

  4. Check in other ways and stop at the right point

    Check on the points scale: 80 − 60 = 20 points, more than the required 15 points. Check by addition: Sami's weight plus the threshold gives 0.60 + 0.15 = 0.75; Iris, at 0.80, is well above this. These checks reach the same decision.

    Dominance has settled the disagreement: a majority is not then asked to confirm the result. To see the difference, count only supporters: n = 1 + 1 = 2 people in total; nm = 1 person in each of the sides tied for the largest number of supporters. n and nm are counts of people. The share of the largest side is p = nm/n = 1/2 = 0.50, with no unit. It does not exceed q = 0.60. There would therefore be no sufficient majority, but this does not cancel the dominance tested first.

  5. Understand the referral branch

    In the exercises, we will keep the same trolley and the same positions while bringing the weights closer together. If δ no longer exceeds τ, move on to the test p > q. If that test fails, request mediation. Escalation then changes who examines the disagreement; it changes neither the weights nor the threshold to manufacture a winner.

    The request passes on the proposal, the positions and arguments, the known weights, the thresholds and the tests already performed. If mediation is unavailable or reaches no outcome, request a decision from the manager and state “disagreement open”. Sending this request, receiving an answer to it and implementing a decision are separate events.

Hide the calculation. Explain why Iris's position can be accepted despite the equal numbers of supporters. Then describe the path followed if the gap does not exceed the threshold, without inventing the mediator's answer.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The highest weight always wins.”

    A ranking tells you who is in the lead. You still need to calculate the gap to the next highest weight and check that it strictly exceeds the threshold. Work out the margin in the example again instead of looking only at Iris's weight.

    Review the ranking and dominance test

  • “At the threshold, dominance is sufficient.”

    The rule contains >, not ≥. At equality, the margin is 0: the dominance test fails. You must then examine the majority and refer the disagreement onward if that does not settle it either.

    Solve the case exactly at the boundary

  • “The case has been referred, so the conflict is resolved.”

    A referral proves only that a request has been directed to another decision maker, provided the referral itself is verified. Keep the disagreement open until there is evidence of an outcome; the mediator's unavailability does not make the person ranked first right.

    Reread the path to the final decision request

Compare with the solution

Iris remains in the lead. The gap is 0.75 − 0.60 = 0.15, exactly τ. The margin δ − τ is 0. So δ = τ, and δ > τ is false: there is no sufficient dominance.

This result does not select Sami or close the case. It indicates that you must move on to the next rule. Equality is preserved exactly in the fractions, rather than being created by rounding.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1How do you distinguish first place from sufficient dominance?

First place comes from the ranking. Sufficient dominance also requires the gap to the next highest weight to strictly exceed the stated threshold, on the same scale.

Recall 2What does the rule do when the gap is exactly equal to the threshold?

The strict test fails. Examine the majority next; if it does not settle the disagreement, request mediation. Equality does not select any winner through dominance.

Recall 3Why does the equal number of supporters not cancel the result in the example?

The rule tests dominance first, and it is sufficient in the example. The majority is tested to make a decision only if dominance fails. A different order would be a different rule.

Recall 4What does escalation change, and what does it pass on?

It assigns the review to another decision maker. It passes on the proposal, positions, arguments, weights, thresholds and tests performed; it does not change the numbers to produce a winner.

Recall 5Why does a request for a decision leave the disagreement open?

A request sent is not a decision received. If mediation is unavailable or reaches no outcome, a final decision is requested without inventing its result or declaring that work has been completed.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without rereading, reconstruct the sequence by naming the input, each test and the possible outputs: comparable weights → ranking → gap and strict threshold → majority if necessary → mediation → decision request if necessary, with the disagreement still open. Then explain why an outcome accepted under a rule proves neither that the opinion is correct nor that the replacement has been carried out.
