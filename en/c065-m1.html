Level 1 · C065·M1

Regression

Your goalFit a straight line to printing times, interpret its slope, and distinguish an estimated value, an observation, and a causal conclusion.

Before you start
c040-m5 : least squares · c056-m5 : effect size · c037-m5 : controlled cleaning

✿01 See the idea

You time print jobs to predict how long you will have to wait. In our fictional scene, 1 page takes 4 s, 2 pages take 5 s, and 3 pages take 9 s. The points rise overall, but they do not lie on a straight line. What simple rule can we keep without claiming to reproduce every recorded time?

Regression describes one quantity using another by fitting a rule to observations. Here, the quantity to estimate is the time; the quantity used to estimate it is the number of pages. We choose a straight line: a slope describes the predicted increase per page, and an intercept allows the whole line to shift up or down.

The line is a compromise among the observed jobs. It may give 6 s for 2 pages when the stopwatch showed 5 s. The estimate summarizes the chosen trend; it does not replace the observation.

The 3 pairs are invented so that we can follow the entire calculation. The same printer, the same settings, and pages assumed to be comparable: these are the conditions for our approximation. We have no readings from a real printer here.

The rule to remember

Choose what you are estimating, fit the line by minimizing the squares of the vertical differences, then interpret its slope with its units and limitations.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

A straight line for the same print jobs

Keep the pairs (pages; seconds): (1 ; 4), (2 ; 5), (3 ; 9). We will find the slope and offset that minimize the sum of the squared differences measured in seconds.

  1. Name the rule, the data, and the conditions

    For each job indexed by i, xᵢ is the number of pages and yᵢ is the observed time, in seconds. n = 3 counts the jobs and has no physical unit. The hat on ŷᵢ means “value estimated by the line.” The fitted model is written ŷᵢ = a + bxᵢ: a is the intercept, in seconds; b is the slope, in seconds per page.

    The pairs remain matched job by job after we check the units. We treat the page counts as known without error and give each time the same weight. We choose an affine approximation over the observed range, from 1 to 3 pages. Affine here means that an offset a is allowed: the line does not have to pass through the origin.

    Calculating this compromise for a given list does not require the errors to follow a normal distribution. Using the line afterward for new jobs assumes that the relationship remains relevant under the conditions considered; the example data do not demonstrate this.

    y^i=a+bxi

  2. Specify what fitting means

    The difference eᵢ = yᵢ − ŷᵢ is vertical, in seconds. A positive difference places the observation above the line. We seek the coefficients a and b that make Q(a,b), the sum of the squares of these differences, as small as possible. Q is in seconds squared; the symbol ∑ tells us to sum over all the jobs.

    Q(a,b)=∑(yi−a−bxi)2

    Squaring prevents a positive difference from canceling a negative difference and gives greater weight to large differences. The least squares criterion from the prerequisite applies here to a line with an offset. We define the differences to perform this fit; their detailed analysis belongs to another unit.

  3. Find the center of the observations

    Start with the means, written x̄ and ȳ: the bar means the sum divided by the number of jobs. The page counts give 1 + 2 = 3, then 3 + 3 = 6 pages. So x̄ = 6/3 = 2 pages.

    The times give 4 + 5 = 9, then 9 + 9 = 18 s. So ȳ = 18/3 = 6 s. Centering each coordinate on its mean separates the overall offset from the tilt of the line.

  4. Calculate the spread and the centered products

    The differences from x̄, in job order, are -1, 0, and 1 pages. Those from ȳ are -2, -1, and 3 s. Sxx denotes the sum of the squared differences in x; Sxᵧ denotes the sum of the products of the paired differences in x and y.

    Sxx=∑(xi−x¯)2=1+0+1=2 pages²

    The middle job has no difference in x: its squared contribution is 0. For the products, keep each pair together: (-1) × (-2) = 2, (0) × (-1) = 0, then (1) × (3) = 3. Their unit is a page multiplied by a second.

    Sxy=∑(xi−x¯)(yi−y¯)=2+0+3=5 page·s

    Sxx is strictly positive: the page counts vary. If they were all identical, no unique slope could be determined from this list; the division that follows would be impossible.

  5. Find and interpret the coefficients

    For a line with an offset and equal weights, least squares gives b = Sxᵧ/Sxx, then a = ȳ − bx̄. The quotient converts the centered products into a predicted change per page. The second operation requires the line to pass through the center (x̄ ; ȳ).

    b=52=2.5 s/page

    The product bx̄ is 5/2 × 2 = 5 s. So a = 6 − 5 = 1 s. The slope means that, on the line, 1 additional page adds 2.5 s to the estimated time. The offset is the time that this line assigns to 0 page, outside the observed range: it is not a reading of the startup time.

    y^=1+52x

    This notation uses the numerical values of x in pages and ŷ in seconds. The coefficients keep the stated units; you cannot change seconds to minutes without converting them.

  6. Return to each job and calculate the criterion

    For 1 page: bx = 2.5 s, then ŷ = 1 + 2.5 = 3.5 s. For 2 pages: bx = 5 s, then ŷ = 1 + 5 = 6 s. For 3 pages: bx = 7.5 s, then ŷ = 1 + 7.5 = 8.5 s.

    The differences, observation minus estimate, are 4 − 3.5 = 0.5 s; 5 − 6 = -1 s; 9 − 8.5 = 0.5 s. Square them before summing: 0.25 + 1 = 1.25, then 1.25 + 0.25 = 1.50 s².

    The signed differences cancel here: 0.5 + (-1) + 0.5 = 0 s. Yet their sum of squares is not 0. The cancellation of signs therefore does not mean that the line reproduces every recorded time.

  7. Check the minimum and limit the conclusion

    First check: for x = x̄ = 2 pages, a + bx̄ = 1 + 5 = 6 s = ȳ. The units of the slope quotient are consistent too: (page·s)/page² gives s/page.

    A complete check of the minimum uses the following identity, obtained by expanding the squares. A denotes any candidate intercept, in seconds; B denotes any candidate slope, in seconds per page. The numbers in this formula represent the same quantities and units as in the preceding calculation.

    Q(A,B)=1.50+3(A+2B−6)2+2(B−52)2

    The terms added to 1.50 s² are squares multiplied by positive coefficients. They are simultaneously 0 only for B = 2.5 s/page and A = 1 s. The calculated line therefore is the unique minimum among lines with an offset, for these pairs and this criterion.

    We have estimated a relationship within this list. A prediction for a new job still needs to be compared with its actual time. Nothing in this calculation alone establishes a causal effect: changes in content, settings, or startup could also accompany changes in job size. No prediction accuracy on new jobs has been measured.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The slope is a time: simply 2.5 seconds.”

    The slope is in seconds per page. Multiply it by the number of pages to obtain a time, then add the offset in seconds. For 2 pages, the product is 5 s and the estimated time is 6 s.

    Review the coefficients and their units

  • “The line is optimal, so it must pass through all the observations.”

    Optimal here means that the sum of squares is minimal. The line estimates 6 s for the job recorded at 5 s. The difference still exists, even at the minimum. Compare the circles with the line before rereading the criterion.

    Review the observations and the line

  • “I have a formula, so its value for 10 pages is a guaranteed time.”

    The formula can be evaluated at 10 pages, but the observed jobs range only from 1 to 3 pages. Extending the line outside this range is extrapolation. Check whether the same mechanism remains plausible, then compare the prediction with new observations that you are authorized to collect.

    Work through the objection in the last exercise

Exercise 1 · Direct application

Estimate while keeping the stopwatch reading

With a = 1 s and b = 2.5 s/page, calculate the estimated time for 2 pages. Compare it with the observation 5 s. Which of these numbers comes from the line?

Compare with the solution

The product bx is 2.5 × 2 = 5 s. Adding a gives ŷ = 1 + 5 = 6 s. This is the number supplied by the line.

The observation remains y = 5 s. The difference, observation minus estimate, is 5 − 6 = -1 s: the job took less time than estimated. You should neither change the recorded reading to make it match nor claim that the optimum requires a 0 difference.

Recover the line from the pairs

Start again using only the pairs (1 ; 4), (2 ; 5), (3 ; 9), in pages and seconds. Recover the means, the centered sums, b, then a. Then check that the line passes through the center of the data. Explain why different page counts are needed.

Hint 2

Calculate Sₓₓ and Sₓᵧ, then b = Sₓᵧ/Sₓₓ and a = ȳ − bx̄. Ask yourself when the denominator becomes 0.

Compare with the solution

The totals are 6 pages and 18 s for 3 jobs; the means are 2 pages and 6 s. The differences in x are -1, 0, 1 pages; the differences in y are -2, -1, 3 s.

The squares in x give 1 + 0 + 1 = 2 pages². The centered products give 2 + 0 + 3 = 5 page·s. So b = 5/2 = 2.5 s/page, then a = 6 − 5 = 1 s.

At the center, 1 + 2.5 × 2 = 6 s, matching ȳ. If all the x values were equal, their differences from the mean would be 0: Sxx = 0. A unique slope could not be identified, even if the times varied.

  • Recover 2 pages² and 5 page·s before the coefficients.
  • Check the center and explain the case where the slope cannot be identified.

Exercise 3 · Transfer and objection

Is a numerical output enough?

Someone enters 10 pages into the same line, then announces: “The next job will take exactly the calculated time; the slope proves the causal cost of a page, and a measures startup.” Calculate the numerical output, then assess these claims separately. Suggest a useful check before actual use.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Compare 10 pages with the range of jobs used for the fit.

Hint 2

Distinguish what a line defines, what has been observed, and what would require controlled conditions to isolate a cause.

Compare with the solution

The formula gives 2.5 × 10 = 25 s, then 1 + 25 = 26 s. This output can be calculated. However, it extends the relationship beyond 3 pages: it is an extrapolation, with no guarantee for the next recorded time.

The slope describes the change predicted by this line. It does not prove that adding a page, with all other conditions held constant, would cause exactly this additional time. The pairs alone do not rule out other possible changes.

a = 1 s is the value of the line for 0 page, outside the observed range. No startup time has been directly isolated. To evaluate actual use, we would collect new comparable jobs within the intended range, without reusing their times to claim we had predicted them. A causal conclusion would also require a protocol that controls other possible causes. This page does neither.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1In the printing scene, what does regression seek, and what does the hat mean?

It fits a rule that estimates a time from a page count. ŷ is the time given by the rule; y is the observed time. They may differ.

Recall 2What criterion selects our line? Why not just sum the signed differences?

It minimizes Q, the sum of the squared differences yᵢ − a − bxᵢ, in seconds squared. The signs can cancel while each observation remains far from the line. The differences are vertical, in seconds.

Recall 3How do you recover b and a, with their units and the condition on the pages?

b = Sₓᵧ/Sₓₓ in s/page, then a = ȳ − bx̄ in s. Sₓₓ sums the squared centered page counts; Sₓᵧ sums the centered products of the pairs. Sₓₓ must be strictly positive: the x values must vary.

Recall 4What does our slope of 2.5 s/page say? What does a not automatically measure?

On the line, adding 1 page increases the estimate by 2.5 s. This does not guarantee the difference between two recorded times. a is the estimate at 0 page; without an observation or a suitable protocol, it is not a reading of startup time.

Recall 5Why is the value calculated for 10 pages insufficient to prove either an exact future time or a causal effect?

It extrapolates a line fitted only from 1 to 3 pages. The relationship may change, and a job may differ from the estimate. Observations alone do not control other causes; each claim needs appropriate evidence.
