Level 3 · C220·M1

Profiling

Your goalAttribute the time taken by a process to its functions, check the accounting and choose an area to investigate without claiming an unmeasured improvement.

Before you start
c176-m5 : correlated simulation · c103-m5 : regularization · c050-m5 : conditioning · c017-m5 : reproduction protocol

✿01 See the idea

A script reads a batch of temperatures, calculates a summary of their spread and writes a report. You are waiting for the report: is reading taking the time, calculating the summary, or repeating a small calculation? The length of the code cannot tell you.

Profiling means observing where time goes during a defined workload. Here, traiter_lot calls lire and then resumer; resumer calls ecart for each of the 300 temperatures. These names identify functions: blocks of program code that can be called. In this sequential scenario, a call suspends the work of the calling function until the called function returns.

A function’s inclusive time covers its own operations and the calls it waits for. Its self time excludes those calls. Thus, the 600 ms spent in ecart are already included in the 900 ms spent in resumer, which are themselves included in the 1200 ms for the complete process. The diagram shows what is included within what; its branches do not represent simultaneous work.

All temperatures, durations and traces on this page are fictional. They serve to check reasoning, without describing a real machine. The numerical summary is assumed to be correct and identical across repetitions; we are studying only how time is attributed. Techniques for speeding up the program will follow this unit.

The rule to remember

Measure a specific workload; follow inclusive times down the call tree to locate the work, then use self times to build a budget without overlap.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Account for the wait before changing the script

The batch contains 300 fictional temperatures. Timing runs from entry into traiter_lot until the report has finished being written. The clock readings are 100 ms at the start and 1300 ms at the end, measured from the same arbitrary origin. The fictional function report is shown in the first diagram.

  1. Define the question and the clock

    The question concerns the wait for the complete report. Elapsed time, also called wall time, includes computation, waiting and interruptions by the system. A process’s CPU time counts the time during which its threads use the processor; waiting without computation does not add to it. A long elapsed time alone therefore does not establish that the processor is saturated.

    To measure a duration, take the difference between readings of a monotonic clock, which is not adjusted like a calendar clock. Here, h_d and h_a denote the readings at the start and at the end; T is the duration of the batch. All are expressed in milliseconds, abbreviated ms; 1000 ms equals 1 s. The clock’s origin cancels out in the subtraction.

    T=ha−hd=1300−100=1200 ms=1.20 s

  2. Define what the report adds up

    tkdnfl nutpmn mujtnx rdkrxl cllxvd dubsqg yvamsu ohuxxk lkxgtp zdsbxp sgdwtz eivvks lzkzvc wq zfflwj ibzzdd ztkf The subscripts B, L, R and E denote traiter_lot, lire, resumer and ecart respectively. The fictional report uses the same elapsed-time clock throughout.

    This budget assumes sequential execution, with no recursion or overlap; every call to ecart belongs to resumer; all calls within the interval are represented. The supplied durations are treated as exact for the calculation. A real tool adds measurement work and rounding: these equalities are not a promise about all its reports.

    Instrumentation adds observations, for example at function entry and exit. A function profile can attribute time and count calls. A simple timer around the batch gives only T. The former helps identify where to look; the latter also measures the duration without all this instrumentation.

    CB=PB+CL+CR;CR=PR+CE

  3. Follow the cost down into the function that contains it

    The complete process takes 1200 ms. resumer contains 900 ms of this, a fraction of 3/4 of the total, or 75 %. But its own operations take only 300 ms. Subtract this self time to find the duration of its only descendant function, ecart, accumulated across all its calls.

    ecart makes no calls in this model: its inclusive time is also its self time. We obtain 900 − 300 = 600 ms. We have located time within a function; we have not yet identified which instruction or wait causes it.

    PE=CE=CR−PR=600 ms

  4. Separate total cost from average cost per call

    Dividing accumulated self time by the number of calls gives the average self time per call, denoted P̄_f. This division requires N_f to be strictly greater than 0. The average does not describe each call individually: some could take longer and others less time.

    For ecart: 600 ÷ 300 = 2 ms per call. lire takes 200 ms during its single call, so it appears more expensive per call. Yet ecart accumulates more time over the batch. When seeking to reduce the total wait, the accumulated budget makes ecart worth examining, without guaranteeing that its time can be reduced.

    P¯f=PfNf;P¯E=600300=2 ms par appel

  5. Reconcile the budget and calculate a share

    Add the self times, which do not overlap: lire and resumer contribute 200 + 300 = 500 ms. With the 100 ms of self time in traiter_lot, this makes 600 ms outside ecart. Add its 600 ms: the budget reaches 1200 ms, equal to the difference between the clock readings. This checks the result using a different breakdown.

    The share for ecart is its self time divided by the total duration. The ratio has no unit, since the ms cancel out. Multiplying this ratio by 100 gives the number to write before the % symbol.

    PET=6001200=0.50=50 %

  6. Turn the finding into the next measurement

    The finding is limited: ecart accounts for 50 % of the elapsed time in this fictional instrumented trace. A share of time is neither a success rate nor a promised improvement. The next measurement could isolate what happens inside ecart, then investigate whether its calls are necessary for the expected result.

    Before a comparison, keep the same input file, options, expected result, program version and machine conditions. State whether reading benefits from a cache: a copy already available in memory. Separate startup and preparation from the repetitions if the question excludes them; include them if the question concerns the first report produced.

    Repeat the timing without the detailed profiler and keep the durations, instead of keeping only the most favorable one. The median is the central value of the sorted durations when their count is odd; the range is the largest minus the smallest. They summarize the repetitions without guaranteeing the duration of the next batch.

    The instrument itself can slow down frequent calls. A ranking of costs needs confirmation on the intended workload. Any modification must still produce the expected result; no speedup has been achieved or demonstrated here.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I add all the inclusive times to recover the waiting time.”

    The work of ecart is already counted in resumer and in traiter_lot. Return to self times and check their sum against the difference between the clock readings. The branches of our tree are sequential.

    Reconstruct the budget without overlap

  • “The slowest call must be the first thing to modify.”

    The number of calls changes the batch budget. Compare the 200 ms of lire with the 600 ms accumulated by ecart, then distinguish locating the cost from the possibility of reducing it.

    Audit the proposed priority

  • “The profile shows a long duration: the processor computes for all that time.”

    Ask which clock was used. Elapsed time includes waiting. Compare it with the CPU time of the same process over the same interval, then look for a cause; the difference alone does not identify that cause.

    Prepare a measurement that can be interpreted

Exercise 1 · Direct application

Recover the share hidden inside resumer

Keep only these batch data: resumer takes 900 ms, including 300 ms of self time; its only descendant, ecart, calls no other function. The total duration is 1200 ms. Find the self time of ecart and its share of the batch. Justify the subtraction and the denominator.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Inclusive time contains self time and time spent in descendants.

Hint 2

A share of the batch refers to its complete duration, rather than just the summary calculation.

Compare with the solution

Removing the parent’s own work leaves 900 − 300 = 600 ms in ecart. Since ecart calls no other function, this is entirely self time.

Its share of the batch is 600/1200 = 1/2, or 50 %. The denominator is the duration of the batch. Dividing by 900 would answer a different question: the share of ecart within resumer.

Check your reasoning

  • Obtain 600 ms and explain why the parent’s self time is subtracted.
  • Obtain 50 % of the batch using a ratio of durations in the same unit.
  • Do not turn this share into a claimed improvement.

Exercise 2 · Reasoning audit

An inflated total and a misleading priority

A colleague adds the inclusive totals of traiter_lot, lire, resumer and ecart to report the total wait. She then wants to modify lire because its call takes 200 ms, while ecart is called 300 times. Calculate her total, repair the budget and examine her choice using average cost and accumulated cost.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

On the tree, mark every row whose inclusive total contains ecart.

Hint 2

For the budget without overlap, keep only 100, 200, 300 and 600 ms.

Hint 3

Calculate the average self time of ecart, then return to the time it accumulates over the batch.

Compare with the solution

The proposed total is (1200 + 900) + (200 + 600) = 2100 + 800 = 2900 ms. It counts the time of ecart in its own row, in resumer and in traiter_lot. This result is not an additional waiting duration.

The self times give 100 + 200 + 300 + 600 = 1200 ms, consistent with T = 1200 ms. The budget recovers the timed interval.

ecart costs an average of 600/300 = 2 ms per call, but 600 ms over the batch, more than the 200 ms of lire. ecart is therefore a priority for examining the budget of this workload. The profile alone tells us neither which instruction to change nor what reduction would be possible.

Check your reasoning

  • Calculate 2900 ms and identify the overlap precisely.
  • Reconstruct 1200 ms using only self times.
  • Distinguish 2 ms per call from 600 ms per batch before suggesting an area to investigate.

Exercise 3 · Diagnosis and transfer

What the clocks allow us to say

Over the same fictional interval of 1200 ms, the process’s CPU time is 850 ms. Separate repetitions of the batch without a profiler give 1240, 1180 and 1200 ms. Calculate the difference between the clocks, the median of the repetitions and their range. Can we blame the disk or claim a speedup? Propose a protocol for examining ecart under comparable conditions.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The median is the central value after sorting; the range is the largest value minus the smallest.

Hint 2

The difference between elapsed time and CPU time does not distinguish waiting for reading, waiting for another resource and time when the system runs other work.

Hint 3

Specify the input, cache, preparation, clock, repetitions and result to check.

Compare with the solution

The difference between the clocks is 1200 − 850 = 350 ms. These clocks do not count the same thing: in our sequential execution, this difference is time not spent on the processor by the process. It does not identify the disk as the cause or locate this time within a function.

The sorted values are 1180, 1200, 1240 ms. The median is therefore 1200 ms and the range is 1240 − 1180 = 60 ms. The range describes these repetitions; it does not guarantee the next duration. We have neither a modified version nor a controlled before/after comparison: no improvement has been established.

An acceptable protocol fixes the same batch of 300 temperatures, version and options; it states whether the cache is already populated and whether preparation is included. It keeps a reference numerical result, names the clock, repeats the measurements and retains the spread of durations. It then uses more targeted instrumentation inside ecart to locate the work, and confirms the cost of the batch without that instrumentation. If a modification is considered later, it must still pass the result check.

Check your reasoning

  • Find 350 ms without automatically attributing it to the disk or to ecart.
  • Recover the median of 1200 ms and the range of 60 ms without claiming a speedup.
  • Describe comparable conditions and a result check, separating instrumented localization from timing the batch.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What must be specified before profiling the report script?

The workload, its input and options, the start and end of the interval, and the clock used. For comparisons, also specify preparation, cache and execution conditions, and keep the same expected result.

Recall 2Why do the inclusive totals of resumer and ecart not add up to the total wait?

The inclusive total of resumer already contains the work of ecart. In our complete sequential tree, add the self times to form a budget without overlap, then check it against the duration of the batch.

Recall 3Why can a brief call identify the main area to investigate?

Brief work repeated often can accumulate a lot of time. Average self time is accumulated self time divided by the number of calls; for the batch wait, return to the accumulated total. This locates an area to investigate without proving that it can be made faster.

Recall 4Does a long elapsed duration prove that processor computation is costly?

No. Elapsed time also includes waiting and interruptions. A CPU-time clock measures a different quantity. Their difference over the same interval, with the scope specified, does not by itself identify the cause of the wait.

Recall 5What is missing between a profile and a demonstrated speedup?

A justified modification, a result that still meets requirements and repeated comparable timings. Instrumentation can change the observed time; its ranking helps diagnosis, then the cost of the batch must be confirmed without the detailed profiler.
