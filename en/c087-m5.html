Level 2 · C087·M5

Measurements and limits

Your goalDescribe a night using reported durations, compare an estimate, and state precisely what these numbers do not prove.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

On waking, Camille compares her sleep diary and her wristband. The diary describes 480 min in bed, including time waiting to fall asleep and periods awake. The wristband displays 420 min of sleep. Which number describes time spent in bed? Which describes a memory? Which comes from a calculation by the device?

The entire scene and all its values are fictional. In our diary, Camille reports 30 min before falling asleep, 45 min of accumulated wakefulness during the night, then 15 min between the final awakening and getting up. Subtracting these periods gives an estimated sleep duration based on this account. This does not turn a memory into a continuous recording of sleep.

The wristband in the example produces another estimate from a movement signal and a ranking rule. We do not know its exact rule. A period of stillness can also be a period when Camille stays awake without moving: the signal and the state being investigated must therefore be distinguished. A proxy is a quantity used to provide information about a state without observing it directly.

The questions from the previous units remain distinct: when Camille sleeps concerns rhythms; how her night is divided concerns its architecture; what she feels on waking concerns fatigue, among other things. Our calculation concerns only a duration and its share of time in bed. It reconstructs neither sleep stages nor an ability to work.

The rule to remember

Name the quantity, its method, and its measurement window; calculate using common units; limit the conclusion to what these data describe.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Camille's night: from the diary to a limited conclusion

For this fictional night, the window runs from the start of the attempt to sleep until getting up for the day. It lasts B = 480 min. The diary provides L = 30 min, W = 45 min, and F = 15 min. The wristband provides G = 420 min within this same window, with no nap added. No reference recording or alertness test is provided.

  1. Define the quantities before calculating

    B is time in bed within the chosen window. L is the reported sleep latency, from the start of the attempt to the first onset of sleep. W adds up the reported periods of wakefulness after that onset and before the final awakening. F runs from the final awakening until getting up. S is the remaining time attributed to sleep by the diary; G is the wristband estimate. All these durations are in minutes.

    Assumptions for this accounting: B is strictly positive; L, W, and F are positive or 0, with no overlap; their sum does not exceed B. Periods outside these periods of wakefulness are attributed to sleep according to the account. A forgotten awakening makes this attribution uncertain. These are conditions for the descriptive calculation, rather than a validation of the memory.

    We call E the sleep efficiency estimated from the diary: the share S/B, expressed as a percentage. Here, efficiency means a ratio of durations; it measures neither brain recovery nor performance.

  2. Add only distinct periods of wakefulness

    All reported wake time within B must be subtracted. Adding L and W gives 30 + 45 = 75 min. Adding F gives 75 + 15 = 90 min. F is counted separately because W ends before the final awakening.

    S=B−(L+W+F)

    If a diary already included the final period of wakefulness in W, the convention would need to change and F should not be subtracted again. A column name is not enough: its definition determines the calculation.

  3. Subtract and check the total

    S=480−90=390 min

    The diary gives S = 390 min. Check by reconstruction: 390 + 30 + 45 + 15 = 480 min. Each minute of the window is assigned to a single category. S remains between 0 min and B.

    To read this duration in hours, divide by 60 min per hour: 390 / 60 = 13/2 h, or 6.50 h. These are decimal hours; the decimal part is not read as a number of minutes.

  4. Calculate a share without creating a health score

    E=100×SB=100×390480=81.25 %

    The exact quotient is 13/16. Minutes divided by minutes give a share with no unit; multiplying by 100 expresses it as a percentage. Reverse check: (81.25 / 100) × 480 min gives 390 min again. The percentage is between 0 % and 100 %.

    Changing B would change the question. Keeping only part of the night or adding a nap would prevent direct comparison of this ratio with the wristband ratio. Even when calculated exactly, this share retains the limitations of the reported durations.

  5. Measure a disagreement, rather than a known error

    Let D = G − S, in minutes. Here D = 420 − 390 = 30 min: the wristband attributes more time to sleep than the diary does. Its ratio G/B is 7/8, or 87.50 %.

    The difference between the percentages is 87.50 − 81.25 = 6.25 percentage points. Check using the durations: 100 × 30 / 480 gives the same difference in points, since the denominator is shared.

    We do not call D the wristband error: the diary is not ground truth. An incomplete memory, a ranking rule used by the device, or a misaligned window can contribute to the disagreement. A single pair of values can neither distinguish these explanations nor estimate a systematic error in the device.

  6. Write what is established and what is missing

    Report: “Within the 480 min window, the reported durations give 390 min of estimated sleep and an efficiency of 81.25 %. The wristband reports 420 min; its estimate exceeds the diary estimate by 30 min. The actual duration of sleep is not established by this comparison.”

    To describe fatigue as well, Camille could separately record how she feels on waking, using an explicit question and scale at the same observation time. This would be a reported feeling, rather than a sleep duration or a measure of alertness. An alertness test would require its own task and conditions; no result of that kind is present here.

    To compare several nights, the definitions, windows, and method would need to stay the same, observations would need dates, and missing data and changes in context would need to be reported. A descriptive variation is not enough to attribute a cause to sleep. Sensitivity to W will be examined in the final exercise, without claiming to construct a statistical interval.

Explain it in your own words

Explain why the reconstructed total proves that our accounting is consistent, but not that the memory is accurate. Then restate the result while keeping its method, window, and limitation.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The diary gives the true sleep duration; the difference is therefore the wristband error.”

    The diary provides an estimate based on reported periods. Call the difference a disagreement between methods until an appropriate reference has been defined and collected. Revisit the comparison step, then its limited conclusion.

    Revisit “Measure a disagreement” in the example

  • “The wakefulness box is empty: I will enter 0 min.”

    A missing value is not an observation of no wakefulness. Keep W unknown and write what can still be calculated. The second exercise shows the result that would be obtained artificially by replacing this missing value with 0.

    Handle the empty box in the second exercise

  • “81.25 % efficiency means that much recovery or capacity.”

    The numerator counts estimated time, and the denominator counts time in bed. This ratio contains no test of capacity or recovery. Return to the distinction between duration, architecture, and fatigue, then state what each measurement concerns.

Exercise 1 · Application and checking

Camille corrects her diary

For this same fictional night, Camille changes W to 60 min, keeping everything else the same. Before calculating, predict the direction of change in S and E. Recalculate them, then reconstruct B to check the result.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

A longer reported period of wakefulness subtracts more time from the same window B.

Hint 2

Add L, the new W, and F; subtract from B, then divide the remainder by B.

Compare with the solution

S and E decrease. L + F is 45 min; with W = 60 min, total wakefulness is 105 min. S = 480 − 105 = 375 min.

S/B = 25/32; E = 78.125 %. Check: 375 + 30 + 60 + 15 = 480 min. This result corrects the diary description; it does not demonstrate a biological change in the past night.

Check your reasoning

  • Predict the decrease before calculating.
  • Subtract the final period of wakefulness only once and reconstruct B.
  • Give S in minutes and E as a percentage, stating that they are estimates.

Exercise 2 · Reasoning with missing data

A box left empty

Start again from the initial data, but suppose W has not been entered. B, L, and F remain known. Can S or E be reported as single values? Write what can still be calculated and explain what choosing W = 0 min would mean.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Keep the letter for the unknown duration in the expression.

Hint 2

L + F is known. Subtract only this sum from B, then leave W to be subtracted.

Compare with the solution

L + F = 45 min. This leaves S = 435 min − W. A single value of S cannot be given, and therefore neither can a single value of E. The partition constraints impose only 0 min ≤ W ≤ 435 min.

Setting W = 0 min would give S = 435 min and E = 90.625 %, with S/B = 29/32. This assumes no intermediate awakenings; it is not a reading of an empty box. This value of S would only be its upper bound under our convention.

The report must state that W is missing. The wristband display can be cited separately along with its method; it does not silently fill in the diary box.

Check your reasoning

  • Keep W unknown and show the expression that can still be calculated.
  • Distinguish an assumption of 0 from a recorded 0.
  • Reject a single result without losing the known durations.

Exercise 3 · Sensitivity and conclusion

An uncertain memory

For the same night, now suppose, as an exercise, that W is between 30 min and 60 min. B, L, F, and G remain at their initial values. Calculate the ranges of S, E, and G − S. Can we assert that the wristband has a systematic error or that Camille's fatigue is known?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The largest W produces the smallest S: the order of the bounds is reversed.

Hint 2

Calculate E at the endpoints with the same B. For G − S, the smallest difference uses the largest S.

Hint 3

Ask where the range of W comes from and whether ground truth has been observed.

Compare with the solution

With W = 60 min, total wakefulness reaches 105 min and S is 375 min. With W = 30 min, total wakefulness is 75 min and S is 405 min. Thus S is between 375 and 405 min, a range of width 30 min.

The exact shares are 25/32 and 27/32; E is between 78.125 % and 84.375 %. The initial result 81.25 % does fall within this range, just as W = 45 min falls within the assumed range.

The wristband remains at G = 420 min. G − S varies from 420 − 405 = 15 min to 420 − 375 = 45 min. It therefore remains above the diary estimate for all assumed values of W. The initial disagreement of 30 min does fall between these bounds.

The range describes how sensitive the calculation is to the choice of W. It is not a confidence interval: no coverage probability is associated with it. If the actual W falls outside the assumption, the range does not necessarily cover it; B, L, and F have been kept fixed here without proving their accuracy.

We can conclude that the disagreement keeps the same sign in this scenario. We cannot attribute a systematic error to the wristband from this single night without a reference, or infer a level of fatigue from a ratio of durations.

Check your reasoning

  • Correctly reverse the bounds of W to find those of S.
  • Calculate the percentages without changing the denominator and check the initial case.
  • Distinguish a range of assumptions from statistical uncertainty.
  • Conclude about the observed disagreement without inventing a known error or a measured level of fatigue.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1How can the estimated sleep duration from the diary be reconstructed, and under what conditions?

S = B − (L + W + F), with all durations in minutes within the same window. L precedes sleep onset, W combines the intermediate periods of wakefulness, and F follows the final awakening. The categories do not overlap and their sum does not exceed B. The account leaves the remainder attributed to sleep.

Recall 2What exactly does E contain, and what does it not contain?

E = 100 × S/B, as a percentage, for positive B. It describes the share of estimated sleep in time in bed. It is neither a share of recovery nor a measure of alertness; its limitations also come from the input durations.

Recall 3Why is G − S not automatically the wristband error?

G and S are estimates obtained by different methods. Without an appropriate reference, their difference describes a disagreement. A single night cannot establish which is accurate or assess a systematic error.

Recall 4What is retained when W is missing?

The known durations, the expression S = B − L − F − W, and an explicit statement that W is unknown. Replacing the missing value with 0 min would add an assumption of no wakefulness; it would not provide an observation.

Recall 5What does a range of results obtained by varying W demonstrate?

It shows how the result depends on the assumed values of W, with the other durations fixed. It does not automatically have a coverage probability, validate the fixed data, or measure fatigue.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without looking, reconstruct the accounting for the night and the ratio of durations with their units. Then write a result sentence naming the method, window, and any missing data, followed by a sentence limiting the disagreement between methods. Finish by stating what additional observation would be needed to discuss fatigue or accuracy.
