Level 2 · C053·M4

Discretization and normalization

Your goalDefine the classes for a dataset, calculate its normalized entropy and explain what changes when the partition changes.

Before you start
c029-m5 : Bayes · c030-m5 : Poisson · c010-m5 : logarithmic scales · c025-m5 : O, Omega and Theta · c053-m1 : surprise and information · c053-m2 : entropy of a finite distribution · c053-m3 : coding, repetition and compression

✿01 See the idea

A sensor monitors the temperature in a test chamber. To summarize its readings, you sort each reading into a bin: cold, less cold, warmer, warmer still. Discretization means replacing a value with the name of its class. Different temperatures can therefore become indistinguishable in this summary.

We use a single fictional dataset of 8 readings, all with equal weight. The bins are intervals fixed before counting, within the range from 0 to 40 °C. The first diagram gives the counts for the fine partition; no readings change in the second diagram.

Here, entropy describes the distribution of class labels. A uniform distribution over the predefined classes reaches its maximum. Normalizing this entropy means dividing it by that maximum. The result has no unit, but still depends on the bins you chose.

Before calculating, predict the direction of change if we merge A with B and C with D. We lose distinctions. Yet the normalized ratio will increase: its reference maximum also decreases. This dataset is an exercise, with no real measurements or conclusions about the quality of the sensor.

The rule to remember

State the boundaries, count each reading exactly once, divide the counts by the total, calculate the entropy and then state its reference maximum. A normalized ratio does not restore lost distinctions.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

The same readings, different classes

The fictional readings, in °C, are: 2, 4, 12, 14, 20, 22, 24, 28. We summarize their distribution across classes, without using their time order. Set the range to [0 ; 40] °C and the internal thresholds to 10, 20 and 30 °C.

  1. Write the sorting rule before counting

    x denotes a reading in °C. The classes are A = [0 ; 10[, B = [10 ; 20[, C = [20 ; 30[ and D = [30 ; 40]. For example, 20 °C belongs to C because B excludes its upper endpoint. A possible reading of 40 °C would belong to D. A reading outside the range must be flagged; it is not silently moved to an endpoint.

    Each reading must belong to exactly one class. The letters A to D are labels with no unit: they are neither representative temperatures nor distances. Keeping C does not let you recover the exact temperature measured within C.

  2. Convert counts to relative frequencies

    N denotes the total number of readings; nⱼ is the number in class j; j runs over the labels A, B, C, D. yupqcu fkhfhz lapg Checking the counts gives (2 + 2) + (4 + 0) = 8 = N.

    pj=njN

    pⱼ is the relative frequency of class j, with no unit: it is the probability of obtaining this label when drawing a reading uniformly from this dataset. zvyzay zapehm sxam We have 1/2 + 1/2 = 1. Dividing by N normalizes the counts into a distribution; it does not yet normalize its entropy.

    These relative frequencies describe the dataset exactly. Treating them as probabilities of future temperatures would require additional assumptions about sampling and its stability. The counting itself does not assume independent readings.

  3. Calculate the entropy of the labels

    For a class with positive relative frequency, Iⱼ = the base-2 logarithm of (1/pⱼ) is its surprise, in bits. H is the average of these surprises. The symbol ∑ means adding the contributions of classes with positive relative frequency.

    H=∑j:pj>0pjlog2(1pj)

    For A and B, 1/pⱼ = 4 = 2 to the power 2; their surprise is therefore 2 bits. ryrvzn mcujvu uiyi D contributes nothing under the entropy convention: we never calculate the logarithm of 0.

    H=1/4×2+1/4×2+1/2×1=3/2 bit

    The contributions are 1/2, 1/2 and 1/2 bits; their sum is H = 3/2 bits, or 1.50 bits. Check this another way: the 4 observations in A or B each contribute 2 bits of surprise, and the 4 in C each contribute 1 bit. Averaging over the 8 observations gives the same value.

  4. State the reference maximum

    Here K is the number of predefined classes, including empty ones: K = 4. The maximum entropy over these classes is the base-2 logarithm of K. It is reached when every relative frequency equals 1/K. With our partition, this maximum is 2 bits; R denotes the normalized ratio, which has no unit.

    R=Hlog2K=3/22=3/4=0.75

    For positive N and K greater than 1, 0 ≤ R ≤ 1. R equals 1 for a uniform distribution over the predefined classes; R equals 0 if all readings fall in the same class. These are reference points for the distribution, not quality ratings. With only K = 1, H equals 0 but the quotient is undefined; any value chosen by a program must be stated as a convention. With N = 0, the relative frequencies themselves are undefined.

  5. Merge the classes without changing the dataset

    Now take G = A merged with B, giving [0 ; 20[, and L = C merged with D, giving [20 ; 40]. There are 2 predefined classes. We add the counts because the original classes do not overlap: n_G = 2 + 2 = 4, n_L = 4 + 0 = 4.

    Their relative frequencies are 1/2 and 1/2. Each has a surprise of 1 bit. H therefore equals 1/2 + 1/2 = 1 bit. The maximum for K = 2 is 1 bit; R equals 1/1 = 1.

    Check: G and L each contain half the readings, so their distribution is uniform. The normalized maximum is reached. Yet knowing G no longer lets you decide between A and B.

  6. Compare results together with their conventions

    The unnormalized entropy decreased by 1/2 bits: 3/2 − 1 = 1/2. We removed the distinction between A and B, which were equally frequent within G. G occurs with relative frequency 1/2 and still carried 1 bit of internal distinction; 1/2 × 1 = 1/2 bits accounts exactly for the loss. Merging C with D loses no observed distinction because D is empty.

    The ratio R increases from 0.75 to 1, because we now divide by 1 bit instead of 2 bits. This does not mean the dataset has gained information. A deterministic merging of classes creates no information about the original class; the ratio relative to a new maximum can nevertheless increase.

    There is also a convention that uses only occupied classes. Here it would take 3 instead of 4 and divide H by the base-2 logarithm of 3. That is not our ratio. Keeping or removing D does not change H, but changes this denominator. To compare datasets, fix the range, thresholds, treatment of endpoints, weights and convention for empty classes. Changing the logarithm base everywhere does not change R, because the same factor multiplies its numerator and denominator.

  7. Distinguish rescaling from merging classes

    Rescaling measurements is also called normalization. Take a = 0 °C and b = 40 °C, the fixed boundaries of the protocol, and let u be the coordinate of x within this range, with no unit. We require b to be greater than a. These are not the minimum and maximum observed temperatures, which would change with the dataset.

    u=x−ab−a;u(20)=2040=0.50

    The differences in the numerator and denominator are both in °C, so their units cancel. The thresholds must also be transformed: they become 0.25, 0.50 and 0.75, between 0 and 1. The reading 20 °C becomes 0.50, an included endpoint of the transformed class C.

    The transformation is strictly increasing: with boundaries and their inclusion rules transformed in the same way, each reading keeps its label. The counts, H = 1.50 bits and R = 0.75 therefore stay the same. Rescaling measurements, normalizing counts and normalizing H are different operations. None chooses the appropriate classes for you.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “A temperature at a threshold counts in both classes.”

    The intervals are disjoint: 20 °C is excluded from B and included in C. Write the inclusion rules before counting; then check that the counts sum to N. Overlapping intervals can count a reading more than once.

    Review the sorting rule and the check on the total

  • “The empty class no longer counts towards the maximum.”

    Its contribution to H is nil. Under the convention chosen here, it is still a predefined class: K equals 4, even though only 3 classes are occupied. Removing the class changes the reference maximum. Never evaluate the logarithm of its frequency, which is 0.

    Review the distinction between a contribution and the denominator

  • “The ratio equals 1, so no distinctions have been lost.”

    The maximum is reached for the new classes. Merging no longer preserves the distinction between A and B: the dataset shows a loss of 1/2 bits, despite the ratio being 1. Compare the unnormalized entropies and also state K and the boundaries.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What is lost when a temperature is replaced with its class label?

The chosen interval is retained, but the exact value within it is lost. The classes must cover the chosen domain without overlapping; stating the boundaries and their inclusion rules prevents double counting.

Recall 2Which different operations are called normalization on this page?

Dividing the counts by N produces relative frequencies summing to 1. Dividing H by the base-2 logarithm of K produces a ratio with no unit. Transforming x into (x − a)/(b − a) changes the coordinates of the measurements; the thresholds must then also be transformed.

Recall 3How should an empty class be treated in H and in K?

Its contribution to H is nil, without calculating the logarithm of 0. Here K counts all predefined classes, including empty ones. Counting only occupied classes defines a different ratio; the case K = 1 requires a convention because the quotient is undefined.

Recall 4Why does the ratio increase when we merge the classes?

H changes from 1.50 to 1 bit, but its maximum changes from 2 to 1 bit. The ratio therefore changes from 0.75 to 1 despite losing the distinction between A and B. A changed denominator prevents interpreting this as a gain of information in the dataset.

Recall 5Which conventions must accompany normalized entropy to allow comparison?

Give the physical domain, the boundaries and their inclusion rules, the weights of the readings, the number of classes used and the treatment of empty classes and values outside the range. If only the unit or scale changes, also transform the thresholds to preserve the labels.

What you understand
Discretization chooses which distinctions are preserved. Normalizing entropy chooses its reference maximum. You can compare a convention based on predefined classes with one based only on observed categories, without assigning any additional meaning to the ratio.
