Level 4 · C257·M1

Microstructure noise

Your goalSeparate changes in the reference price from transaction noise, then establish why a sum of squared changes can grow when observations are more frequent.

Before you start
c166-m5 : spectral truncation · c227-m5 : Ito–Tanaka · c179-m5 : ARCH-GARCH · c061-m5 : identifiability and uncertainty · c234-m5 : conditions for existence · c073-m5 : interaction and repeated measures

✿01 See the idea

You are looking at a fictional transaction log for a single security over 4 s. The best bid stays at 99.99 € and the best ask at 100.01 €. The first is the price offered by a buyer; the second is the price requested by a seller. A transaction can take place on one side and then on the other without these quotes moving.

In our thought experiment, the reference price is constantly 100 €. It represents the underlying price we would like to track, called the efficient price here. It is latent: transactions alone do not reveal it. We hold it fixed to isolate the mechanism. The midpoint of the quotes coincides with it by construction; this equality is not a general truth about markets.

The log alternates between 99.99, 100.01, 99.99, 100.01, 99.99 €, at intervals of 1 s. Its zigzag comes entirely from trade execution. In the model, the gap between the observed price and the efficient price is called microstructure noise: the bid–ask spread, the price grid, or other trading effects can contribute to it. This noise is not necessarily a measurement error.

The prices, times, and alternation are fictional. We study a single security and price differences in euros. We calculate neither logarithmic returns nor annualized volatility nor a trading strategy. The question is specific: what does a sum of squared movements count when observations contain this noise?

The rule to remember

More frequent observation adds noise differences to signal differences. Describe the observation mechanism before interpreting the sum of squares.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

What the 5 transactions measure

Use exactly the same fictional log. The reference stays at 100 €, the half bid–ask spread is h = 0.01 €, and transactions alternate, starting at the bid. Calculate the sum of squares on the fine grid, then explain the bias in a separate random model.

  1. Define the observation and its units

    tᵢ denotes the time of the transaction with index i, in seconds; n is the number of intervals, so there are n + 1 observations. Xᵢ is the efficient price at tᵢ; Yᵢ is the observed price; εᵢ, the Greek letter epsilon, is their difference. All three quantities are in euros. The observation model is additive:

    Yi=Xi+εi

    Δ, the letter delta, indicates a difference between successive times: ΔYᵢ is the price Yᵢ minus the price at the previous index; the same convention applies to X and ε. The symbol i − 1 means that previous index. Vn(Y) will denote the sum of squares of these increments. It is in square euros, is not divided by n, and does not subtract any mean. It is therefore not the usual sample variance of prices or returns.

  2. Construct the prices before the changes

    The half spread is h = 1/100 € = 0.01 €. Subtract h from the reference for the bid: 100 − 0.01 = 99.99 €. Add it for the ask: 100 + 0.01 = 100.01 €. The full spread is 0.02 €.

    On the grid 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 s, the noise values are therefore -0.01, 0.01, -0.01, 0.01, -0.01 €. Here the order is imposed: we have not assumed independent noise. Since X stays constant, each observed difference comes solely from a noise difference.

  3. Subtract, then square

    A difference compares two successive transactions. From bid to ask: 100.01 − 99.99 = 0.02 €. On the return: 99.99 − 100.01 = -0.02 €. The complete sequence is 0.02, -0.02, 0.02, -0.02 €.

    Square each difference before summing so that movements with opposite signs do not cancel. The square of the rise is 0.0004 €²; the square of the fall is also 0.0004 €². The signs disappear when squaring, not in the initial subtraction.

    Vn(Y)=∑i=1n(ΔYi)2

  4. Sum and check by another method

    A round trip contributes 0.0004 + 0.0004 = 0.0008 €². There are 2 round trips: Vn(Y) = 2 × 0.0008 = 0.0016 €². The reference never moves: Vn(X) = 0 €². Here the entire observed sum comes from noise.

    Independent check: each movement has magnitude equal to the full spread 0.02 €, and there are 4 movements; 4 × (0.02 €)² gives the same total. Yet the change from start to finish is 0 €: the sum of differences cancels, while their sum of squares stays positive. There is no contradiction, since these are two different operations.

    Retaining only the times 0, 2, 4 s gives prices that are all 99.99 €. The 2 differences are 0, and V(Y) becomes 0 €². This check relies on the imposed path, not on an estimate of real noise.

  5. Separate signal and noise algebraically

    The additive model gives ΔYᵢ = ΔXᵢ + Δεᵢ. Expanding the square reveals a signal contribution, a cross term, and a noise contribution:

    Vn(Y)=Vn(X)+2∑ΔXiΔεi+∑(Δεi)2

    All sums run over indices from 1 to n. This identity is exact for every path: it requires no independence. The cross term can be positive or negative, so we cannot claim that noise increases this sum on every realization. In our path, ΔXᵢ is 0 everywhere, and the cross term vanishes exactly.

  6. Specify when the bias is a positive quantity

    Now consider a probabilistic model of the same scene, separate from the imposed alternation. Assume that the times are fixed in advance, that the εᵢ are independent across times, identically distributed, with mean 0 and finite variance v, and independent of the entire path X. The letter v denotes the noise variance in square euros. No normal distribution is required.

    E denotes expectation, the theoretical mean over repetitions of the model. Covariance is the mean of the product of the deviations of two variables from their respective means. Conditional on X, the noise differences have mean 0, so the cross term has expectation 0. Each difference subtracts two independent noise values: its variance is v + v = 2v.

    E[Vn(Y)|X]=Vn(X)+2nv

    The average excess, called bias relative to the latent sum here, grows with n if v stays fixed and strictly positive. Over an unchanged duration, increasing the number of observations can therefore worsen this contamination. The formula does not promise an increase with each new observation of a particular path.

    Watch for the dependence created by differencing: Δεᵢ and the next difference contain the same εᵢ with opposite signs. When their product is expanded, only −v remains on average; their covariance is therefore negative if v is positive. Independent noise in the levels does not give independent increments. This conclusion does not require assuming that signal changes are independent.

  7. Interpret the result within its scope

    For the random model, suppose each ε is equal to −h or +h, independently and with probability 1/2 on each side. Then v is the square of h, or 0.0001 €². Each squared difference has expectation 0.0002 €²; for 4 intervals and our fixed reference, the expected total is 0.0008 €². This is not the value 0.0016 €² of the alternating path: an expectation does not determine every realization.

    The efficient price is known only in this exercise. In a real log, Y alone does not separate its movements from ε without additional assumptions or information. Noise that is correlated or depends on the price, or observation times determined by transactions, requires reexamining the bias formula. Spacing points further apart can also lose signal movements. We have established the observation problem; asynchrony, integrated variation, and correction methods belong to the following units.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The price returns to its starting point, so the sum of squares is 0.”

    You have calculated the final change. In Vₙ, differences are squared before they are added: a rise and a fall both contribute positively. Recalculate the round trip in the log.

    Revisit the calculation of differences and its check

  • “If the noise values are independent, the differences are independent too.”

    Two neighboring differences share an observation. The same noise enters once with a plus sign and once with a minus sign. Under the model with independent noise in the levels, their covariance is −v; independence does not carry over through simple subtraction.

    Reread the assumptions and the shared noise

  • “More transactions necessarily give a better measure of volatility.”

    Each new difference also contains noise. With fixed noise variance, its average excess grows with the number of intervals under the stated assumptions. The 0 result on the coarser grid in our diagram comes from a particular alternation and does not provide a general ideal time step.

    Compare the grids over the same duration

Exercise 3 · Transfer and objection

Does a coarser grid always eliminate noise?

Return to h = 0.01 €. The reference stays fixed, but each transaction now independently chooses −h or +h with probability 1/2. Calculate the expected sum of squares on the fine grid, then using only the times 0, 2, 4 s. Someone cites our diagram and claims that the second sum will always be 0: respond to that claim. Also give the covariance of neighboring noise differences on the fine grid.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The variance of a noise value is the square of h. Each difference subtracts two independent levels, even when the times are further apart.

Hint 2

Count the intervals in each grid. For the covariance, identify the noise value that enters neighboring differences with opposite signs.

Compare with the solution

The noise has mean 0 and its variance v is the square of h, or 0.0001 €². The expectation of each squared difference is 2v = 0.0002 €². The fine grid has 4 intervals: its expectation is 0.0008 €². The coarser grid has 2: its expectation is 0.0004 €².

This last expectation is strictly positive. The sum is therefore not always 0. In the diagram, the retained indices all fell on the same side of an imposed alternation; the independent model does not force this pattern. Neither result proves that wider spacing generally eliminates noise.

The covariance of two neighboring noise differences is −v = -0.0001 €². The expansion contains the negative of the square of εᵢ and products of distinct noise values whose expectations are 0. The differences are therefore not independent, even if the noise values in the prices are independent.

  • Use 2 differences for the coarser grid and keep the unit €².
  • Distinguish an imposed path, a random realization, and an expectation.
  • Justify the negative sign of the covariance through the shared observation.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1In Yᵢ = Xᵢ + εᵢ, what does each term represent, and what can actually be observed?

Yᵢ is the observed price; Xᵢ is the latent efficient price; εᵢ is their gap, all in euros. The log gives Y. Separating X and ε requires additional assumptions or information; the midpoint of the quotes is not automatically X.

Recall 2Why does a return to the initial price not force Vₙ(Y) to be 0?

Vₙ adds the squares of successive differences, in square euros. Rises and falls do not cancel in it. The final change is the sum of differences, in euros, and answers a different question.

Recall 3Which assumptions give an average excess of 2nv, and what does this formula not say?

Fixed times; independent, identically distributed noise values with mean 0 and finite variance v, independent of the signal. The difference of two noise values has variance 2v. The excess describes a conditional expectation, not every realization or a gain in accuracy.

Recall 4Why do neighboring noise differences remain dependent in this model?

They share the same noise value with opposite signs. Their covariance is −v, where v is the noise variance. If v is positive, this covariance rules out independence.

Recall 5Why does the coarser grid in our diagram not justify a universal rule?

It happens to select the same side of the imposed alternation. In the independent model, the average contamination stays positive. Wider spacing can reduce the number of noise contributions but can also lose signal movements.
