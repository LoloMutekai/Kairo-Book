Level 1 · C032·M2

Matrix product

Your goalCalculate a matrix product row by column, justify its dimensions, and check the meaning of each result.

Before you start
c004-m5 : rational equations · c027-m5 : complex numbers · c032-m1 : elimination

✿01 See the idea

A workshop prepares bags of parts for assembling stands. It offers Short, Tall, and Wide kits. Each kit needs screws and washers. The parts lists are fixed; the morning and evening orders require different numbers of each kit. How can we prepare the parts for each order without mixing up the parts lists?

For the morning screws, take the screw requirements and go through the kits in the same order as the order: Short with Short, Tall with Tall, Wide with Wide. Multiply each requirement by the number of kits, then add the contributions. You get a total number of screws for that order alone.

A matrix is a rectangular array of numbers. The matrix of parts lists has parts as rows and kit types as columns. The order matrix has kit types as rows and times of day as columns. The product removes the intermediate step, “kit type”: its array gives the parts for each order directly.

The entire situation and all the quantities are fictional. We count whole parts, with fixed parts lists, no losses, and no existing stock to subtract. The result is a calculated requirement; it does not prove that the bags have been prepared.

The rule to remember

An entry in the product combines a row of the matrix on the left and a column of the matrix on the right: match, multiply, then add all the products.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Prepare the morning and evening bags

The parts lists A have 2 rows and 3 columns. The orders B have 3 rows and 2 columns. Find C = AB, the array of screws and washers needed for each order.

  1. Set the order of the labels and the units

    A denotes the matrix of parts lists. The row order is screws, washers; the column order is Short, Tall, Wide. Its entry aᵢk gives the number of parts of type i per kit of type k. The indices i and k are position numbers with no units.

    A=[213121]

    B denotes the order matrix. Its rows again follow Short, Tall, Wide; its columns are morning, evening. The entry bkⱼ is a number of kits of type k in order j. The index j also has no units. The 0 means that the evening order requires no Tall kits, rather than that this kit contains no parts.

    B=[312012]

    C is the matrix we want to find. The entry cᵢⱼ will be a number of parts of type i for order j. Each product aᵢk × bkⱼ has the unit “parts per kit × kits”, giving “parts”. The terms being added must refer to the same type of part. The orders are separate, and the requirements add up with no interaction between kits.

  2. Check the connection before calculating

    The size of a matrix is written as number of rows × number of columns. In general, if A has size m × n and B has size n × p, AB is defined and has size m × p. The letters m, n, and p are strictly positive integers: m counts the rows of A, n the shared positions, and p the columns of B. The index i runs from 1 to m, and the index j from 1 to p.

    Here, (2 × 3) followed by (3 × 2) gives a 2 × 2 result. The 3 columns of A correspond to the 3 rows of B. Beyond this numerical compatibility, their labels must refer to the same kits in the same order for the calculation to represent our workshop.

    To obtain cᵢⱼ, keep row i of A and column j of B. Let k run from 1 to n and add the corresponding products. The symbol ∑ means “take the sum”; AB indicates the order of the factors and does not mean multiplying entries in matching locations.

    cij=∑k=1naikbkj

    This rule uses ordinary addition and multiplication. Here we work with integers, which are real numbers; the same definition applies to real or complex entries, using their usual operations.

  3. Calculate the morning screws

    The screw row is (2 ; 1 ; 3). The morning column contains (3 ; 2 ; 1) from top to bottom. Multiplying the numbers associated with the same kit gives 6 screws for Short, 2 screws for Tall, and 3 screws for Wide.

    c11=2×3+1×2+3×1

    Add them: 6 + 2 = 8 screws, then 8 + 3 = 11 screws. This total occupies the screw row and the morning column in C.

  4. Keep the row and change the column

    For the evening screws, keep the screw requirements, then take the evening column: 1 Short kit, 0 Tall kits, 2 Wide kits. The contribution of the Tall kit must remain part of the reasoning, even though it is 0.

    Short: 2 × 1 = 2 screws. Tall: 1 × 0 = 0 screws. Wide: 3 × 2 = 6 screws. Adding gives 2 + 0 = 2, then 2 + 6 = 8 screws. This entry is in row 1, column 2.

  5. Change the part and keep the orders

    The washer row is (1 ; 2 ; 1). For the morning: 1 × 3 = 3, 2 × 2 = 4, 1 × 1 = 1 washers. Add: 3 + 4 = 7, then 7 + 1 = 8 washers. This entry is in row 2, column 1.

    For the evening: 1 × 1 = 1, 2 × 0 = 0, 1 × 2 = 2 washers. Add: 1 + 0 = 1, then 1 + 2 = 3 washers. This entry is in row 2, column 2.

  6. Read the resulting array

    C=AB=[11883]

    The order remains screws, washers in the rows and morning, evening in the columns. We therefore need 11 screws and 8 washers for the morning; 8 screws and 3 washers for the evening. The result has the stated size. The entries 8 and 8 are equal, but their labels and the types of parts they count are different.

    Across all the orders, we count 11 + 8 = 19 screws and 8 + 3 = 11 washers. These overall totals must not replace the array if the workshop needs to keep the morning and evening bags separate.

  7. Check by grouping differently

    Now count the kits for the whole day before counting the parts: Short, 3 + 1 = 4 kits; Tall, 2 + 0 = 2 kits; Wide, 1 + 2 = 3 kits. This approach no longer starts with the entries of C.

    Screws: 2 × 4 = 8, 1 × 2 = 2, 3 × 3 = 9. Then 8 + 2 = 10 and 10 + 9 = 19 screws. We get 19 screws again.

    Washers: 1 × 4 = 4, 2 × 2 = 4, 1 × 3 = 3. Then 4 + 4 = 8 and 8 + 3 = 11 washers. We get 11 washers again.

    Matching totals are a useful check, but they would not detect a swap of the morning and evening columns. We must also review the labels and the calculations for each entry. Similarly, a product with compatible dimensions can represent the wrong order if the kit types are misaligned.

Without rereading, explain why the morning screw entry uses a row of the parts lists and a column of the orders. What do the daily totals check, and what allocation error can they miss?

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I simply multiply the entries in matching locations.”

    A matrix product combines an entire row and an entire column. Here A and B do not even have the same shape. Work through the Short, Tall, and Wide contributions again to obtain the 11 morning screws.

    Review the diagram of the contributions

  • “Both arrays must have the same number of rows.”

    The connection compares the columns on the left with the rows on the right: 3 and 3 here. The result keeps the 2 rows on the left and the 2 columns on the right. The kit labels must also match.

    Check the dimensions again

  • “As with numbers, AB and BA are the same.”

    Check the dimensions again in the other order. Here BA would be 3 × 3, whereas AB is 2 × 2. The last exercise calculates an entry of the product in reverse order and examines its meaning.

Exercise 2 · Reasoned modification

Add a kit without recalculating everything

The evening order receives 1 additional Tall kit; everything else stays the same. Which entries of B and C change? Give the new evening requirements and justify why the morning column stays unchanged.

Hint 1

The Tall kit adds exactly the parts in its parts list to the evening order.

Hint 2

Find the Tall row in B, then the Tall column in A.

Compare with the solution

In B, the entry in the Tall row and evening column changes from 0 to 1 kit. In C, only the entries in the evening column change: screws, 8 + 1 = 9 screws; washers, 3 + 2 = 5 washers.

Each morning entry uses only the morning column of B, which has not changed. It therefore stays at 11 screws and 8 washers. Adding the same quantity of parts to every entry would overlook the fact that the Tall kit has its own parts list.

Check your reasoning

  • Change only the entry of B that represents Tall in the evening.
  • Add the Tall kit parts list: obtain 9 screws and 5 washers.
  • Justify each unchanged entry by referring to the column it uses.

Exercise 3 · Diagnosis and justification

Reverse the factors

Return to the original arrays. Someone proposes BA in place of AB. Considering only the arrays of numbers, is this product defined, and what is its size? Calculate its top left entry. Then explain why this result does not give the array of parts per order.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

In BA, B is now the array on the left: compare its columns with the rows of A.

Hint 2

For the top left corner, take (3 ; 1) in B and the column (2 ; 1) in A.

Compare with the solution

Numerically, BA is defined: (3 × 2)(2 × 3) gives 3 × 3. It therefore cannot equal the array AB of size 2 × 2.

Its top left entry is 3 × 2 + 1 × 1 = 6 + 1 = 7. This operation uses a row of B and a column of A, following the numerical rule.

However, the connection now matches the morning, evening positions with the screws, washers positions. These labels do not describe the same intermediate step: we no longer go from the kits ordered to the parts in those kits. BA exists as a product of numerical arrays; it does not represent our order. Some other pairs of matrices do not even allow both orders: compatibility must be checked every time.

Check your reasoning

  • Distinguish numerical existence from meaning in the situation.
  • Justify the size 3 × 3 and obtain 7 in the top left corner.
  • Identify the incompatible labels at the connection and explain why AB is the useful order.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1Which sizes allow AB, and what is the size of the result?

If A is m × n and B is n × p, the shared dimensions n match and AB is m × p. To represent a situation, the labels of the shared positions must also match in the same order.

Recall 2How can you reconstruct an entry of the product without reciting the final array?

Choose its row in the array on the left and its column in the array on the right. Multiply the values associated with each shared position, then add all these products. In the workshop, each shared position refers to a kit type.

Recall 3Why do parts lists multiplied by orders give a number of parts?

A parts list expresses parts per kit; an order expresses kits. Their product counts parts. Within a single entry, all the terms refer to the same type of part and the same order.

Recall 4Why can you not swap the matrices without checking?

Reversing the factors changes which rows and columns meet. The product may become undefined, change size, or give different numbers. Even if it exists numerically, its meaning and units must be examined again.

Recall 5What check did we perform, and what error can it miss?

We totalled the kits for the day before counting the parts, then compared with the totals of each row of the result. This overall check can miss a morning–evening swap: the entries and their labels must also be checked.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Reconstruct from memory the chain parts lists → kits ordered → parts per order. Draw the sizes of the arrays, explain what connects, calculate an entry, and justify its unit. Add a check by regrouping, then explain why this check is not enough to guarantee the correct allocation.
