Level 2 · C053·M5

Proxy, construct and validity

Your goalCalculate an indicator of instruction diversity, then test what it can tell us about shared meaning.

Before you start
c029-m5 : Bayes · c030-m5 : Poisson · c010-m5 : logarithmic scales · c025-m5 : O, Omega and Theta · c053-m1 : surprise and information · c053-m2 : entropy of a finite distribution · c053-m3 : coding, repetition and compression · c053-m4 : discretization and normalization

✿01 See the idea

In a fictional workshop, one person sends the instructions “turn” and “push” to another person, who operates a handle. The log contains 4 occurrences of “turn” and 4 of “push”. A gauge reports maximum word diversity. Can it also report that the people understand each other?

Imagine that the recipient swaps the meanings of the words: they push when they hear “turn” and turn when they hear “push”. The log of what was sent stays identical. A measure calculated from this log cannot detect this change, even though agreement on meaning has changed.

The construct is the property we want to study: here, a shared meaning for these instructions in this task. We need to define it precisely so that we can test it. The proxy is an observable quantity used as an indirect indicator: here, the diversity of words in the log. Naming the proxy does not turn it into a direct measure of the construct.

Validity concerns the justification of an interpretation and a use of the score in a given context. “This score describes the diversity of these words” and “this score proves shared meaning” require different evidence. A calculation can be exact while the second interpretation is unjustified.

All the workshop instructions and quantities are fictional data chosen for reasoning. We are not reporting any trial conducted with people. Previous units introduced entropy and compression; here we use a small entropy calculation to examine an interpretation, without developing their theory again.

The rule to remember

Name the construct, describe what the proxy counts, then look for a situation where the score stays the same while the target property changes.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

An accurate gauge, a conclusion to test

The log alternates “turn” and “push” over 8 instructions. The fixed repertoire contains K = 2 word categories. Let us calculate its entropy H, then the normalized score h. We will then discuss the claim “maximum h proves that the people share the meaning of the instructions”.

  1. Define what is counted and what we want to know

    An instruction here is a whole word, with no spelling variants. N is the number of instructions in the log; nt and np are the numbers of occurrences of “turn” and “push”. These quantities are occurrence counts. The repertoire contains K categories and is fixed before the calculation; we do not confuse this repertoire with the list of actions understood.

    N = nt + np = 4 + 4 = 8 instructions. The question about the construct is: do the people assign the same actions to the words in this task? Counting does not yet answer this question.

  2. Convert counts into proportions

    Choose a position in the log with the same probability for every position. pt and pp denote the empirical probabilities of finding each word there: they are the counts divided by N, with no unit. This definition describes this log; it assumes neither independence of the instructions nor that the log represents other workshops.

    pt=ntN=48=12

    Similarly, pp = 4/8 = 1/2. Check: pt + pp = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1. No word is omitted or counted in both categories.

  3. Calculate the information of each word

    For a strictly positive probability p, I(p) = −log to base 2 of p is the information, in bits. I is the name of this function; p is its argument. The logarithm asks which exponent of the base gives the number in question. The base 2 sets the unit as the bit; it does not represent a number of actions.

    I(12)=−log2(12)=1 bit

    Why is this result exact? 2 to the power −1 equals 1/2; the logarithm therefore equals −1, then the minus sign gives 1 bit. Both words have the same probability, so they have the same information in this distribution.

  4. Take the average, then normalize

    H is the empirical entropy of the words: the average of their information values, weighted by their probabilities. Here it is expressed in bits per instruction. Multiplying each information value by its proportion avoids counting rare words as if they were as frequent as the others.

    H=ptI(pt)+ppI(pp)

    Contribution of “turn”: 1/2 × 1 = 1/2 bit per instruction. Contribution of “push”: 1/2 × 1 = 1/2 bit per instruction. Their sum gives H = 1 bit per instruction.

    Hmax denotes the maximum entropy for this repertoire: log to base 2 of K. With K = 2 and base 2, Hmax = 1 bit per instruction. The score h, a lowercase letter, is the quotient H/Hmax; the units cancel.

    h=HHmax=11=1.00

    Check: the 2 categories are equally probable, so the entropy does reach this maximum. h lies between 0 and 1 for this repertoire, provided that K is greater than 1. It is not a probability of understanding.

  5. Construct a case the score cannot detect

    In the Agreement case, “turn” means turning the handle for the people communicating; “push” means pushing it. In the Mismatch case, only the recipient’s interpretation is reversed. We stipulate these mappings in the exercise: these are constructed situations, not real observations.

    The transmitted log is identical: nt = 4, np = 4, so H = 1 bit per instruction and h = 1.00 in both cases. Yet operational agreement on meaning differs. A rule that infers this agreement from maximum h alone gives the same conclusion for situations it should distinguish. This refutes that general rule; it does not prove that every use of h would be useless.

    The log alternates the words perfectly. Its entropy calculated from frequencies ignores this order, even though a short description could exploit the alternation. Neither this entropy nor a compressed length, with a coding scheme and compressor that would need to be specified, demonstrates shared meaning on its own. These properties of the message remain distinct from its interpretation.

  6. Check the lower limit too and bound the conclusion

    In the same workshop, a log could repeat only “turn” over 8 instructions. The repertoire remains fixed at 2 categories. Then pt = 1 and pp = 0. A certain word provides −log to base 2 of 1 = 0 bit. An absent category contributes 0 by the usual convention; we do not calculate the logarithm of 0.

    We obtain H = 0 bit per instruction and h = 0.00. Yet the people can agree to turn at every instruction. The lower bound therefore does not prove an absence of shared meaning either.

    Supported conclusion: in the balanced log, the marginal word diversity is maximal for this repertoire. “Marginal” here means that we count words without taking their neighbors into account. To support the interpretation in terms of shared meaning, we would need to define the expected actions before the trial, keep the handle and context fixed, then observe interpretations when only the word–action mapping changes. This check is proposed, not carried out; it would apply only to the task and conditions studied.

    The observed action is also evidence that needs interpretation: difficulty operating the handle could produce an incorrect action despite the instruction being understood. We would need to distinguish this from disagreement on meaning, for example by asking the person to restate the instruction. Adding an indicator does not remove the need to justify what it means.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the results. Work out the proportions, H and h again; say what each measures. Then explain how the recipient can change the meaning of the words without changing the score, and what observation would reveal this.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “h equals 1, so the people share the meaning of the words.”

    h describes the balance of frequencies in the chosen repertoire. Revisit the Mismatch case: same log, same score, different expected actions. Observe the mapping between the word received and the action chosen before drawing a conclusion about meaning.

    Revisit the workshop counterexample

  • “0 entropy means there is nothing to understand.”

    The constant log provides no surprise about the next word. A repeated instruction can nevertheless retain a shared and useful meaning in the task. Recalculate the lower limit while keeping word predictability separate from interpretation of the action.

    Revisit the check using the constant log

  • “Normalization turns the score into a universal measure.”

    Dividing by the maximum removes the unit but retains the choice of repertoire, word segmentation and context. Write down what h actually counts and the additional evidence required for the conclusion about meaning.

    Separate the proxy, the construct and its interpretation

Set the expected action for each word before the trial, then record the action chosen without giving the correction during the attempt. This page does not report that this trial was carried out.

The counterexample is logically sufficient to invalidate the general guarantee: h alone does not distinguish these mappings. It is not enough to establish a frequency of disagreement, causality in every workshop, or the uselessness of all indicators. An observation limited to this handle would not automatically validate another use.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1In the workshop, what difference do you make between the construct and the proxy?

The construct is agreement on the meaning of the instructions in the task. The proposed proxy is word diversity calculated from the log; its link to that agreement needs justification.

Recall 2How can you work out H and h from the balanced log, and what are their units?

The proportions are 1/2 and 1/2, and each information value is 1 bit. H is their weighted average: 1 bit per instruction. The maximum is 1 bit per instruction for this repertoire; h = H/Hₘₐₓ = 1.00 has no unit.

Recall 3What change can alter shared meaning without changing the entropy of the words sent?

Reverse the recipient’s mapping between “turn”, “push” and the actions. The log stays the same, so the score does too, while the expected actions are no longer shared.

Recall 4Why is a score normalized between 0 and 1 not a probability of understanding?

Its bounds come from entropy and the chosen repertoire. Normalization removes the unit; it provides neither a probabilistic model of understanding nor evidence linking words to actions.

Recall 5What additional evidence should we seek to support interpreting the score in terms of shared meaning?

Define the expected actions, then observe how instructions are interpreted under specified conditions, particularly when the word–action mapping changes. A copy of the log or the calculation alone cannot replace this observation.
