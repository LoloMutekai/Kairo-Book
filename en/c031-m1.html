Level 1 · C031·M1

Limit of a sequence

Your goalJustify a finite limit by finding, for a given margin, an index from which all terms remain close enough to the target.

Before you start
c006-m5 : composition · c027-m5 : complex numbers · c011-m5 : binomial · c014-m5 : invariants

✿01 See the idea

A tank can hold 8 L. It starts empty. With each addition, you add exactly half the amount still needed to fill it. The volume therefore goes from 0 L to 4 L, then to 6 L, then to 7 L. The additions get smaller, but the tank continues to fill.

Imagine marking a region around the capacity, with a margin on either side. The useful question is: after which addition will the volume be inside this region, and will it stay there for all subsequent additions? We can then make the margin as small as we like.

Saying that the volume tends to 8 L means that every positive margin eventually works for the rest of the filling process. This does not require an addition to reach the capacity exactly. Being close enough for just one margin is not sufficient either.

This situation and its values are fictional. The model assumes exact volumes, no leaks, and a rule maintained indefinitely. A real tank, a pump, or readings with limited precision cannot carry out infinitely many operations: we are studying the mathematical sequence defined by the rule.

The rule to remember

Choose a positive margin; find an index; justify that all terms from that index onward stay strictly within that margin. A limit requires being able to repeat this for every positive margin.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

From which index is the shortfall less than 0.25 L?

Keep the tank with capacity C = 8 L and its exact rule. We will calculate the volume, find the first index that works for the margin ε = 0.25 L, then justify the limit for any positive margin.

  1. Describe the sequence and its units

    n is the integer number of additions already made, starting at 0; it has no unit. un is the volume after these additions, in litres. A sequence is this ordered list of values, one for each index n. The symbol u with subscript n + 1 denotes the volume after the next addition.

    To the current volume, we add half the shortfall C − un. The factor used to divide the shortfall has no unit. The rule and the starting value define every term, without any new measurement:

    u0=0 L ; un+1=un+C−un2

  2. Calculate the shortfall instead of repeating the additions

    Let en = C − un be the shortfall, in litres. It is positive in this model. Initially, it is 8 L. The first addition is 8 ÷ 2 = 4 L, giving a volume of 4 L. The shortfall is then 4 L; the next addition is 4 ÷ 2 = 2 L, and the volume becomes 4 + 2 = 6 L.

    After an addition, half the previous shortfall remains. We recover this by subtracting the filling rule from C:

    en+1=C−(un+C−un2)=en2

  3. Obtain an expression for every index

    The initial shortfall is divided by 2 with each addition. After n additions, the denominator is therefore 2 to the power n. The formula holds initially because 2 to the power 0 equals 1. If it holds at index n, dividing by 2 again makes it hold at the next index: this is a justification by induction.

    en=C2n ; un=C−C2n

    Check using the original rule: at index 3, the shortfall is 1 L and the volume is 7 L. The next addition is 1 ÷ 2 = 1/2 L; it gives 7.5 L, as does the direct formula. Every subtraction involves litres.

  4. State precisely what a limit means

    ℓ is a candidate limit, in the same unit as the terms. The Greek letter ε, read as “epsilon”, denotes a strictly positive margin, in litres here. |un − ℓ| is the absolute value of the difference: the distance between the term and the candidate. N is an integer index, with no unit. It may depend on the chosen margin.

    ∀ε>0,∃N∈ℕ,∀n≥N:|un−ℓ|<ε

    ∀ is read as “for every”; ∃ as “there exists”; ℕ here denotes the integers starting at 0. Read the statement in this order: for every positive margin, there exists an index from which every term is at a distance strictly less than that margin. The index may change when we tighten the margin. We are testing ℓ = C.

  5. Find the first index for the chosen margin

    Since un stays below C, the distance |un − C| is the shortfall en. For ε = 1/4 L, we therefore ask when C divided by 2 to the power n becomes strictly less than ε. Multiplying and dividing by positive quantities preserves the direction of inequalities:

    82n<14⇔2n>32

    The sign ⇔ means that the conditions are equivalent. At index 5, the denominator is 32: the shortfall 0.25 L equals the margin, so the test fails. At index 6, the denominator is 64: the shortfall is 8/64 = 1/8 L, or 0.125 L, strictly less than 0.25 L.

    The volume is then 8 − 1/8 = 63/8 L, or 7.875 L. It is indeed between 7.75 L and 8.25 L, with the boundaries excluded.

  6. Check all subsequent terms

    With each addition, the positive shortfall is divided by 2 again. It can therefore no longer exceed the shortfall at index 6. For every n ≥ 6, en ≤ 0.125 L < 0.25 L. The index N = 6 works for the whole remaining sequence, rather than just one term.

    It is the first suitable index: at index 5, the shortfall still equals the margin; at all earlier indices, it is larger. Another check: at index 7, we obtain 7.9375 L and a shortfall of 0.0625 L, halved once again.

  7. Move from this margin to every positive margin

    The previous calculation handles a particular margin. To prove the limit, now take any ε > 0 L. The ratio C/ε has no unit. Choose an integer N strictly greater than this ratio; this is possible because the integers are unbounded.

    We use the bound 2 to the power n ≥ n + 1. It holds initially: 1 ≥ 1. To move to the next index, doubling n + 1 gives at least n + 2, since n ≥ 0. This induction justifies the bound without assuming the limit.

    n≥N⇒2n≥n+1≥N+1>Cε
    |un−C|=C2n<ε

    This choice of N may be much larger than necessary. It is sufficient for the existence required by the definition. Finding the very first index is useful for our particular calculation, but is not required to prove a limit.

  8. Conclude and state what the proof establishes

    un→C=8 L quand n augmente sans borne

    The arrow means “tends to”. The definition is satisfied for every positive margin: the sequence converges to 8 L. At every finite index, the shortfall remains strictly positive; no term therefore equals the limit in this model.

    We have proved a property of the exact rule continued indefinitely. The fractions here give exact values; their displayed decimal forms are also exact. A finite set of readings resembling these values does not prove that the same rule will govern all future additions.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “To converge to the capacity, the volume must eventually reach it.”

    At every finite index, the shortfall is C divided by a positive power of 2: it remains positive. The limit concerns being able to satisfy every margin, rather than equality at a final index.

    Review the expression for the shortfall and the conclusion

  • “I have seen a volume close enough to the target, so the limit is proved.”

    You must check all subsequent terms, then be able to do so for every positive margin. A finite list leaves later additions unknown; the last exercise compares a proved rule with a simple set of readings.

    Compare possible continuations for the same tank

  • “At the index where the shortfall equals the margin, I am already strictly inside it.”

    At index 5, the shortfall 0.25 L equals ε. The test eₙ < ε excludes this equality. With the margin in the example, the first acceptable index is 6.

Exercise 1 · Direct application

Volume and distance from the target

For the same tank, calculate the shortfall and the volume at index 4. Is this term at a distance strictly less than 0.25 L from the capacity? Justify your answer with units.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

At index 3, the shortfall is 1 L; the next addition fills half of it.

Hint 2

Then calculate C minus the shortfall. Compare the shortfall, rather than the volume, with the margin.

Compare with the solution

The shortfall is 8/16 = 1/2 L, or 0.5 L. The volume is 8 − 1/2 = 7.5 L. Calculating with the rule gives the same result: 7 + 0.5 = 7.5 L.

The distance from the target is 0.5 L, greater than 0.25 L: this term fails the test. This finding at a single index does not contradict convergence, which allows waiting for a larger index.

Check your reasoning

  • Distinguish the shortfall 0.5 L from the volume 7.5 L.
  • Compare distances in the same unit.
  • Explain why this local failure does not disprove the limit.

Exercise 2 · Threshold to justify

Tighten the margin

Keep exactly the same rule, but take ε = 0.125 L. Find the first index N that works, then prove that all subsequent indices work too.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The shortfall must be strictly less than 1/8 L. What value must the denominator exceed?

Hint 2

The shortfall at index 6 equals this new margin. Equality does not pass the strict test.

Compare with the solution

The inequality 8/(2 to the power n) < 1/8 is equivalent to 2 to the power n > 64. At index 6, this power equals 64: equality, so the test fails. At index 7, it equals 128: the shortfall is 1/16 L, or 0.0625 L < 0.125 L.

N = 7 is the first index that works, because all previous shortfalls are at least as large as the shortfall at index 6. For every n ≥ 7, the shortfall can only be divided by 2 again: it remains at most 0.0625 L, hence strictly below the margin. The volume at the first acceptable index is 7.9375 L.

Check your reasoning

  • Explicitly reject the index where the shortfall and the margin are equal.
  • Give N = 7 and the corresponding shortfall.
  • Justify all future indices using the rule, going beyond the calculated term.

Exercise 3 · Reflect on the proof

A set of readings can have several continuations

You only know the observed volumes up to index 6, identical to those in the example. An alternative continuation for the same tank is proposed: after these readings, empty it and keep it empty from index 7 onward. Are the readings sufficient to guarantee a limit of 8 L? Give the limit of this alternative continuation and explain what made the proof in the lesson valid.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The two continuations have the same initial volumes, but different future volumes.

Hint 2

In the emptied continuation, every term from index 7 onward is 0 L. Test the definition around this value.

Compare with the solution

The readings alone are not sufficient. Let vn be the volume of the alternative continuation, in litres: it equals un up to index 6, then equals 0 L for every n ≥ 7. This continuation agrees with the readings, but then abandons the rule of filling half the remaining space.

It converges to 0 L: for any margin ε > 0 L, N = 7 works because |vn − 0| = 0 L < ε at all subsequent indices. It does not tend to 8 L: its future distance from that capacity remains 8 L, so it exceeds, for example, the margin 0.25 L.

The lesson did not infer the future from a set of readings. It started from a rule stated to hold at every index, then derived an expression and a bound valid for all future indices. This assumption made the proof possible.

Check your reasoning

  • Distinguish a finite set of readings from a rule defined for every index.
  • Justify the limit 0 L of the alternative continuation using an index and an arbitrary margin.
  • State that the counterexample changes the rule after the readings, without challenging the proof under its assumptions.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What precisely does “uₙ tends to ℓ” mean?

For every strictly positive margin ε, we can find an index N such that every term with index n ≥ N is at a distance strictly less than ε from ℓ. The terms, the limit, and the margin have the same unit.

Recall 2Why do we choose the margin before the index?

The required index may depend on the precision requested. The definition requires an answer for every margin; it does not require the same index for all of them. We must then check all subsequent indices.

Recall 3Why can the tank tend to its capacity without ever reaching it?

At every finite index, the shortfall C/(2 to the power n) is positive. Yet it becomes smaller than any chosen positive margin, then stays smaller. The limit does not require equality at a finite index.

Recall 4For the margin 0.25 L, why does index 5 fail and index 6 work?

At index 5, the shortfall 0.25 L equals the margin: the boundary is excluded. At index 6, it is 0.125 L, strictly less. All subsequent terms work because the shortfall continues to be divided by 2.

Recall 5What must be added to a few volumes close to the target to obtain a proof of the limit?

A rule or a bound justified for all future indices, allowing every positive margin to be satisfied. A finite set of readings leaves different continuations possible.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Write the order “every margin → an index → all subsequent terms”, recover the strict threshold in the example, then explain why the ideal rule allows a proof that the finite set of readings does not provide.
