Transformations

Your goalPredict and then calculate how the filling graph shifts when the tap opens later or when the water bottle already contains more water, remembering to recalculate the bounds of the trial.

✿01 See the idea

Return to the water bottle from the previous units. In the reference trial, the stopwatch and the tap start together. The bottle already contains 2 dL (decilitres), and the tap adds 2 dL per minute, up to the capacity of 10 dL. These values are fictional, chosen for the exercise; they do not describe any measured bottle.

First change: you start the stopwatch, but the tap opens only 2 minutes later. The way the bottle fills does not change; everything simply happens later. The start of the graph shifts to the right along the time axis, and the end of filling shifts by the same amount.

Second change: before opening the tap, you add an extra 1 dL into the bottle. At each instant during filling, the bottle contains 1 dL more than it would without this addition. The graph moves up along the volume axis. The tap stays the same: each minute still adds 2 dL.

These two adjustments have different units. The delay is measured in minutes and acts on the input to the rule, time. The addition is measured in decilitres and acts on its output, volume. Confusing them would mean adding minutes and decilitres.

There is a trap when reading the formulas. To describe the delay, we will write f(t − d): there is a minus sign, yet the graph moves to the right. The rule f uses only the time elapsed since the tap opened, which we will call internal time. This internal time equals 0 when t − d = 0, that is, at time t = d, later than the start of the stopwatch.

Moving a graph without changing its shape is called a translation. Here, a horizontal translation comes from the delay, and a vertical translation comes from the addition. Only the starting bound of the trial simply follows the delay; the end also depends on how much water remains to be poured.

The rule to remember

To delay by d minutes: replace t with t − d in the rule; the start shifts d to the right. To add a dL: add a to the output; every level rises by a and the flow rate stays the same. Then recalculate the bounds: start at t = d, end when the level reaches the capacity.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

A delayed start and a bottle with extra water already added

Fictional trial: the tap opens 2 minutes after the stopwatch starts, and you have poured 1 dL into the bottle before opening it. What volume does it contain at time t = 3 min? What formula describes the entire trial, and over what time interval can it be used?

  1. Name the quantities, their units and the assumptions

    t is global time, in minutes, read on the stopwatch started at the beginning of the trial. d is the delay before the tap opens, in minutes, with d ≥ 0. u = t − d is internal time, in minutes: the time elapsed since opening.

    p is the flow rate, in dL per minute; here p = 2. b is the starting level of the reference, in dL; here b = 2. a is the amount added before opening, in dL: positive for an addition, negative for a removal. C is the capacity, in dL; here C = 10.

    The reference rule is f(u) = pu + b, in dL, used for u ≥ 0 and until the bottle is full. The transformed trial is described by:

    h(t)=f(t−d)+a=p(t−d)+b+a

    Assumptions: the flow rate stays constant from opening; the additional water is poured in all at once before opening; filling stops when the bottle is full, with no overflow; nothing evaporates. We also need b + a ≥ 0 (we cannot remove more water than there is) and b + a ≤ C.

  2. Convert global time to internal time

    The rule f counts minutes since opening, rather than since the stopwatch started. We therefore subtract the delay from the time read: u = 3 − 2 = 1 min.

    Check the meaning: u is positive or 0, so the tap is already open at t = 3 min. A negative internal time would indicate an instant outside the trial.

  3. Apply the reference rule

    The tap is the same as in the reference; after 1 minute of flow, the rule gives:

    f(1)=2×1+2=2+2=4

    This is the volume the reference bottle would contain at the same internal time: 4 dL.

  4. Add the output adjustment

    The water poured in before opening is still in the bottle, in addition to what the tap has poured in. We add it to the volume, rather than to the time: h(3) = 4 + 1 = 5 dL.

    Remember the order: first the input (internal time), then the rule, then the output (addition).

  5. Expand the formula for the entire trial

    To describe all instants at once, replace u with t − 2 in the rule, then add a:

    h(t)=2(t−2)+2+1=2t−4+3=2t−1

    Check by another route: using the expanded form, h(3) = 6 − 1 = 5 dL, the same result as in the previous steps.

  6. Transform the bounds as well as the formula

    Start: the tap opens at t = d = 2 min. The level there is b + a = 2 + 1 = 3 dL; the expanded form confirms this, 4 − 1 = 3.

    End: 10 − 3 = 7 dL remains to be poured, at 2 dL per minute. We therefore solve h(t) = C, working from the start:

    t=d+C−(b+a)p=2+72=2+3.5=5.5

    Check: h(5.5) = 11 − 1 = 10 dL, the capacity. The formula h describes the trial for 2 ≤ t ≤ 5.5 min, with volumes from 3 to 10 dL.

  7. Check the flow rate and duration

    Flow rate: one minute later, h(4) = 8 − 1 = 7 dL. The difference is 7 − 5 = 2 dL in one minute, exactly the reference flow rate: the addition has not changed the slope.

    Duration: the reference fills in (10 − 2) ÷ 2 = 4 min; here filling takes 3.5 min. The difference of 0.5 min is the time the tap would have needed to deliver the extra 1 dL, namely a ÷ p. This is why the end does not simply shift by d: it shifts later by d, then earlier by a ÷ p.

Explain it in your own words

Without looking, explain why the start of the trial shifts by exactly 2 minutes, while the end does not occur 2 minutes after the end of the reference. Use the words internal time, addition and capacity.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “A minus sign in the input makes the graph move to the left.”

    Find the instant when the input to f becomes 0 again: t − d = 0 gives t = d. The start of the reference therefore happens later, to the right on the time axis. With d = 2 min, filling starts at t = 2 min, and the tap pours in nothing beforehand.

    Review the delay diagram

  • “Adding water at the start increases the flow rate.”

    Compare the volumes one minute apart: h(4) − h(3) = 7 − 5 = 2 dL, exactly the reference flow rate. The addition raises every level by the same amount; it brings forward the time when the bottle is full, without changing the slope.

    Return to the flow rate and duration check

  • “Once expanded, the formula is valid for all times.”

    With h(t) = 2t − 1, calculating at t = 0 would give −1 dL, an impossible volume, while the tap has not yet opened. The bounds 2 ≤ t ≤ 5.5 min come from the situation; they accompany the expanded formula wherever it is used.

    Return to the bounds of the trial

Exercise 1 · Guided practice

A delay with no addition

Same bottle and same tap. This time, the tap opens 1 minute after the stopwatch starts, and nothing is added. What volume does the bottle contain at t = 3 min? Over what time interval does the formula describe the trial?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

First calculate the time elapsed since the tap opened.

Hint 2

The start is at t equal to the delay; with no addition, the same amount remains to be poured as in the reference.

Compare with the solution

Internal time: 3 − 1 = 2 min. Volume: f(2) = 2 × 2 + 2 = 4 + 2 = 6 dL. With no addition, h(t) = f(t − 1) has no output adjustment.

Bounds: start at t = 1 min. There is 10 − 2 = 8 dL still to be poured, taking 4 min as in the reference; end at 1 + 4 = 5 min. The interval is 1 ≤ t ≤ 5 min.

Check: the reference contains 6 dL at internal time 2 min, that is, at t = 2; the delayed trial reaches this volume 1 minute later, at t = 3. This is indeed a shift to the right.

Check your reasoning

  • The delay is subtracted from time, never from volume.
  • The result is given in dL and the interval in minutes.
  • The interval starts at the delay and lasts as long as the reference.

Exercise 2 · Practice

An addition and the time when the bottle is full

The tap opens as soon as the stopwatch starts, but you have first poured 2 dL into the bottle. After how many minutes is the bottle full? Has the flow rate changed? Justify both answers.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Start with the level at the moment of opening.

Hint 2

Divide the volume still to be poured by the flow rate of the tap.

Compare with the solution

Level at opening: 2 + 2 = 4 dL. Remaining volume: 10 − 4 = 6 dL. Duration: 6 ÷ 2 = 3 min. The delay is 0, so the bottle is full at t = 3 min.

The flow rate stays 2 dL per minute: the addition raises all levels by the same amount, so the change over one minute stays the same. Yet the bottle is full 1 minute earlier than the reference, which took 4 min: this is the time the tap would have taken to deliver the extra 2 dL.

Check: h(t) = 2t + 4 gives, at time 3, the volume 2 × 3 + 4 = 10 dL.

Check your reasoning

  • The initial level is calculated before the duration.
  • The duration is in minutes and checked by calculating a volume.
  • The answer clearly distinguishes the flow rate, which is unchanged, and the time when the bottle is full, which is earlier.

Exercise 3 · Notebook challenge

A delay and a removal

The tap opens 2 minutes after the stopwatch starts. Before opening it, you have emptied 2 dL from the bottle: the addition is negative, a = −2 dL. Write h(t) in expanded form, give its interval of use, calculate h(3) in two ways, then explain what the expanded formula would say at t = 1 min.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Replace t with t − 2 throughout the rule f, then add a with its sign.

Hint 2

The level at opening is b + a; check that it is nonnegative.

Hint 3

For the end, find when the level reaches capacity, starting from t = 2.

Compare with the solution

Formula: h(t) = 2(t − 2) + 2 − 2 = 2t − 4 + 0 = 2t − 4.

Bounds: the level at opening is 2 − 2 = 0 dL; the bottle is empty, so this is the largest permitted removal. There is 10 − 0 = 10 dL left, taking 10 ÷ 2 = 5 min, and the end occurs at 2 + 5 = 7 min. Interval: 2 ≤ t ≤ 7 min.

Value: h(3) = 6 − 4 = 2 dL. Second route, using internal time: 3 − 2 = 1 min, f(1) = 4 dL, then 4 − 2 = 2 dL.

At t = 1 min, the expanded form would give 2 × 1 − 4 = −2 dL, a negative volume. This is no prediction: the tap is not yet open and t is outside the interval. Expanding preserves the calculation, without making it valid everywhere.

Check your reasoning

  • Correct expanded form, preserving the sign of the removal.
  • Complete interval: start at the delay, end calculated at full capacity, initial level checked to be nonnegative.
  • h(3) obtained by two routes that agree.
  • The negative value is rejected as outside the interval, never presented as a volume.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1In h(t) = f(t − d) + a, what does d represent, in what unit, and which way does the graph move?

The delay before the tap opens, in minutes. Internal time t − d equals 0 at t = d: the start shifts d to the right.

Recall 2What does the addition a change, and what does it leave unchanged?

It raises every level by a dL at a fixed time; the level at opening becomes b + a. It leaves the flow rate p unchanged. With a positive, the bottle becomes full a ÷ p minutes earlier.

Recall 3How do we find the interval over which h can be used?

Start at t = d; end when h(t) = C, giving t = d + (C − b − a) ÷ p. We also need 0 ≤ b + a ≤ C.

Recall 4Delay 2 min, addition 1 dL: what volume at t = 3 min, and through which steps?

Internal time 3 − 2 = 1 min; rule f(1) = 4 dL; addition: 4 + 1 = 5 dL.

Recall 5Why should we avoid using 2t − 1 at t = 0 to describe the delayed trial?

The tap opens only at t = 2 min; before then, the formula does not describe the trial and would give a negative volume. Expanding keeps the calculation; the domain still has to be specified.

Reconstruct the reasoning

To delay by d minutes, I replace t with t − d: the rule counts only from opening, so the start shifts to the right. To add a dL, I add a to the output: every level rises by a and the flow rate stays p. I calculate in the order internal time, rule, addition. Then I transform the bounds along with the formula: start at t = d, end when full, which happens earlier if a is positive.
