Level 0 · C004·M5

Rational equations

Your goalFind how many people can share a given cost, keeping track of the allowed values through to the final check.

Before you start
c003-m5 : scientific notation · c004-m1 : expressions · c004-m2 : linear equations · c004-m3 : polynomials · c004-m4 : factorization

✿01 See the idea

For a picnic, the shared equipment costs 12 € regardless of the size of the group. Each person also pays for their meal, at 3 € per person. Everyone shares the equipment cost equally. These prices are fictional; they are used to explain the calculation.

The meal remains an individual expense. The equipment is paid for only once: each person covers just a share of its cost. Finding how many people would allow everyone to pay exactly 5 € means finding how many shares this common expense can be split into.

Now imagine that nobody comes. The equipment still costs the same, but there is no recipient for an individual share. The cost per person is then undefined. Writing 0 € would suggest that the cost had been shared for free.

The diagrams separate the shared bill from the meals and show what is missing when the group is empty. Only then will we express this sharing as an equation.

The rule to remember

Before transforming an equation with a letter in the denominator, write down the excluded values. Keep track of them until you have checked the candidate in the original equation and in the situation.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Paying exactly 5 € per person

The shared equipment still costs 12 € and the meal costs 3 € per person. Let us find the number of people needed for everyone to pay exactly 5 €, with no discount, other expense, or rounding.

  1. Name the quantities and state the allowed values

    n is the number of people. F denotes the fixed equipment bill, in euros; p is the meal price and B is the requested price per person, in euros per person. Here F = 12 €, p = 3 € per person and B = 5 € per person. We will write the equations using their numerical values in these units.

    n is a count: it must be a positive integer, so n ≥ 1. Division already requires n ≠ 0, which means “n is different from 0”. The situation also excludes negative numbers and fractions of a person. Let us write these conditions before doing any calculation and keep them throughout the solution.

  2. Express the sharing as an equation

    The equipment share is F/n: the numerator F is the amount to share, and the denominator n counts the people sharing it. Adding the meal gives a cost per person of p + F/n. The equation requires this cost to equal B.

    p+Fn=B
    3+12n=5

    A rational expression is a quotient of polynomials: expressions made from sums of numbers multiplied by nonnegative integer powers of the letter. Here the letter n appears in the denominator. An equation containing such an expression asks for the values of n that make the equality true and all the divisions possible. We deal only with the sharing equation p + F/n = B.

  3. Subtract the meal cost to isolate the shared contribution

    The meal already accounts for 3 € per person out of the requested price. Subtracting this price from each side of the equality leaves the share available for equipment: 5 − 3 = 2 € per person.

    12n=2; n entier, n≥1

  4. Multiply each side by the same nonzero number

    We have excluded n = 0. Multiplying each side by n therefore also allows us to reverse the step by dividing by n. On the left, n × (12/n) gives 12 again; on the right, the same n multiplies 2.

    n×12n=n×2⇒12=2n

    The arrow reads “therefore”. The transformed equation keeps exactly the same solutions among the allowed values. It does not allow a value excluded at the start. Here 2n is the total of the equipment shares, in euros.

  5. Find a candidate

    Each person contributes 2 € for equipment. Dividing the 12 € bill by this contribution counts the number of people needed. The divisor 2 is nonzero, so this new division is allowed.

    n=122=6

    The candidate 6 is a positive integer. We still need to check the original price. No other allowed value can solve the equation: the reversible calculation requires this single value of n.

  6. Substitute into the original equation

    Substitution means replacing the letter with the candidate in the original expression. The quotient is possible there because 6 ≠ 0. The shared contribution is 12/6 = 2 € per person, and the full cost is then 3 + 2 = 5 € per person.

    3+126=5=5

    We obtain the requested price, and the count is possible: the solution for this situation is 6 people. A transformed equation alone does not replace this return to the original sharing.

  7. Check against the group bill

    Another approach: the group pays 6 × 5 = 30 €. The meals cost 3 × 6 = 18 €; with the equipment, the bill is 18 + 12 = 30 €. The money collected covers the expenses exactly.

    This bill gives another expression for the same cost per person: (pn + F)/n. For n ≠ 0, dividing each term of the numerator gives pn/n + F/n = p + F/n. We can simplify the product pn divided by n; we cannot cancel n across the entire sum pn + F.

    pn+Fn=18+126=306=5

    The restrictions remain written down, even in a form where the quotient is less visible. The total budget and the price per person agree; this checks our fictional model without establishing a real price.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “With nobody present, the price per person is 0 €.”

    The shared bill does not disappear. With n = 0, no individual share can be formed: the quotient is undefined. Write down this absence of a value, then keep n ≠ 0 before any transformation.

    Review the diagram of impossible sharing

  • “The denominator has disappeared, so I can forget the exclusions.”

    Multiplying by n is reversible only for n ≠ 0. Copy the restriction beside the transformed equations and check each candidate in the original expression. Even the simplification pn/n = p retains this condition.

    Review multiplication and substitution

  • “If the calculation gives a fraction of a person, I can just round it.”

    The requested price is exact: rounding changes the sharing. A candidate must satisfy the equation and count a positive integer number of people. A well-defined quotient can therefore give a candidate that the situation excludes.

    Test prices that work for no group

What price can we quote?

Exercise 2 · Application · solving and then checking

Start again from the whole bill

The requested price becomes 7 € per person, with the same costs. Solve the equation below, keeping the restrictions. Check the result in this fraction and in the breakdown between equipment and meals.

3n+12n=7
Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

First write that n is a positive integer. Multiplying each side by n applies to the entire sum in the numerator, rather than just the meal.

Hint 2

After multiplying, subtract 3n from each side. The remaining coefficient is 7 − 3.

Compare with the solution

Allowed values: n is an integer and n ≥ 1, so n ≠ 0. Multiply by n: 3n + 12 = 7n. Subtract 3n: 12 = (7 − 3)n = 4n. Divide by 4, which is nonzero: n = 12/4 = 3.

The candidate is a positive integer. The group meals cost 3 × 3 = 9 €. With the equipment, the numerator is 9 + 12 = 21 €. The original fraction is 21/3 = 7 € per person: this is the requested price.

Another way to read the check: 12/3 = 4 € for equipment, plus 3 € for a meal, making 7 € per person. The equipment shares add up to 12 €. The solution is therefore 3 people.

Check your reasoning

  • The allowed values appear before multiplication and remain in force to the end.
  • You transform the whole fraction and justify the operations on each side.
  • You find 3 people and substitute into the original equation, including the units.

Exercise 3 · Transfer · justifying a rejection

Can an equation give no possible group?

With the same costs, first request exactly 8 € per person, then exactly 3 € per person. Treat each request separately. Explain which case gives a candidate from a division that must be rejected, and which case makes the division to find n itself impossible. Rounding is not allowed here.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

For a requested price B, subtracting the meal cost and multiplying by n gives F = (B − p)n, still with n a positive integer.

Hint 2

Before dividing by B − p, check whether this difference is 0. If it is not, then check that the quotient is a positive integer.

Compare with the solution

For B = 8, the shared contribution would have to be 8 − 3 = 5 € per person. The equation requires n = 12/5 = 12/5 = 2.4. This number is positive and nonzero; the division is defined, but it does not count whole people.

The algebraic check does give 12 € in equipment shares and 8 € per person. This is not enough: the counting condition fails. No group of whole people pays exactly this price in this model. For example, 3 people pay 7 € each, rather than 8 €; rounding would change the problem.

For B = p = 3, the difference B − p is 0. The transformed equation would be 12 = 0 × n. Its right side is 0 for every allowed n, whereas 12 ≠ 0: the equation is impossible. Do not calculate 12/0 or replace it with any number.

We can also reason directly: with a positive shared bill, every finite nonempty group must pay something for equipment in addition to the meal. The price per person therefore cannot be exactly the price of the meal alone. The requests fail for distinct reasons: a count that is not an integer in one case, and an impossible equality in the other.

Check your reasoning

  • For 8 € per person, you keep the exact quotient 12/5 and reject the candidate because it is not an integer.
  • For 3 € per person, you identify B − p = 0 before any division and explain the contradiction.
  • You distinguish “defined quotient, excluded candidate” from “impossible division” and give no rounded solution.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What should you check before multiplying an equation containing F/n by n?

The denominator must not be 0: n ≠ 0. For the picnic, n must also be a positive integer. These conditions still apply after the transformation.

Recall 2Why does moving from F/n = B − p to F = (B − p)n preserve the allowed solutions?

We multiply each side by the same nonzero n. We can reverse the step by dividing by n. This reversibility applies among the allowed values, without allowing the empty group.

Recall 3How can you check the candidate 6 beyond the transformed equation?

6 is a positive integer. In the original equation, 3 + 12/6 = 5 € per person, as requested. The group bill is also 30 €, covered by 6 contributions of 5 €.

Recall 4Why does a defined quotient not guarantee a solution for the picnic? And what should you do if B − p is 0?

The candidate may be fractional or negative, whereas n must count a positive integer number of whole people. If B − p = 0, we do not divide: we reread F = (B − p)n. With our positive F, this equality is impossible.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Reconstruct without looking: what the denominator represents; the allowed values; the operation that isolates the shared contribution; the reversible transformation; the candidate; the check in the original equation and in the total bill. Explain when you could reject a candidate.
