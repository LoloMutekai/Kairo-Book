Level 3 · C201·M3

Positions

Your goalReplay executions in a ledger of signed quantities and cash, then check the value of a long or short position.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

In a historical replay, your ledger already contains 6 Aster shares and 400.00 € in cash. The price is 100.00 € per share. A sale of 10 shares is then executed. What remains? The shares you own are not enough: the sale first closes the long position, then creates an obligation to return borrowed shares.

A position is the holding recorded in the ledger. We count it with a sign: positive for shares owned, 0 when the ledger is flat, and negative for shares still to be returned. A short position does not mean owning negative objects; the sign represents this obligation. Buying can therefore reduce a short position before opening a long one.

An order expresses a request. An execution states what was actually traded in the simulation. Only this executed quantity changes the position. A new price changes the value of the position without changing its number of shares.

Aster, the prices, and the trades are fictitious. We will follow the same ledger until the position is closed. Cash received from a short sale does not measure an increase in wealth: there is still a debt in shares to value. We call the sum of cash and the signed value of this position equity.

The rule to remember

Keep track of signed quantity and cash; apply each execution with the sign of its trade direction; value the holding at the current price. At the same price and without fees, exchanging shares for cash preserves equity.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

The Aster ledger, from a long position back to flat

The initial ledger holds +6 shares and 400.00 €. A sale of 10 shares is fully executed at 100.00 € per share. The price then moves to 97.50 €, with no trade in between. At this new price, we buy back exactly the shares owed. Let us reconstruct quantity, cash, and equity at each step.

  1. Define the ledger and its assumptions

    q is the signed quantity, in shares. C is cash, in euros. p is the price, in euros per share. E is equity at the current valuation, in euros. The product pq is therefore a signed value in euros. Δq, read as “change in q”, denotes the signed executed quantity: positive for a purchase and negative for a sale. The prime mark in q′ and C′ denotes the state just after this execution.

    We track a single security, in a single currency, with whole numbers of shares. Each execution is assumed to occur exactly at the price used to value the ledger at that instant. No cash deposits or withdrawals occur. The shares needed for short selling are assumed to be available to borrow. Fees, interest, income, and other events are excluded from this step; financing and costs will come in the following units.

    Executions are supplied as inputs to the replay, in clock order. The scene does not infer that they exist from an order being sent. Prices are those available at the instant considered; no later price is used to set an earlier quantity.

    q′=q+Δq;C′=C−pΔq;E=C+pq

    The subtraction in C′ represents payment for a purchase. For a sale, Δq is negative: subtracting pΔq then credits cash.

  2. Value the starting state

    The shares held are worth 6 × 100.00 = 600.00 €. We add cash because equity must include both components.

    E=400+6×100=1000 €

    This initial state is given. Without its acquisition history, we will calculate a change from this instant, rather than a gain since the original purchase of the shares.

  3. Apply the sale to the quantity

    The executed sale of 10 shares becomes Δq = 0 − 10 = -10 shares. Add this movement to the holding: q′ = 6 + (-10) = -4 shares. The sale closes the holding of 6 shares, then leaves 4 shares to be returned.

    To aim for a specific holding, you must calculate a difference. If q_cible is the target holding, in the same unit as q, the requested change is q_cible − q. Here, aiming for -4 from 6 requires -4 − 6 = -10 shares. This request becomes a movement in the ledger only to the extent that it is executed.

  4. Record the cash flow and check the offset

    The signed product pΔq is 100.00 × (-10) = -1000.00 €. The rule C′ = C − pΔq gives 400.00 − (-1000.00) = 1400.00 €. The sale has therefore brought in cash.

    The short position is now worth -4 × 100.00 = -400.00 €. Buying it back immediately at the same price would require paying the opposite of this value.

    E′=1400+(-400)=1000 €

    Check: equity remains 1000.00 €. Comparing only 1400.00 € with the starting cash balance would leave out the debt in shares.

  5. Change the price without inventing a trade

    The new price is 97.50 € per share, or exactly 195/2 €. Neither q = -4 shares nor C = 1400.00 € changes. Only pq is recalculated: -4 × 97.50 = -390.00 €. Equity becomes 1400.00 + (-390.00) = 1010.00 €.

    Another way to check: Δp denotes the new price minus the old price, in euros per share; ΔE is the change in equity, in euros. With quantity and cash held constant, the difference between the two valuations gives the following rule.

    ΔE=qΔp=-4×(-2.50)=10 €

    The price has fallen: buying back the shares owed would cost less. We again obtain 1010.00 − 1000.00 = 10.00 €. This fictitious gain is still carried by an open position; no new money was received during the fall.

  6. Buy back the shares owed and close the position

    Returning to q′ = 0 requires a purchase of Δq = 0 − (-4) = 4 shares. Its cost is 4 × 97.50 = 390.00 €. The quantity becomes -4 + 4 = 0 shares; cash becomes 1400.00 − 390.00 = 1010.00 €.

    The shares bought back are used to return the borrowed shares. The closed position is worth 0 × 97.50 = 0.00 €. Equity is 1010.00 + 0.00 = 1010.00 €. Closing at the same price does not create a second gain: it converts a value already counted into cash net of the cost of returning the shares.

  7. Recover the invariant before trusting the ledger

    An invariant is a relation that must remain true under stated conditions. Here, for each execution at the same price p, without fees or other flows, the change in cash exactly offsets the change in the value of the shares. Substituting the rules for C′ and q′:

    E′=C−pΔq+p(q+Δq)=C+pq=E

    When the price changes, this conservation is no longer expected: qΔp then explains the change. Before accepting an entry in the ledger, check the unit and sign of the executed quantity, its price, the new cash balance, and the valuation. An execution at a price different from the valuation price would require distinguishing the two prices; our immediate invariant would no longer apply as stated.

    Scene totals: start 1000.00 €, sale 1000.00 €, price fall 1010.00 €, closing 1010.00 €. Here, the only change comes from the price while the short position is held.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the calculation and explain why the sale crosses the flat position without creating equity, then why a price fall increases the net value of the short ledger. Next, recover the purchase needed to return to flat and explain why the gain must not be counted a second time.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “I requested a sale of 10 shares, so the position fell by 10.”

    A request may be partially executed. Read the quantity actually traded, give it the sign of a sale, then add it to the previous holding. The partial sale exercise deliberately keeps the same request and changes its execution.

    Replay the partial sale in the first exercise

  • “A purchase always opens a long position; a sale always opens a short position.”

    The direction of a trade is not enough: look at the holding before execution. Selling first closes shares owned; buying first reduces shares owed. The sign of the holding after addition determines the remaining position.

    Review the passage through the flat position

  • “Cash has risen to 1400.00 €: I have earned that whole amount.”

    At this instant, the -4 shares represent an obligation valued at -400.00 €. Add it to cash: equity is still 1000.00 €. To calculate a change, compare two complete equity values in the same currency.

    Revisit the offset between cash and shares

Exercise 1 · Ledger application

The sale is only partially filled

Start again from the initial ledger: +6 shares, 400.00 € in cash, and a price of 100.00 €. The requested sale is still for 10 shares, but only 2 shares are executed at this price. Calculate Δq, the remaining holding, cash, and equity. Is the ledger already short?

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

First write the sign and quantity of the trade that actually occurred. The requested number does not replace this input.

Hint 2

The sale credits cash; the remaining shares must still be included in the valuation.

Compare with the solution

The execution gives Δq = -2 shares. The holding becomes 6 + (-2) = 4 shares: it remains positive, so the ledger is still long.

The product pΔq is -200.00 €. Cash becomes 400.00 − (-200.00) = 600.00 €. The remaining shares are worth 4 × 100.00 = 400.00 €. Thus E = 600.00 + 400.00 = 1000.00 €.

Check: equity is identical to its starting value because the trade took place at the valuation price. Reporting -4 shares here would mean recording a quantity that was not executed.

Check your reasoning

  • Use the executed quantity with the sign of a sale.
  • Keep the holding positive and identify it as a long position.
  • Recover the initial equity by adding cash and the value of the shares.

Exercise 2 · Inverse calculation

Reach the target holding

Continue from the state given in the answer to the first exercise: 4 shares and 600.00 €. The price remains 100.00 €. You now aim for a position of -4 shares. The unfilled remainder of the old request is cancelled before any new request. What change must you request? Then assume this new quantity is fully executed and calculate the final cash balance.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

A target is a holding after the operation: subtract the current holding to find the movement.

Hint 2

Check the result against the ledger obtained when the entire sale in the example was executed.

Compare with the solution

The requested change is the target minus the current holding: -4 − 4 = -8 shares. You must therefore sell 8 shares. Requesting a change of only -4 shares would bring the holding to 0, without reaching the short target.

After execution, q′ = 4 + (-8) = -4 shares. The product pΔq is -800.00 €, so C′ = 600.00 − (-800.00) = 1400.00 €.

Check by grouping the trades: selling 2 shares then 8 at the same price is equivalent to selling 10 shares. We recover the quantity -4 and cash of 1400.00 € from the example. Cancelling the unfilled remainder before replacing it prevents an additional request for the same shares from being sent.

Check your reasoning

  • Calculate the target minus the current holding, in the same unit.
  • Distinguish the new request from the assumption of full execution.
  • Recover the same state by grouping the sales at the same price.

Exercise 3 · Transfer and checking

The price rises before a purchase larger than the debt

Consider another possible continuation of the same ledger just after its full sale: -4 shares and 1400.00 €. This time, the price rises directly from 100.00 € to 105.00 €, without passing through the low price in the example. Calculate equity and its change before any trade. A purchase of 6 shares is then fully executed at 105.00 €. Calculate the new state and explain which part of the purchase closes the debt.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Before the purchase, the quantity remains short. Calculate its value with its sign at the new price.

Hint 2

Within the purchase, separate the shares needed to return to flat from those that will remain owned. Then check the invariant at the same price.

Compare with the solution

Before the purchase, the position is worth -4 × 105.00 = -420.00 €. Equity is 1400.00 + (-420.00) = 980.00 €. Its change since the sale is 980.00 − 1000.00 = -20.00 €. Another calculation: qΔp = -4 × 5.00 = -20.00 €. The rise makes returning the shares more costly.

The purchase represents Δq = +6 shares, for 6 × 105.00 = 630.00 €. It leaves q′ = -4 + 6 = 2 shares and C′ = 1400.00 − 630.00 = 770.00 €.

The first 4 shares bought cover the debt; the other 2 open a long position. Its value is 2 × 105.00 = 210.00 €. Final equity is 770.00 + 210.00 = 980.00 €, equal to its value before the purchase. The loss was caused by the rise during the short position; this trade at the same price does not erase it.

Check your reasoning

  • Value the debt with a negative sign before the purchase.
  • Recover the change both by subtracting equity values and by using qΔp.
  • Separate closing the short position from opening the new long position.
  • Check that equity is conserved during the purchase at the same price.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What is the difference between an order, an execution, and a position?

The order requests a trade; the execution gives the quantity actually traded in the replay; the position is the signed holding remaining after this executed quantity is added.

Recall 2Why is the cash from a short sale not enough to measure equity?

There are still shares to be returned. Their quantity q is negative; at price p, their value pq enters E = C + pq with that sign. A larger cash balance can therefore accompany the same equity.

Recall 3Under what conditions does an execution immediately preserve the equity of the ledger?

When the execution price and valuation price are identical, without fees or other flows: q′ = q + Δq and C′ = C − pΔq offset each other in C′ + pq′.

Recall 4How do you move from a current position to a target position?

Calculate the target minus the current position to obtain a requested change. Then apply to the ledger only the part actually executed, with the sign of its trade direction.

Recall 5Without a trade or other flows, how does a price change affect the ledger?

Quantity q and cash C remain constant. Only the valuation changes: ΔE = qΔp, where Δp is the new price minus the old price. A rise therefore hurts a negative quantity.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Reconstruct the path of the ledger without support: assign a sign to the holding; distinguish target, request, and execution; add the executed movement to the quantity; record the opposite flow in cash; value the holding with its sign. Recover the offset during a trade at the same price and the change qΔp when only the price changes. Finally, explain which purchase brings a short position back to flat.
