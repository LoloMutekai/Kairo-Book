Level 2 · C051·M5

Error diagnosis

Your goalDiagnose discretization error, rounding and a model limitation by checking the volume calculated for the same tank.

Before you start
c046-m5 : approximation error · c050-m5 : conditioning · c015-m5 : diagnosis · c036-m5 : annotated graphs · c051-m1 : floating-point numbers · c051-m2 : root finding · c051-m3 : interpolation · c051-m4 : numerical integration

✿01 See the idea

A tank receives water for 2 min. The flow rate increases as the tank fills. You want to know the volume added: on the flow rate graph, this is the area under the curve. The calculation replaces this curved area with trapezoids. Narrower trapezoids fit the graph more closely; they do not tell you whether the chosen flow rate law describes the tank accurately.

In this entirely fictional scene, we can calculate an exact reference value. It acts as a ruler for examining the method. The tank does not overflow, leak or lose water; the flow rate is assumed to be known without noise. None of the values is an actual reading.

Discretization replaces the continuous problem with pieces of width h, called the step size. Rounding replaces a number with one that can be represented at the chosen precision. Model error occurs when the law used does not represent the intended phenomenon. These causes can coexist: refining the step size does not change all of them.

Before calculating, predict what will remain invisible if the screen keeps only 1 decimal place. To investigate, you will need to keep the unrounded values, change the step size while holding the model fixed, then examine the model separately.

The rule to remember

State the target quantity and its tolerance; change one cause at a time; distinguish evidence of convergence from proof of accuracy.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

How accurate is the calculated volume?

The assumed flow rate is q(t) = a × t × t, with a = 1 L/(min·min·min), and filling runs from 0 to T = 2 min. We accept an absolute numerical error of at most ε = 0.05 L relative to the exact volume of this model. Fractions are kept until the result is displayed.

  1. Set the reference and define the errors

    t is the time in minutes since the start; T is the final duration. q(t) is the flow rate in L/min and a is its known coefficient. V denotes the exact volume added according to this law, in litres. An antiderivative of a × t × t is a × t × t × t / 3: its derivative gives the flow rate again. Its difference between the endpoints gives the integral, hence the volume.

    V=aT33=83 L≈2.6667 L

    T_h, with h as a subscript, will be the volume approximated by trapezoids of step size h, in litres. This symbol is distinct from the duration T. E_h = |T_h − V| is the absolute discretization error; the bars give the distance, without a sign. The relative error E_h/|V| is dimensionless, defined here because V is nonzero. ε is the absolute tolerance chosen for this exercise, rather than a guaranteed property of the method.

  2. Reconstruct the calculation with the coarse step size

    The trapezoidal rule averages the flow rates at the endpoints of each interval, then multiplies by its duration. When the intervals are added together, each interior flow rate contributes to the neighbouring trapezoids. This gives h × [(q(0) + q(T))/2 + sum of interior flow rates]. h must divide the duration T exactly in this formula.

    For h = 1 min, the flow rates are q(0) = 0, q(1 min) = 1 and q(2 min) = 4, all in L/min. The endpoint contribution is (0 + 4)/2 = 2 L/min. The only interior flow rate adds 1 L/min.

    Th=1×(2+1)=3 L

    Check using the separate areas: the first trapezoid has area 1 × (0 + 1)/2 L; the next has area 1 × (1 + 4)/2 L. Their sum gives 3 L again. The error is 3 − 8/3 = 1/3 L. Its positive sign agrees with the diagram.

  3. Divide the step size without changing the flow rate

    Now take h = 0.5 min. The interior times are 1/2, 1 and 3/2 min; the corresponding flow rates are 1/4, 1 and 9/4 L/min. Their sum is (1 + 4 + 9)/4 = 14/4 = 7/2 L/min.

    Th/2=0.5×(2+144)=11/4 L

    The h in the subscript here refers to the initial coarse step size. The sum in parentheses is 11/2 L/min. We obtain 2.75 L; the error relative to the reference is 1/12 L, or approximately 0.0833 L. The law and duration stay the same: this comparison isolates the change in discretization.

  4. Check the fine calculation against an explicit tolerance

    At step size h = 0.25 min, the squares of the interior times have the same denominator 16. Their numerators are 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36 and 49. Their sum gives 140, so the interior flow rates total 35/4 L/min.

    Tfin=14×(2+14016)=43/16 L

    The weighted sum is 43/4 L/min. The volume is 2.6875 L; its absolute error is 1/48 L, or approximately 0.0208 L. Since 1/48 < 1/20, the tolerance of 0.05 L is met for this model and this exact calculation.

    The relative error is 1/48/(8/3) = 1/128, or approximately 0.0078, with no unit. This ratio must not be compared with a tolerance in litres. The exact reference here supports a stronger conclusion than a simple resemblance between results.

  5. Interpret differences between refinements with their assumptions

    If V were unknown, we could still calculate the distances between successive results: 3 − 11/4 = 1/4 L, then 11/4 − 43/16 = 1/16 L. Their ratio is 4. Dividing the step size by 2 here accompanies a difference divided by 4.

    Why this pattern? If the dominant error term is C × h × h, with C independent of the step size over the range studied, then dividing h by 2 divides this term by 4. C has units L/(min·min). The difference between the coarse calculation and the next then equals 3 times the signed error of the next calculation. The same relation holds between the intermediate and fine calculations.

    Efin≈|Tmoyen−Tfin|22−1=1/163=1/48 L

    In our example, this estimate matches the error checked against V. For another curve, the relation requires smooth behaviour and a quadratic term that actually dominates; internal rounding or an irregular curve may disrupt it. An observed ratio alone does not prove these assumptions, and this estimate is not a general bound.

  6. Reveal the false plateau in the display

    Refine further to h = 0.125 min. The sum of the squares of the interior times is 1240/64, so the calculation gives 1/8 × (2 + 155/8) = 171/64 L, or 2.671875 L. Its error is 1/192 L, approximately 0.0052 L.

    Round the fine result 2.6875 L and this new result 2.671875 L to a display increment of 1/10 L: both display 2.7 L. The displayed difference is therefore 0 L, although the fractions differ. Rounding for presentation adds 1/80 L and 9/320 L respectively to the calculated numbers.

    The error of the display relative to V remains 1/30 L, approximately 0.0333 L; it is nonzero. Here we simulate decimal rounding for presentation. The internal calculations in this example are exact: this plateau does not measure any limitation of floating-point numbers on a machine. To investigate suspected internal rounding, we would compare different calculation precisions or algebraic expressions at a fixed step size, keeping the model unchanged.

  7. Test the model limitation, then state the diagnosis

    Suppose, still fictionally, that another law is possible: q_alt(t) = q(t) + b, with constant b = 0.1 L/min. b is a flow rate omitted from the first model. Its contribution is b × T = 0.1 × 2 = 0.2 L. The exact volume for this other law is V_alt = 43/15 L, approximately 2.8667 L.

    The same fine calculation based on the first law differs from V_alt by 43/240 L, approximately 0.1792 L. Reducing h brings this calculation closer to V, rather than V_alt. The model difference of 0.2 L therefore does not disappear with the trapezoids; the signed model and discretization errors can even partly cancel.

    The diagnosis has limits: discretization error checked to be below 0.05 L for the chosen law; false plateau explained by display rounding; physical suitability of the law unestablished. The alternative law is a calculable objection, rather than a reading from the tank. Choosing between the laws would require authorized observations and their uncertainty.

    Keep this procedure: specify the target and units, keep the numbers before rounding, compare step sizes without changing the law, check against an independent reference when one exists, then separate the numerical question from the model question. Do not change several settings together and then attribute the improvement to just one.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The decimal digits no longer change, so the error is 0.”

    Compare the fractions before rounding. 43/16 L and 171/64 L give the same display of 2.7 L. Stable text on the screen guarantees neither a small internal error nor a correct model.

    Revisit the false plateau in the display

  • “The difference between results at successive step sizes is exactly the error of the last calculation.”

    This difference compares approximations. A reference or a justified error law is needed to convert it into an error. Here, quadratic behaviour leads us to divide 1/16 L by 3, then the reference confirms the result.

    Revisit the assumptions behind diagnosis using refinements

  • “An even smaller step size will correct the omitted flow rate.”

    Changing h keeps the flow rate law. The omitted contribution of 0.2 L belongs to the model question. Return to the transformations in the diagram and name the one affected by the setting.

Compare with the solution

The differences are 1/4 L and 1/16 L; their ratio is 4. This agrees with the assumed quadratic behaviour.

Let e denote the signed error of the fine calculation. Under this dominant law, the error of the intermediate calculation is 4 × e. Their difference is therefore 3 × e: the absolute error estimate is 1/16/3 = 1/48 L, approximately 0.0208 L.

This result supports a conditional diagnosis. It proves neither that the quadratic term dominates at other step sizes, nor a universal bound, nor the flow rate law. Revealing the reference afterwards only confirms the error in our case.

Check your reasoning

  • The differences are calculated without reusing V.
  • The divisor 3 is explained by the difference between the assumed errors.
  • A conditional estimate is distinguished from a proven bound.

Repair an overconfident report

A report says: “At step sizes 0.25 and 0.125 min, the screen shows 2.7 L; the tank volume is therefore exact.” Repair this conclusion. Use the unrounded fractions, the tolerance of 0.05 L and the objection of an additional flow rate b = 0.1 L/min.

Hint 1

Separate what concerns the displayed text, discretization and the flow rate law.

Hint 2

An additional constant flow rate accumulates over the entire duration T.

Compare with the solution

The calculated values are 43/16 and 171/64 L. Their identical display comes from rounding to an increment of 1/10 L; the error of this displayed number relative to V is still 1/30 L.

The fine calculation has a discretization error of 1/48 L, below 0.05 L. This is proof for the assumed law and the exact arithmetic of the exercise, rather than proof that the physical volume is exact.

The alternative law adds 0.1 × 2 = 0.2 L and leads to 43/15 L. It shows why refining the step size does not settle the choice of model. A corrected report calls for observations with documented uncertainty to assess the law; it does not present this fictional objection as an observation.

Check your reasoning

  • Identical displays are distinguished from equal calculated numbers.
  • The tolerance is assigned to the correct error and reference.
  • The contribution of 0.2 L is justified and remains a fictional assumption.
  • The need for evidence about the phenomenon is stated without declaring an overall failure of the model.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What must be set before calling a calculation accurate?

The target quantity, its reference, the assumptions and the tolerance. The absolute error |T_h − V| is in litres; the relative error |T_h − V|/|V| is dimensionless and requires nonzero V. A mathematical reference validates a model calculation, rather than the phenomenon.

Recall 2How can discretization error and internal rounding be investigated separately?

For discretization, vary the step size with the model and arithmetic fixed. For suspected internal rounding, compare different calculation precisions or algebraic forms at a fixed step size. Keep the numbers before display rounding and change only one cause at a time.

Recall 3Why divide the difference between neighbouring calculations by 3 here?

With a dominant error term C × h × h and a step size divided by 2, the error of the coarse calculation is 4 times that of the next calculation. Their difference is therefore 3 times the latter error. Without justification for this law, the difference alone does not give the error.

Recall 4What does an identical display of 2.7 L fail to prove?

It does not prove that the results before rounding are equal: 43/16 and 171/64 L differ. Nor does it prove 0 error, a limitation of floating-point numbers on the machine or a correct flow rate law.

Recall 5Why does refining the trapezoids fail to correct an omitted flow rate?

The trapezoids approximate the integral of the given law. The omitted flow rate b would change this law and add b × T of extra volume. Information about the phenomenon is needed to assess the model; its numerical convergence is insufficient.
