Level 1 · C029·M3

Conditioning

Your goalChoose the right reference set to calculate a probability given some information, then check when this calculation is meaningful.

Before you start
c028-m5 : choice trees · c024-m5 : measurement quality · c029-m1 : events · c029-m2 : axioms

✿01 See the idea

In a fictional bicycle workshop, a box contains the repair records for the day. You draw one at random without reading it yet. You want to know whether it says “brakes”. Then someone gives you reliable information about this same record: it is for the morning. Should you still compare the “brakes” records with the whole day?

Imagine the records laid out on a table. To answer given “morning”, cover up the afternoon records. Among the records that remain possible, count those that say “brakes”. You have not changed any repairs: the available information has changed the reference set for your calculation.

The batch is entirely invented: in the morning, 6 records say “brakes” and 2 say “lights”; in the afternoon, the respective counts are 3 and 9. Each record has a single time slot and a single main task. A record represents a booking, which does not necessarily mean a different bicycle.

We assume that each record has the same probability of being drawn and that the time slot announcement is accurate. In this model, counting records is enough to calculate probabilities. These fictional proportions are neither observations from a real workshop nor predictions of its next bookings.

The rule to remember

Given B, keep only what is compatible with B. Among these possibilities, find those that also satisfy A, then divide their probability by that of B. B must have a strictly positive probability.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Brakes, given that the record is from the morning

A record is drawn uniformly from the batch described above. The announcement “this record is from the morning” is reliable. Let us calculate the probability that it says “brakes”, distinguishing this calculation from the probability of drawing a record that is both morning and brakes.

  1. Name the events and the units

    Ω, the letter omega, denotes the set of all possible records. A is the event “the record says brakes”; B is the event “the record is for the morning”. A ∩ B reads “A and B”: the same record satisfies both descriptions.

    P(A) denotes the probability of A in the initial draw. P(A | B) reads “probability of A given B”; the vertical bar introduces the information taken as certain. Counts are expressed in records. Probabilities are numbers without units between 0 and 1.

    The draw is uniform: each record has the same chance. The announcement concerns the record already drawn, with no classification error or further selection linked to the repair. These assumptions justify using counts in place of probabilities.

  2. Check the relevant counts

    The morning contains 6 + 2 = 8 records; the afternoon contains 3 + 9 = 12. The total is therefore 8 + 12 = 20 records.

    For A alone, add the brakes records from both time slots: 6 + 3 = 9 records. For A ∩ B, keep only the morning brakes cell: 6 records. The word “and” narrows the possibilities retained.

    Cross-check the total using a ranking by repair type: lights account for 2 + 9 = 11 records. Brakes and lights together account for 9 + 11 = 20 records, with none missed or counted twice.

  3. Calculate before receiving the information

    Before the announcement, each probability refers to the whole batch. For brakes, we get P(A) = 9/20 = 0.45, or 45 %. For morning, P(B) = 8/20 = 2/5 = 0.40.

    The probability of drawing a record that is both morning and brakes is P(A ∩ B) = 6/20 = 3/10 = 0.30. This question is asked before knowing whether B occurs. Its denominator counts the whole day.

  4. Restrict the set, then rescale the reference to unity

    For an event B with strictly positive probability, conditioning is defined by:

    P(A|B)=P(A∩B)P(B), avec P(B)>0

    In the numerator, the intersection removes cases incompatible with the information. In the denominator, P(B) becomes the new reference: dividing by this quantity gives a total probability mass equal to 1 on B. This rescaling to unity is called renormalization.

    P(A|B)=620820=68=0.75

    The same factor 1/20, assigned to each record, cancels out. The ratio of counts therefore gives 3/4 = 0.75, or 75 %. Converting to a percentage multiplies the proportion by 100. If the records were not equally likely, the definition using P would still apply, but counting records might no longer be enough.

  5. Check the new sample space

    Within the morning, the records that do not say brakes are exactly the lights records. We write Aᶜ for the complement of A: here, “not brakes”. P(Aᶜ | B) = 2/8 = 1/4.

    P(A|B)+P(Ac|B)=0.75+0.25=1

    The superscript c means “complement”, not a numerical power. The two shares do complement each other within B. Another check: P(B | B) = 8/8 = 1. Given morning, morning is certain. We do not physically remove records from the box: we describe the same draw with additional information.

    The result 0.75 exceeds the initial probability 0.45 because brakes make up a larger share of the morning than of the whole day. This does not mean that the time slot causes the repair.

  6. Recognize the limit of the definition

    The batch contains no night records. Let C be the event “the record is for the night”; P(C) = 0/20 = 0. The preceding formula does not define P(A | C), because its denominator would be 0.

    Do not replace an undefined conditional probability with 0. A 0 numerator with a positive denominator does give 0; a 0 denominator prevents this division. In this finite sample space where each record has a positive probability, a “night” announcement requires checking the announcement or the model before continuing.

Explain it in your own words

Cover the formulas and explain why the reference set changes from 20 to 8 records. Then reconstruct the division of probabilities, the ratio of counts, and the check using complementary shares. Finally, explain why a “night” announcement poses a different problem from a repair that is absent.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “Given morning, I keep the whole-day total in the denominator.”

    6/20 calculates morning and brakes before any information. Given morning, the reference set is B: only its 8 records. Draw a box around the morning before dividing; you get 6/8.

    Review restriction and rescaling to unity

  • “Brakes given morning is the same as morning given brakes.”

    The word after “given” selects the reference set. Morning contains 8 records; brakes contains 9. The same intersection counts 6 records, but its two denominators give 3/4 and 2/3. Rephrase each question using “among”.

    Compare the two questions in the interpretation exercise

  • “If the information has 0 probability, the answer is 0.”

    If P(B) is 0, it is the denominator that is 0. The quotient then has no value under this definition. Return to the batch and ask how the announcement could have been produced; do not complete the calculation with a substitute value.

The complementary repair

In the same uniform draw, you know that the record is from the morning. What is the probability that it says “lights”? Write the counts for the numerator and denominator, then check using the brakes share.

Hint 1

Start by saying “among the morning records”. The afternoon records no longer answer the question.

Hint 2

In this batch, brakes and lights are the only possible main tasks, and they cannot both occur on the same record.

Compare with the solution

Morning provides the reference set of 8 records. Of these, 2 say lights: the requested probability is 2/8 = 1/4 = 0.25, without units.

The check gives 3/4 + 1/4 = 1. Adding morning and afternoon lights records would have kept records incompatible with the information.

Check your reasoning

  • The denominator is the morning count, justified by “given”.
  • The numerator counts lights in the morning only.
  • The sum of the two conditional shares is 1.

Exercise 2 · Application · read and reverse the question

Same cell, different reference sets

Using the same batch, calculate P(B | A), the probability of morning given that the record says brakes. Compare it with P(A | B), then with P(A ∩ B). Write each question starting with “among” and name the denominator.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

For P(B | A), the reference set is all the brakes records, across both time slots.

Hint 2

For “morning and brakes” with no prior information, the reference set returns to the whole day.

Compare with the solution

Among the 9 brakes records, 6 are from the morning. Therefore P(B | A) = 6/9 = 2/3 ≈ 0.6667.

Among the 8 morning records, 6 say brakes: P(A | B) = 6/8 = 3/4. The two values differ, even though the numerator counts the same cell.

Among all 20 records, 6 are both morning and brakes: P(A ∩ B) = 6/20 = 3/10. The “given” bar and the “and” symbol ask distinct questions; copying only the numerator is not enough to select a probability.

Check your reasoning

  • The reference sets brakes, morning, and whole day are named in that order.
  • The three quotients share the same intersection, with different denominators.
  • The rounded value of 2/3 is still identified as an approximation.

Exercise 3 · Diagnostic · decide whether the calculation is allowed

An announcement incompatible with the batch

The same box contains only morning and afternoon time slots. Yet an announcement claims “the record is for the night”. One person proposes P(A | C) = 0; another proposes P(A | C) = 9/20 by keeping the initial calculation. Can either answer be justified using the model on this page? What should be checked?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

First calculate the probability of C, the announced information, before attempting to condition on it.

Hint 2

A fallback value chosen by a program is an additional decision; it does not make division by 0 valid.

Compare with the solution

The batch gives P(C) = 0/20 = 0. The definition P(A | C) = P(A ∩ C)/P(C) therefore does not apply. Neither proposed value is justified as a conditional probability by this model.

The value 0 claims that brakes are impossible after accepting some information. Here, the information itself is incompatible with the batch. The value 9/20 returns to a question without that information; this choice would ignore the announcement and would need a separate justification.

Check whether the announcement is wrong or the description of the box is incomplete. Without new evidence, report that the calculation is undefined. If the drawing procedure also changed, its probabilities would need to be known before using ratios of counts.

Check your reasoning

  • P(C) is calculated before any attempt at division.
  • The result remains undefined within the stated model.
  • A concrete check of the announcement or the batch is proposed, without inventing records.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does “brakes given morning” mean in the batch of records?

Among records compatible with morning, find the share that also say brakes. Morning becomes the reference set; we do not keep the whole day in the denominator.

Recall 2What is the definition of P(A | B), including its condition of use?

P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B)/P(B), with P(B) strictly positive. The intersection keeps compatible cases; the division rescales their mass to the reference set B.

Recall 3When can a ratio of probabilities be replaced with a ratio of counts?

When the outcomes of the finite model are equally likely and B contains at least one of them. The same weight per record cancels out; without this assumption, their probabilities must be added.

Recall 4Why does swapping A and B generally change the question?

The intersection stays the same, but the reference set becomes A instead of B. In the batch: 6/8 = 3/4 for brakes given morning, compared with 6/9 = 2/3 for morning given brakes.

Recall 5What is the difference between a 0 numerator and a 0 denominator?

With a positive denominator, a 0 numerator gives a 0 conditional probability. With a 0 denominator, this definition provides no value; the information or the model must be reviewed.
