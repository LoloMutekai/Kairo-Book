Level 2 · C022·M3

Evidence of consent and revocation

Your goalJustify that an approval covers the exact request, then check whether it still authorizes that request after withdrawal.

Before you start
c021-m5 : origin, authentication and authorization · c018-m5 : contract tests and test doubles · c012-m5 : counterexamples · c022-m1 : asset, threat and scope · c022-m2 : identity and rights

✿01 See the idea

Camille is preparing the plan for a community garden. She asks an assistant to copy the reviewed version of plan-jardin.pdf to the Club folder. She specifies “one copy only”. Then she changes her mind before copying starts. The assistant still has the approval message: can it still use it?

The message can prove that approval was given. It is not enough to show that the approval still applies. Evidence of consent links an authorized person, their affirmative answer and a precise request. Revocation means withdrawing that approval for future uses. Keeping a record of the past is compatible with removing current permission.

Everything that follows is fictional: the document, people, events and durations. In this scene, Camille can authorize the copy. Her channel is authenticated: the system knows who sent the message, rather than trusting a name typed in the text. A receipt means the record of this approval together with its provenance. An assistant writing “Camille said yes” itself does not provide that provenance.

We study only the validity of this specific approval. The copy remains subject to the other rights already checked. The course covers neither the law of consent nor the technical construction of an authentication channel.

The rule to remember

Link the approval to the exact request, then check its validity again when the action is to start: time, withdrawal and prior use matter separately.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

The receipt still exists; what does it authorize now?

At t = 2 min, Camille approves a single copy of the reviewed plan to Club, for 12 min. The system examines the request at 6 min without executing it. The withdrawal is recorded at 8 min, then the same request returns at 10 min. All times use the same fictional clock; no copy has taken place yet.

  1. Build evidence that covers the right request

    The request describes the authorized actor, the operation “copy without modifying”, the reviewed version of plan-jardin.pdf and the destination Club. The receipt accord-jardin retains this request, Camille’s affirmative answer, the reference to her message in the authenticated channel, its issue time and its duration. The reference allows the source message to be retrieved; it does not provide authentication on its own.

    We assume that the assistant cannot replace the receipt or the document version, and that the withdrawal status being checked is up to date. If the document or destination changes, it is no longer the approved request. An old “yes” detached from its question is not enough.

  2. Define the rule before inserting numbers

    t is the time of the check, in minutes since the scene began; a is the approval time, d its duration and f its scheduled end, also in minutes. We choose the interval a ≤ t < f: the check must take place at or after approval and before the end. The end boundary is excluded. This convention belongs to the example, not to all software.

    The following indicators are dimensionless: 1 means that the fact is established, 0 that it is not. E means “affirmative receipt with verified provenance, issued by the authorized person”; P means “request identical to the approved request”; T(t) means “time within the interval”; R(t) means “receipt revoked”; U(t) means “receipt already used”. The letters in parentheses remind us that these facts can change with t.

    A(t)=E×P×T(t)×(1−R(t))×(1−U(t))

    A(t), which is dimensionless, represents only “this approval authorizes this request at this time”. The product requires all checks to pass: a 0 factor gives 0, and all factors equal to 1 give 1. This encoding is neither a probability nor a measure of the degree of consent. A field merely being filled in never establishes a favorable indicator.

  3. Calculate the validity interval

    Add the duration to the issue time to obtain a boundary, rather than treating a duration as a point in time:

    f=a+d=2+12=14 min

    At 6 min, the receipt’s age is 6 − 2 = 4 min. There are 14 − 6 = 8 min left before the scheduled end. Check both boundaries: 2 ≤ 6 < 14, so T(t) = 1. At exactly 14 min, T(t) would equal 0.

  4. Check before withdrawal and distinguish approval from action

    At 6 min, E = 1, P = 1 and T(t) = 1. The receipt has been neither revoked nor used: R(t) = 0 and U(t) = 0. The complements are 1 − 0 = 1 and 1 − 0 = 1.

    First check evidence and scope: 1 × 1 = 1. Then the time window: 1 × 1 = 1. Finally withdrawal and use: 1 × 1 = 1, then 1 × 1 = 1. This approval would permit the copy, assuming the other rights checks have passed.

    Here we examine the request without starting the copy or consuming the receipt. The favorable result is therefore not evidence that a file was copied. Before later execution, the check must be repeated using the current state.

  5. Apply withdrawal to the next request

    At 8 min, the authenticated channel receives “I withdraw approval accord-jardin” from Camille. The system marks this receipt as revoked. It keeps both the initial message and the withdrawal: erasing history is not necessary to remove future permission.

    At 10 min, the age is 10 − 2 = 8 min and there are 14 − 10 = 4 min left. The time window is therefore still open: T(t) = 1. Evidence and scope remain valid, but R(t) = 1; its complement becomes 1 − 1 = 0.

    The calculation repeats the same checks: 1 × 1 = 1, then 1 × 0 = 0 and 0 × 1 = 0. Copying must not start under this receipt. This result comes from withdrawal, not from expiry or prior use.

  6. Check another way and identify the limit

    Without multiplying, reread the sequence of facts: approval given, no use, withdrawal recorded, new request. A new copy would contradict the latest state expressed by Camille. This chronological reading gives the same result, 0. An independent time check gives 4 + 8 = 12 min and 8 + 4 = 12 min; the durations are consistent on both sides of withdrawal.

    Revocation acts here before copying starts. It does not delete an already copied file or, on its own, cancel an action in progress. In a real concurrent system, checking and then waiting would allow a withdrawal to occur between check and action: the start must be coordinated with verification and single use. We do not implement that mechanism in this unit.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the calculations. Explain why the same request is authorized at the first check and then blocked at the next, even though the receipt and its end time have not changed. What observation would still be missing before you could claim that a copy took place?

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “It says “Camille: yes”, so I have evidence.”

    Use the authenticated channel to establish who sent this message, then find the exact request it answers. Text fabricated by the assistant can imitate a name and an answer without proving their provenance.

    Review what the receipt links

  • “There are 4 min left, so withdrawal can wait.”

    The open time window establishes only T(t). Withdrawal changes R(t) independently: its complement becomes 0, which is enough to block the action. Recalculate the factors after withdrawal.

    Repeat the calculation before and after withdrawal

  • “Revoking means erasing the approval and undoing the copy.”

    Withdrawal prevents future uses of this receipt in our model. The initial record can be retained to understand past decisions. Removing an already produced copy would require a separate operation whose effect would need to be verified.

    Reread the scope of withdrawal

Exercise 1 · Identify the relevant evidence

The right document, a different destination

At 6 min, before any withdrawal or use, the assistant wants to copy the same plan to the Public folder. It presents the receipt authorizing Club. Which condition fails? Give the decision and the remedy.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Compare the requested destination with the one Camille approved; a correct identity does not change that destination.

Compare with the solution

The receipt does come from Camille: E = 1. But the scope does not match: P = 0, because Public differs from Club. E × P = 0, then 0 × 1 = 0. The factors for absence of withdrawal and use equal 1 and do not change this result.

Either return to the request for Club or present the modified request to Camille and obtain new, verifiable approval. Replacing the word Club in the existing receipt would destroy the link with what she actually approved.

Check your reasoning

  • Identify the destination as the difference in scope.
  • Retain the correct provenance without inferring broader permission from it.
  • Propose new approval for the new request without altering the old receipt.

Exercise 2 · Calculate and distinguish causes

No withdrawal, but after the scheduled end

A variation on the same scene: Camille has not withdrawn approval and no copy has taken place. The exact request returns at 15 min. Calculate the scheduled end, the time past it and the decision. What would happen exactly at the end boundary?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Start again from the approval time and the duration; the end boundary is excluded even if no withdrawal has taken place.

Compare with the solution

The end remains 2 + 12 = 14 min. The time past it is 15 − 14 = 1 min. This time is outside the interval: T(t) = 0, while R(t) = 0 and U(t) = 0.

E × P × T(t) = 1 × 0 = 0. With no withdrawal, 0 × 1 = 0; the absence of use changes nothing. The cause is expiry, distinct from revocation. At exactly 14 min, the receipt has already expired under the chosen convention. New approval is needed for a later copy.

Check your reasoning

  • Add the time and duration, then subtract comparable times.
  • Give durations in minutes and the decision without a unit.
  • Distinguish expiry from withdrawal and handle equality at the boundary.

Exercise 3 · Diagnose an outdated decision

The displayed approval still indicates permission

Return to the main timeline. A screen retains the favorable decision calculated at 6 min. At 10 min, after withdrawal at 8 min, the copying service reads only this screen. Explain the defect and propose a check with expected observations. Final variation: if the single copy had already used the receipt before withdrawal, could it be repeated?

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Ask which state was used for the displayed verdict and which state must govern starting now.

Hint 2

For the variation, change only U(t); a consumed single-use approval does not become available again because its time window is still valid.

Compare with the solution

The displayed verdict describes the check at 6 min, not the state at 10 min. The current withdrawal requires R(t) = 1 and A(t) = 0. The remedy is to consult the current revocation state at the start and coordinate this check with taking action, rather than treating an old display as lasting permission.

A contract check can use a fake copier that counts its calls without copying a file. In separate trials, an identical request before withdrawal must pass the consent check; after withdrawal it must be refused and leave the fake copier with no calls. Keep the approval and withdrawal references, the verdict and the call count. Testing only the screen text does not verify that the consumer respects the refusal. This check prepared here has not been run on a real service.

In the prior-use variation, U(t) = 1: availability is 1 − 1 = 0. Even starting again from the other favorable conditions of the first check, 1 × 0 = 0. The receipt permits no new copy. Withdrawal does not remove the copy that has already taken place.

Check your reasoning

  • Link each verdict to the state and time actually checked.
  • Verify refusal and the absence of copier calls after withdrawal separately.
  • Distinguish withdrawal, single use and cancellation of a past effect.
  • Keep an answer obtained after a hint classified as an assisted answer.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What must be linked for the receipt to constitute evidence of approval in our scene?

The authorized person, their affirmative answer with authenticated provenance and the precise request: actor, operation, document in its approved version, destination and limits on use. The message reference makes this provenance verifiable; Camille’s name alone does not authenticate it.

Recall 2Why might an unexpired receipt no longer authorize the copy?

Camille may have revoked it, or the single use may have been consumed. The time window, withdrawal and use are checked separately when the new action is to take place.

Recall 3Why retain an old approval after its withdrawal?

It explains a past decision. Withdrawal changes its validity for future uses; it does not require erasing this history and does not, on its own, undo an already produced copy.

Recall 4Under our convention, is the exact end time still authorized?

No: the interval includes the issue time and excludes the end. Both boundaries must be checked on the same clock, independently of any withdrawal.

Recall 5What is missing between valid approval and evidence that a copy occurred?

The executing service must respect the current check, and the effect must then be verified. An old favorable decision proves neither current authorization nor the copy. In particular, a withdrawal must reach the check on the path before the action.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without rereading, reconstruct Camille’s receipt, then the approval–check–withdrawal–new request timeline. Define the indicators again and recover the validity product. Explain separately why another destination, expiry or prior use would invalidate the receipt. Finish with the difference between authorization, a copier call and a verified effect.
