Level 2 · C098·M1

Chronology, delay and available information

Your goalReconstruct what a decision could use at the time it was made, then place its application after an explicit delay.

Before you start
c097-m5 : risk and horizon · c067-m5 : pipeline · c090-m5 : stationarity · c037-m5 : controlled cleaning

✿01 See the idea

In a fictional greenhouse, a sensor sends its readings to a ventilation controller. A packet may have to wait before it can be used. The temperature recorded on the packet tells us what the sensor measured; it does not tell us when the controller could read it.

At minute 5, you must make a decision. Reading B concerns minute 4, but it only becomes usable at minute 7. In the complete archive, its value of 28 °C is visible. An honest replay of the decision nevertheless requires hiding it until it arrives.

The diagram shows the archive after all the packets have been received. To return to minute 5, you therefore need to reconstruct the information accessible at that time. Sorting only by reading times is not enough: B already describes the past, while remaining unknown to the controller.

We follow only this greenhouse and this fictional rule: request ventilation if the most recent admissible reading reaches 25 °C. This threshold illustrates a calculation; it is not a recommended setting. The result of the decision can only be applied after 0.5 min. No effect on temperature is assumed.

The rule to remember

Filter by availability time, then choose the reading according to a fixed rule, and assign separate times to the decision and its application. An archive expanded later does not rewrite what was known earlier.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

What the controller could decide at 5 min

Fictional archive data: A, measured at 0 min, is 20 °C and waits 1 min; B, measured at 4 min, is 28 °C and waits 3 min; C, measured at 6 min, is 24 °C with no waiting time. The rule and the threshold of 25 °C are fixed before the replay. The decision takes place at 5 min; its application follows after 1/2 min, or 0.5 min. A check is scheduled at 8 min.

  1. Give the times a meaning

    For a reading named i, eᵢ is its reading time; dᵢ is its delay until use; aᵢ is its availability time; vᵢ is its temperature, in °C. The index i identifies A, B or C and has no unit. All times and durations are in minutes from startup, on the same clock. The delays dᵢ are positive or 0.

    Availability here includes receiving, checking and preparing the value. Data that have been received but cannot yet be used are therefore not available. Temperatures are not revised afterwards; the reading times are distinct. If there were a later correction, it would have its own availability time.

    t is the decision time. We process arrivals timestamped t before the decision: a value available exactly at t can be used. This convention must be stated; timestamps that are too coarse do not let us infer this order.

  2. Calculate when each value becomes usable

    The delay starts at the reading time: addition therefore gives the availability time, without changing the temperature.

    ai=ei+di

    A: 0 + 1 = 1 min. B: 4 + 3 = 7 min. C: 6 + 0 = 6 min. C therefore becomes usable before B, even though it was measured later.

  3. Reconstruct the accessible information

    I(t), read as “information at time t”, denotes the set of available readings. The braces group identifiers; the bar is read as “such that”; ≤ means “less than or equal to”. In our scene, the following condition is enough because availability already includes all the necessary preparation.

    I(t)={i|ai≤t}

    A is admitted because 1 ≤ 5. B is excluded because 7 > 5; it needs another 7 − 5 = 2 min to become available. C is excluded because 6 > 5; the reading itself is still 6 − 5 = 1 min in the future. So I(t) contains only A.

    The trap is B: 4 < 5 describes a past reading, but does not prove 7 ≤ 5. Using B because it appears in the archive would let later information enter the past decision.

  4. Apply the rule to the right value

    Among the readings in I(t), the rule selects the one with the greatest reading time eᵢ. “Most recent” refers to the reading, after filtering for availability. If I(t) were empty, the result would be “insufficient information”, without arbitrarily substituting a temperature.

    At 5 min, A is the only admissible reading. Its temperature is 20 °C. Its difference from the threshold is 20 − 25 = -5 °C, which is negative: the rule does not request activation. This says neither whether ventilation is already on nor whether the greenhouse is actually cool.

    Its reading is 5 − 0 = 5 min old. Available means usable, without promising that this old value still describes the current temperature. Reading B would give a difference of 28 − 25 = 3 °C and change the decision, using a value still unknown at that time.

  5. Place application after the decision

    ℓ, the letter ell, denotes the delay between the decision and its application; x is the application time. This delay is distinct from the reception delay dᵢ of a reading. We assume transmission always succeeds and the delay is fixed: these are simulation assumptions.

    ℓ=12 min;x=t+ℓ

    The result of the rule can therefore be applied at 5 + 1/2 = 11/2 min, or 5.5 min. We have calculated a scheduled time without observing a device. Subsequently replacing the inputs to this decision with packets that arrived later would change the replay.

  6. Distinguish the horizons

    r is the time of the scheduled check, here 8 min. h is the horizon from the decision; q is the time remaining after application. They are expressed in minutes. The check does not yet provide any known temperature.

    h=r−t;q=r−x=h−ℓ

    From the decision: 8 − 5 = 3 min. After application: 8 − 5.5 = 2.5 min, or exactly 5/2 min. Claiming that 3 min are available after application would overlook the delay of 0.5 min.

  7. Check the calculation by another route

    Let us replay the events instead of sorting the readings: A becomes ready at 1 min; at the decision time of 5 min, B is not yet ready and C has not yet been measured. We find A again without consulting the excluded temperatures.

    Duration check: 5 + 0 = 5 min recovers the decision time. 5 + 2 = 7 min recovers the arrival time of B. Finally, 1/2 + 5/2 = 3 min recovers the total time until the check. In each sum, the terms are in minutes.

    To make the replay verifiable, keep the decision time, the admitted readings with their times, the fixed rule and the scheduled application time. Later reception of a packet expands the archive; it does not alter the inputs to the decision already made.

Explain it in your own words

Hide the calculations. Why is B excluded even though its reading time precedes the decision? Then reconstruct the difference between availability delay, application delay and the horizon to the check.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The reading is dated before my decision, so I could use it.”

    Separate the reading time eᵢ from the availability time aᵢ. For B, 4 < 5 is true, but so is 7 > 5. Recalculate availability, then filter again before looking at its temperature.

    Repeat the calculation of accessible information

  • “The last packet received must contain the most recent reading.”

    B arrives after C, but its reading is older. After filtering the available values, compare their reading times. The exercise on the late arrival requires you to distinguish these two orders.

    Replay the late arrival of B

  • “I can apply immediately and fill in the inputs while waiting.”

    Set the application time using x = t + ℓ and keep the inputs to the decision at t. A decision recalculated after a new arrival must have its own time. The second diagram separates the steps; bringing application forward or changing the inputs changes the simulated assumption.

Exercise 1 · Identify and calculate

A past reading, still unknown

Return to B: its reading time is 4 min and its availability delay is 3 min. Calculate its availability time and its remaining wait at t = 5 min. Someone uses 28 °C to request activation at that time: explain exactly what invalidates their reasoning.

Hint 1

The delay starts at the reading time. Add before comparing with the decision time.

Hint 2

A temperature visible in the final archive may have been unknown at the time being replayed.

Compare with the solution

The availability time is 4 + 3 = 7 min. The remaining wait at the decision time is 7 − 5 = 2 min. Check: decision 5 min + wait 2 min = availability 7 min.

B concerns the past but arrives after the decision. Its value of 28 °C does exceed 25 °C; however, this comparison is not admissible at 5 min. The correct replay selects A, 20 °C, and does not request activation. Delaying only application without changing the decision time would not make B admissible retroactively.

Check your reasoning

  • You obtain 7 min for availability and 2 min for the remaining wait, stating their starting points.
  • You justify exclusion by availability after the decision, rather than by temperature.
  • You keep A and the decision corresponding to the initial time.

Exercise 2 · Apply a convention

An arrival exactly at the decision time

A new decision takes place at t = 6 min, with the same packets and the same rule. Arrivals are processed before the decision. Which readings are admissible? Which one is selected, and what request follows? What could you conclude if the order of events within that minute were unknown?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The inequality allows equality, under the stated convention.

Hint 2

Once you have found the admissible readings, compare their reading times. B only becomes ready at 7 min.

Compare with the solution

A is already available and C becomes ready at 6 min, exactly at the decision time. The convention allows C. B remains excluded because 7 > 6. The admissible inputs are A and C.

C has the most recent reading time, 6 min. Its difference from the threshold is 24 − 25 = -1 °C. It is negative: the new decision does not request activation.

If the order of arrival and decision were unknown, we could not assert that C was usable. We would need a more precise record or a conservative convention stated before the replay. Identical timestamps do not prove the actual order of operations.

Check your reasoning

  • You name A and C as admissible, using the stated convention.
  • You select C for its reading time, then compare its temperature with the threshold.
  • You leave the admission of C undetermined when the actual order has not been established.

Exercise 3 · Transfer and check

B arrives after a more recent reading

At t = 7 min, B has just arrived, after C. All readings are now admissible. Someone proposes replacing C with B because “it is the last packet”. Find the reading that the rule must select, compare the ages of B and C, then calculate the application time with the same delay and the time remaining before the check fixed at 8 min.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The rule selects the greatest reading time among the available readings, rather than the latest reception time.

Hint 2

The age of a reading is the decision time minus its reading time. For application, start from this new decision.

Hint 3

The check remains fixed at 8 min. Subtract the new application time to find the remaining time.

Compare with the solution

The reading times are 0, 4 and 6 min: C remains the most recent. At 7 min, its age is 7 − 6 = 1 min; the age of B is 7 − 4 = 3 min. Arriving last has not made reading B any younger.

The rule therefore selects C at 24 °C and does not request activation, because 24 < 25. Choosing B at 28 °C would request activation: that would be a different rule. This new state of the archive does not change the past decision at 5 min either, which used A.

The new application is scheduled at 7 + 1/2 = 15/2 min, or 7.5 min. The remaining time is 8 − 15/2 = 1/2 min, or 0.5 min. The horizon from the new decision is 8 − 7 = 1 min.

Check by decomposition: delay 1/2 min + remaining time 1/2 min = horizon 1 min. This result measures no ventilation effect and still does not provide the temperature at the check.

Check your reasoning

  • You select C despite the later reception of B, justifying the ages of 1 min and 3 min.
  • You distinguish the fixed rule from selecting the last packet and keep the initial decision in its context.
  • You obtain 7.5 min and 0.5 min, then recover 1 min by adding the durations.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1Which times must be distinguished for a delayed reading?

The reading time eᵢ describes the event; the availability time aᵢ says when the value is ready for use. The delay dᵢ links them through aᵢ = eᵢ + dᵢ, on the same clock.

Recall 2How can you reconstruct the inputs usable at decision time t?

Select the readings whose availability time aᵢ is at most t. Here availability includes reception and preparation, and arrivals at t precede the decision. The reading time alone is not enough.

Recall 3Why might the last packet fail to provide the reading to select?

Packets can arrive out of order. The greenhouse rule chooses the most recent reading time among the readings already available; B can arrive after C without becoming more recent.

Recall 4What is the difference between availability delay and application delay?

The first runs from the reading to its availability. The second, ℓ, runs from decision time t to application time x = t + ℓ. The inputs to a decision remain those admitted at t; a later recalculation has a new time.

Recall 5How can you check the durations until a check scheduled at r?

The horizon from the decision is h = r − t. The time remaining after application is q = r − x. If application precedes the check, delay ℓ + remaining time q = horizon h. You must state the starting point of each duration.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Reconstruct without looking: draw the reading and availability times; place the decision; eliminate what was not yet usable; choose the reading according to the fixed rule; add the application delay; break down the time until the check. Then explain why a late arrival does not change the inputs to a past decision.
