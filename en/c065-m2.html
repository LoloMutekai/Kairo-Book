Level 1 · C065·M2

Correlation

Your goalCalculate a Pearson correlation from paired observations and explain what its sign, magnitude, and limitations allow us to say.

Before you start
c040-m5 : least squares · c056-m5 : effect size · c037-m5 : controlled cleaning · c065-m1 : regression

✿01 See the idea

At a sorting station in a workshop, the number of bins completed and the total duration are recorded for each session. Are sessions in which more bins are sorted also the longest? Look at the scatter plot before looking for a straight line: each point keeps the count and duration from the same session together.

Our data are entirely fictional: A pairs 1 bin with 4 min; B, 2 bins with 8 min; C, 3 bins with 6 min; D, 4 bins with 10 min. The bins are not assumed to have identical contents. We are describing these sessions without claiming to measure actual productivity.

The scatter plot rises overall. Yet C processes more bins than B in less time: the association does not form a perfect straight line. A Pearson correlation summarizes the direction and strength of this linear association, meaning how closely the points line up along a straight line.

The idea is to compare each session with the means of the data. Deviations with the same sign reinforce each other; deviations with opposite signs offset each other. We then divide their combined total by a measure of the spread of both quantities to obtain a coefficient with no units.

The rule to remember

Keep the pairs together, center the values, sum the products of deviations, then normalize using the spreads. A coefficient describes a linear relationship; it does not demonstrate its cause.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Summarize the data without losing the pairs

Using the 4 fictional sessions A to D, let us calculate r, the Pearson coefficient between the number of bins and the duration. The units and pairings stay fixed throughout the calculation.

  1. Define the quantities and the scope of the calculation

    n is the number of sessions; i identifies a session, from 1 to n. xᵢ is its number of bins, yᵢ its duration in minutes. A pair (xᵢ, yᵢ) must come from the same session. All values are finite, present, and expressed in the same units from one row to the next; each session receives the same weight.

    x̄ and ȳ, read as “x bar” and “y bar,” are the arithmetic means: the sum of the values divided by n. uᵢ = xᵢ − x̄ is a deviation in bins; vᵢ = yᵢ − ȳ is a deviation in minutes. Σ, read as “sum,” adds the indicated term over all sessions.

    At least 2 pairs and variation in each quantity are needed for r to be defined. The descriptive calculation requires neither a normal distribution nor independence between sessions. Claiming that these data represent all workshop sessions would require a study of how the sessions were selected and how they depend on each other; we are not making that inference here.

  2. Find the centers for comparison

    The sum of the counts is (1 + 2) + (3 + 4) = 3 + 7 = 10 bins. The sum of the durations is (4 + 8) + (6 + 10) = 12 + 16 = 28 min. Dividing by 4 gives the centers to subtract.

    x¯=104=2.5 bacs ; y¯=284=7 min

    Check: each mean lies between the minimum and maximum of its quantity. The mean count can be fractional: this does not mean that a session actually sorted a fraction of a bin.

  3. Center each pair

    For A, u = 1 − 2.5 = -1.5 bin and v = 4 − 7 = -3 min. Repeat these subtractions for each row: we now compare positions “above or below the mean,” rather than raw values.

    Séancex (bacs)y (min)u (bacs)v (min)A14-1.5-3B28-0.51C360.5-1D4101.53

    Check: -1.5 + (-0.5) + 0.5 + 1.5 = 0 bin; -3 + 1 + (-1) + 3 = 0 min. The deviations from their own mean must sum to 0.

  4. Measure the combined total and the spreads

    Multiply u by v in each row. For A, (-1.5) × (-3) = 4.5 bin·min: below both means, the session reinforces an increasing association. For B, (-0.5) × 1 = -0.5 bin·min: being below the mean count but above the mean duration goes in the opposite direction. A product of two negative numbers is positive.

    Also calculate the squares of u and v, which are always positive or 0. They measure the size of the deviations for each quantity, regardless of their sign. In the formulas below, the exponent 2 means multiplying the quantity by itself.

    SéanceCarré de u (bac²)Carré de v (min²)uv (bac·min)A2.2594.5B0.251-0.5C0.251-0.5D2.2594.5

    Let Sxx be the sum of the squares of u, Syy the sum of the squares of v, and Sxy the sum of the products uv. The letters xx, yy, and xy indicate which quantities are multiplied; they are neither units nor new observations. Rows A and D give the same terms, as do B and C: we can therefore double the partial sums.

    Sxx=Σui2=2×(2.25+0.25)=2×2.5=5 bac²
    Syy=Σvi2=2×(9+1)=2×10=20 min²
    Sxy=Σuivi=2×(4.5+(-0.5))=2×4=8 bac·min

  5. Normalize to remove the units

    Sxy alone changes if we change the unit of duration. The denominator d = √(Sxx × Syy) combines the two spreads. √ denotes the positive square root; d is in bin·min, like Sxy. Dividing one by the other gives r with no units.

    r=SxySxx×Syy

    Here, Sxx × Syy = 5 × 20 = 100 bin²·min². Its square root is d = 10 bin·min, because 10 × 10 = 100 and 10 is positive. This perfect square is specific to the chosen data.

    r=810=4/5=0.80

    The centered sums are sufficient: no division by the number of observations is missing from this Pearson formula. If we divided the three sums by the same positive factor to form a covariance and variances, that factor would cancel between the numerator and denominator. Mixing different factors would give a different result.

  6. Check and interpret the coefficient

    A defined Pearson coefficient belongs to [-1 ; 1]. Its positive sign matches the overall rise in the scatter plot. It does not reach 1: the points do not all lie on the same increasing straight line. The spreads are nonzero, and 8 is less than 10; our quotient 0.80 passes these checks.

    The closer r is to 1, the more tightly the points follow an increasing straight line; near -1, they follow a decreasing straight line. Near 0, the positive and negative products offset each other: this does not rule out a curved relationship. We do not assign a universal “weak” or “strong” label based on the number alone.

    r = 0.80 means neither an additional duration in minutes per bin, nor a probability, nor a percentage of successful sessions. A regression slope would answer the question of the predicted change in duration per bin and would have a unit; here, the result only summarizes the linear association.

    An independent check is possible: replace minutes with seconds. The duration deviations and Sxy are multiplied by the same factor as d; r must stay the same. The second exercise performs this check without changing the sessions.

  7. Keep the limitations visible

    The calculation uses all pairs in the data, rather than a selection of points that would support a preferred story. With 4 fictional sessions, the result proves no general pattern. An extreme point, a pairing error, or a selection of sessions can change the coefficient: always keep the scatter plot alongside the number.

    No session has been compared with itself while imposing a different number of bins. Differences in bin contents, interruptions, or the organization of the work may contribute to the observed association. The sign of r settles neither these explanations nor the direction of causality.

    If all durations were identical, every v would be 0, so Syy = 0 min² and d would be 0. Dividing by 0 is impossible: the correlation would be undefined, rather than equal to 0. The final exercise asks you to distinguish this case from a simple absence of linear association.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “The coefficient is positive: sorting more bins must cause a longer session.”

    We compared different sessions. Bin contents or interruptions may also vary. First state the descriptive conclusion, then name an additional piece of information needed to distinguish between the causes.

    Return to the exercise on the conclusion and constant duration

  • “The result gives 0.80 extra minute per bin.”

    The bin·min units cancel in the quotient: r has no units. A predicted change in minutes per bin would require a regression slope. Check this distinction by converting minutes to seconds.

    Return to normalization and interpretation

  • “A constant duration automatically gives a 0 correlation.”

    A constant duration makes all its deviations 0, so Syy and the denominator are 0. The quotient is then undefined. A 0 correlation instead requires a positive denominator and a total Sxy of 0.

    Return to the conditions for the coefficient to be defined and its limitations

Exercise 2 · Check by changing units

The same data in seconds

Convert all durations to seconds: each minute equals 60 s. Before calculating, predict whether r changes. Starting from Sxx = 5 bin², Syy = 20 min², and Sxy = 8 bin·min, recover the sums and denominator in the new unit, then r.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The mean and all duration deviations are multiplied by 60.

Hint 2

A squared deviation receives the square of this factor; a count–duration product receives it only once.

Compare with the solution

The sessions and the physical pattern of the scatter plot are the same, so r must stay the same. Sxx remains 5 bin². Sxy becomes 60 × 8 = 480 bin·s. Syy becomes 60 × 60 × 20 = 60 × 1200 = 72000 s².

The new denominator is 60 × 10 = 600 bin·s: its square is indeed Sxx multiplied by the new Syy. The square root receives the factor 60, rather than its square, because this factor is positive.

The quotient is 480/600 = 4/5 = 0.80, with no units. A result multiplied by 60 would indicate that you forgot to transform the denominator. Keeping the pairings is essential: changing the unit does not move any duration to another session.

Check your reasoning

  • Predict invariance before calculating.
  • Square the factor in Syy, but not in Sxy.
  • Check the square root by squaring it and recover the same r.

Exercise 3 · Diagnosis and limitation

A hasty conclusion, then a constant duration

A colleague announces: “r = 0.80, so the number of bins alone explains the durations of all workshop sessions.” Repair this statement without discarding the calculation. Then imagine that the same 4 sessions had all lasted 7 min: could we assign them r = 0?

Hint 1

Separate what the observed pairs describe from what would need to be demonstrated about all sessions.

Hint 2

Compare with the solution

Repaired statement: “In these fictional data, the number of bins and the duration show a positive linear association, summarized by r = 0.80. This result demonstrates neither an exclusive explanation nor a relationship that holds for all sessions.” Bin contents and interruptions are possible explanations to document, not causes established by r.

If all durations are 7 min, their mean is also 7 min. Each deviation is 7 − 7 = 0 min. Syy and Sxy are then 0; the denominator is 0. The quotient is undefined: writing r = 0 would hide an unmet condition for the calculation.

Studying causes would require a protocol that distinguishes between competing explanations. Extending the finding beyond these data would require justifying which sessions were selected. The arithmetic accuracy of the coefficient replaces none of this work.

Check your reasoning

  • Keep the descriptive conclusion, restricted to these data.
  • Name a competing explanation as a possibility to investigate.
  • Show that the denominator is 0 and conclude “undefined.”

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1Why must the counts and durations from the same session stay together?

Each product of deviations compares two observations associated with the same session. Permuting just one column changes the pairs and may change the correlation, even if the means stay the same.

Recall 2How can you reconstruct the Pearson coefficient from deviations from the means?

Form u = x − x̄ and v = y − ȳ; sum uv for Sxy, the squares of u for Sxx, and the squares of v for Syy; calculate r = Sxy/√(Sxx × Syy). The sums cover the same pairs, with strictly positive spreads.

Recall 3What do the sign and units of our result tell us?

The positive sign summarizes an association that increases overall. The units cancel: 0.80 has no units; it is neither a slope in minutes per bin nor a probability.

Recall 4What is the difference between a 0 correlation and an undefined correlation?

0: Sxy equals 0 and the denominator is positive; this does not rule out a curved relationship. Undefined: a spread is 0, so the denominator is 0. A constant quantity does not provide evidence that no relationship exists.

Recall 5Why does an exact calculation not demonstrate causality?

It describes the selected pairs. On its own, it does not distinguish the effect of the number of bins from that of their contents, interruptions, or the selection of sessions. Evidence suited to distinguishing between the explanations is needed.
