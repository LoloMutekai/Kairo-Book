Level 4 · C261·M1

Extreme values

Your goalEstimate a tail exponent from losses that exceed a threshold, derive a return level beyond the sample, and measure how the tail assumption changes that level.

Before you start

✿01 See the idea

Imagine a fictional portfolio whose loss has been recorded for each trading session over 400 sessions, in basis points (bp; a basis point is one hundredth of a percent of the portfolio value). All the values on this page are invented for the exercise: they are neither measurements nor market results.

The average loss tells us little about the worst session. And the largest observed loss depends on the sample size: with 400 sessions, the data reliably describe only events that recur roughly once every 400 sessions. A loss level that would recur only once every 3200 sessions has usually not been reached in any observed session.

To discuss these levels, we do not model the entire distribution. We keep the sessions whose loss exceeds a threshold u set before examining these losses, and describe how the probability of exceeding a level decreases as the level rises. A power-law decrease, as in the Pareto distribution, is slower than an exponential decrease: this is called a heavy tail.

The page follows a single line of reasoning: estimate a tail exponent using the 8 sessions above the threshold, derive the level reached once every 3200 sessions, then see how much this level moves if the exponent changes. The extrapolation comes from the chosen model, rather than from the data alone.

The rule to remember

Above the threshold, assume that the probability of exceeding x is ρ times (u over x) to the power α; estimate α as one plus u divided by the mean excess; set this probability equal to the reciprocal of the return period to obtain the level. The data support the threshold; the assumption supports the rest.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

The loss level for one session in 3200

We have 400 fictional session losses in bp. Threshold u = 100 bp. The 8 losses above the threshold are 110, 120, 125, 130, 140, 160, 190 and 225 bp. What loss level does the Pareto model associate with one session in 3200?

  1. Define the quantities and assumptions

    L is the loss in a session, in bp. n = 400 is the number of observed sessions. u = 100 bp is the threshold, chosen before examining the losses that exceed it. k = 8 is the number of sessions with L > u. For each one, the excess is E = L − u, in bp. The symbol ρ denotes the proportion of sessions above the threshold, with no unit.

    Model assumptions. The sessions are independent and identically distributed. Above the threshold, the probability of exceeding x follows a Pareto distribution with exponent α, which has no unit, with α greater than 1 so that the mean excess exists:

    P(L>x)=ρ(ux)α pour x≥u

    Nothing in eight points proves these assumptions; they define the calculation that follows.

  2. Relate the mean excess to the exponent

    Given that L exceeds u, the probability of exceeding x is (u over x) to the power α. For a positive variable, the expectation is the area under its survival curve. The mean excess M, in bp, is therefore this area from u to infinity. The integral of x to the power minus α is u to the power (one minus α), divided by (α minus one), for α greater than one: an elementary calculus calculation.

    M=∫u∞(ux)αdx=uα−1

    Rearranging this relationship gives α = one + u over M. It can be used to estimate α as soon as an estimate of M is available.

  3. Estimate the exponent using the observed excesses

    The sum of the 8 losses is 1200 bp. Each loss includes the threshold, so we subtract k × u = 8 × 100 = 800 bp, leaving a total excess of 400 bp. The observed mean excess is 400 / 8 = 50 bp, denoted M̂; the largest excess is 125 bp.

    α^=1+uM^=1+10050=3

    The result 3 is greater than 1, which is consistent with the assumption. This estimator uses only the mean of the excesses. Other estimators, for example ones based on logarithms, exist: they are not calculated here.

  4. Estimate the exceedance frequency

    ρ̂ is the fraction of observed sessions above the threshold: ρ̂ = k / n = 8 / 400, or 0.02. It replaces ρ in the probability of exceeding x.

  5. Solve for the return level

    The return period T, in sessions, is the reciprocal of the exceedance probability: the return level N(T) is exceeded on average once every T sessions, with no guaranteed regularity. Setting ρ (u over N) to the power α equal to the reciprocal of T gives N(T) = u (ρ T) to the power (one over α).

    N(T)=u(ρT)1α

    With T = 3200 and ρ̂ = 0.02, the product ρ̂ T is 64. We therefore need a number r such that r to the power 3 equals 64. Try r = 4: 4 × 4 = 16, then 16 × 4 = 64, equal to 64. The root is exact because the fictional data were chosen that way.

    N=100×6413=100×4=400 pb

  6. Check by substituting into the model

    The check works backwards: u over N = 100 / 400 = 1/4, its square is 1/16, and its cube is 1/64. Multiplied by ρ̂, this probability becomes 1/3200, or 0.000312. This is indeed the reciprocal of 3200.

    Over 3200 sessions, the expected number of sessions beyond 400 bp is 3200 × 1/3200 = 1. The largest observed loss, 225 bp, remains below this level: it was derived rather than observed.

  7. Measure how the tail assumption changes the result

    The same eight excesses do not allow us to rule out α = 2. We then need r to the power 2 equal to 64: r = 8, since 8 × 8 = 64. The level becomes 100 × 8 = 800 bp, compared with 400 bp.

    At 400 bp, the probability is then 1/800 instead of 1/3200: the risk is multiplied by 4. Over 3200 sessions, we expect 4 sessions instead of 1.

  8. State what the sample does not show

    The highest possible empirical quantile is the worst observed loss, 225 bp. Both levels, 400 bp and 800 bp, therefore come from the model. They rely on 8 points, on the independence of the sessions, on the choice of threshold and on fictional values. An honest result reports the level together with the assumption that produces it and the difference caused by another assumption.

Explain it in your own words

Why is the level of 400 bp a consequence of the model rather than an observation? Name the assumption that supports the extrapolation and say what happens with α = 2.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “No session exceeded 300 bp, so the probability of exceeding this level is 0.”

    A frequency of 0 in 400 sessions does not imply a probability of 0. Here the model assigns 0.000741 per session to this level, or 0.30 expected session in the sample: see the second exercise.

    Redo the exercise on observing 0 events

  • “With the best estimate of the exponent, the return level is known.”

    The level depends strongly on the assumption: 400 bp with α = 3, 800 bp with α = 2, for the same excesses. A value without the assumption and its alternative is incomplete.

    Review sensitivity to the tail

  • “A return level for 3200 sessions occurs exactly once every 3200 sessions.”

    The return period describes an expected count: 1 session on average over 3200. The level can be exceeded twice in a row or never over that period.

    Review the definition of the return level

Exercise 1 · Explained application

Without the worst session

Remove the session with the largest loss, 225 bp, and keep the threshold at 100 bp. Recalculate the mean excess over the 7 remaining excesses, then α̂. What does the change show about the reliability of the estimate?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The total excess without the worst session is the total excess minus its largest excess.

Hint 2

Divide by 7, the new number of exceedances, rather than by 8.

Compare with the solution

Remaining total excess: 400 − 125 = 275 bp. Mean excess: 275 / 7 = 275/7, or 39.29 bp. Ratio: 100 divided by this mean excess gives 28/11. Exponent: 28/11 + 1 = 39/11, or 3.5455.

The estimate changes from 3 to 3.55 when just one session out of 400 is removed. The tail appears lighter without the worst loss. With eight excesses, the estimate is fragile: removing or adding an extreme session shifts it substantially.

Check your reasoning

  • Divide by 7 rather than by 8.
  • Distinguish between a loss and an excess.
  • Conclude that the estimate is fragile, without declaring either value exact.

Exercise 2 · Interpretation

Observing 0 does not mean a probability of 0

No session in the sample exceeded 300 bp. With α = 3 and the same ρ̂, what probability does the model give for a session beyond 300 bp? How many sessions are expected among the 400? Compare with the observed count.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Calculate u over the level, then raise it to the power α.

Hint 2

Multiply by ρ̂, then by n.

Compare with the solution

The ratio is 100 / 300 = 1/3. Its square is 1/9, and its cube is 1/27. The probability per session is 1/50 × 1/27 = 1/1350, or 0.000741.

Over 400 sessions, the expected count is 400 × 1/1350 = 8/27, or 0.30 session. The observed count is 0. This expected count is less than one: observing no such session is the most likely outcome. This neither eliminates the risk nor validates the model.

Check your reasoning

  • Calculate the probability and then the expected count, two distinct quantities.
  • Compare the expected and observed counts without concluding that the event is impossible.

Exercise 3 · Reasoned decision

Distinguish between two exponents

One colleague proposes α = 2, another α = 3, with the same ρ̂. No observed session reached 400 bp. For each exponent, calculate the expected number of sessions beyond this level among the 400 sessions. Does the sample distinguish between the two assumptions?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

For α = 3, the probability at this level was calculated in the example; for α = 2, it was too.

Hint 2

Multiply each probability by 400, then compare with the observed count (0) and with one.

Compare with the solution

With α = 3: 400 × 1/3200 = 1/8, or 0.125. With α = 2: 400 × 1/800 = 1/2, or 0.500.

Both counts are less than one and the observed count is 0: the sample is compatible with both. It does not distinguish between them. Over 3200 sessions, the expected counts differ (1 versus 4), but no data cover that horizon. A correct answer states the chosen assumption, the reason for that choice beyond the data, and the difference in level compared with the other assumption (400 versus 800 bp).

Check your reasoning

  • Calculate both expected counts using the same base of 400 sessions.
  • State that the sample is compatible with both exponents.
  • Name the difference in level that remains between the assumptions.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What is an excess on this page?

The difference L − u between a loss above the threshold and that threshold, in bp; only sessions above the threshold are kept.

Recall 2How is α estimated here, and under what condition?

α̂ = 1 + u / M̂, where M̂ is the observed mean excess. The tail is assumed to follow a Pareto distribution above the threshold, with α greater than 1. Here: 100, 50 and 3.

Recall 3What does the return level N(T) mean, and what formula gives it?

The level exceeded on average once every T sessions: N(T) = u (ρ T) to the power one over α. Here N = 400 bp for T = 3200 and α = 3.

Recall 4Why does a return level beyond the sample depend on the assumption?

No observation provides information about the tail there: the chosen shape extends it. The same excesses give 400 bp with α = 3 and 800 bp with α = 2.

Recall 5Does observing 0 prove a probability of 0?

No. With an expected count below one, such as 0.30 here, observing no such session is the most likely outcome.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Set the threshold u before examining the excesses; calculate the excesses L − u and their mean M̂; estimate α̂ = one + u / M̂ under a Pareto tail with α greater than one; estimate ρ̂ = k / n; solve ρ̂ (u / N) to the power α equal to the reciprocal of T to obtain N(T); check by substitution; repeat with another α and state that the extrapolation depends on the assumption.
