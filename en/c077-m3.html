Level 2 · C077·M3

Transcription as a service

Your goalDescribe a transcription call, check its audio input and decide what to do with its result, including when no usable text comes back.

Before you start
c027-m5 : complex numbers · c011-m5 : binomial · c036-m5 : annotated graphs · c021-m5 : origin, authentication and authorization · c077-m1 : sample, frequency and PCM · c077-m2 : window and time measurement

✿01 See the idea

You record a voice memo: “Bring the blue lamp.” The application now has audio. To display proposed text, it sends this audio to a transcription service. If the engine is missing or processing fails, it must also be able to explain this.

Here, a service is a capability called through a contract: what the caller supplies, what processing accepts and what it returns in each situation. The caller is the part of the program that requests transcription; the engine is the part that recognizes speech. The service organizes their exchange. It can stay entirely within the same program: the word service does not require a remote connection.

The contract protects the application from a confusion: receiving a reply does not mean receiving a sentence, and receiving a sentence does not prove that it reproduces the memo exactly. The output is a proposal to present; it does not carry out what the voice requests.

The memo, its bytes, its times and the results described on this page are fictional. We follow a completed recording, processed as a single block. We study neither the recognition algorithm, nor speech synthesis, nor a quality measurement campaign.

The rule to remember

Supply the audio with its format; wait for an explicit outcome; examine status, text and reason together; keep the transcription as a proposal.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

From the “blue lamp” memo to a usable result

The application sends the memo identified as “memo-lampe”: signed 16-bit PCM, least significant byte first, 1 channel, frequency 16000 Hz, 48000 bytes of audio without a header. The requested language is English. The declared duration is 1500 ms. The fictional contract expects a result no later than 1000 ms after the call.

  1. Write down what crosses the boundary

    The request contains the memo identifier, the bytes, the audio format, the requested language and the time budget. The identifier links the result to the correct memo; it does not describe the audio. PCM means that the bytes directly encode the samples. The language is a hint for the engine, not proof that the content is English.

    The result in our contract contains the identifier, a status, optional text and an explanatory reason. The statuses are “proposed text”, “no text”, “unavailable”, “input rejected”, “error” and “timed out”. This teaching contract is a choice for the scene; the fields in the actual code will be examined at the milestone.

  2. Check that the bytes describe the declared memo

    Let B be the number of audio bytes; b the number of bits per sample per channel; c the number of channels; f the frequency in frames per second, numerically in Hz. A frame groups the simultaneous samples from all channels. A byte contains 8 bits. The size of a frame is therefore c × b / 8 bytes.

    Here, 16 / 8 = 2 bytes per sample, then 1 × 2 = 2 bytes per frame. Dividing the total size by the size of a frame counts the frames: 48000 / 2 = 24000. The count is an integer; no frame is cut off.

    D=Bf×c×(b/8)=2400016000=1.50 s

    D is the duration of the audio, in seconds. The units cancel: frames divided by frames per second give seconds. Since a second contains 1000 ms, D equals 1.50 × 1000 = 1500 ms: the declaration agrees. This formula assumes uncompressed PCM of fixed width, no header counted in B, and a constant frequency. It does not apply directly to a compressed file.

  3. Decide whether the engine can be called

    The service checks that the memo is nonempty and complete, that the format is accepted and that the declared duration agrees with the content. The fictional engine accepts exactly the given format. Incompatible input receives “input rejected”, with a reason; changing only a format label does not convert the bytes.

    The service then selects the available engine. An engine is a concrete implementation of processing; the application can keep the same contract if this implementation changes. If no engine is usable, the result reports “unavailable”. No substitute sentence is invented.

  4. Track the call on a single clock

    The memo has already been recorded. The following time markers are expressed in milliseconds since the call: start at 0 ms, engine start at 120 ms, engine finish at 720 ms, result received at 800 ms. We assume a single monotonic clock: it measures durations without moving backwards when civil time is corrected. The stages are sequential, with no overlap.

    Before the engine: 120 − 0 = 120 ms to check, prepare and wait. Engine: 720 − 120 = 600 ms. After the engine: 800 − 720 = 80 ms to prepare and then return the result.

    L=120+600+80=800 ms

    L denotes the caller’s total wait. The recognition stage alone takes 600 ms; the contract must handle the whole wait, 800 ms. The audio duration D is not added here: our starting point is after recording.

  5. Apply the time budget

    Let T be the budget, in milliseconds. Our rule accepts a result that arrives when L ≤ T. Here, 800 ≤ 1000: the result arrives in time. The margin is T − L = 1000 − 800 = 200 ms. Equality would still be accepted under this specific contract.

    The budget is an application rule, not a promise that the engine will finish in time. If the time limit expires, the application reports “timed out” and discards a late return for this request. Stopping the wait does not prove that computation has been interrupted: actual cancellation would require a separate mechanism.

  6. Interpret the outcome instead of blindly displaying a string

    The fictional return matches “memo-lampe”, arrives in time and contains “Bring the blue lamp.” The service returns “proposed text”; the application can present it for review. The punctuation and words remain the engine’s hypothesis. No instruction to bring the lamp is carried out.

    An empty return must come with an outcome: “no text” means that processing finished without producing words; this does not demonstrate acoustic silence. “Unavailable” means that processing could not be provided. “Error” signals a processing failure. The application preserves these differences and displays the useful reason; it does not infer status from text length alone.

  7. Check another way and define the scope

    Audio check: 16000 × 2 = 32000 bytes per second; multiplied by 1.50 s, this gives 48000 bytes again. Wait check: 800 − 0 = 800 ms, as does the sum 720 + 80 = 800 ms. The two checks concern different quantities.

    We have checked input consistency and compliance with the budget for this fictional trace. We have not demonstrated the text’s accuracy: it would need to be compared with the audio. A local call and a remote call can share this contract; the presence of a service therefore demonstrates neither a network, nor actual availability, nor correct words.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “Changing the declared frequency is enough to adapt the memo to the engine.”

    The frequency indicates how to read the frames. Changing this information alone changes the interpreted duration; resampling requires samples to be recalculated. First check the format actually produced by the recording.

    Solve the exercise on the incorrectly declared frequency

  • “The engine finishes within the budget, so the caller receives the result in time.”

    The budget runs from the call to the return. It also includes preparation, waiting before the engine and output processing. Compare the return with the limit set by the contract, using the same clock.

    Review the stages of the wait calculation

  • “The return has no error: the memo must have been understood.”

    A return without an error can be empty or signal unavailability. Nonempty text can also be wrong. Examine status, text and reason together, then distinguish completed processing from accuracy relative to the audio.

Exercise 1 · Warm-up · check the input

The incorrectly declared frequency

The same memo still contains 48000 bytes, with 1 channel and 16 bits. Someone changes only the declared frequency to 32000 Hz, although the recording was actually made at 16000 Hz. What duration does the service calculate? Is it consistent with the declared 1500 ms? Propose a repair.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

The frame size stays the same; only their declared number per second changes.

Hint 2

Divide the 24000 frames by the new frequency. To repair this, distinguish incorrect metadata from actual audio conversion.

Compare with the solution

The service still counts 24000 frames. With the 32000 Hz label, it calculates 24000 / 32000 = 0.75 s, or 750 ms. This contradicts the declared 1500 ms.

The contract in the scene rejects this inconsistency before the engine. Since the actual recording frequency is known, restoring the 16000 Hz metadata repairs the label. If another engine required a different frequency, the audio would need to be converted through resampling; renaming the format would not be enough.

Check your reasoning

  • Calculate 0.75 s while keeping the units.
  • Identify the contradiction with the declared duration.
  • Justify the repair using the actual recording frequency.

Exercise 2 · Application · track the whole call

The result arrives after the limit

The same memo and the same budget of 1000 ms are kept. The engine still starts at 120 ms, but finishes at 1020 ms; the application receives the result at 1100 ms. Calculate the engine duration, the output duration and the total wait. Which outcome should apply, and what do we know about stopping computation?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Subtract consecutive time markers; the total wait starts at 0 ms.

Hint 2

Compare the return time with the budget before examining the words. An expired wait and a cancelled computation are two separate facts.

Compare with the solution

Engine: 1020 − 120 = 900 ms. Output: 1100 − 1020 = 80 ms. Total wait: 120 + 900 + 80 = 1100 ms, confirmed by 1100 − 0.

The budget is exceeded by 1100 − 1000 = 100 ms. The outcome is “timed out” as soon as the budget expires; this late result is not presented as a new success for the request. Yet the engine worked for only 900 ms, less than the budget: its duration alone was not enough to decide.

We know that the caller must stop waiting for this request. We do not know that cancellation interrupted the engine; the contract would need to define this cancellation separately if it were necessary.

Check your reasoning

  • Obtain 900 ms, 80 ms and 1100 ms while naming each duration.
  • State the overrun of 100 ms and discard the late return according to the given rule.
  • Do not infer cancellation from the expiry of the wait.

Exercise 3 · Transfer · repair the interpretation

A reassuring indicator and no words

In a variant of the return for the same memo, the library provides a “no error” indicator, empty text and the reason “no engine available”. The application displays “You said nothing”. Explain the defect, propose the result to return and describe a different case that must remain distinct. No result arrived after the budget.

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

What does the reason say about the processing that was actually possible?

Hint 2

Compare recognition completed without words with recognition that was impossible because no engine was available.

Compare with the solution

The application interpreted the absence of words as an observation of the audio. But the reason indicates that recognition was not provided. The result must report “unavailable”, keep the reason and the memo identifier, then offer another attempt when the engine is usable. It must neither claim silence nor invent text.

An available engine that finishes without producing words belongs to “no text”. Even in this case, silence cannot be asserted: speech that is inaudible to the engine or poorly recognized remains possible. An error reported during processing receives yet another outcome. The contract relies on explicit information from processing, not on an inference from the empty string.

Check your reasoning

  • Identify unavailability from the reason, without equating the indicator with successful transcription.
  • Preserve the link to the memo and a useful explanation.
  • Distinguish unavailability, a result without words and a processing error.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What does the contract of a transcription service define?

The audio input and its format, the accepted conditions, the outcomes returned with text and a reason, and the waiting rule. It allows the caller to use a capability without depending on the engine’s details.

Recall 2Why are bytes not enough to describe the memo?

Their interpretation depends on the encoding, sample width, channels and frequency. For our PCM, duration is the number of bytes divided by the byte rate. Changing the declared frequency does not resample the audio.

Recall 3Why does empty text not allow us to conclude that nobody spoke?

It can accompany recognition without words, unavailability or an error. The outcome and its reason must be read. Even recognition completed without words does not constitute certain detection of silence.

Recall 4Which interval does the budget cover, and what does its expiry imply?

In our contract, it covers the call through to the return, after the memo has been recorded. Its expiry produces a timeout outcome and causes the late return to be discarded. It does not prove that the engine’s computation was cancelled.

Recall 5Does text received in time demonstrate its accuracy and execution of the spoken request?

It demonstrates only an output received according to the contract. Comparing the text with the audio is a separate check. Transcribing “Bring the blue lamp” brings no lamp and authorizes no action.

Reconstruct the reasoning

Without looking at the diagram, reconstruct the memo’s path: bytes and format checked, engine called if available, result linked to the request, budget applied, outcome interpreted with text and reason. Then explain what an empty string and a plausible sentence still leave unknown.
