Level 0 · C010·M3

Exponential function

Your goalRecognize the same factor over equal steps, predict the side length of a photo, and distinguish this zoom from a fixed addition.

Before you start
c006-m5 : composition · c010-m1 : exponents · c010-m2 : roots

✿01 See the idea

You adjust the zoom of a photo whose side initially measures 4 cm. Over each full interval, the setting multiplies the current side length by 2. Picture the next frame before reading its dimensions: what repeats is the proportion by which it is enlarged.

The side length goes from 4 to 8 cm, then from 8 to 16 cm. The first enlargement adds 4 cm; the next adds 8 cm. The same factor applies to a length that has already changed.

For the same photo, let us compare another setting: always adding 4 cm to the side length. The settings agree after the first interval, then diverge. The frames below start from the same edge and use the same scale; we compare their widths, rather than their areas.

All these dimensions are fictional, chosen to explain the rule. A step means a chosen zoom interval; we assign it no duration in seconds. No physical mechanism or actual photo software is being measured.

The rule to remember

Exponential change preserves a factor over equal steps. Look for a constant quotient between successive side lengths; a constant difference describes the other setting.

✿02 Follow an example

Worked example

Predict the zoom, then check what stays constant

The initial side length is 4 cm and doubles over each full interval. Let us calculate the side lengths up to step 3, compare them with adding 4 cm, then decide how to divide an interval into identical half-steps.

  1. Give the symbols meaning

    t denotes the number of intervals elapsed since the start: it has no physical unit. L(t), read as “L of t,” is the side length at setting t, in centimetres. L0 denotes its initial value, 4 cm. The subscript in L0 marks the start. The factor a is the multiplier per interval, with no unit; here a = 2.

    We assume the initial side length is strictly positive and the setting is the same over each equal interval. After a whole number t of steps, repeated multiplication by a is written as a power:

    L(t)=L0×at=4×2t cm

    In the last expression, 4 is the numerical measure of the side length in centimetres. The power counts the factors; it does not simply multiply the number a by t. At the start, a raised to 0 equals 1: the side length is therefore indeed 4 cm.

  2. Apply the factor to the current side length

    At the first step: 4 cm × 2 = 8 cm. At the next, start from this new side length: 8 cm × 2 = 16 cm. Finally: 16 cm × 2 = 32 cm.

    L(3)=4×23=4×8=32 cm

    Why 8? Group the factors: 2 × 2 = 4, then 4 × 2 = 8. Check in reverse: 32 cm ÷ 2 = 16 cm, the side length at the previous step.

  3. Distinguish the factor from the length added

    To check the factor, divide the side length at the end of each interval by the length at its start. The centimetres cancel: 8/4 = 2, 16/8 = 2, 32/16 = 2. The result has no unit.

    To find the addition, instead subtract the lengths. The results are still in centimetres: 8 − 4 = 4 cm; 16 − 8 = 8 cm; 32 − 16 = 16 cm. The additions change, while the ratios stay equal.

    The relative increase divides the added length by the length just before that step. At the first step, 4/4 = 1, or 100 % after multiplying by 100. At the second, 8/8 = 1, again 100 %. The same relative increase applies to a larger base: it adds more centimetres.

  4. Cover the same steps with a fixed addition

    Let A(t) be the side length, in centimetres, under the additive setting. Each step adds c = 4 cm; c denotes this fixed length per step. For t steps, add t copies of c: A(t) = L0 + ct.

    The side lengths become 4, 8, 12, 16 cm at steps 0, 1, 2, 3. At the last step, the total addition is 4 × 3 = 12 cm, so the side length is 4 + 12 = 16 cm.

    Check how the settings diverge: at step 2, the multiplicative zoom exceeds the other by 16 − 12 = 4 cm; at step 3, by 32 − 16 = 16 cm. Agreement at the first step therefore does not make the rules identical.

  5. Choose the factor for a half-step

    A half-step corresponds to t = 1/2, or 0.5 of an interval. Require 2 identical half-steps: their positive factor, denoted b, must compose with itself to give the factor for the whole step. b has no unit; a positive length cannot be multiplied here by a negative factor.

    b×b=2
    b=2;L(12)=4×2 cm

    Keep the root exact, without inventing a decimal value. The check composes the 2 half-steps: (4 × √2) × √2 = 4 × 2 = 8 cm, because √2 × √2 = 2. The fractions of an interval add: 1/2 + 1/2 = 1 interval; their factors multiply.

    Simply dividing the first addition in two would give 4 + 2 = 6 cm at the midpoint: that is the additive setting. For multiplicative zoom, the composition of the factors determines the midpoint.

  6. Recognize the family and limit the conclusion

    The rule that assigns aᵗ to t, with a strictly positive and different from 1, is called the exponential function with base a; L0aᵗ is a version scaled by the initial length. For positive L0, a > 1 increases the side length; 0 < a < 1 reduces it; a = 1 would keep the side length constant. “Exponential” therefore describes a rule; it is not a synonym for “very fast.”

    If h denotes an admissible step size, with no physical unit, the same proportion means L(t + h)/L(t) = aʰ. Indeed, powers satisfy a^(t + h) = aᵗ × aʰ and the factor L0 cancels in the quotient. Here, h = 1 gives the factor 2; h = 1/2 gives √2.

    We have constructed whole steps and half-steps from the powers and roots already studied. Defining this function for every real number and justifying its continuous curve requires an extension that is not proved here. Our check establishes the consequences of the fictional setting; it does not prove any law of growth in the real world.

✿03 Common mistakes

  • “Exponential just means that something increases very quickly.”

    Look for the rule over equal steps: the zoom quotients all equal 2. The size of an addition or an impression of speed is not enough. A positive base less than 1 even describes a reduction.

    Review the quotients and the definition

  • “An increase of 100 % always adds the same length.”

    First name the base: 100 % of 4 cm adds 4 cm; 100 % of 8 cm adds 8 cm. The percentage has no unit; the added length is in centimetres and depends on the current side length.

    Compare the additions on the frames

  • “Halfway through a doubling, the factor must be 1.5.”

    Test its composition: 1.5 × 1.5 = 2.25, which does not give 2 again. The required positive factor is √2. The half-step exercise asks you to check both proposals against the same endpoint.

    Test the factor for the half-steps

Exercise 2 · Application · compare on paper

Same start, same first result: same rule?

For the same photo, one setting doubles the side length and the other adds 4 cm per step. List the side lengths at steps 0, 1, 2, 3. Compare the additions, the quotients between neighbouring values, and the final difference. Is agreement at the first step enough to identify an exponential rule?

Rate your confidence before comparing

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Write a row for each setting. Each new cell starts from the previous cell.

Hint 2

Calculate the differences and then the quotients in separate rows; keep the same reference when comparing the settings.

Compare with the solution

Multiplicative zoom: 4, 8, 16, 32 cm. The additions are 4, 8, 16 cm; the quotients are 2, 2, 2.

Fixed addition: 4, 8, 12, 16 cm. Each difference equals 4 cm. The quotients are first 2, then 3/2 (or 1.5), then 4/3: they vary.

At the last step, 32 − 16 = 16 cm. The first shared result is not enough to choose between these rules: the repeated operations over the following steps distinguish them. Even several matching points would not, by themselves, prove that a real photo will always follow the chosen law.

Check your reasoning

  • Write the side lengths in step order with the unit cm.
  • Identify a constant quotient for the zoom and a constant difference for the fixed addition.
  • Find the final difference of 16 cm and explain why the shared first step is not enough.

Exercise 3 · Transfer · correct a proposal

Two half-steps that must compose to give a doubling

To double the side length over one interval, someone proposes multiplying by 1.5 at each half-step. Test the result after two half-steps starting from 4 cm. Then propose the correct positive factor, give the exact side length at the midpoint, and justify your check without rounding the root.

Assisted answer: try this exercise without help later.

Hint 1

Both half-steps use the same factor; compose it with itself before calculating the final length.

Hint 2

If b is this positive factor, what equation links b × b to the factor for the whole step?

Compare with the solution

The proposal corresponds to the exact factor 3/2. Repeating it gives 3/2 × 3/2 = 9/4, or 2.25. The final side length would be 4 × 9/4 = 9 cm, instead of 8 cm: 1 cm too much.

The factor b must be positive and satisfy b × b = 2. The positive root therefore gives b = √2, with no unit. At the midpoint, the side length is exactly 4√2 cm.

(42)×2=4×2=8 cm

The check gives exactly the side length for the whole step. Taking half the addition would have given 6 cm at the midpoint: this average of the lengths belongs to the additive setting, rather than to two identical multiplicative factors.

Check your reasoning

  • Refute the proposal by obtaining 9 cm after two half-steps, with a difference of 1 cm.
  • Justify choosing the positive root using b × b = 2.
  • Give 4√2 cm as the exact length at the midpoint and compose the steps to obtain 8 cm without rounding.

✿05 Recall from memory

Close the lesson, answer each question, then reveal the answer. Explain any missing steps in your own words.

Recall 1What stays constant in exponential zoom over equal steps?

The quotient between successive side lengths: the same factor a multiplies the current side length. The additions in centimetres can vary. The rule applies to equal steps, rather than an impression of speed.

Recall 2In L(t) = L₀aᵗ, what does each symbol denote, and in what unit?

t counts the intervals since the start, with no physical unit; L(t) is the side length in cm; L₀ is the initial side length in cm; a is the factor per interval, with no unit, and is strictly positive. For this exponential family, a differs from 1.

Recall 3Why does the same percentage increase not always add the same length?

The percentage applies to the side length at the start of the step. With a 100 % increase, 4 cm gives an addition of 4 cm; 8 cm gives an addition of 8 cm. The proportion stays equal; its base changes.

Recall 4How can you find the factor for a half-step without dividing the addition in two?

Two identical half-steps must compose to give a whole step: b × b = 2. The positive factor is √2; repeating it gives exactly a doubling.

Recall 5Does calculating the side lengths of our photo prove a general law of real growth?

No. It checks the consequences of a fictional, explicit setting. For a real situation, we would need to establish the rule, the meaning of the steps, and its limits; agreement between a few side lengths is not enough.
